# Teardown template

Every teardown uses these sections so they can be compared. Give numbers wherever you can: a range with its source beats a guess. Label every number **measured** (from data, a map or a tool), **derived** (calculated from something measured) or **estimated**.

## Units
- **Screen** = one camera view at the game's default zoom. Also give sizes in **player heights**, so they convert to *Tallage*: player 80 px tall, 64 px tiles, and one screen is 30 × 16.9 tiles, or 1920 × 1080 px, which is 13.5 player heights tall.

## Sections
1. **Overview:** role in the game, when the player arrives, which abilities they have, first-pass play time, and total rooms/scenes.
2. **Room size distribution:** width and height in screens and player heights (min, median, max), the share of horizontal vs vertical vs large "set-piece" rooms, and a table of the 5 largest rooms.
3. **Graph structure:** nodes and edges, loops, dead ends, hub degree, one-way drops, shortcuts (count, and where they unlock), entrances and exits to other areas, and critical path length vs total.
4. **Rest and map:** spacing of save points (rooms and minutes between them), how the map is acquired, and death-run length.
5. **Landmarks and sightlines:** memorable landmarks (count, spacing, type), vistas and reveals, and how the player stays oriented.
6. **Traversal rhythm:** the typical length of continuous movement chains, platforming density, hazards, and the ratio of calm to intense stretches. Ori areas: the flow chains.
7. **Encounters:** enemy density per screen, types, placement patterns, and arena vs roaming fights.
8. **Secrets and rewards:** density, types, and how they're signposted.
9. **Area identity:** palette, lighting, parallax and foreground layering, music and ambience, silhouette and shape language, environmental storytelling, and the terrain shape vocabulary (rectilinear vs organic, slopes, curves).
10. **Camera and transitions:** camera behaviour, room transition style and timing, seamless vs cut.
11. **Teaching:** how abilities and threats are introduced (introduce, test, twist, combine).
12. **Why it works:** qualitative analysis of composition, pacing, curiosity hooks and reveals. What the numbers miss.
13. **What *Tallage* should steal, adapt or avoid**, with concrete target numbers in our units.
14. **Sources:** links for every factual claim.
