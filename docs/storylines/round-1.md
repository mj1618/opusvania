# Ten storylines for the moth

2026-10-16. The only fixed point is the hero: the dark moth with glowing eyes. Every pitch below follows the rules in
`docs/research/story-direction.md`:
- the story fits in one sentence
- the threat lives in the level as a system, not in a cutscene
- the opening is playable within 10 seconds and shows one lit goal
- there is at most one companion, and any relationship is carried by a mechanic, not by acting

Reference games for each pitch are in `memory/reference-games.md`.

## Moth or butterfly? Moth.

- **Our art is night art.** Matt keeps asking for darker scenes and small, precious lights. Butterflies fly by day and need
  bright, saturated worlds. Moths own the dark.
- **Light is a ready-made mechanic and theme.** Moths fly to the moon and get fooled by lamps. That one fact gives us a
  verb, a danger and a symbol, and light is the art team's strongest skill. Butterflies have nothing as strong; the
  closest is migration.
- **It fits the audience.** Hollow Knight, Ori and Rain World players love small underdog creatures with a melancholy, slightly eerie edge. Haiku's
  "cute yet creepy" look was credited with selling it. A moth reads as mysterious and underdog. A butterfly reads as pretty and
  gentle, which leans toward cozy games and a younger audience.
- **The internet already loves moths** (the moth-and-lamp memes). That is free goodwill for a trailer.
- **Our character already is one:** fuzzy, dark plum, glowing eyes.

Pick a butterfly only if we later want a brighter, gentler, broader game. That isn't the game the demo is.

## 1. Moonward

**In one line.** Every moth flies by the moon, but someone has hung a thousand false moons over the world, and moths circle
them until they drop. Wing flies for the real one.

**The real moth fact.** Moths steer by keeping the moon's light above their backs. A lamp close by breaks this, and they spiral
around it until they're exhausted.

**What you do.** Lights pull on her like gravity. At first lamps are traps you fight free of. Then they become slingshots
you whip around (like Ori's Bash). Later she can snuff them out. The dark is safe, the light is danger and speed.

**Opening.** She hatches on a branch under a pale real moon. Across the valley, a hundred warm lamps glow, and a cloud of moths circles the
nearest one. She drifts toward it, the pull catches her, and she has to beat free. The real moon rises above it, and the title appears.

**World question.** Who hung the lamps, and why do they never go out?

**Bosses.** A mantis that waits under the brightest lamp. A lantern-jar she breaks out of from the inside. The lighthouse
at the top of the world.

**Ending.** She puts out the last light, and the sky opens to the real moon and every star. The circling moths fly free.

**Study.** Journey (the goal in view), Ori (Bash), Inside.

**Why it suits us.** It's built on lighting, our best skill. The goal is visible from the first minute, so the game is never aimless. No metroidvania has
this mechanic. **Risk:** the pull has to feel great, never annoying.

## 2. The Long Flight

**In one line.** Wing hatched one night too late and missed the great migration. She must cross the whole country alone, following
the signs the swarm left, to reach the mountain cave where her kind sleeps out the summer.

**The real moth fact.** Bogong moths fly up to 1,000 km to the same alpine caves every year, steering by the stars and
Earth's magnetic field. Billions used to make it. Far fewer do now.

**What you do.** Each region is a leg of the route: farm, town, river, mountains. The swarm's traces are the trail: wing-dust on
walls, a moth asleep on a windowsill. Abilities include longer glides, riding the wind, and reading the stars to open doors locked by constellations.

**Opening.** Her cocoon is the last whole one among a thousand split ones. Overhead, the tail of the swarm, a river of wings,
crosses the stars. She chases it to the edge of the wood and falls short as it disappears over the ridge. Title.

**World question.** Why do fewer moths reach the cave every year?

**Bosses.** A harvester in the wheat. The bat colony under the bridge. A glass tower whose lights kill migrating insects.

**Companion (optional).** An old moth with a torn wing, met halfway. She can pull him up on updrafts (Brothers style).

**Ending.** She reaches the cave. Its ceiling is carpeted with sleeping moths, or there are far fewer than there should be.

**Study.** Journey, Rain World, Ori.

**Why it suits us.** A journey always gives direction, and every region is a new set for the art team. **Risk:** journeys are
linear. Backtracking needs strong reasons, such as the swarm's hidden caches and shortcuts.

## 3. One Night

**In one line.** Once a century a giant white flower at the heart of the forest opens for a single night, and only a moth can
carry its pollen. If no moth does, the forest dies with it. Wing has until sunrise.

**The real moth fact.** The yucca moth and the yucca plant can only reproduce through each other. Many night flowers open only for moths,
and many adult moths live only a few days.

**What you do.** Glowing scent trails lead toward the flower. Pollen she shakes off makes plants burst into growth,
which turns into new platforms and bridges, so pollinating is how she gets around. The moon crosses the sky as she finishes each region,
which shows progress without a timer.

**Opening.** Dusk. A bud the size of a house glows on the hill above the canopy, and a thread of scent drifts past her antennae.
Title, over the bud.

**World question.** Why has no moth reached the flower in a hundred years?

**Bosses.** A spider that has webbed over the scent paths. A toad in the pond that eats anything bright. The dried husk of the
hawkmoth that tried a century ago.

**Ending.** She reaches the flower as the sky turns grey. Dawn breaks over a forest blooming white, and she rests on a petal as the
light takes her. It's bittersweet, Ori style, and played rather than watched.

**Study.** Ori, Gris (colour coming back), Journey.

**Why it suits us.** Every room ends with plants blooming, and a dawn finale shows off the lighting. It fits the current forest
demo directly. **Risk:** her death at the end has to be earned.

## 4. Half-Formed

**In one line.** Something is tearing the wood's cocoons open too early. Wing crawls out half-made, with crumpled wings and stub
antennae, and has to finish becoming a moth while she hunts down whatever is doing it.

**The real moth fact.** Inside a cocoon, a caterpillar dissolves and rebuilds itself. And parasitic wasps really do lay their eggs inside
caterpillars.

**What you do.** Each ability is her body finishing its change: the wings unfurl (glide), then harden (dash), the eyespots
appear (flash), and the antennae grow (sense). Every upgrade visibly changes the character, so she gets more beautiful as you play.

**Opening.** Dark and cramped. The silk tears, and she drops to the ground with wet, crumpled wings. She can only crawl. Above, every
cocoon hangs open and empty. Something big moves off through the trees. Title.

**World question.** Who is opening the cocoons, and what became of everyone born too early?

**Bosses.** A giant stuck halfway between caterpillar and moth. The early-born, grown strange (tragic, Hollow Knight style).
The wasp queen.

**Ending.** She breaks open the wasp nest. The stolen cocoons hatch whole, and she is the first of them.

**Study.** Hollow Knight (tragic enemies), Metroid (the body as the upgrade), Animal Well.

**Why it suits us.** It puts the character, our best asset, at the centre, and makes every upgrade a visible art beat. **Risk:** the first
hour has a plainer moth, and she's the thing Matt loves.

## 5. The Owl Wood

**In one line.** Every night a great owl hunts the wood, and it has eaten every moth but one. Wing has to survive it, learn it,
and finally face it with nothing but the eyes painted on her wings.

**The real moth fact.** Many moths have eyespots that they flash like an owl's face to startle birds. Some can hear bat
sonar and drop out of the air.

**What you do.** The owl is a predator that roams the whole map, like Rain World's lizards. Its shadow crosses the moon before it strikes.
She can freeze against bark (moths match their bark), drop, or flash her eyespots at the last instant to make it flinch. She fights the
small things normally. Abilities include bigger eyespots, hearing that lets her sense the owl through walls, and better camouflage.

**Opening.** She rests on bark beside another moth. A shadow crosses the moon, the other moth is gone, and a feather spins down. Hide or
fly. The title appears over the owl's silhouette against the moon.

**World question.** Why is the owl eating everything? It's starving. Something has emptied the wood of mice.

**Bosses.** A bat swarm (sonar duels). The shrike's larder, a grim tableau of prey on thorns. The owl in its hollow.

**Ending.** Two endings: she stares the owl down, or she fixes what is starving the wood.

**Study.** Rain World, Shadow of the Colossus (one great foe), Inside (hiding).

**Why it suits us.** One unforgettable creature means one unforgettable trailer image and tension that plays well on stream. It fits the forest demo
as it is. **Risk:** stealth can frustrate. The owl must be learnable, never random.

## 6. Honey Thief

**In one line.** Wing is a thief who can smell like a bee. She breaks into a golden hive-city the size of a mountain to steal
the royal jelly that will save her dying brood.

**The real moth fact.** The death's-head hawkmoth raids beehives for honey, mimicking the hive's scent so the bees leave it
alone. It squeaks when caught.

**What you do.** A heist metroidvania. Scent is her disguise: while she smells right, the guards ignore her. If she's hit or smells wrong, the hive turns on her
and the fight goes loud. Each caste has its own scent, and each scent opens a district.

**Opening.** Night in a meadow. The hive glows gold inside a hollow oak like a lit city. She rolls in a flower bed, slips between two
guards who sniff her and let her pass, and the title appears over the golden interior.

**World question.** Why has the hive walled itself up like a fortress? The queen is dying, and the hive is hoarding.

**Bosses.** The guard captain, the wax architect, the queen's champion, the queen.

**Study.** Dead Cells (attitude), Hollow Knight (the Hive), and heist games outside the genre.

**Why it suits us.** It's a fresh hook. Gold honeycomb against her dark plum makes a striking palette, and the goal is clear. The tone is cheekier
than the others. **Risk:** Hollow Knight has a Hive area, and the forest demo becomes only the outside hub.

## 7. The Glasshouse

**In one line.** Wing hatched inside a vast abandoned greenhouse that machines still run long after the people left. She has never seen
the sky. The machines pollinate everything now, and they treat insects as pests.

**What you do.** She climbs a tower of glass terraces toward one cracked pane at the top where the moonlight gets in. The machines
are both hazards and traversal: misting fans to ride, sprinkler drones, robot bees, and UV bug-zappers whose light lures and kills moths.

**Opening.** She hatches on a fern under glass while condensation runs down the panes. Far above, through one broken pane, is the moon. A robot bee
drifts past and zaps a beetle. The title appears over the cracked pane.

**World question.** Where did the gardeners go, and who is the garden still being kept for?

**Bosses.** The pest-control unit, the irrigation heart, and the head gardener machine, still waiting for someone.

**Ending.** She squeezes through the broken pane into a wild night full of moths, or she opens the roof for every insect inside.

**Study.** Inside, Animal Well, Tunic.

**Why it suits us.** Glass, rain, light and plants are all rendering strengths, and the vertical goal is easy to read. It keeps a gentle
echo of Matt's original "machines took over" idea. **Risk:** machines against nature is familiar territory.

## 8. The House

**In one line.** Wing lives in the walls of an old woman's house, and the house is being packed away box by box. Before the last
light goes out, she explores it from a moth's height and pieces together the life that was lived there.

**The real moth fact.** Clothes moths live their whole lives indoors, in wardrobes and wool.

**What you do.** A human house is the metroidvania map at tiny scale: the wardrobe, the piano, the attic, the garden shed. Objects tell the story.
There are photos, a wedding dress, a child's drawings, and letters, with no words except what's written on things. The threats are the house spider, the
cat, mousetraps, the vacuum and the movers.

**Opening.** It's dark inside the wardrobe among the wool coats, with a strip of light under the door. She squeezes out into a moonlit bedroom of
boxes and sheeted furniture. One lamp is still lit in the window downstairs. Title.

**World question.** Who lived here, and what happened to her?

**Bosses.** The cat, the spider in the piano, the vacuum, the moving van.

**Ending.** The last room completes the woman's story. Wing slips out of the window into the night as the house goes dark.

**Study.** Inside and Gris, plus What Remains of Edith Finch and Unpacking from outside the genre.

**Why it suits us.** The story is told with props instead of acting, which is exactly what our team can stage well. A tiny moth in a giant human house makes a striking
trailer. It's the most human and the most original pitch. **Risk:** it needs a new art kit for interiors, and the forest becomes the garden.

## 9. The Blind Giant

**In one line.** A huge, old, nearly blind beetle is lost far from home, and Wing's glowing eyes are the only light he can follow. She
flies, he climbs and smashes, and neither gets anywhere alone.

**What you do.** The relationship is the mechanic (like Ico and Brothers). He follows her light. She scouts, lights the way and opens paths
for him. He lifts her past gates, carries her through water and breaks walls. Some stretches force her to leave him and come back, and
you feel it. Landing on his head to rest is the save point.

**Opening.** A storm and a fallen tree. A massive beetle lies on his back in the mud with his legs waving. She lands and her eyes glow, and he turns
toward the light. A few flights later he's upright and following her. The title appears over them: a small light and a big shadow.

**World question.** Where is his home, and why did he leave it?

**Bosses.** Birds diving at him while she defends. A river crossing. A spider that drags him off. The last climb home.

**Ending.** They reach home: a hollow full of his kind, or he's the last one. He lifts her up toward the moon one more time.

**Study.** Ico, Brothers, Journey.

**Why it suits us.** It gives the relationship Matt asked for, with no dialogue or staged acting, and it plays very well on stream. **Risk:** the companion AI has to
be excellent (Ico took years), and a slow partner can drag the pace.

## 10. Soul Moth

**In one line.** In the old stories, moths carry the souls of the dead up to the moon. Wing is the last soul-moth, and the lights of the
dead are piling up in the dark with no one to carry them.

**The real moth fact.** In folklore across many cultures, a moth at the window is a soul visiting.

**What you do.** She gathers drifting soul-lights, which literally light her way: the more she carries, the more of the world she sees, but the
more hungry things notice her. Each light shows a brief wordless memory as an image, not acting. That gives cheap, optional story at scale.
She releases them at moon shrines.

**Opening.** Lights float through a dark wood like dandelion seeds. She touches one and it settles glowing on her back. A path of old
moth shrines climbs toward a low moon. Title.

**World question.** Why did the other soul-moths stop flying?

**Bosses.** The Hoarder, who keeps souls. The ferryman bird. The moon gate.

**Ending.** She carries the last of them up and becomes a light herself.

**Study.** Hollow Knight, Ender Lilies, and Spiritfarer from outside the genre (letting go).

**Why it suits us.** The world lights up as you collect, which pays off the lighting work, and the mythic tone fits the Hollow Knight audience. **Risk:** death and spirit
themes are common in the genre.

## 1 + 2 merged: Moonward, to the caves

**In one line.** Every spring the moths fly by the moon to a cave in the mountains to sleep. This year the lamps came, and the swarm
broke apart circling them. Wing flies for the real moon, and the cave beneath it.

**The real moth facts that hold it together.**
- Bogong moths steer by the night sky to the same alpine caves every year.
- Bogong moths really do get lost in city lights on the way. Huge swarms have ended up circling the floodlights of Parliament House in Canberra.
- Cave glowworms really do hang glowing threads from cave roofs that look like a starry sky, and they snare flying insects with them.

**How it fits together.**
- **The goal is always in view.** The mountain sits under the moon on the horizon from the first frame. It's the same view for the whole game.
- **The lamps stand between the wood and the mountain:** farms, a town, a road, a beacon on the pass. They are
  traps, then slingshots, then things she puts out, as in Moonward.
- **The swarm replaces the villain.** At every lamp, moths from the broken swarm are circling. Each lamp she puts out frees a cloud of them, and they fly on
  ahead to the mountain. No one needs to have "hung the lamps on purpose". They belong to a world that never notices moths.
- **Freeing moths drives the backtracking.** Lamps you can't reach yet are the reason to come back with new abilities. The tally of freed
  moths is the completion counter.
- **The cave is the last region, and it's the twist.** Inside it's black, and then the roof lights up like a sky full of stars.
  They're glowworms. The moths that went in first hang caught in their threads. It's the game's lesson turned upside down: even the stars can lie.
  She has to climb through it by what she has learned: stay out of the light.
- **Ending.** The true sleeping chamber is deep inside, in total darkness, and it's full of sleeping moths. Every moth you freed flies in and settles
  beside her. How full the ceiling is depends on how many you freed, so the last shot is your playthrough. The dark that was safety all
  game is now home.

**Opening (playable).** She is the last to hatch. Overhead the swarm streams past the moon toward the mountain. She chases it
over the edge of the wood and sees it break apart over the valley lamps, a hundred glowing knots of circling moths. One lamp catches her too. She
beats free, the moon rises over the mountain, and the title appears.

**Bosses.**
- The mantis under the brightest lamp.
- The bat colony under the bridge, from The Long Flight.
- The beacon on the pass.
- The glowworm roof, a whole region that works like a boss.

**What it takes from each.** From Moonward: the mechanic, the theme and the lodestar. From The Long Flight: the destination, the swarm, a reason
to care (getting her kind home), and an ending that pays off how much you did. Its weakness is gone too. The route is no longer a one-way journey,
because the lamps are scattered around a metroidvania map with the mountain at the far end.

**Cost.** The cave and the mountain are new art kits, but late ones. The first half stays in the forest and the valley. The swarm needs
a cheap way to draw a crowd of moths, and particle work like that is a strength.

## Moonward, to the caves: revision 1 (after Matt's questions and an AI-feasibility pass)

**Changes**
1. **Who lit the lamps: the glowworms that took the cave.** The lamps are living lures: glowing pods the brood hangs on
   threads through the woods along the migration route, to catch the moths flying to the cave. "This year the lamps came" because
   this year the glowworms moved into the moths' cave. The threat in the opening is the threat in the finale. Real biology: cave
   glowworms are fly larvae that catch insects with light and sticky threads. A side benefit: the lamps are organic, so there's no human town to
   build, and every region stays in the natural art kit (wood, valley, marsh, pass, cave).
2. **The mountain at vistas, not every frame.** You see it through canopy gaps, from clifftops, and in a camera lift each time a lamp goes out.
   It looks bigger in each region. Underground, the freed moths flying off show the way.
3. **The opening is a place, not an event.** She hatches on a branch. In front of her, a lure in the valley with moths circling it. Above it, the moon over
   the mountain. Control comes within 10 seconds. That's the baseline. The staged version (the swarm chase and break-up) gets tried as an upgrade when we reach it.
4. **Bosses are mostly lights and places, plus slow, big creatures:**
   - a grove of lures that pull from every side
   - the beacon on the pass (a lure the size of a tree)
   - the bat colony (reusing the swarm system)
   - the cave roof
   - the brood queen, a huge, slow, glowing larva

   - the mantis under the brightest lamp, a fast fighter (kept: we try it, and cut it only if it fails)
5. **The swarm carries the emotion.** Moths she frees fly alongside her for a while before heading on. The final chamber fills with
   every moth she saved.

**The new one line.** Glowworms have taken the moths' cave and hung false moons along the way to catch them. Wing flies by the real
moon to take the cave back.

**How well AI can build each part**

| Part | Difficulty for us | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Lamp pull and slingshot | medium | It's a plain force in the deterministic sim, and the game already has light-based rules. Making it *feel* good needs playtests with Matt. |
| Putting out lamps, freed-moth count | easy | The reverse of the lantern goal that already exists. |
| Lures, moon, mountain vistas | easy | Lighting and sky layers are our strongest work. |
| The swarm (circling, freed, the final crowd) | medium | Particles and flocking, not acting. The risk is moths reading as dots. |
| Regions (valley, marsh, pass, cave) | medium–hard | The amount of art is the real cost. The forest took many rounds. All natural kits, no buildings. |
| Light bosses (lure grove, beacon, cave roof) | easy–medium | Environmental puzzles made of lights. |
| Brood queen (slow, big, glowing) | medium | Big and slow means little animation. Readable telegraphs matter most. |
| A fast fighter boss (the mantis) | hard, **try it** | Needs expressive, fast animation. Build it when we get there; cut it only if it doesn't work. |
| Scripted scenes (swarm chase, acting) | hard, **try it** | Failed in the old prologue, but the models keep getting better. Try them when we get there; cut them only if they don't work. |

**My pick: B, the last firefly.** It's the only option with a character you feel for, and it gives the game a moving, wordless ending without needing a written plot.
Fallback: **A** if the firefly's acting doesn't work.

### Does an enemy that isn't really bad work?

Yes. Ori's owl Kuro, the colossi in Shadow of the Colossus, and the Hollow Knight itself are all antagonists who aren't evil. Rain World's
predators are just hungry. It works when:
1. **The fighting is against things that really are dangerous.** Predators gather wherever moths circle: spiders, the mantis, bats, toads.
   That happens in real life at lights too. The firefly is the *cause*, not the everyday enemy.
2. **The firefly is still a real threat.** Every lamp she puts out is its hope going dark, so it fights for its lights. That's a clash of wants, not good against evil.
   The final fight is hard. The mercy is in the ending, not in the difficulty.
3. **The reason comes out late.** Early on it's a light that flees and relights lamps behind her. The truth lands near the end, as Kuro's does.
4. **The stakes stay real.** Moths really die at the lamps. Saving them is the motivation, so no villain is needed to hate.
