**How far I got:** the whole area, from the cocoon to "STUB: the canopy road (area 2 starts here)". That took **6:32 of game time**, which is 392 s of sim time. The tally at the end read lamps 8/30 and moths 40/150. I got glide at 1:39 and wall jump at 5:08. I beat the Old Burrback without taking a hit. I backtracked once: down to the roots, then up the oak.

## 2. Best 3, worst 5, lost stretches, unfair hits, boredom

**Best 3**
1. **The aimed lamp-sling** (`0401-after.png` shows the arc aimed at the next pillar; `0633-after.png` then `0635-after.png` show me aiming up-right in the oak and landing on the ledge). Being grabbed turns into a launch you control. That's the game's best idea.
2. **The Old Burrback** (`0447-after.png` windup with the red floor line, `0461-after.png` belly-up, `0487-killed-a-oldBurrback.png`). Honest telegraphs, a clear punish window, no cheap hits.
3. **Abilities with a story reason, and the hidden charm** (`0123-gained-glide.png` "your wings have dried", `0495-gained-wallJump.png` "your claws have hardened", `0271-after.png` Quick wings behind the bark wall). Each one changed how I saw rooms I'd already passed.

Honourable mention: the crown (`0646-after.png`, `0652-burst.png`): moon, moths, fireflies. A place.

**Worst 5**
1. **The stair before I knew about aiming** (`0350-after.png`, `0370-burst.png`, `0407-burst.png`: hitting the pillar wall just under the lip). About 7 falls, about 40 s of game time, and more like 10 minutes at the desk. The route depends on a mechanic the game never shows.
2. **Shelled beetles before the boss teaches you** (`0101-hit-by-burrback-1-4-left-.png` 4 to 1 health in the burrow; `0155-after.png` a beetle parked under a lamp in the glidewalk). Three areas of "my sword sparks off it and I don't know why".
3. **Hit by a hawker while held in a lamp** (`0254-hit-by-hawker-3-4-left-.png`). I couldn't act at all.
4. **A spider drops on the pillar I'm put back on** (`0383-hit-by-dangler-2-4-left-.png`). A fall should be a reset, not a second punishment.
5. **Locked camera moments that look like a freeze** (`0191-after.png` to `0197-after.png` in the hollow; `0652-burst.png` to `0661-after.png` in the crown). Five and ten seconds of my presses doing nothing, with no letterbox or other "this is a scene" sign.

Also: tiny steps (a lamp base, `0282`) stop a walk dead until you hop. The oak/roots seam bounced me between rooms (`0548` to `0556`). The thorn strip on the trunk wall punished wall-jump overshoot (`0616`, `0622`).

**Lost or stuck stretches**
- 0:50 to 0:57, the first lights: how do I put out the high lamps? I never found out.
- 2:45 to 3:18, hawkers' clearing: the nook. Going over failed; the way in was under. Stuck, but I solved it, which felt good.
- 3:40 to 4:22, the stair: stuck until I discovered aiming by accident.
- 5:10 to 5:50, the chimney and trunk: mostly my own wall-jump clumsiness, plus the room-seam bounce.

**Unfair hits:** the burrow chain at 1:20 to 1:26 (lamp grab, then dropped onto the beetle); the hawker while I was held at 3:05; the dangler on the respawn pillar at 4:12.

**Boredom:** only the boss's last 3 cycles, same pattern with no escalation. The glidewalk's middle was a touch empty, but short.

## 3. One journey or unrelated rooms?

**One journey, mostly.** Three things pulled me forward the whole time:
- **The flock and the big tree.** The hatching branch shows the moths flying away. The chase streams them into the dark tree with a halo, and that silhouette stays on the horizon and grows. I always knew where "there" was.
- **The lamps.** Every room has the same language: false lights that grab moths, cut them to free moths and heal, and their arcs become routes later. That threads the rooms together.
- **The companion turning up at each moon patch.** That made the patches feel like stops on one road.

**What the big tree meant:** the moths' home, and my goal. Walking straight through its base and out the far side at 2:40 deflated me; I'd arrived and it shrugged. But getting wall jump, thinking "the oak shaft", and climbing it to the crown with the moon was the emotional peak of the run. In hindsight the walk-through set up the return, but in the moment I needed a hint that I'd be back. For example: the camera pan up the trunk could end on something I want, and I could fail one visible climb.

**The little moth:** I'm the moth, freshly hatched, and the freed moths swirling round me made that feel good. The tally made freeing them feel like the point. The little hunched companion was a quiet presence. I liked finding it by every save, but it never did anything and I never learned its name. At the crown it sat next to me while my moths flew off to the moon, and I wanted *one* beat there: a look, a line, anything. Without that, the ending reads as "your moths left you" rather than a triumph or a goodbye.

## 4. Was it fun? Would I keep playing?

**Yes, and yes.** I want to see the canopy road.
- **Most fun:** aiming a lamp-sling up the oak, the Old Burrback, the moment wall jump made me think of the oak, and finding Quick wings.
- **Least fun:** the stair before I knew about aiming, beetles before the boss explains them, and being hit while helpless in a lamp.
- What would make me quit: one more "untaught rule" stretch like the stair.

## 5. Compared with the first 30 minutes of Ori and Hollow Knight (not visuals)

**As good:**
- Abilities given with a story reason ("wings dried", "claws hardened"), like Ori's tree abilities, and gated so the world reopens. The oak shaft is a textbook HK "remember that wall?".
- Moon patches work like HK benches: save, heal, a place to breathe. They're frequent enough.
- Falls cost no health and put you back nearby, which is Celeste-kind. I never felt the urge to quit after a fall.
- Enemy telegraphs: the hawker target rings and the burrback's red floor line are clearer than most of early HK.
- One clear landmark that pulls you on (the tree), like Ori's Spirit Tree.

**Missing:**
- **Teaching in safe spaces.** HK's first crawlers and Ori's early wall jump teach one thing with no penalty. Here the lamp arc, the beetle rule and the bark rule were each first met under pressure, or found by accident.
- **Voice and people.** Ori has the narrator and Sein; HK has Elderbug and the fallen-knight lore. I had two one-line ability texts and a tally stone. The companion is a hook with no payoff yet.
- **Reward texture.** A boss with no drop or fanfare. Moths and lamps tally up, but I don't know what they buy me. No currency, no map-buying, no charm slots beyond one find.
- **Combat depth.** One cut, and most enemies shrug it off from the front. Fine for area 1, but HK's first half-hour already has the nail feel varied by enemy. Here, hitting things is mostly sparks.
- **A sense of danger.** 0 deaths in the whole area. Generous is good, but nothing ever made me careful.

## 6. Top 5 changes

1. **Teach the lamp-sling before the stair.** Put one lamp over a short, safe gap (the first lights or the glidewalk) where the arc obviously has to be aimed onto a ledge. Show "hold a direction to aim" once, the first time a moth is grabbed. After that, the stair is a test, not a riddle.
2. **Teach the beetle rule before the burrow's adult.** Give the first adult room to charge into a wall and flip on its own, so I see the belly once. Or flash the belly on a missed charge. Don't park an adult under a lamp (the glidewalk) until the rule is known.
3. **Make the stair kinder.** Make the first gap glide-able so the lamp is the better route, not the only one. Soften the lamp tug on a passing glide. Keep danglers away from respawn pillars.
4. **Make story moments read as story and give them a payoff.** Use a letterbox or vignette when the camera takes control, so I don't think it froze. Give the companion one beat at the crown. Make the Old Burrback's death give something visible (the claws from its shell rather than a separate pickup).
5. **No hits while helpless.** No hawker or enemy hits on a moth held in a lamp, or at least a very clear snatch telegraph. Also, small feel fixes: step up tiny ledges (lamp bases) automatically, and stop the oak/roots room-seam bounce on the climb.
