**How far I got:** all of area 1. I went from the cocoon through the chase, the first lights, the burrow, glade 1 (glide), the glidewalk, the oak hollow, the hawkers' clearing, the silk hall and the roots, and **killed the old burrback in the den**. That gave me wall jump. I climbed back up into the hollow, up the trunk to the crown, and walked off its right edge into `STUB: the canopy road (area 2 starts here)`. Sim clock at the stub: **7:49**. I'm a burst-playing agent, so a human would spend roughly 2–3× that in wall-clock time. Even so, the area is about 8 minutes of play, well short of the 25–35 the brief asked for. That isn't because I got stuck. The content ran out.

Final state: 22/35 lamps, 110/175 moths, health 5 (from 4), glide, wall jump, Keen blade, Quick wings, nightEyes. 1 death, 9 "fell and was put back", 17 hits taken.

A caveat on my own play: I act in bursts with the game paused between them, so I'm worse at split-second timing than a human. I've marked where that probably made something look harder than it is.

## 3. The boss (old burrback, the den)

**How I found it:** the camera pulled out over the roots and showed a huge shelled beetle sitting in an outlined box with a lamp above. It was clearly a boss arena, and I was excited. I died to ants just before and re-formed at a moon patch right outside, so the run-back was nil.

**What happened, as I saw it:**
- It woke when I stepped near. A red outline, a glowing orange top, a white plate on its face and a red streak along the floor toward me read as "it will charge". Good.
- I jumped over and pogo'd it, got a "cut", and it flipped belly-up: a pale shell, green outline, green bar. Hits landed while it was flipped, and the cracks on its shell grew with damage. Great feedback.
- Upright, my hits mostly **sparked off with no damage**, and touching it hurt, even from behind. For a while I didn't know whether pogo, back hits or side hits were "right".
- Mid-fight a crack of light opened in the bark ceiling with a beam down to the floor, and it **curled into a red-outlined ball** that rolled across the arena. Pogoing the ball bounced me high but mostly sparked. I guessed the ceiling crack was the real target (a "taproot"?) but never reached it. It turned out to be the kill reveal.
- There were floor chevrons and a ring around it a couple of times (a slam / shockwave warning?). I stayed airborne and never got hit by one, so I can't say if I read it right.
- What finally made sense: **back off, wait for the charge wind-up (red outline, red bar filling), jump over it. It slams into the arena edge and flips, then I run in and cut the belly.** I did that twice more and it died. I finished at 2/5 after one lamp heal mid-fight.

**Did I understand it?** About halfway in. The charge → dodge → flip → punish loop is a good HK-style lesson, but the game never makes it unambiguous that *only* the flipped state takes damage. The sparks look like "you're hitting it, just not hard enough", not "that's armour". The ball phase and the ceiling beam both suggested a mechanic (pogo the ball up to the crack?) that didn't seem to exist, or that I never found.

**Did it feel like a peak?** **The ending did. The fight was about 70% there.** The arena, the wake-up, the growing cracks and the kill (flash, ceiling collapse, moonlight, moths, wall jump, exit into the hub) are the best minute of the area. The middle was muddier: too much time hitting armour and not knowing why, and the roll phase felt like a waiting game. My burst-play made the dodge timing harder than it would be for a human, so the fight is probably fairer than my 4 hits suggest. The readability problem is real all the same.

## 4. One journey or unrelated rooms? Fun? Would I keep playing?

**One journey, clearly.** I hatched under the moon, followed the moths, met the false lights, put them out to free moths, got my wings, followed the lights to the oak, went down into its roots to the beetle under it, came out with climbing claws, climbed to the crown where the freed moths rise past the moon, then took the canopy road on. Every room knows what it's for in that story. The hollow works as a hub: the den loops back into it and the trunk climb starts from it. The ability gates are honest: the trunk shaft I couldn't climb at 3:30 is the one I climbed at 6:55. The hunched creature that kept turning up next to me is intriguing but never explained. I'd like one beat with it.

**Fun:** yes. Movement feels good (glide especially), the lamp-and-moths loop gives every room a small goal, and the secrets reward curiosity. The low points are rules the game never teaches (lamp catch, boss armour) and a few hazard placements (ants, thorns) that bite without warning.

**Would I keep playing:** **yes.** I walked into area 2's stub wanting more, and I'd go back for the 13 lamps I missed now that I have wall jump.

## 5. Compared with the first 30–40 minutes of Ori and Hollow Knight (not visuals)

**As good or close:**
- **Opening and premise.** Hatching under the moon, the moth swarm and the LAMPFALL title card hold up against Ori's prologue as a first 30 seconds of mood and motive.
- **Abilities tied to the story.** "Your wings have dried" and "your claws have hardened" are better than HK's shrine-pickup framing, and as good as Ori's Spirit Tree beats.
- **Boss payoff and hub loop.** The den kill → ceiling collapse → wall jump → climb out into the hub is HK False Knight / Ori Ginso tier as a *moment*.
- **Secrets that reward backtracking** (bark shrines). HK's breakable walls, same pleasure.
- **Generous death:** respawn at the nearest moon patch with nothing lost. Easier than HK, and fine for this tone.

**Missing or weaker:**
- **Length.** About 8 sim minutes (maybe 15–25 human minutes) against 30–40 of content before either game's first proper boss. The area works as a slice but is short.
- **Teaching the core verb.** Ori's Spirit Flame and HK's nail are understood in seconds. The lamp-catch rule took me 4 minutes and still felt flaky. The game's signature interaction needs Ori-level clarity.
- **Combat depth.** HK's first 30 minutes give you a handful of enemy types with crisp tells, plus a currency and a reason to fight. Here enemies are mostly obstacles. The ants hurt but don't read as enemies, and I never knew whether killing anything mattered.
- **Characters / NPCs.** HK has Elderbug, Cornifer and Quirrell in the first half hour, and Ori has Naru and Kuro. Here the only character is the silent hunched creature. One spoken line would go a long way.
- **Map and navigation fiction.** HK's map-buying and Ori's map stones give the map a place in the world. Here it's free and complete, which is fine for greybox, but it removes the "where am I" tension HK uses well. It also gave the roots route away, which I needed.
- **Upgrade clarity.** Keen blade and Quick wings were explained well. nightEyes wasn't. The health increase to 5 happened with no message.

## 6. Top 5 changes

1. **Teach the lamp catch in the first lights room.** When a lamp catches you, show the escape pips *and* a clear "now" tell on release (the lamp flickers open, the ring flashes) so the beat-free-then-strike loop is obvious. Or make a strike before the catch land reliably when you fly straight at it. Right now the core verb feels random for the first ~10 lamps.
2. **Fix the ants (carriers).** Don't let them chip a standing player repeatedly. Add knockback-and-pass, i-frames that cover their whole column, or have them walk around you. Keep them visible: the zoomed-out den camera hides them entirely. Never place an ant column on the ledge above a moon patch. My only death was to ants while doing nothing.
3. **Make the boss's armour state unmistakable.** Upright hits should *clang*: a distinct deflect, a recoil, maybe a one-time hint. The flipped window should be loud (sound or flash, and a "now" cue). Cut contact damage from behind, or make it obvious. Either make the roll phase's ball and the ceiling crack *do* something the player can find (pogo the ball to reach the taproot?), or don't suggest a target that isn't one.
4. **Re-place the trunk thorns.** Not on the wall directly above a moon patch, and not where a lamp's beat-free release can throw you. Have the camera look ahead upward during wall-climbs so the hazard is on screen before you commit.
5. **Polish the small readability hits.** Give "nightEyes" real text ("Night eyes: unseen lamps show on your map"). Letterbox or caption the two input-locking camera pans so they don't look like a freeze. Smooth out the ~15 px floor bumps that stop a run. Mark the void shafts in the roots (haze, fog, a different floor line) so "put back" isn't the only way to learn they're pits. Announce the health-drop increase.

Also worth noting for planning: the area is **short** (about 8 sim minutes for a thorough first run). Before area 2, more rooms or a second loop through the hub would bring it closer to the 30–40 minute benchmark.
