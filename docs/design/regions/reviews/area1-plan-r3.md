## 1. The first five minutes, redesigned for teaching (hatch → wicks → burrow → glade)

### What fails now
- **The first-timer:**
  - put out **0 lamps in 3:27**;
  - "pressed attack and saw no slash", so believed she had no attack yet;
  - took the juvenile for a floor block;
  - was grabbed by a lamp each time she jumped near an enemy;
  - saw the throw land short of its own marker;
  - quit at R4's bark floor after ~110 attempts.
- **On video:**
  - **R3:** the first catch happens *from the drop into R3* in 9 of 9 runs, before the room is seen. After the knob
    everyone falls into the dark dip under W2 and stands there (0:44–5:02).
  - **R4:** the juvenile hits within 1.5 s of the door in 8 of 9 runs, under the door wick's pull.
  - **R1:** the trunk gap still costs up to 6 catches in 11 s.
  - **The glade lip:** a catch loop of up to 16 catches in 22 s.
- **What does work:** the catch itself. "Lights trap moths, but I can use them" was the first-timer's own sentence
  at 0:40.

### The standard we hold it to
- **Celeste 1A:** one idea per screen. It first appears where failing is free and the obvious action is the
  solution, and the next screen adds one twist.
- **HK King's Pass:** the verb is taught because the way on needs it (doors that crack under the nail). The first
  enemy is harmless fodder on flat floor. The pogo is optional.
- **Ori's Sunken Glades:** the first use of a thing can *happen to you*, a prompt appears once, and the consequence
  is always shown big.

So, for each lesson:
1. One new thing per framed screen (a camera zone that holds the whole lesson).
2. The thing is shown working on something else before it touches her.
3. The obvious action succeeds.
4. The consequence on screen states the rule.
5. A miss costs ≤ 5 s and never leaves her somewhere new.

### What else this needs
- **The throw arc must be honest.** The first-timer saw it "landed short of its own marker". With nothing held the arc
  swings round the orbit. Fixes:
  - the predicted arc is the sim's own throw, frame for frame (`player.lureArc`), so a test asserts the landing equals
    the arc's end;
  - with a direction held, the arc locks to it;
  - with nothing held, the arc fades to half and shows "outward".
- **No lamp's pull overlaps a creature's patrol in R3–R5.** This is the first-timer's "every jump near an enemy turns
  into a grab". Lamp and creature compose from R6 on, never before.
- **What keeps pulling her (minutes 1:40–5:00):**
  - the stragglers she followed are on screen until she frees them, and then they lead;
  - the oak is in R3–R6's backdrop (§2);
  - Tatter is seen ahead, on its own dark way.

  The want never goes off screen.

## 4. What I'd change in my own redesign (no sunk cost)

The evidence says three of my calls were wrong and two were under-specified. The director doc is edited to match
(`wood-area1-director.md`, "Round 3 revisions" at its top).

### 4.1 One room for the oak (was: the hub + R7b)
- I split the tree across two rooms and drew the glimpse into the upper one. The camera can't leave a room, so the
  question was never on screen, and the seam strobed 4–12 times a climb.
- **The oak is one room:** the floor (P3, the stone, Tatter, the arch, the mesh), the trunk, and the crown.
- The builder's shortening (7e1c353) stands: the trunk's height is the hub's 860 plus R7b's climb. It is now one
  column with the glimpse, the climb and the finale in it.
- The roots below and the canopy stub above stay separate rooms.
- Area 1 is 10 rooms.

### 4.2 The light/dark rule was too general
- "Every critical-path room with a lamp also has a dark way" turned the dark way into *where you fall when the light
  line fails* (video: 9 of 9 in R3 after the throw; R8 mostly after a miss). In the teaching rooms it also put a fork
  where Celeste would put one idea.
- **Revised rule:**
  - **R1–R5 have no forks.** A miss is a retry, ≤ 5 s.
  - **From R6 on,** a dark way exists only where its entrance is **in view from the spot where the light line is
    taken**: R8's tunnel mouth, the oak's chimney, R4's tunnel as a fallback only.
  - **The dark way is Tatter's way.** Tatter, who can't fly, walks the dark routes. The player sees it go into R8's
    tunnel mouth and come up out of R4's tunnel at P1, so the slow way is a road someone uses.
- Core's permanence rule is still met where it matters: R3's W1, R4's W-gap and R8's W-throw each keep a fallback
  route. It is the same geometry, but it isn't presented as a choice before R8.

### 4.3 The hawker's twist: time the catch, don't race it
- "It dives at the caught" is in the sim (`huntCaught`), and it hit no one in 9 runs. A catch lasts ~0.4 s for
  anyone mashing, and a tell plus strike takes over a second.
- **Revised:** hawker B **hunts the orbit on a rhythm.** Stragglers circle W-throw, and it dives through the orbit
  every few seconds, taking one. She reads the rhythm and goes in just after a pass. Caught during a pass, she is
  struck down into the tunnel.
- It is readable, it is ecology you can watch (moths really die), and it is a timing skill like Celeste's moving
  blocks.

### 4.4 R3 and R4: one idea per screen (under-specified before)
- My R3 put the catch, the throw, the snuff and a dark fork in one space. It placed the stragglers on the catch lamp
  and left a hole after the knob.
- My R4 put the juvenile under a lamp by the door.
- §1 replaces both.

### 4.5 Grow what sings
- R6 is the best 90 s in the build. The lamp chain (item 10) makes it the throw's development room, so R8's timing
  twist has something to twist.

### Kept, and confirmed by the video
- The bowl and the oak as the centre.
- One predator per moment of the lamp verb: R9 reads cleanly, and every run went for the flame at the composition.
- The loop under the oak.
- Claws in glade 2: the most earned moment.
- The cloud as the finds' meaning: the HUD count carries it, and it now needs the hold.
- The cut list.
