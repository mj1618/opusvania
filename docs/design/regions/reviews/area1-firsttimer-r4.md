# Area 1, first-timer playtest, round 4 (ft4)

**Who:** a first-time player (some Ori, a bit of Hollow Knight). I read only the first-timer section of `memory/playtest-harness.md`: no design docs, room files or code.
**Build:** pinned clean build 8780dfa ("The oak's finale framing…"), greybox past the opening branch.
**Session folder:** `playtest-reports/2026-10-17T19-34-23-firsttimer-ft4/` (`journal.md` has every act and note with its screenshot; `report.md`; heatmaps; `shots/`).
**How far I got:** 6:50 of game time. I went through hatch, chase, first lights, burrow, glade 1 (glide), glidewalk, the oak's hollow (walked under it), hawkers' clearing (found the Quick wings secret), silk hall, and down through bark into the roots. I got stuck at a pillar gap in the roots. Pressing R put me in glade 2 with wall jump. I climbed the stair back into the oak's trunk as far as a moonbeam, failed to read the climb above it, and stopped. That is far short of the 25–35 minutes asked for. Each burst costs a screenshot, so I ran out of turns before I ran out of game. 80 moths and 16 of 31 lamps.

## 2. Moments

### Best 3
1. **LAMPFALL title card over the moon mid-run.** `shots/0026-after.png`. It was cinematic, it came at the right moment, and it said "this is a real game".
2. **Wings dry, then the first glide.** `shots/0178-gained-glide.png`, `shots/0184-burst.png`. It's the story and the mechanics in one beat, and the glide feels great.
3. **The Quick wings alcove behind the bark wall.** `shots/0340-after.png`. It was a proper secret, it rewarded curiosity, and it told me "getting caught" is part of the design.

Honourable mentions: snuffing a lamp **heals** (`shots/0140-…`), a lovely link between saving moths and restoring my light. Climbing back into the oak from below (`shots/0534-after.png`) was the one time the world folded back on itself.

4. **First burrback death.** `shots/0109-hit-by-burrback-0-4-left-.png`. The juvenile's contact damage had already taken me to 2/4. The burrback's armoured front gave only a small spark. I learned the rule by dying.

## 3. One journey, or a series of unrelated rooms?

**The first half is one journey. The second half drifts into a series of rooms, until the climb back into the oak re-knits it.**

What pulled me forward:
- the moth swarm streaming toward the moon and the tree
- the haloed tree on every horizon
- room names that read like a path: chase → first lights → burrow → wings
- wanting the next ability

The **haloed tree was the magnet**. It read as "the destination, where the moths are going, home". Walking under it at 3:30 without being asked to climb it deflated that. After the oak I was clearing hawkers, silk and roots for a number ("moths 40/160") without knowing what the moths do once freed or who I'm doing it for. The hunched companion appears three times beside moonbeams but never acts. I wanted it to lead me or say something. When I climbed up into the trunk from the roots, the oak became a place again: the hub I orbit, not a backdrop.

What it's about, as I understood it: I'm a moth who just hatched. False lights trap my kind. The moon is safety and a save. I put out the false lights to free the others, and the big tree is where it all leads. That's clear and moving in the first three rooms, then it gets thin.

## 4. Was it fun?

**Yes, in patches. It's most fun when glide and lamps combine. It's least fun when the lamp catch and the camera fight me.**

- **Most fun:** the glidewalk and hawkers' clearing (2:15–4:10). I'd glide across a gap, flick a lamp out on the way past, get healed, dodge a hawker. That loop, *move through the dark, snuff the lights, your light comes back*, is the game. Also: the secret, the first glide, the title card.
- **Least fun:** the roots pillars (4:58–5:40), the first burrback fight, and every lamp before I noticed the strike ring. Also the R teleport, which made me distrust saves.

## 5. Top 5 changes

1. **Teach the lamp in one calm moment, and make "caught" a clear, aimable state.** Put one lamp at ground level in an empty room. The first time it pulls, slow time and show the strike ring with the attack button. Then make the break-free fling aimable with the stick, so a catch is a tool, not a dice roll. *Ori* introduces Bash on one lantern in a safe room with a prompt, and the launch goes where you aim. *Hollow Knight* teaches pogo on spikes over a soft floor before it ever matters.
2. **R must return to the last moonbeam you rested at, with progress intact, and must never grant an ability.** If R is a dev restart, hide it from players. In *Hollow Knight* a bench is a bench. *Celeste* respawns you at the room entrance. In both, the checkpoint is somewhere you've seen.
3. **Frame glide gaps: camera look-ahead in the travel direction, and vertical framing that shows the landing.** Don't let a route depend on a lamp the player can permanently put out, or relight lamps on a fall-respawn. *Celeste* fits the whole challenge on screen. *Ori*'s camera leads you and frames the landing. *HK* locks the camera on set-piece gaps.
4. **Hits need honest feedback both ways.**
   - Bark should "tink" and shake on every contact, including near-misses on the edge.
   - Armoured fronts need a loud clink and recoil.
   - Enemy telegraphs must show the real path: draw the dangler's arc, not a vertical line.
   - Lower or remove the juvenile's contact damage.
   - Don't let enemies arrive off-screen during a lamp catch.

   *HK*'s nail clink and recoil on armoured enemies teaches you in one hit. Its telegraphs are always truthful.
5. **Make the oak a payoff and give the tally a meaning.** When you first enter the oak, frame the trunk going up into the crown (a vista beat). Have the companion climb a little and wait, so it's clear the path up opens later. Show freed moths gathering at the crown, so "moths 40/160" is a crowd you can see growing, not a counter. In *HK*, the Forgotten Crossroads keeps showing you the Temple of the Black Egg and the Hollow Knight's prison, and Quirrel waits there. In *Ori*, the Spirit Tree is a scene you return to and watch change. Sein speaks.
