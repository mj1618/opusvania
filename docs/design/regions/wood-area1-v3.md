# Wood area 1, v3: discovery, challenge, contrast

2026-10-18. A redesign of area 1 after Matt played it. **It is a proposal.** Matt plays a toy room with the new
fling and the new attack first (§8). If they feel right, builders execute this doc, and it then supersedes
`wood-area1-director.md` for area 1. Until then that doc describes the build.

## What Matt said, and what it means

Matt's notes (2026-10-18, `STUDIO.md` "Matt judges fun"):

| What he said | What it means |
|---|---|
| "do this, do this, do this" | A corridor of instructions. Every room hands you the next step |
| "bland", "very obvious", "no challenge or discovery or exploration" | Nothing is hidden for real, nothing is hard unless it's required, and nothing is off the path |
| "no massive highs or lows, very monotone" | Every room sits at the same intensity: a little threat, a lamp, a door |
| "the lamp catch is super annoying" | A verb that takes control away. It's being replaced by the fling (below) |
| "the attack feels average" | Being rebuilt to Hollow Knight's standard |
| "movement feels good: jump, glide, double jump" | Our best asset. Build the area around moving |

Why we got here: we tuned to what AI testers can measure, and every time a first-timer agent got stuck we added a
signpost. A person reads a room far better than an agent. So the area became a guided tour: guide moths,
moonlight whispering over every secret, glyphs, held camera beats, one way out of every room.

**This doc trusts the player.**

**The new verb, the fling** (in flight now):
1. Near a flame, press the Lamp button. Time slows while she aims.
2. She launches, and the flame snuffs as she leaves it.
3. Chains of flames become flow, and every launch is a rescue.
4. She never loses control.

This doc builds the area around it.

## 1. What made the reference first hours exciting

### Exploration

| What | Hollow Knight (King's Pass → Crossroads) | Ori (Sunken Glades → Hollow Grove) | What we lack |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The wrong way pays first** | The cave to the left of the fall pays geo in 10 s | An energy cell in the cave by the start | We go right |
| **Secrets that matter** | Mask shards, grubs, a charm behind a breakable wall. The rewards change what you can do | Life cells and energy cells are more health and more power, always just off the path | Ours pay "5 moths", which changes nothing |
| **Optional hard bits** | The spike pit's pogo to the Fury charm, never required. Later, Gruz Mother as an optional boss | Cells behind tricky platforming; the hard way to a Spirit Light container | We have none. Everything optional is easy and everything hard is required |
| **Branching** | The Crossroads is a web: 4–5 exits, locked ones that read as locks, and you're meant to wander | Keystone doors send you round a small area in your own order | One way out of every room. The hub opens one door at a time |
| **Landmarks you return to** | Dirtmouth and the well; the temple's sealed door; the stag station | The Spirit Tree; the gate you saw before you could open it | The oak (good), but you pass through it once and come back once, both on rails |
| **The map is earned** | No map until you find Cornifer, whose humming and paper trail you follow | The map stone, found by exploring | The map is free from the start. Lost-and-found never happens |
| **Risk and reward** | Death drops your geo where you died: the shade walk | Soul Link saves cost energy: you choose your checkpoints | A death costs nothing, so nothing is risky |

### Peaks and valleys

| | Hollow Knight | Ori |
|---|---|---|
| **Peaks** | False Knight dropping from the ceiling; the floor breaking under him; Gruz Mother's babies bursting out; the first bench after the pass | The prologue's grief; finding Bash; the Ginso Tree escape, rising water at full speed; each ability tree |
| **Valleys** | Dirtmouth's silence; the Crossroads' slow hum; the empty houses | Quiet platforming with music; the Spirit Well; the long glides |
| **Why it isn't monotone** | Silence against noise, tight tunnels against open halls, being lost against suddenly knowing, nothing happening against a boss | Speed (escapes, Bash chains) against stillness (wells, vistas), grief against joy |

**What we take from this:**
1. A hub you are meant to wander.
2. Secrets whose rewards change the play.
3. Optional spaces that are genuinely hard.
4. A map you have to earn.
5. Contrast on purpose: in space, sound, speed, light and threat.
6. At least one stretch at full speed.

## 2. The web

### The geography (kept)

- **The bowl:** the ridge and the hatching tree high in the west, the floor sloping east down to the hollow, and the
  great oak at its lowest point.
- **Three layers:** canopy, floor, roots. The den is under the oak. The crown sits above the canopy.
- **What changes is how the player moves through it.** It was a line with one loop. It becomes a hub with five
  directions.

### The oak as an open hub
She reaches the oak at ~9 min. From its floor there are five ways, and the player chooses.

| Direction | What | Gate | Pays |
|---|---|---|---|
| **East: the clearing** | The way on toward the roots (R8 → R9 → its floor gives way → the roots → the den) | open | the critical path; the fights |
| **North-east: the lamp road (C1)** | A vertical fling gauntlet up the bowl's east wall to a ledge above the canopy | open, but hard | **sharp wings** (a hollow, below), plus a drop back into the oak's crown window: a view of the trunk from above and a shortcut |
| **Up: the trunk** | The climb to the crown | claws | the finale; C2 branches off it |
| **Down: the mesh** | The den is under it. She can see down through it into the dark | sealed from above | the boss's way out |
| **The crack at the trunk's foot** | Too small for her; Tatter sits by it | opens from the roots' side | a shortcut from the roots back into the oak |
| **West: back** | R6, and with claws its high bough to R5's trunk, R4's high shelf and R1's ridge | claws for the high parts | the claws' return payoffs |

The player can try the lamp road first, or the clearing, or neither. Wandering the hub's options is the point.
HK's Crossroads is the model: small, several exits, locks that read as locks, and you're meant to wander.

### Secrets with real rewards
Every secret pays something that changes the play or the story. **Nothing optional pays moths alone.** Moths stay
the rescue story, the cloud and the ending, never a reward for a secret.

| Secret | Where | How it's found (no signposts) | Reward |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The moth-stone** (the area's map) | Behind the oak's floor, west of P3: a bark wall that looks like the others | Hit things. The map is withheld until she finds it | **The map.** Before it, Tab shows only the rooms she has walked, as rough outlines (HK's Cornifer, cheaper) |
| **The firefly's cache** | Above the clearing: a gap high in R8's east wall, reached by a fling chain whose last flame hangs out of the obvious line | A player who looks up at the clearing's ceiling sees one flame that doesn't belong. No glow trail, no moonlight leak | **A moon-drop (+1 max health)** and the firefly's lore (§2.5). **A surprise:** a playground of flames |
| **Long breath** (a hollow) | R4's high shelf, behind the wick she couldn't reach at minute 4 | Seen at minute 4, reached with claws on the way back | The fling's slow-time lasts longer: aiming at leisure |
| **Firefly signs** (lore) | R3's left hollow (the "wrong way" at 1:50), the cache, the roots | Exploring | The firefly's rhythm and its loneliness, a little at a time |
| **Shortcuts** | The crack (roots → oak); the lamp road's drop into the oak's crown window; R6's high bough (claws) to R5 | Opened from the far side | Minutes saved, and the place understood |
| **Two optional challenges** | C1, C2 (below) | In view from the hub and the trunk: the challenge is visible, the way through isn't | Sharp wings; a moon-drop |

**Health comes from found moon-drops, not moth milestones.** There are two in area 1, one in the cache and one at
the end of C2. Each is whole (no fragments). This replaces core §6's milestone rule. Moths become the story and the
completion count only. Matt asked for real rewards, and health is the realest (HK's masks, Ori's life cells).

### Two optional challenges that are genuinely hard
They are White-Palace-lite or Celeste B-side-lite: no enemies, precise movement, a checkpoint at each screen, and a
retry of seconds. A skilled player needs 2–4 minutes, an average one 10+, and nobody is required to finish.

**C1, the lamp road** (fling mastery; from the oak floor, before the claws)
- **Shape:** a chain of flames climbing the bowl's east wall over thorns, about three screens up.
- **Screen 1:** two-flame chains with generous angles.
- **Screen 2:** a chain that needs a sharp angle change mid-chain, and a glide between two flames.
- **Screen 3:** a chain where one flame swings (the swinging lure exists), so the timing of the launch matters.
- **Retry rule, embers:**
  - A flame she flings from goes to embers while she's in the air.
  - If she touches thorns or falls, the screen's embers relight and she re-forms at the screen's start.
  - The flames go out for good only when she lands on the screen's top ledge.
  - Fiction: a flame dies when its moths are safe.
- **Reward:** **Sharp wings.** A fling's launch cuts through anything she passes: creatures take a hit, and silk
  breaks. It changes every fight in the rest of the area, including the boss's second phase, without making it
  trivial (a creature hit by a fling flinches but doesn't die).
- **The way out:** a drop from the top into the oak's crown window, a view of the whole trunk from above before she
  can climb it. It's a look at the finale.

**C2, the thorn chimney** (claws, fling and glide together; from the trunk, after the claws)
- **Shape:** a side shaft off the trunk, lined with thorn bands.
- **The skill:** kick, kick, fling from a flame between the faces, glide across a thorn gap, kick again.
- **Screens:** four, each a checkpoint, with the embers rule.
- **Reward:** **a moon-drop.** The second +1 of the area, and the hardest one to earn.

### Surprises
1. **The firefly's cache.** A hidden room full of small flames, the firefly's half-finished work:
   - glow-caps strung but not hung;
   - a ring of lights arranged in its flash pattern;
   - in the middle, a lamp shaped like a firefly. This is the marsh's reveal, foreshadowed for the ones who look.

   For the player it's a **fling playground**: flames everywhere and no thorns, pure flow for its own sake, and the
   moon-drop at the top. Nothing tells you it exists.
2. **The rock that breathes.** The roots are dark and quiet. Midway, she walks along a long, low, curved ledge that
   looks like rock. **It's the old burrback's shell, asleep.** The den is where the ledge ends. When she drops into
   the den, the "rock" behind her rises: it was the beetle all along, and the fight begins.
   - Sim: a sleeping creature whose back is a floor until it wakes.
   - It's a small surprise that recasts the walk she just took.

## 3. The fling as the core joy

The fling is our Bash and our identity: rescue and flight in one press. Where it lives:

| Use | Where | What makes it fun |
|---|---|---|
| **The first fling** | R3 | One flame over a gap. Time slows, the arrow, the launch, the snuff, moths bursting out behind her. One glyph for the new button, the first time only |
| **The first chain** | R3, next screen | Two flames. The joy of not touching the ground |
| **Fling into glide** | R6 | The longest lines in the area: fling, open the wings, sail, fling again. R6 was the best room; this makes it sing |
| **Chains through danger** | R8, the clearing | Hawkers hunt the air between flames. Slow-time lets her pick an angle away from a dive. A chain through a guarded room snuffs every flame, so the predators scatter behind her: flow, rescue and clearing in one line |
| **Chains as combat** | R9; the boss's phase 2; with sharp wings, everywhere | Fling past a dangler and cut its silk on the way. Fling down onto the rolling beetle |
| **Chains as challenge** | C1, C2 | Precision angles, a swinging flame, glide gaps, all with the embers retry |
| **Chains as discovery** | Every room | A single flame where it doesn't belong is an invitation. A chain leading off-screen upward is a secret. The player learns to read flames as roads |
| **Chains as a chase** | The finale (§5) | The firefly flees up the trunk, hanging flames ahead of her, and she flings after it at full speed |

**Level rules the fling brings:**
- **Every flame is used once (permanence).** So a critical-path chain is always **one-way forward, ending in a
  shortcut back**: a bark door or a drop opened from the far side. That is how HK turns a one-way gauntlet into a
  loop.
- **Embers** give challenge chains their retry.
- **Main-path chains are generous; optional ones are not.** Skill expression lives off the path.
- **She can still cut a flame** to snuff it in place, without launching.

**Contrast on purpose:**

| Kind | Against |
|---|---|
| **Space** | R4 tight, R6 wide, the oak tall, R8 open sky, the roots low |
| **Light** | The lamp-lit clearing against the black roots |

The roots must be quiet, not filled. That empty minute is what makes the den hit.

### The highs

| High | Feeling |
|---|---|
| The vista | wonder |
| R6's flow | joy |
| The cache | surprise |
| The den | fear, then triumph |
| **The firefly chase up the trunk** | speed: our Ginso escape, small |
| The crown | release |
| C1, C2 | mastery, for those who want it |

**The chase, in more detail:**
- **What happens:** the climb's top half is a race. The firefly flees up the open crown, hanging glow-caps as it
  goes. They are dim, fresh, and burn for only ~4 s. She has to keep flinging from cap to cap to keep up.
- **Failing:** if she falls behind, the caps go out, she drops to the last ledge, and the firefly waits higher up,
  flickering. There is no death and no reset beyond the ledge, so the pressure is speed, not punishment.
- **The ending:** a fling off its last cap into the open sky. It escapes; she lands at the crown.
- **The story:** it unknowingly builds her road, and every cap she flings from frees the moths it had already
  caught. This is one of the firefly's two scripted relights (core §3), moved here from the Weaver.
