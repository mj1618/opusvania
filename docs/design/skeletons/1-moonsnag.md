# 1. The Moonsnag (hub and spokes)

The data, the map and the ghost run are in `1-moonsnag.json`. It is about 23 minutes on the critical path and ~32 with the
secrets, across 43 screens.

## The through-line
**Wing wants out of the wood after the swarm, through the one hole in its roof, and to take every moth she frees with
her.** The Moonsnag is a lightning-split dead giant, and its hollow is the only place the real moon reaches the floor.
Freed moths fly there to wait in the moonlight. The firefly hangs its lights in the brightest place it can find, so it
builds a chandelier under the moon hole: a **false moon under the real one**. Moths drift up to it, and an orb-weaver
webs beneath it. So the place where her moths gather is turning into a trap, and that is the story's idea in one room.

## The arc
Wonder (the hatch, the vista with the snag's ring across the gap) → home (the hub's first quiet) → flow (the Lampwalk)
→ wings (the glade) → the snap back (gliding in through the hub's window) → close and dark (the Burrows) → the peak
under the floor (the old burrback breaks it) → dread (the black roots) → claws in the hub's own moonlight → **the
stakes seen** (visit 3: a full web, her moths drawn off the walls) → the gauntlet over the roof → the hub as the arena
→ release (the last fling out through the moon hole, every moth lifting with her).

## Map logic
The hub is one tall room, floor to moon hole, in the middle of the map. Everything leaves from it and comes back into
it, each time from a new side:
- **Visit 1** from the high west ledge, looking down into it.
- **Visit 2** gliding in through the east window.
- **Visit 3** breaking up through the floor crack.
- **Visit 4** dropping through the web from the rim.

Four entrances, four views of one space. That is how it becomes known like Dirtmouth.

There are two spokes at the start, and she picks the order:
- **east, the Lampwalk:** warm, lit and fast; glide at the end;
- **west, the Burrows:** dark, tight and a fight; claws under the hub.

Each spoke works with either kit and has extra pockets for the other ability. Most first-timers will go east first
because the arch glows, the way a moth goes to a lamp. That is the joke, and it is fine.

The third spoke needs both abilities. **The Silk Loft** runs over the canopy, and its only exit is down into the hub.
Every spoke ends in a shortcut back to the centre, so the area folds up like HK's Crossroads instead of unrolling like
our old corridor. Landmarks:
- the moon hole, seen from 7 regions;
- the false moon, which gets bigger every time she sees it;
- the Lampwalk's warm line, seen from above on the High Bough;
- the hatching branch, seen again from the rim at the end.

## The best moments
1. **Gliding in through the window** (minute 8). She has just found glide. The bough runs west with the snag ahead the
   whole way, and the last glide carries her into the hub from above. Her freed moths are on the walls now, and three
   lights hang where there was one.
2. **The floor breaks** (minute 12). The old burrback's last stomp drops them both into the black roots. A silent
   minute follows, then the moon column through the crack, and she gets claws in the hub's own light from beneath it.
3. **The look down** (minute 18). From the rim she sees, through the web, the false moon, her moths circling it and
   the Weaver at its heart. She cuts the anchor thread and drops in. No cutscene: she chooses the moment.
4. **The last fling** (minute 22). The final launch off the heart snuffs it and throws her up through the moon hole as
   every moth she freed lifts past her. Then the firefly relights one wick behind thorns on the rim, and Gust blows it
   out.

## Density on every visit
- **In the spokes:** at any point there are 2–3 things in reach. Every lamp holds stragglers, and fern and bark are
  there to hack, with seed pods in the Burrows. A predator watches each lit pool: beetles where she lands, danglers
  where she stops, hawkers in the air between flames. A dark slow line sits beside each lit fast line, and there is
  always something just off the path.
- **In the hub,** every visit adds four things:
  - a rest;
  - a visible change (more lights, more silk, moths on the walls, then moths leaving them);
  - one new creature or hazard (the dangler over the west door on visit 2);
  - one pocket the new ability opens (glide: the upper west niche; claws: the split; Gust: the thorn niche).

  The hub is never walked through twice in the same state.
- **Quiet on purpose:** the Fern Glade and the roots' first minute are quiet by design, so the contrast stays.

## The secrets layer
Every tell is honest, and none is a glow trail or a hint.
- **The moth-stone (the map):** on visit 2 her moths settle on every wall of the hub except one patch, where air moves
  behind the bark.
- **The firefly's cache:** one flame hangs out of line high in the glade. Behind it is a fling playground full of the
  firefly's unhung work and a firefly-shaped lamp (lore set up for the marsh), with a moon-drop at the top.
- **The thorn chimney:** hard and optional, Celeste-lite, with embers that relight on a miss. Its moon-drop is shown at
  second 10, hanging under the hatching branch.
- **The sunk pool:** a dead moth falls through a patch of the Lampwalk's floor. Below are long breath and the
  drowned-roots stub.
- **The hub's thorn niche:** a wick behind thorns, seen on every visit and finally opened by Gust.

Health comes from the two moon-drops, which are real rewards. Moths are the rescue story, not the pay.

## The boss
**The Weaver under the false moon**, fought in the hub. It keeps the Weaver Matt liked and its one exam a phase, and
re-roots it in the fling:
- **P1, the ring (fling):** she chains between the chandelier's five wicks, and each launch snuffs a wick and drops its
  spoke of web. The weaver runs to wherever she touches the silk.
- **P2, the drops (claws and glide):** the web falls in sections, and the weaver swings on telegraphed threads from
  knots in the walls. She kicks up walls that are hers now.
- **P3, the floor (pogo):** the weaver lunges, then lies winded, and only a pogo on its abdomen lands.

The arena is a room she has crossed three times, so she knows its walls before the fight starts.

## What it borrows
- **Hollow Knight:** Dirtmouth's quiet centre, the Crossroads' choice of door and its shortcuts that open from the far
  side, False Knight's breaking floor, and the boss in the middle of the map you've walked around.
- **Ori:** the Spirit Tree as the place everything returns to, and a final launch as the release.
- **Hollow Knight's Infected Crossroads:** the hub changing between visits, compressed to 20 minutes.

## Biggest risk
**The hub has four jobs:** it is a safe rest, a crossroads, a stage that changes four times and a three-phase arena.
Each state costs art and layout, and a geometry that fights well may not rest well. If the states read as small
tweaks, the core idea dies, so each one needs one change you can see from the doorway.

There are two smaller risks:
- **Choice of order:** either spoke can be first, so neither may lean on the other's ability.
- **The false moon could read as punishment** for freeing moths. It has to read as stakes, never as moths lost: no
  count ever drops.
