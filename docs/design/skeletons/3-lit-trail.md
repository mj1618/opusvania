# Skeleton 3: The Lit Trail

**Lens:** a directed journey with rich side branches, like Ori WotW's Inkwater Marsh into Wellspring, or Nine Sols'
opening. The data is in `3-lit-trail.json`. ~23 min on the critical path for a first-timer, ~35 with the branches.
The map is 44 screens.

## The through-line
**Catch the tail of her swarm before the firefly's trail of lights leads every last moth into the web at the Lamp
Loom.** The want is set in the first ten seconds (the swarm leaves without her) and never goes off screen. The
stragglers, the Tail, are a visible ribbon of moths ahead of her. The firefly is always one step ahead of them,
hanging fresh lamps along their path, and each lamp snares a knot of them. Each knot she frees rejoins the Tail, so
the ribbon ahead grows as she plays. The destination is a place she sees from the first vista on: the wood's great
lamp, a gold blaze under the canopy. Once the player gets there they find out why every light led there: an
orb-weaver has strung its web between the firefly's flames, and the whole Tail is spun into it.

## The arc
Left behind → chasing → **losing them** → hunted → **catching up** → setting them free.
- Minutes 0–9 are pursuit: the canopy run, the leap, the trail, the Kindling.
- At 9 the floor drops her into black, silent roots, and for the first time nothing pulls. That loss is the turn.
- She then gets claws in the Moonwell and becomes the one being chased: the old burrback up the Split Oak.
- She comes out above the trees, in moonlight, closer to the Loom than ever.
- The Weaver is the catch-up, and the freed Tail resting at the Mooring is the release.

Intensity alternates on purpose:
- wonder: the hatch, the leap;
- tense: the trail;
- intense: the Kindling;
- the trough: the roots;
- wonder: the Moonwell;
- intense: the escape;
- quiet: the boughs;
- intense: the gauntlet and the boss;
- quiet: the hub.

No quiet stretch lasts over 2 minutes, and no intense one over 3, except the boss.

## The map logic
**Forward is east, and the line drops before it climbs:**
1. canopy (the hatch and the Swarm Road);
2. down the Lip to the floor;
3. through the floor (the Dell, the Trail, the Kindling);
4. down into the roots;
5. up the Split Oak to the high boughs;
6. down the Silk Gate into the Loom's clearing;
7. out to the Mooring at the wood's east edge, over the valley.

The player crosses all three layers, so the wood has height, and the boughs can look back west to the hatching tree.

**Every side branch is visible from the main line, and every one rejoins it further on.** That rule is the shape of
the map: curiosity never costs a walk back, so the forward pull and the detours stop competing.

| Branch | Leaves from | Rejoins at | Pays |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cocoon Loft | the hatch (pogo up the dead cocoons) | the Lip | firefly sign 1, the highest view |
| Glowcap Cellar | the Dell (a hollow floor) | the middle of the Trail | **the moth-stone: the map** |
| Thorn Canopy (hard) | the Trail's high line | the Kindling's ledge | **sharp wings** |
| Wing Gallery | the Roots (a cold draft) | the Moonwell | **long breath**, firefly sign 2 |
| Firefly's Cache | the Boughs (a flame with no moths) | the Silk Gate | **a moon-drop**, the firefly-shaped lamp |
| Old Den (optional mini-boss) | the hub (Gust tears the silk) | the Moonwell (shortcut) | **a moon-drop** |

The hub comes at the end, as Wellspring does, and it's a real one:
- its exits go east (the valley), north (double jump, later), west (the den's shortcut back into the wood) and down
  (the drowned roots stub);
- the den tunnel plus the fall shaft (claws) turn the line into a loop;
- Gust, found minutes earlier, reaches the lamps left burning back west, such as the thorned wick over the Dell.

## The best moments
1. **The Leap** (2:00). The vista holds 1.5 s at most, with control kept: the valley's lamps, the swarm breaking into
   knots, the Loom's gold below, the title over the moon. She jumps after the stragglers and her wings open in
   mid-air. Getting glide *is* the set piece. There is no glade to walk to.
2. **The Kindling** (8:00). The firefly flies ahead, and lamps flare on one by one as she comes. The Tail swerves into
   each, and she fling-chains after it at full speed with a hawker diving at the chain. There is no fail state: a lamp
   she hasn't reached waits with its moths, so falling behind costs speed, never a reset. Then the moth-carpeted floor
   gives way under her.
3. **The rock that breathes** (12:00). She has walked the long curved "rock" in the silent roots once already. Coming
   back from the Moonwell with her claws, it unfolds behind her: the old burrback. She climbs the Split Oak with
   brand-new claws while its rams shake bark loose above her and break the chimney away below. It's Ginso, small, and
   it's the area's only reversal: she is the prey.
4. **Out into the moon** (13:40). She bursts out of a knot-hole into moonlight and silence, with the hatching tree far
   behind and the Loom blazing ahead. The quietest frame in the area comes straight after the loudest.

## Density, on every visit
Every main-line screen layers at least three of these:
- a lamp to fling from or snuff (the Tail's knots: a rescue you can see);
- a creature placed for its moment (the beetle where she lands, the dangler where she stops, the hawker in the air
  between flames, the bombardier across a bridge, a weaver on a web);
- something to cut (leaf curtains, glowcaps, pods; they spill small glow pickups that top up health);
- a second line (high or low, which cross twice in the Trail).

The branches are a fourth thing, always in view. On a revisit, the den shortcut brings her back with claws and Gust.
Gust tears silk and reaches wicks behind thorns, and the claws climb the fall shaft. The Trail's and the Dell's
leftover lamps become the revisit's density.

## The secrets layer
None of these is signposted. Each tell is something in the world that is slightly wrong: a bough that carries on
west, a ring of dead moths on a floor that sounds hollow, a cold draft in the dark, a flame with no moths around it,
moths dropping through a grate with something breathing below. The rewards change play:
- the map (it is withheld until the moth-stone);
- sharp wings and long breath;
- two moon-drops;
- two firefly signs, the cache's firefly-shaped lamp and the gallery's old migration stains, which start the story's
  late reveal for anyone who looks.

## The boss: the Weaver of the Loom
This is the lab Weaver Matt liked, with one exam a phase, each phase the area's own lesson:
- **P1, the ring:** fling from the five ring flames. Each launch snuffs one and drops a spoke of moths free, the heart's
  pull bends every flight, and the weaver runs to wherever she touches the web.
- **P2, the drops:** threads show first, as the danglers taught. Swings cover half the arena with the safe side opposite,
  and she kicks up the trunks with her new claws.
- **P3, the floor:** pogo the abdomen, and with sharp wings a fling through the weaver staggers it. Then the heart
  goes out.

It ends in the wood's first stars, the whole Tail bursting free, Gust in the moon shaft, and the firefly's alarm
flash as it flees east over the valley. That flight is the next pursuit's hook.

## What it borrows
- **Ori WotW, Inkwater Marsh → Wellspring:** a pursuit that drags you forward, abilities gained mid-journey rather
  than at shrines, side paths that rejoin ahead, and a hub reached as the payoff.
- **Ori BF, Ginso:** the escape climb.
- **Nine Sols:** a gauntlet that rehearses the boss, then a heal at the door.
- **Hollow Knight:** the wrong way pays first (King's Pass), and the map is earned (Cornifer).
- **Celeste:** the embers retry on the hard branch.

## The biggest risk
**A directed journey can slide back into "do this, do this, do this".** It's the exact complaint about wood area 1,
and set pieces make it worse if they're staged rather than played. The defence is in three rules, and all three must
survive building:
1. The pursuit pulls, it never instructs: no held beats over 1.5 s, and no failure states in the Kindling.
2. Main-line rooms keep two lines and three things in reach.
3. The branches stay tempting and visible.

The second risk is cost. The Kindling's scripted firefly and the escape's ram timeline are the expensive pieces, and
both need real attempts before anyone judges them. The fallbacks:
- the Kindling becomes a pre-lit lamp corridor with the firefly ahead;
- the escape becomes a climb with falling bark on a timer and the beetle below.
