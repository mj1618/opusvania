# 5. The Turning Night (wildcard)

**One small wood, played three times as the night turns.** The level's structure is a change of state. The map is
compact (about 42 screens) and every main room is visited in two or three states of the same night: **Dusk**,
**Lampfall** and **Silk**. Each state has its own creatures, lamps, roads and secrets on the same geometry. The night
is the main gate, and abilities come second.

## Through-line and arc
**Wing wants her swarm out of the lit wood and through the Moongate to the moon, because tonight is the night they
fly and they are dying at the lights.**

The arc is a causal chain the player can retell: *light brings the moths, the moths bring the hunters, the hunters
bring the silk.*

| State | Minutes | Feeling | What the wood is |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dusk | 0–4 | wonder, quiet | dark and safe. Her kind sleeps in sheets on every trunk. One warm light moves through the dark: the firefly, stringing unlit lamps |
| Lampfall | 4–15 | speed, intensity | the firefly kindles the whole wood in one sweep. The waking swarm rises into the lights and breaks into three great knots, and predators crowd every pool of light |
| Silk | 15–24 | dread, then release | every cloud she frees flies for the Moongate and lands in a web that grows with each one. After the third the wood is dark again, and spun over |

## Map logic
- **The bowl:** the hatch is high on the west rim. The **Roost Gum**, the hub, stands in the middle with its roots
  over the cellar. The **Moongate** is east and high: the one gap in the canopy where the moon shows, and where the
  migration has always left.
- **Why the web is at the Moongate:** orb-weavers really do build across flyways. The firefly hung its Crown there
  because that is where moths funnel, and the spider followed the moths.
- **The Moongate tells the time.** It is in view from eight of the fifteen regions. At dusk it is empty moon. At
  Lampfall it holds the Crown's light. In Silk it holds silver lines, then spokes, then a full wheel full of her moths.
- **At dusk she sees roads that don't exist yet.** From the snag the wood shows bare hooks on the high boughs and a
  curtain of dark wicks on the wall. Lampfall lights exactly those, so the canopy and the curtain open up.
- **Flings use up lamps** (embers go out on safe landing). Every lit road is therefore one-way-ish and ends at a
  drop or a shortcut. The wood goes dark behind her as she plays.
- **Silk closes and opens:** strands across old paths, the guy lines that make the Loom climbable, larders in the
  rooms she has already emptied.

The middle act is open. From the Roost floor all three knots are in view: the Hawker Chandelier overhead, the Wick
Curtain to the west, the Drowned Lantern glowing up through the roots. She does them in any order. Claws come from
the cellar's knot and are needed only for the Loom. A player with claws can climb early and watch the web being spun.

## The best moments
1. **Lampfall (4:15).** On the snag, full dark falls. The swarm lifts off every tree in one river toward the moon.
   Then the firefly flies its circuit and the wood kindles behind it in a wave of hundreds of lamps. The river bends
   into the lights and shatters. The title comes over the moon. She is never held: she can glide off mid-wave and
   watch it from the air.
2. **The Wick Curtain (11:00).** The wall where she walked through sleeping moths two hours ago is now a sheet of
   flames with her kind spinning in it. She pogoes down it flame by flame, and from the hub you watch it go out.
3. **The first cloud hits the web (9:00).** Freed from the Chandelier, the cloud streams east toward the moon in
   triumph and stops dead in a few silver threads. It is the turn of the act, and it is caused by her own success.
4. **The web falls (22:00).** Every moth she freed pours out through the gate at once. The firefly, caught in the
   same web, is freed with them. It darts to the Crown, relights it behind her and flees. Her new Gust puts it out
   from across the gap, and the stars come out over the whole bowl.

## Density on every visit
The night refills the rooms. When the lamps are used up, the silk brings new things.

| State | Always in reach |
|---|---|
| Dusk | sleepers to burst (they resettle), moss and ferns to hack, juvenile beetles eating the fallen, bark to break, the firefly's light to follow |
| Lampfall | flames to fling or snuff, stragglers to free, a predator at every light (a beetle where she lands, a dangler where she stops, a hawker in the orbit), roads to new places |
| Silk | strands to cut (cutting makes routes), larder bundles to cut open (each a small cloud), spiderling fodder, danglers that hunt by touch, webbed leftover lamps |

Leaving lamps lit has a cost: in Silk they are webbed. No state is a corridor of one idea. In each, the old idea is
still around and the new one is layered on top.

## Secrets: each hour hides something
- **The Sleepers' Ring (dusk).** The moths pack in a ring round a bare patch that leaks cool mist. Behind it is the
  long-breath hollow. After dusk the patch is only bare, a weaker tell.
- **The shadow with no caster (lit).** The Roost's limb lamps throw the shadow of an unseen ledge onto the trunk.
  Behind the leaf cover is a moon-drop. Snuff those lamps first and the shadow is gone.
- **The Firefly's Wake.** If you watched the Lampfall instead of leaving, you saw one line of lamps lit in a single
  stroke that ended upward. It is a hard optional fling gauntlet along the firefly's own flight, ending in **its
  cache**: a moon-drop, a firefly sign and its stash of unhung lights.
- **The Hollow of Wings.** In the cellar's still air, wings drift sideways out of a bark wall. Behind it: the first
  firefly sign and the drowned-roots stub.
- **The big larder (silk).** One bundle twitches, twice the size of the rest. Cut its line from the Loom's first
  kick for a cloud and a moon-drop.

## The boss: the Weaver at the Moongate
The fight is gentle, about 2 minutes, and follows the lab Weaver Matt liked. Each phase is something the level taught.
1. **P1, the web.** Five lanterns anchor the web. She flings from anchor to anchor, and each one snuffed drops a
   section of web. Touching silk slows her (she is never grabbed), and the weaver runs to where she touched.
2. **P2, the drop.** The weaver drops on silk, and its thread shows first, as the danglers taught. She kicks up the
   gate's trunks to stay above it.
3. **P3, the limb.** She pogoes its abdomen, then puts out the Crown flame by flame.

The firefly caught in the web is a setup for the ending's mercy: the first time, she frees it by accident, and it
relights her lamp. Tatter's want pays off on the same beat. At the Loom's foot it could not climb, and her fight
brings down the web that becomes its ladder.

## What it borrows
- **Super Metroid's Crateria:** empty and raining when you land, and when you come back the lights are on and the
  pirates have arrived. The same rooms, made dangerous by an event.
- **Hollow Knight's infected Crossroads:** the hub you know, turned against you.
- **Ori's Ginso and Forlorn restorations:** a world event re-reads old rooms.
- **Kuro, from Ori, for the rival seen from afar:** a creature that works on the world while you play and reacts to
  what you undo.
- **Outer Wilds and Majora's Mask** for a world you read by the hour, here driven by progress and never by a clock.

## Biggest risk
**Three states triple the design and test surface, and one dull state drags the room down with it.** On top of that,
"free three knots" could read as a checklist.

Mitigations:
- The states are data layers on one geometry (lamps, creatures, strands and covers keyed to a `night` flag). That
  means one bake per room and a grade per state.
- The solver runs every state with lamps randomly pre-snuffed, so no state can be stranded by what was used before.
- The knots are where her swarm is, visible from the hub and different in kind: a skill chain, a descent and a fight.

If Silk fails a real attempt, it shrinks to a short dark climb to the boss, and Dusk and Lampfall still stand.
