# North star: the spaces of *Tallage*

Status: **v2, Phase W (L5)**, revised after `docs/design/north-star-critique.md` and the user's fun feedback (STUDIO §1d). Owner: world and level design. Date: 2026-09-28.

**Fun comes first. The numbers here are guard-rails, not goals.** A room that hits every metric and bores the player has failed.

**Units.** Tile = 64 px. PH (player height) = 80 px = 1.25 tiles. Screen = 30 × 16.9 tiles = 13.5 PH tall at zoom 1.0. **Cell** = one GridVania world cell = 30 × 17 tiles ≈ 1 scr² (one screen of area). Run speed = 9 tiles/s, so one screen width takes 3.3 s.

---

## 1. The feeling

Tallage is a boomtown on the loudest night of its fever, and you can hear who owns what. Streets are stacked on streets, and bunting hangs between leaning towers. A Ticker Board shouts prices over a crowded square, and the Exchange Bell tower is always somewhere above you. Everything that hums belongs to somebody, and Kid Tallow is the bailiff who can take it: the sound cuts to silence, and the thing stops existing.

The player should grin at least once a minute:
- because something fell, flew, broke or opened;
- because the city reacted;
- because they found something they weren't meant to;
- or because Kid did something only a disgraced featherweight would do.

From Hollow Knight we take **cohesion**: one fixed origin, roads you can map in your head, and dead ends that always pay. From Ori we take **flow and spectacle**: runs where the ground never asks you to stop, and tight spaces that burst into reveals. We copy neither. HK's hub is lonely; ours is **loud, crowded and alive**. Going down, the city gets heavier, warmer and lower in pitch. **You navigate by ear as much as by eye.**

## 2. The proof: the most fun 8 minutes we can make (build this next)

**Seven rooms, about 55 scr², about 8 minutes blind: arrival to the Great Scale.** If seven rooms aren't fun and don't feel like one place, twenty-eight won't be either. The full region (§5) is the next step, and only after this passes.

We assume Kid is an animated character and that hits and enemies have impact and personality (parallel streams).

## 3. Decisions

### 3.1 Camera: HK scale by default, zoomed out for spectacle
- **Default zoom 1.0 (13.5 PH).** This is HK's measured scale, and our short-reach boxing kit needs it: the jab reaches 1.06 tiles, a Seize reaches 52 px, and telegraphs are colour fills. Ori's 21 PH works because its attack auto-targets. Ori's flow comes from geometry, not zoom.
- **Zoom is a property of the zone**, held for as long as you're inside it: `open` 0.9 for big chambers and runs, `vista` 0.75–0.8 for set-piece spaces. Only the *framing bias* toward the shot is one-shot. Arenas are `lock` at 1.0, and rooms with an active enemy stay at ≥ 0.9. Limits are 0.75–1.1, and changes ease over 45–60 f.
- **Every set piece declares its shot**: a rectangle in tiles, a zoom, and the point it's taken from. The world PNG draws it.
- **Look up and down goes to about 6 tiles (35% of the view)**, as in HK, so tall squares and balconies can be read.

### 3.2 Terrain: rectilinear bones, organic skin, three floor slopes
- **Collision:** the 64 px grid plus **floor-only slopes at 1:4 (14°), 1:2 (26.6°) and 1:1 (45°)**, as integer heightfield tables.
  - No ceiling slopes and no curved collision. A curve is a baked chain of slopes, and only the skin draws the spline.
  - 1:4 is included because 27–28% of Ori's floor lies at 5–15°, which is gentler than 1:2.
- **Slopes add no momentum:** `vx` is constant, ground-stick holds you downhill, no crest launches you, and a jump from a slope is a normal jump. That keeps the reach table exact.
- **Limits:** 45° makes up ≤ 5% of any flow chain. Walkable up to 45°, and anything steeper is a wall.
- **Mix by region** (shares of walkable top length):

| | Flat | 1:4 | 1:2 | 45° |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tally hub | 55–65% | 20–30% | 5–15% | ≤ 5% |
| Cellars | 30–40% | 25–30% | 20–25% | 10–15% |

- **The skin** follows `level-toolchain.md` §4.1's honesty rules. Straight runs are handled differently per region:
  - **Cellars:** no wall or ceiling collision runs straight for more than 12 tiles.
  - **The hub:** facades stay straight, and props (signs, awnings, bunting, window grids) break them up.
- **Brushes:** vault stamps for Cellars chambers, and `blob` or `tunnel` only for spoil and connecting tunnels.
- **The dead band is deliberate.** Plain masonry can't be clung to (W4), so there's a lint: any bot chain that reaches a wall at |vx| > 6 px/f with no facing, spring or drop within 2 tiles is flagged.

### 3.3 Room model and authoring
- **Author the region as one section, then cut rooms.** Rooms are 1–8 × 1–5 cells at real world positions. Strata sit at fixed rows (§2.2).
- **Seams sit at necks** (an opening ≤ 8 tiles on its axis) **or at turns.** At every seam, the floor stays at the same height and is flat for 3 tiles either side.
- **Edge exits keep** `vx`, `vy`, facing, coyote and the Slip. They take ≤ 8 f with no black. The camera keeps its world position and never re-snaps. **Neighbour peek is required**: there's never void past an open exit.
- **Doors** (for interiors and lifts) fade in ≤ 250 ms. Hidden cuts are fine if the same signature object shows on both sides.
- **Seamless merge is deferred.** Switch to it if the seam-pair review or the runner flags seams in two consecutive loops.
- **A brass lot-line stud marks each exit** as a waymark. It no longer resets anything.

### 3.4 Carry rule: this replaces world-design W2's room-exit reset
**Rule.** The bag, the objects you've levied, and the sources you've ghosted all **persist across seams and doors within a district**. Everything resets, in one visible moment where the sounds ribbon home, when you:
- **rest at a Corner** ("filing your haul");
- **die**;
- or cross a **district line**. A district line is a marked Registry threshold, placed only at a hatch, a lift, a gate or a neck, never mid-flow. The first region has one, at the Cross hatch, between the Tally and the Cellars.

What else changes:
- Latches, shortcuts and boss flags stay as save flags.
- A district holds at most 6 placed objects; when you place another, the oldest goes home.
- A FIFO push re-arms a source wherever it is.
- **Enemies default to resetting on rest or death**, not on re-entering a room. HK-style room reset is the A/B test, and the runner decides between them.

**Why.**
- In big rooms with peek and momentum, a reset at the seam is a visible undo: a wall you ghosted reappears in the peek.
- A room reset also forbids any sound-assisted chain across a seam.
- It kills the carry-back fantasy, which is the core "put back" verb.
- One reset moment, at rest, matches HK's bench and Silksong's approach.

**Impact on gates and the validator.**
- **G3 becomes district palette arithmetic.** A reach gate holds against the kit, plus the **carry-in palette**, plus fever. The carry-in palette is the colours of every source reachable in the same district, upstream of the gate, with the kit available at the gate's earliest visit. The validator computes it as a union over its existing reachability pass, with no inventory search. Bot proofs for reach gates start from a worst-case bag seeded with that palette.
- **The consequence:** in any district where pink can be carried, distance alone can't hold a reach gate under about 18 tiles of height or 22 tiles of gap (pink chain bound 16.5 up, about 21 of drift). Such gates must instead be:
  - **sealed** (W3 already prefers this);
  - **lintel-bounded**: a ceiling of ≤ 3 tiles over a gap, which kills spring height and jump arcs. It's a new gate rule, and it fits the Cellars' low lintels;
  - or teach gates (G8), which hold only against sources upstream at the earliest visit.
- **World-design's slice needs re-checking:**
  - e13 (Slip gap) becomes a lintel Slip gap.
  - e14 (the rope-cut shaft) becomes a sealed cage.
  - The Bourse Stairs become their own district, with a line at their foot, so B8's updraft keeps a Stairs-only palette.
  - The Short Sale break (§4.4) is re-derived.
- **Seize walls re-arm on rest.** Any seize wall on a return route must be seizable from both sides, or be a latch flag.
- **Combat spec C6** ("emptied when you leave a room") changes to this rule.
- **Kill criterion:** revert to room reset *for gate rooms only* if either of these happens:
  - more than a third of the region's intended reach gates can't be held;
  - the blind runner hoards sounds and flattens the platforming.

### 3.5 Tallage-only space rules
- **Seizing changes the room, not just a door.** Lights and machines powered by a source die when it's seized (the C02 furnace). Every region has at least one room whose shape depends on what you took.
- **Foreclosed spaces are silent:** dashed ghost walls you walk through, no hum, no light, struck through on the ledger. "Quiet means gone."
- **Every district has a signature object with ≥ 7 uses.** In the Cellars it's the scale and counterweight:
  1. landmark (the Great Scale);
  2. lock (weigh-plate);
  3. platform (pans);
  4. elevator (the counterweight cage);
  5. shortcut (the store lift);
  6. hazard (the wrong chain);
  7. room twist (the weigh-bridge corridor);
  8. reward (the Copyist's ledger scale).
- **The night gets later:** the Bell tolls every 6 min of play, and the crowd and ticker step up.
- **The Board is a map in the fiction.** Its lines name lots and places ("LOT 19 · RING SHOES · DISTRAINT STORE").

## 7. Experience rubric

### 7.1 The fun review (first, every loop)
Asked of the blind runner, the critic, and the user (who watches the loop's video):
1. **What was the most fun moment?** (with a timestamp)
2. **What was the most boring moment?** Any answer is a top-priority bug.
3. **When did you want to stop?**
4. **Would you keep playing?** Yes or no, and why.

**Pass:** every room in the route has a fun beat that at least one reviewer names, nothing is called boring, and the answer to "keep playing" is yes.

### 7.2 Protocol
1. **The blind runner.**
   - A fresh agent with no repo docs, no `__game.state()`, and no ledger until the game gives it one. It works from screenshots and key presses (playwright-cli), with the control strip and one in-fiction goal.
   - It writes a line every 30 s of play: *goal / see / want*.
   - At minute 10 (6 in the proof) it writes an adjacency sketch with up and down marked, and a 100-word route description for another player.
2. **The instrumented pass.** The bot runs the designated routes, and `world -- metrics` plus the traces produce the automated checks. The CLI's timing is too coarse for the runner to judge flow, so flow comes from here.
3. **The critic.** Reviews the runner's clip and the bot's clip, at 0.25× for seams and pops and at 1× for flow and composition, plus the seam-pair images. Answers with timestamps.
