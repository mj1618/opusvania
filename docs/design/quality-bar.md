# The quality bar: reference first areas torn down, and a rubric by layer

Draft 1, 2026-10-18. This is **the bar** the review panel (`STUDIO.md`, the onion loop) scores against. It covers
everything except art: layout, pacing, encounters, feel, wayfinding, secrets, teaching, the camera, readability and how
the story is staged. The references are Hollow Knight (HK), Ori and the Blind Forest (Ori), Nine Sols and Celeste,
judged on their first areas at the room and chunk layers and on their whole games at the game layer. **Ori sets the
target length** (Matt, 2026-10-18: "an Ori-length game"). **All four set the pacing.**

How to read the numbers:
- Counts marked *(src)* come from the walkthroughs and wikis listed at the bottom.
- Counts marked *(est.)* are our reading of the games, from walkthrough order and typical first-run times. They are
  good to about ±30%. A reviewer who checks one against a timestamped longplay should correct it here.
- "First-timer minutes" means a first run without a guide, not a speedrun.

The rubric in §3 is what reviewers score. §1–2 explain where its numbers come from. §5 is the panel's starting list of
suspects for the wood.

## 1. Teardowns

### 1.1 Hollow Knight: King's Pass → Dirtmouth → Forgotten Crossroads → False Knight (≈ 45–70 min first run)

| # | Space | Job | What happens | Why it is remembered |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The fall into King's Pass | story, teach | The Knight drops in from the cliffs. Control comes in seconds. A short cave to the **left** of the spawn holds the first geo deposit *(src)* | The first input that pays is going the "wrong" way: the game rewards curiosity in the first ten seconds |
| 2 | The tunnel | teach (strike) | Breakable doors block the way, with Crawlids between them *(src)*. The nail is taught because the way on is a wall you hit | Doors that crack under the nail |
| 3 | First platforming room | teach, reward | A geo deposit in the bottom corner, a climb to a tablet that teaches **Focus** (heal) *(src)* | A big vertical room: the first time the camera shows height |
| 4 | Side room and the upper room | test, reward | A flying enemy (Vengefly) on the climb, a geo deposit, and a **Lifeblood cocoon** at the top *(src)* | The cocoon: an odd blue thing that gives extra masks |
| 5 | The collapsing floor | twist | The path shakes and gives way. An alcove off the drop holds a deposit *(src)* | The floor betrays you once, safely |
| 6 | Spike crossing | test | Spikes crossed on platforms. Spikes cost one mask and put you back on safe ground | The first real hazard |
| 7 | The optional spike pit | secret, test | **Pogo off the spikes** (down-slash) across a pit to a chest: the **Fury of the Fallen** charm *(src)*. The pogo is never required here | A risk line paid with a charm, where a new player first discovers the pogo by trying |
| 8 | The board and the gate | rest, vista | Break a board to open a hole, read the tablet at the edge of Hallownest, break the great gate *(src)* | The gate: "you are entering a kingdom" |
| 9 | Dirtmouth | rest, story, hub | A near-empty town in the rain: Elderbug, shut houses, a bench, the well. One way down | Silence and space after the pass; the well is the only question |
| 10 | Crossroads entrance | rest, teach | Down the well. A bench close by. Husks that walk and charge | A second "start": the map is now blank |
| 11 | Crossroads, first hour | explore | 14 enemy kinds across the area *(src)*; Quirrel at the Temple of the Black Egg (story, a sealed door); **Cornifer's paper trail and humming** lead to the map (30 geo) *(src)*; 5 grubs, one behind a breakable wall, one reached by pogoing a Vengefly *(src)*; ~14 geo caches *(src)*; a hot spring; 2 benches plus a stag station *(src)* | Each room has one thing: the temple door, the acid pool, the spiked pit, the stag bell |
| 12 | Gruz Mother (optional) | boss | A sleeping fly in an arena. After it falls, 7–8 Gruzzers burst out and must be cleared *(src)* | A boss you can walk past, then a twist at its death |
| 13 | False Knight | boss | A large hall in the centre of the area. He drops from the ceiling. Slam, leap, a floor shockwave, then rocks from the ceiling in later phases. After each stagger he falls on his belly and the **head is the target**. In the last phase he crashes through the floor, you follow him down, and the maggot inside bursts out *(src)*. 200 geo and the City Crest *(src)* | The armour is a lie and the floor breaks: a reveal, staged through mechanics |
| 14 | Ancestral Mound | reward, loop | Up through a switch near the arena, the first spell (**Vengeful Spirit**) *(src)*. A lever near False Knight opens a shortcut back toward Cornifer *(src)* | The first spell lands just after the first boss |

**Density.** King's Pass is about 5 minutes and holds 5 geo deposits, a cocoon, a charm, a tablet, two enemy kinds,
several breakable doors, a collapse, two spike sections and a gate *(src)*. That is **about 5 notable events a
minute**, with **one find every ~40 s**. The Crossroads drops to about 1–2 events a minute because it adds map reading,
and it holds **14 enemy kinds, 5 grubs, ~14 caches, 2 bosses, 3–4 NPCs and 2 benches** *(src)*. A first-timer meets
roughly 8–10 enemy kinds before False Knight *(est.)*.

**Challenge types in the first 45 min** *(est.)*: walker, charger, flyer, leaper, shielded or armoured, spitter (Aspid),
spikes, acid (a lock, not a hazard), collapsing floor, pogo over hazards, two bosses. That is **10+ kinds, with
something new every 3–5 minutes**.

**Getting lost and found.** HK withholds the map on purpose: you have none until Cornifer, and his trail of paper and
humming is a breadcrumb you can follow by sound. Benches are rest and orientation at once. The Crossroads is a
crossroads: four or five exits with locked ones that read as locks (the acid, the temple door, a gap too high). It is
the one place in our references where a first-timer **is meant to wander**, and it is bounded by being small.

**Loops.** Within the first hour: the lever shortcut after False Knight, the stag station (paid), and the well back up.
Later, the whole area transforms (Infected Crossroads) so that the return feels new (GMTK, Silksong essay).

**Encounters.** Usually one or two enemy kinds plus terrain: a Vengefly over a climb, a Husk on a narrow ledge, Aspids
in a pit. They are composed with the terrain, not set out on flat floors.

**First boss setup.** The arena is a big empty room in the middle of the map that you pass near; the boss is a giant
in armour, which a first-timer fears; the punish target (the head) is shown by the stagger; the reveal (the maggot) is
the story. A bench is a short walk away.

**Pacing.** Spike (King's Pass enemies) → rest (Dirtmouth, ~2 min of quiet) → slow open exploration → optional spike
(Gruz Mother) → main spike (False Knight) → reward and loop (spell, shortcut).

**What a first-timer feels.** Small and curious in the pass, lonely in Dirtmouth, oriented by the first bench, then
mildly lost and pleased when the map and the paper trail resolve it. The first boss feels big and is beaten in 1–4
tries.

### 1.2 Ori and the Blind Forest: prologue → Sunken Glades → Hollow Grove → Moon Grotto (≈ 1 h 30 to Double Jump)

| # | Space | Job | What happens | Why it is remembered |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Prologue (Spirit Tree, Naru) | story | The playable ~10-min prologue that Moon spent 8 months on (`metroidvania-craft.md`). Ori is orphaned | The emotional anchor: you are playing when it hurts |
| 2 | Sunken Glades, west | teach, secret | A cave with the first Spirit Light Container right at the start; an energy cell where a creature **rams a wall open** for you *(src)* | Enemies used as tools in the first minutes |
| 3 | The Spirit Tree's light | story, ability | Sein joins: **Spirit Flame** (auto-aimed attack) *(src)* | The companion arrives as a light |
| 4 | Spirit Well | rest | Save and heal *(src)*. Ori also saves anywhere with a **Soul Link** that costs energy | The player decides where to rest |
| 5 | Keystones and the gate | teach | A boulder to pull, swinging logs, four keystones open a Spirit Gate *(src)* | A small fetch quest that tours the area |
| 6 | First Ancestral Tree | ability | **Wall Jump** (Fil) *(src)*; an energy cell up the wall right after | The tree ritual, then an instant payoff |
| 7 | Map Stone | wayfinding | A fragment found nearby unlocks the area map *(src)* | Map as a reward you fetch |
| 8 | Visible-but-locked | promise | One secret in sight but unreachable until later skills; an Energy Gate you cannot open yet *(src)* | Two promises in the first area |
| 9 | Hollow Grove | ability, explore | A creature **stomps the ground open**; a lever lowers a log; **Charge Flame** from Ano's tree, which breaks certain walls *(src)*; spike plants, purple water, bounce pads, a spider, a web holding a rock *(src)* | Each area has a material toy (logs, webs, bounce pads) |
| 10 | Moon Grotto | story, chase | **Gumo leads you**, steals, traps you in a room with a creature, then drops you into his hideout *(src)*; fireball creatures break barriers for you; two lasers timed with a gap and a health plant *(src)* | A character who moves the player around the map |
| 11 | Gumo's Hideout | ability | Spikes, moving pillars, two keystones, **Double Jump** from Leru's tree, an energy cell up top only reachable with it *(src)* | Instant payoff again: the new jump opens the next pickup |

**Density** *(src, counted from the walkthrough)*: Sunken Glades (~25 min) holds 4 Spirit Light Containers, 2 energy
cells, 1 life cell, 4+ keystones, a map stone and fragment, a well, two abilities and two visible locked secrets.
That is **about one find every 2 minutes plus two promises**. Hollow Grove (~25 min) has one of each cell kind, a map
stone, a well, an energy door and an ability. Moon Grotto to Double Jump (~40 min) has 3 containers, 2 energy cells, 2
keystones, a fragment, a well and 3 Gumo beats.

**Ability cadence in the opening** *(est.)*: Spirit Flame ~0:12, Wall Jump ~0:30, Charge Flame ~0:55, Double Jump
~1:30. That is **four abilities in 90 minutes, each with a payoff within one or two rooms**.

**Challenge types.** Ori's density is in movement, not combat: logs, boulders, bounce pads, swinging spikes, lasers,
moving pillars, webs, water, and creatures used as tools. **Every sub-area adds one terrain toy.** Enemies are few
(3–5 kinds in the first hour, *est.*) and act mostly as moving hazards.

**Getting lost and found.** The map is fetched (map stone plus fragment) area by area. Keystone doors pull you through
every corner of a small area. Gumo literally leads you. The world is one continuous space, so you rarely feel lost: you
feel "not yet able".

**Loops.** A breakable wall in Hollow Grove leads back to Sunken Glades *(src)*. Abilities reopen visible secrets almost
at once.

**Pacing.** Ori's opening has no boss. Its peaks are the prologue (emotion), each tree (ability), and the chase into
Gumo's trap. The first combat-style climax is the **Ginso Tree escape** at roughly 2–2.5 hours, a rising-water chase
that tests the abilities learned so far.

**What a first-timer feels.** Wonder, then competence that grows every 20–30 minutes, then the thrill of the escape.

### 1.3 Nine Sols: the village → Apeman Facility → Baichung → Yingzhao (≈ 2–3 h to the first Sol, *est.*)

| # | Space | Job | What happens | Why it is remembered |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The village (Peach Blossom) | story, teach | After the intro, ShuanShuan teaches movement; then the combo attack against worms *(src)* | A home that the story will take away |
| 2 | The pig chase | optional, teach | A pig to chase with a rope climb, a jump and the **grappling hook**, for the Crimson Hibiscus *(src)*; a blocked path over water to remember *(src)* | A comic chase that teaches the grapple |
| 3 | The ceremony | story | Talk to everyone; cutscenes with Ruyi and Shennong; descend the lift *(src)* | The calm before the facility |
| 4 | Apeman Facility, upper | teach, test | A Root Node (save) near the start; a charging enemy at the lift exit; a heavy enemy to dodge; dash through lasers; wall jump; hack a lift *(src)* | The tone flips from village to industrial horror |
| 5 | Facility, to Baichung | test, secret | Grapple to ledges, moving platforms, the grapple in combat; a **gauntlet** wave fight; a secret room with a component; a gap you can't cross yet ("remember it"); **a healing station just before the boss** *(src)* | The gauntlet is the first "everything at once" |
| 6 | Baichung (Red Tiger Elite) | boss | Four sword attacks, three of them parryable *(src)*. **If you die, you meet Lear, who teaches basic combat** *(src)*. A win gives the Stasis Jade | Failure moves the story forward instead of resetting it |
| 7 | Path to the Radiant Pagoda | explore, test | Shielded enemies, heavy enemies, a rushing dog; a spinning switch; the elite **Jiaoduan** (kite him into the lasers); a call from Heng and a cutscene; a breakable wall to a secret room; a shop; the Shanhai 9000 names the goal *(src)*. Abilities in use: grapple, wall jump, **Tai-Chi Kick**, **Mystic Nymph** *(src)* | Hazards that also hurt enemies; the goal named by an NPC |
| 8 | Yingzhao | boss | A large room with electric floors at both edges; 1400 HP then 2100 HP; P1 slam-and-sweep and a charge that ends in a parryable stab; **P2 adds a Tai-Chi kick check when he glows green** and a crimson slam *(src)*. A Root Node right before *(src)* | The exam for parry, plus the newest ability as a phase-two tell |
| 9 | Kuafu | story, reward | After Yingzhao, Kuafu's memories and the first Sol Seal *(src)* | The boss guards a person, and the person is the payoff |

**Density.** Nine Sols front-loads story (village, ceremony, calls) and then runs dense combat: **4–6 enemy kinds, an
elite and a gauntlet before the first boss** *(est.)*, a mini-boss, and two named bosses in the first 2–3 hours.
**Every boss is preceded by a save and a heal.** Its secrets are breakable walls and chests (one Jin chest or item
every few rooms: the Apeman Facility alone lists 12 items and 6 Jin chests *(src)*).

**Encounters.** Its signature is **one readable colour language** for attacks (parryable vs not) and hazards that
work on enemies too (lasers, electric floors). Fights are composed: a shielded enemy next to a heavy, a dog in a
corridor with lasers.

**First boss setup.** Baichung comes after a gauntlet that rehearses his rhythm. The fail-forward (Lear) turns the
first wall a new player hits into a tutorial. Yingzhao's second phase tests the ability gained right before him.

**What a first-timer feels.** Grief and unease in the village, then pressure. Every boss is a lesson you will lose
several times and then perform.

### 1.4 Celeste, chapter 1 (Forsaken City): the gold standard for room-by-room teaching (≈ 20–40 min, *est.*)

| Checkpoint | Rooms *(est.)* | What it introduces *(src)* | Strawberries *(src)* | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Start** | ~12 (6 mandatory *(src)*) | the dash in the opening room; springs off walls (room 2); smashable walls; disappearing (crumble) platforms; **traffic blocks** (zippers) in room 4 with their momentum | 6 | A **winged strawberry** flies off if you dash, so you must reach it without dashing (via crumble blocks). The first **berry room** is signposted by light from the left |
| **Crossing** | ~12 | falling platforms and **dash crystals**; traffic blocks used against you | 9 | Theo, the first person; the **Crystal Heart**, a bird puzzle whose dash order is shown by a flashing screen *(src)* |
| **Chasm** | ~10 | traffic blocks in harder combinations | 5 | The **Cassette** (B-side). Ends at the memorial, a campfire, sleep: the rest beat |

**Density.** Each room is one screen and one idea, solved in 10–60 s, with an input decision every second or two.
That is **about 1 strawberry every 1.5 rooms**. Something new is introduced every 3–5 rooms. Each mechanic follows
the four beats: shown safe, developed, twisted (used against you), then combined.

**Teaching.** A new mechanic first appears where failing is free and the solution is the obvious action. The next room
adds one twist. Strawberries are the **risk line**: always visible or hinted (a gap in the wall, light from an exit), and
always harder than the main path. Death is instant and costs a few seconds (rooms are checkpoints), so a hard room
never wastes time.

**What a first-timer feels.** "I can do this", then "I did that". The strawberries feel like dares, never chores.

Climb and Charge Jump also arrive in the second half (*est.*). Ori has **about 11 movement or combat skills plus 3
elements in about 9–10 hours**: **4 in the first 90 minutes, then about one every 50–70 minutes**. It has almost no
conventional bosses. Its peaks are **three escapes** at roughly 2.5, 6 and 9 hours, Kuro encounters between them, and
each area's signature puzzle mechanic (water, gravity, mist, lava). Rest is wells plus a save anywhere, so the
player sets the rest cadence. The difficulty climbs area by area, with each escape as a spike.

**HK's cadence** *(est.)*: Vengeful Spirit ~0:45, Mothwing Cloak (dash, after Hornet) ~2–3 h, Mantis Claw ~4–6 h,
Desolate Dive and Crystal Heart ~8–10 h, Dream Nail ~10 h, Monarch Wings ~12–15 h, Isma's Tear and Shade Cloak late.
That is **about 10 abilities over ~27 h, front-loaded (3 in the first 5 h), then one every 2–3 h**. With optional ones
counted, there are 40+ bosses, which is **about one boss an hour on a first run**. Benches sit **every 5–10 rooms**
within an area, and the run back to a boss is sometimes long (a common complaint). Dirtmouth and the stag network act
as the hub. Return trips get faster as the stag stations open.

**Nine Sols' cadence** *(est.)*: one Sol per zone, **one major boss every ~2 h** with elites and mini-bosses between,
a **return to the hub (the Four Seasons Pavilion) after each Sol** for story and upgrades, **a Root Node and a heal
before every boss**, and about 8 abilities, one per 2–2.5 h, front-loaded.

**Scaled to an Ori-length game (9–10 h first run)**, the three games agree on these rates:
- **The first hour is the densest.** Ori gives 4 abilities in 90 minutes, HK 2–3 by its first boss, and Nine Sols 3–4
  by Yingzhao. After that the cadence slows to about one per 50–75 minutes (Ori) or one per 2 h (HK and Nine Sols at
  2–3× Ori's length; scaled to 9 h, that is one per 45–70 minutes).
- **A peak every 30–60 minutes.** A boss, an escape or a set piece. HK's rate is about one an hour at its density, and
  Ori's area climaxes plus its trees and Kuro beats come every 30–45 minutes.
- **A region climax every 1.5–3 h.** Ori's escapes, Nine Sols' Sols, HK's area bosses.
- **A hub or home visit every 1.5–2.5 h** (the Pavilion, Dirtmouth or the stag network, the Spirit Tree's glades).
- **Areas of 25–60 minutes**, grouped into regions of 1.5–2.5 h.
- **Rest within ~4 minutes of play** in normal rooms and **a save and heal at every boss door** (Nine Sols' rule;
  HK's long runbacks are its most criticised pacing flaw).

## 2. What makes them AAA: concrete principles by layer

**Room**
1. **One idea, with a twist in the same room.** Celeste shows the idea safely, then turns it within the room, and the
   next room combines it. HK's collapsing floor is a twist on a floor you already trust.
2. **The room has a picture.** Every HK room has one object you could draw from memory: the temple door, the acid pool,
   the stag bell, the Focus tablet. In grey, that picture has to come from shape: a silhouette, a scale, an odd
   geometry.
3. **Something to find within sight of the main line.** King's Pass rewards going left in the first ten seconds. Ori
   places a container at the start of its first area. Celeste lights the berry room's doorway.
4. **Terrain and enemies are composed together.** The Vengefly is over a climb, the dog is in a laser corridor, Gumo's
   fireball creatures break walls. An enemy on a flat floor in an empty room is a test fixture, not an encounter.
5. **Failure costs seconds.** Celeste rooms are checkpoints; HK's spikes put you back on safe ground; Ori saves
   anywhere.
6. **The camera frames the question.** Arenas lock, the vertical rooms show their height, and the berry or secret is
   inside the frame or hinted by its light.

**Chunk**
7. **Novelty every 3–6 minutes**, mixing ability, enemy, terrain toy, set piece and story.
8. **A ladder for every verb**: safe, developed, twisted, tested, then retired or combined (Celeste; Hayashida's four
   beats).
9. **Many small finds of several kinds.** HK has geo, grubs, charms, masks, lore and NPCs; Ori has three kinds of
   cells, keystones and maps. Several currencies mean every find feels different.
10. **Visible promises.** Every area shows 2+ places you can't reach yet, and a new ability pays one within minutes.
11. **People.** Every first area has 1–4 characters with a want: Quirrel, Cornifer, Gumo, ShuanShuan, Theo.
12. **A loop closes at least once.** The lever, the stag, the breakable wall back.
13. **The first boss is set up by the space.** The arena is seen or felt before the fight (HK's central hall, Nine
    Sols' Pagoda named by an NPC), the rhythm is rehearsed (Nine Sols' gauntlet), and the reveal is staged through the
    fight (False Knight's floor, the maggot).
14. **Rest is placed against tension.** Dirtmouth after the pass; the village before the facility; the memorial
    campfire after the chasm.

**Region and game**
15. **Each area has a signature mechanic.** Ori: water, gravity, mist, lava. Celeste: one per chapter. HK: acid, dark,
    wind, crystals.
16. **Ability gives are front-loaded, then spaced**, and each one pays something visible within 5 minutes.
17. **The world changes on return** (Infected Crossroads, Ori's restored areas, our skies clearing).
18. **Peaks have a rhythm**: a boss, escape or set piece every 30–60 minutes and a region climax every 1.5–3 h.
19. **Return trips get faster** (stags, Ori DE's well warps, shortcuts).

## 3. The rubric

Each item has an id, a pass threshold, what it is grounded in, and **how to check it**:
- **[bot]**: the persona bots' telemetry (`npm run playtest`);
- **[journal]**: the first-timer agents' journals and screenshots;
- **[map]**: the room graph, the data and the world check (`world.json`, `wood.md`);
- **[play]**: the reviewer's own play with real keys;
- **[lint]**: something the data can check, which should become a validator.

A room or chunk **passes** when every **must** item passes and at most two **should** items fail. Bots run 3–15×
faster than people (`memory/playtest-harness.md`), so any time threshold is judged on first-timer journals or the
reviewer's own play, and bots only flag outliers.

### 3a. Room

| Id | Item | Pass threshold | Grounded in | Check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 **must** | **One clear purpose** | The room's `purpose` tag is one of teach / test / rest / vista / secret / boss / corridor / set piece / hub. A first-timer can say in one sentence what the room was about | Celeste; Dead Cells tags | [map] [journal] |
| R2 **must** | **A memorable feature** | One shape a reviewer can sketch from memory after one pass (a silhouette, a scale change, an odd geometry, a lit object), and it is different from every other room in the chunk. "A flat floor with a wick" fails | HK room identity | [play]: the reviewer sketches each room from memory after the run; two rooms that sketch alike fail |
| R3 **must** | **At least one decision** | At least one real choice of line, order, risk or timing (high or low line, fight or skip, snuff first or dodge first). A corridor may skip this only if it is ≤ 15 s | Celeste; HK King's Pass | [play] [bot]: two personas take different traces |
| R4 **must** | **Something to find** | A reward, secret or visible promise within **one screen (1920 px) of the main line**, or the room is ≤ 1.5 screens. Rooms > 3 screens need two | HK: one find per 40 s in the pass; Ori: 1 per 2 min | [map] |
| R5 **must** | **No dead walking** | No stretch of **more than 6 s** of travel with no decision, threat, find or view. Backtracking corridors: ≤ 10 s | Celeste's 10–60 s rooms; King's Pass at 5 events a minute | [bot]: longest stretch with no input change and no event; [play] |
| R6 **must** | **Readable entry and exit** | Within 2 s of entering, the way on or its light can be seen or inferred. Nothing hostile in range of the door. The exit reads as an exit (light, moths, a shape) | `metroidvania-craft.md` §8 | [journal] (time to first move toward the exit) [play] |
| R7 **must** | **Escalation inside the room** | A teach room adds a twist before it ends; a test room combines ≥ 2 of {verb, hazard, creature, lamp}, and the hardest moment is near the end, not at the door | Celeste's four beats | [play] [map] |

## 4. Greybox quality versus art quality

**Must already be great in grey** (a reviewer may not say "the art will fix it"):
- **Layout and flow**: every room passes R1–R9, R11. The question "is this room interesting?" is answered by shape.
- **Timing and rhythm**: reach, gap margins, tells in frames, punish windows, run backs, chunk minutes.
- **Readability by shape and colour code**: the greybox palette is a language, and must be consistent: hazards one
  colour, kickable faces one, slick faces another, one-ways, breakables (rotten bark must have its tell in grey, as a
  shape or pattern, not a label), lamp core vs pull radius, creature hurt boxes vs their danger parts, the hunt colour
  and quickening flash. Labels off, it still reads (R12).
- **Room identity**: in grey, a room's picture is its silhouette. Odd geometry, a change of scale, one big object (a
  hanging trunk, a web disc, a well) and the lamp layout are what make it memorable. Rooms that only differ in art
  fail R2.
- **Feedback (juice)**: hitstop (snuff 9 frames, hits 4/4/7), screen shake (one trauma value), hit flashes, the
  snuff's dark bloom (even as a flat circle), moths bursting (dots are fine), the Caught orbit, landing dust as a
  shape, the boss phase break. Feel is judged in grey, so it must be in grey.
- **Camera**: follow, look-ahead, locks, zones, vista lifts, the look-up beats. All of it is final in grey.
- **Story staging**: glimpses, the relight, Tatter's positions, the vista holds. Their timing and framing are judged
  in grey with placeholder shapes.
- **Audio cues for tells** may wait (we ship muted), so every tell must already read with the sound off.

**May wait for art**: texture, lighting mood beyond the light/dark code, parallax depth, particles beyond what reads
an event, character animation beyond the rig, the colour grade, the far vistas' content (a flat silhouette and the
title suffice), and the sound. A greybox vista must still be a camera move onto something with a shape.

**The test**: play a chunk with labels off. If a reviewer needs the art to say what a thing is, where to go, or what
just happened, that is a greybox fail.

## 5. Where the wood plan is most likely to fall short

From `docs/design/regions/wood.md` (draft 2), `memory/rooms.md`, `memory/playtest-harness.md`,
`memory/bosses.md`, and the length targets in `world.md` §4. These are suspects for the panel's first round, ranked.

1. **Too few creature kinds and too few compositions (C2, R10).** By minute 20 the player has met two creature kinds
   (burrback, hawker) plus the old burrback; the web and the Weaver come after minute 27. HK shows 8–10 kinds by its
   first boss and Nine Sols 4–6 plus an elite. Nearly every encounter is **one creature under one lamp**, often on a
   flat floor (R8 is "a flat floor the whole way", R9's burrback patrols the floor). Only R11 combines a creature, a
   lamp, walls and thorns. The threat lint (≤ 1.5 a lit screen) keeps rooms sparse. The risk is the market's top
   complaint, "empty".
2. **Room identity and decisions (R2, R3).** The rooms are named for their job (wicks, burrow, glidewalk, hawkers,
   lantern) and are built from reach tables, so several are boxes with one feature. R8 and R9 are both 3200×1600 with a
   floor at y 1280 and one lamp over it. Most rooms have one line through them. The gaps were engineered so that
   "the plain jump misses by ≥ 100", which yields correct rooms, not memorable ones.
3. **One reward kind (C3, G11).** Every find is a lamp worth 5 or 10 moths, the same currency as the critical path,
   and every secret uses one language (rotten bark). HK and Ori each have 3–5 kinds of find in their first area. In
   38 minutes the wood has about 7 optional finds (two high wicks, the pocket, the crown, S1, S2, S3), **one every
   ~5 min**, against the bar's 2–3. The first health milestone at 110 moths is the only non-moth reward.
4. **Length: the wood is compressed, and the game is short of Ori (G1–G3, C14).** The wood packs glide, wall jump, a
   mini-boss, a boss and two set pieces into ~38 minutes. HK's first chunk takes 45–70 minutes for one boss and one
   spell; Ori takes ~55 minutes to its third ability. `world.md` §4 plans **~5.5 h main / ~7 h 100% over 5 regions**,
   against Ori's **~9 h / ~12.5–13 h**: about 60% of the target. Implications:
   - **The wood should be 50–75 minutes**, as one region of two areas (the wood floor to the hub, and the climb to the
     grove), each 25–40 minutes. More rooms and fuller ones, not more walking.
   - **The game needs 9–11 h**: roughly **5 regions of 1.5–2.5 h**, each with 2–3 areas (12–14 areas in all, like
     Ori's), or 6–7 regions by promoting stubs already planned (canopy, underways, deep pools, bat roost) into the
     main path.
   - **Abilities**: 7 named over 9–10 h is one per ~80 minutes, below G3's 40–75. Fill 2–4 slots (the core.md slots
     and verb upgrades, such as a longer glide or a fling upgrade) to reach 9–12.
   - **Peaks**: 6 bosses, 2 minis and 7 set pieces is 15 peaks, enough for one every ~40 minutes at 10 h, if they are
     spread out rather than bunched at region ends.
   - **Tier 1** (wood and valley) would then be ~3–4 h, not 1.5–2 h.
5. **Measured problems already in the build (R7, R9, C8).** The bots found that R6's gap-3 test can be skipped by
   gliding on after W5's throw; W4 catches her on two lines (its throw can land her in gap 2's thorns); L1 is hit in
   2 of 9 timings and catches her in 1–3 of 9 (the paper wanted 3–5 hits, never caught); W3's jump-cut works in 1–2
   of 9 (the paper: 3–5). These are lamp-verb forgiveness failures against principle 2 ("bend every timing window
   toward the player").
6. **Loops (C6).** The wood has one real shortcut (the grate) and an optional 5-minute return to R1–R5. The boss-to-hub
   drop was cut (decision 8), so the boss door is ~50 s from the hub by walking back through cleared rooms (R11, R9,
   R8), which risks R5 dead walking. HK has a lever, a stag and the well in its first hour.
7. **Dead walking (R5).** Several stretches are flat walks by design: "east through the dark rooms she cleared (a flat
   walk)" at 21:30 (~1 min), R8's dark east half, the walk back from R9's lock (~35 s), the return from the boss door.
   Dark rooms are rests by our rules, but a rest still needs a view, a find or a beat.
8. **People (C11).** Tatter appears at three patches and has no want the player acts on; the firefly is a glimpse.
   Every reference's first area has 1–4 characters with a want. This is a story call for Matt, but the panel should
   score whether the wood reads as inhabited.
9. **Teaching stacked late, and a test at the boss (C8, C13).** The pogo exam (the old burrback) and the Weaver's P3
   (pogo only) both test the pogo; P2 tests wall kicks only ~12 minutes after wall jump arrives, with R11 as its only
   test. Check that the Weaver's rhythm is rehearsed by an earlier encounter (Nine Sols' gauntlet). The orb web, the
   Weaver's language, is met once (R12) right before the fight.
10. **Unbuilt and stub rooms.** R10 and R11 are stubs, R12 and R13 aren't built (the Weaver is in `lab-weaver`), and
    greybox rooms draw creatures as hitboxes with no post. Before the first panel round, check that the greybox has
    the juice §4 requires (hitstop, shake, flashes, the snuff bloom), or the feel reviewers will score a missing
    layer, not a weak one.
