# Demo 2: the boss rush (2026-10-19)

One of three genre demos for the direction of Lampfall. Boot: `?world=demo-bossrush&fresh`.

## The pitch

Almost no level design, so everything rides on what already works: the movement, the blade and boss fights. The
inspirations are Hollow Knight's Pantheons, Furi, Cuphead and Titan Souls. A small moonlit hub holds three doors.
Each door leads to a short corridor and then an arena. Each boss teaches the tool you need for the next one, so the
run is a ladder:

| # | boss | where | how it fights | what the win teaches |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Weaver** | the warm hall | gentle, four phases: snuff the ring of flames, read its swinging drops, pogo it on the floor, put out its heart | **Dash** (X / Shift) |
| 2 | **The Gloamweaver** | the black roots | it hunts round its orb web and **lunges** down a line it shows you. Dodge it and it rests open. Bait the lunge into the silver light of a moonwick and it is dazzled, open for longer. P2: the swinging drops. P3: the frenzy, faster lunges | **Moonstep**: your dash passes through blows unharmed |
| 3 | **The Mantis** | the firefly's cache, under the brightest lamp | the fast duellist. It shows a threat display (wings flare, the eyespots flash quicker and quicker), then makes a crouched dash across the floor: jump it or Moonstep through it. It leaps and dives where you stood, and it guards: cut its front and it parries and ripostes; pogo it from above and the guard breaks. It flurries when you are close and hops back when crowded. P3 chains two moves and its dives send a ripple along the floor | the brightest lamp goes out and its moths go free. The rush is done |

The order is fixed: each door's silk opens when the boss before it falls. A first clear takes about 10–15 minutes
for a decent player, including deaths.

## How to play

- **Keys:** arrows or WASD move, Z / Space jump (hold to glide, again in the air for a double jump, at a wall to
  climb), C / J cut (↓ + C in the air is a pogo), X / Shift dash once the Weaver has fallen. To enter a door, press ↑
  while standing in it.
- **Retry:** dying re-forms you at the moon patch by the arena door, whole. Walk in and the fight starts over. From a
  death back to the fight takes about 3 s, and the name card then reads "attempt n".
- **On screen:** a name card as the doors seal, one health bar at the foot of the view (a notch per phase, the last
  blow's share trailing in orange), the phase's name as it begins, a white flash and a held frame on the final blow,
  then a card with the fight's time and what you learned. Back in the hub, a mark over each door shows its state:
  dark (shut), a breathing silver ring (open) or a warm disc (beaten). After all three, the hub shows the run's
  whole time.
- **Debug:** `__game.rush(n)` puts you at boss n's door with what you would have by then (1 Weaver, 2 Gloamweaver,
  3 Mantis, 0 hub). `__game.boss({phase: n})` jumps to a phase. `__game.boss().move` shows the Mantis's current move.

## What's in it

- **World:** `content/worlds/demo-bossrush.json`, with rooms `br-hub`, `br-weaver`, `br-gloam` and `br-mantis`,
  generated by `tools/levels/bossrush.ts`. Everything is painted in the Lanternroot kit: the night band for the hub,
  hall for the Weaver, roots for the Gloamweaver and the cache for the Mantis. The arenas are the tested ones moved
  into place: lab-weaver's and lr-roots' B3.
- **New boss:** the Mantis, in `src/sim/boss/mantis.ts` (tuning: `tuning.mantis`) and
  `src/render/bossrush/mantis.ts`. Its blade has its own hit box (`boss.hit*`), so its reach is not its body.
- **Framework additions:**
  - `boss.grant`: a win teaches an ability or upgrade.
  - The final-blow hold (`bossRush.finalHold`).
  - `boss.checkpoint`: the lock makes the door's patch your re-form point.
  - `boss.rush`: the Gloamweaver fought on its own, with no kindling.
  - `lures[].lit`: moonwicks that burn for good.
  - The Moonstep upgrade.
  - Boss camera shake budgets.
  - The painted Weaver.
  - The boss HUD (`src/render/bossrush/hud.ts`).
- **Tests and bots:** `tests/unit/bossrush.test.ts` checks the world, that both new fights are won by a bot that
  reads the tells (`tools/playtest/rush-bot.ts`), and the grant ladder. `npx tsx tools/playtest/rush-run.ts
  [--react 30] [--no-moonstep]` runs the bot headless. The bot found two softlocks that are now fixed: the
  Gloamweaver sat for ever in permanent silver light, and its P2 swings always stopped out of reach.

## What's good

- **Instant spectacle for little content.** Three rooms of play read as a whole game's worth of moments. The
  painted arenas (the web round the Gloam lamp, the firefly lamp over the cache's rings) are the best-looking
  screens we have.
- **The tell language reads.** A burning ember band on the floor where a strike will run, a reticle that tightens
  where a dive will land, eyespots that flash faster to the hit, silver edges on a guard. Every attack has a window
  after it.
- **The ladder works mechanically.** The dash is your escape from the lunges. The Moonstep turns the Mantis's dash
  into something you can go through instead of over, which is a different way to play the same fight.
- **Fast retries.** About 3 s from death to fight, with "attempt n" on the card. Furi's loop.
- **The Mantis has a duel shape** that our other bosses lack: a parry you must respect, a punish for mashing, and a
  reward for pogoing over it.

## What's weak (honestly)

- **Fun is not proven by a person yet.** The bots win: Mantis about 70–120 s, Gloamweaver about 40 s. Difficulty
  and fairness are guesses until Matt plays. The Mantis's riposte (a 12-frame tell after a parry) may feel unfair.
  The Gloamweaver's first phase falls fast if you bait it well.
- **The Weaver's first phase has no threat.** The heart's bite died with the fling (memory/bosses.md). It is a
  gentle warm-up of snuffing flames; Matt liked it, but it is the weakest minute.
- **The Mantis's art is a code-drawn rig.** The silhouette, wings and eyespots read well, but its walk and
  anticipation are stiff next to Wing, and it needs a real animation pass.
- **Sound is borrowed** from the Gloamweaver's set (still muted by default).
- **The hub is a corridor of doors, not a place.** There is nothing to do in it, and no gauntlet mode yet (a
  natural next step: all three back to back, with a timer).
- **Little to discover.** A rush has no exploration, which is the heart of a metroidvania. It also leaves out the
  "density" Matt likes in the test grove, since every moment is one fight.

## How it would scale to a full game

- **As the whole game:** a Furi or Cuphead shape. A hub that grows into a small world and 12–20 bosses, each with a
  reward. Content per hour is high because each boss is about one week of sim, art and tuning, but an 8-hour game
  would need 25 or more fights, or replayable modes (gauntlets, harder variants, grades). It would drop the
  metroidvania promise of the story (Wing's journey to the cave) to a string of arenas.
- **As a layer of the game (what I would recommend):** keep the hub-and-excursion or dense-grove structure for the
  world, and use what this demo built as the boss layer. That means the framework with grants and checkpoints, the
  painted HUD, the tell language, the Mantis as the valley's boss, a Pantheon-style gauntlet hall unlocked
  late, and a fight timer for mastery. The boss rush shows the combat can carry set pieces. It does not show it can
  carry hours on its own.
