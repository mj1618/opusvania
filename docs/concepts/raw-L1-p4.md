# Raw concepts: L1, pack 4

Seeds: NGC 1560 (a faint, hidden galaxy) · Mike Honeyman (British featherweight champion 1920–21) · "Isa Lang" (Tagalog "just one"; a slow burn, wanting your feelings returned) · words: nonwhite, redistrainer, diathetic, bagful, apobiotic, usefullish · lens: sound, silence or rhythm is physical · mood: feverish.

---

## 1. Associations (pushed past the first idea)

1. **Redistrainer.** *Distraint* means seizing someone's goods to pay a debt, and a redistrainer seizes them a second time. So: a bailiff who repossesses things. Going further, a bailiff who repossesses the *sounds* of things. Going further again, the second seizure means taking back what the creditors took, so the redistrainer quietly becomes a redistributor.
2. **Nonwhite → coloured noise.** White noise is only one colour. Pink, brown, blue and violet noise all have different spectral weight. Brown noise is heavy and rumbling, and violet noise hisses and cuts. Each noise colour could act as a physical material. White noise is the one colour that belongs to nobody. (In-game we'd say "coloured noise" and avoid the word "nonwhite", which has an unrelated racial meaning.)
3. **Featherweight.** This is a weight *class*. It suggests weigh-ins, cutting weight, bulking up, and a scale deciding what you're allowed to do. It also suggests a champion for exactly one year, whose belt could be taken away.
4. **Bagful.** A heavy bag in a boxing gym. A bagful of wind, as with Aeolus. A bailiff's sack. An inventory whose *mass* matters.
5. **Diathetic → diathesis → grammatical voice.** Active, passive and middle voice decide who does the verb and who has it done to them. So a character could switch *who is the subject* of every physical event. (Diathesis also means a predisposition to illness, which ties back to fever.)
6. **NGC 1560.** A dwarf galaxy that spins too fast for the mass we can see, the textbook case of hidden mass. It also sits behind the Milky Way's "Zone of Avoidance", a band of dust that hides it. Hidden mass suggests that silence might be what's heavy. The dust band suggests a place you can only sense, not see.
7. **"Isa Lang" / just one.** Reciprocity: a call only means something if it's answered. Call and response as physics, where a sound becomes solid only when it comes back. The slow burn suggests a relationship across distance that closes very slowly.
8. **Apobiotic.** The local death of one part of an organism. Seen the other way, it's a part that died away from its body and carries on without it, like a voice that outlived its throat.
9. **Feverish.** Heat shimmer, tinnitus, everything too loud and too fast, a speculative boom where prices rise like a temperature chart. A fever chart and a market ticker are the same graph.
10. **Usefullish.** A pawnbroker's shelf of almost-useful things. A bailiff's haul. The joy of a bad tool that's exactly right once.
11. **Structural cross.** Boxing has a *count*, where a knockdown opens a ten-beat window. Rhythm as a rule system, not as music.

## 2. Clichés to steer away from

1. A small silent wanderer explores a ruined kingdom and pieces together its lore from tablets and dying NPCs.
2. A forest spirit restores light and life to dying nature.
3. A lone figure wakes in a lab or facility with no memory and recovers powers that were "always theirs".
4. The world flips between two planes (light and dark, past and present, dream and real).
5. A corrupted god or spreading blight is the final boss, and the answer is purification.
6. (Also) the default toolkit of grapple, double jump and dash with no link to the world, and "it was all a coma dream".

Both concepts below take place in a **living, overcrowded, feverish present**, not a ruin.

## Concept A: DISTRAINT

**Hook:** A disgraced featherweight turned bailiff repossesses the sounds that hold a feverish boomtown together, and every noise she seizes un-makes the thing that made it.

### Premise and world logic
In the city of **Tallage**, a thing's *sound is its title deed*. If a wall hums, it has a legal right to be there. The same goes for a creaking bridge that bears weight or a furnace whose roar keeps it burning. The Hum Registry records every noise in the city, and the economy runs on buying, selling and mortgaging sounds. Tallage is in a fever. It's a speculative boom where everything is borrowed against everything else, and the whole city rattles louder each week as prices climb.

You are **Kid Tallow**. She was featherweight champion of the Tallage circuit for exactly one year, until her belt was distrained for her manager's debts. She now works as a **redistrainer**, a licensed bailiff whose job is to re-seize goods that have already been seized once and then smuggled back. Her private goal is to work her way up the chain of creditors to the top of the city and redistrain her own belt. As she goes, she learns where every stolen sound really went. Her arc runs from enforcer to redistributor.

The metroidvania structure comes from the ledger. Every gate is a sound someone owns. The map screen is a claims ledger: rooms are lots, and seized sounds are marked in red ink.

### Signature mechanic 1: Seize and Levy
- **Seize** is a short jab-grab. It strips a nonwhite sound from an object or enemy and puts it in your **bag**.
  - A muted object loses its claim to physical existence. A humming wall turns into a passable ghost outline. A furnace goes cold and can be climbed. A rattling lift freezes in place. A dog you seize the bark from can no longer lunge, because each enemy's attacks are tied to its sounds.
- **Levy** throws a sound from your bag. It lands and becomes something physical, and what it becomes depends on its **noise colour**:
  - **Brown** (low rumble: furnaces, engines, growls) is heavy. It falls, anchors, crushes and holds down pressure plates.
  - **Pink** (balanced: creaks, footsteps, chatter) settles into a springy platform that bounces you.
  - **Blue or violet** (hiss: steam, whistles, sharpening) is light and fast. It rises, cuts ropes and ricochets.
  - **White static** is legally unclaimable, so it *can't be seized*. White-noise zones are free land and hazards, and your bag slowly leaks there.
- Every puzzle has two moves: **take away** (seize to remove an obstacle) and **put back** (levy to build a route). Fights work the same way. You disarm an enemy by seizing its roar, then throw that roar back at it as a brown-noise slam.
- **The Count.** Knocked-down enemies get a ten-beat count, shown as a physical ring of ticks. Seize their voice during the count and they're repossessed for good, and you get their sound as loot. If they get up, they're furious and louder.

### Signature mechanic 2: Weigh-in
The sounds you carry have mass, so your **bag weight sets your weight class**, and your weight class changes your moveset:
- **Featherweight** (light bag): floaty jump, the skip double-jump, wall cling on humming surfaces, and updrafts can carry you.
- **Middleweight:** your standard kit.
- **Heavyweight:** no knockback, you sink in water, and your drop-punch breaks floors and pushes down scales.

**Weigh-in scales** are gates. Some doors only open for your class. So you cut weight by levying sounds away, or bulk up by seizing brown noise, and players plan routes around what's in the bag. It's inventory as a body.

### Ability list
1. **Seize** (start): jab-grab a sound at close range.
2. **Levy** (early): throw a carried sound as a physical object.
3. **Slip**: a boxer's slip, used as a dash. You get i-frames, and you pass fully through *muted* geometry. Muted walls normally slow you.
4. **Rope Skip**: a double-jump available only at featherweight. It rewards staying light.
5. **Writ**: nail a writ to a distant object to seize its sound at range. The writ's string doubles as a grapple while the object still hums (you swing from its claim).
6. **Corner Satchel**: a second bag compartment. Levy two colours together to combine them (pink + brown makes a heavy bouncer that breaks floors on its third bounce; violet + pink makes a rising platform).
7. **Standing Count**: hold any seized object in a ten-beat count. It hangs frozen in its last position, so a falling lift or a swinging gate becomes a timed platform.

### Biomes
1. **The Bourse Stairs.** A vertical trading floor. Brokers' shouts are physical pressure currents you ride upward. This is the tutorial for seizing, and the room gets louder and more dangerous as the fever ticker climbs.
2. **Mortgaged Row.** A quarter whose sounds have *already* been seized. Everything is ghost geometry, and you have to *levy* sounds from your bag to build floors. It's the reverse of every other biome: you're a builder with a limited budget.
3. **The Foundry Gyms.** Boxing gyms and forges full of brown noise, heavy bags and bellows blasts. Weigh-in scales are everywhere. You fight as a heavyweight or get blown off the ledges.
4. **The Clock Tenements.** Packed with pawned clocks. Platforms exist only on the tick. Seize a room's master clock and its rhythm stops, freezing everything. Levy ticks to make your own beat-platforms.
5. **The Static Flats.** A white-noise marsh at the city edge where the land is unclaimable. Nothing can be seized, your bag leaks, and you cross on what you brought with you.
6. **The Receivership.** A pressure-cooker vault at the top of the city where every distrained sound is stacked in sacks. The fever peaks here, and at the very top is the Exchange Bell that opens each trading day.

### Encounter or boss: The Auctioneer
He fights on a stage built from auction lots. His patter comes out as a stream of physical words, violet darts that speed up as the fever ticker rises. Every "going once… going twice… SOLD" is a three-beat pattern that ends in a gavel shockwave. On "SOLD", a floor lot vanishes because its sound is transferred to the buyer. You counter by **seizing "going twice"** out of the air, which breaks his cadence and stuns him, and by levying brown noise to replace floor lots before they sell. In the final phase he starts auctioning *your bag*, and each "SOLD" strips one of your carried sounds unless you slip out of his line of sight.

### Why it's not generic (honest critique)
- **Closest games:** Kirby and Mega Man (taking enemy powers), Hollow Knight's shade (see "death" below), Baba Is You (removing an object's property), and the gravity-gun school of pick-up-and-place puzzles.
- **How it differs:** *subtraction is the main verb*. Taking a sound away removes the thing's physical existence, so every seizure changes the level as well as your kit. Weight classes turn the inventory into movement physics. The setting is a busy, bureaucratic, boomtown city with a working-class boxer-bailiff, which is the opposite of a melancholy ruin.
- **Death:** when you're knocked out, the Receiver's clerks distrain your bag, and you have to redistrain it from a clerk who flees across the map. That's the shade loop again, but it follows from the fiction rather than being bolted on. **Weak spot:** it's still the shade loop.
- **Tonal risk:** a debt-collector hero can feel cynical. The redistrainer-to-redistributor arc needs to be written well.

### Greybox test (one room, under an hour)
Build one room containing a player box, a **humming wall** (solid, drawn with a vibrating outline), a **creaking ledge** too high to reach, and a **furnace** (brown noise) next to a pressure plate. Seize turns an object into a dashed outline and moves a coloured blob into a bag HUD that shows weight. Levy throws the blob. Pink lands as a bouncy platform, and brown falls and anchors. Jump height scales with bag weight in three steps.
**Question:** can a new player solve a three-step route through that room, and do seize and levy *feel* punchy? Does un-making the wall read instantly, and do they start trying to seize everything? If they do, it works. If they have to read a tooltip to know what seizing will do, it doesn't.

### Risks
- It's easy to overload the player with colours, weights, classes and counts. Colour may have to come in later than weight, or the other way round.
- The rule for what can be seized has to be very clear. Visually, a sound source needs one shared shader "hum" so players never guess.
- Weight classes may cause fiddly inventory management. Keep it to three classes and make levying fast.
- It needs strong audio design to sell the idea that sound equals existence, but that also plays to our Web Audio work.

### Seeds used
- **Redistrainer:** the protagonist's job, the re-seize fantasy, the death loop and the character arc.
- **Mike Honeyman / featherweight:** the protagonist's past, boxing combat verbs (jab-seize, slip, the count) and weight classes as gates.
- **Bagful:** the inventory as a physical bag whose mass is your movement.
- **Nonwhite:** coloured-noise materials, with white noise as unclaimable land.
- **Usefullish:** the pawned-goods economy and the Corner Satchel combinations.
- **Lens:** sound is legal and physical existence, and silence is foreclosure.
- **Mood:** a boom city running a fever, with a ticker that turns up the whole world.

## Concept B: ZONE OF AVOIDANCE

**Hook:** In a hidden world where silence has weight, a fevered surveyor changes the grammar of her own body (who pushes and who gets pushed) to reach the one voice that keeps answering her.

### Premise and world logic
Behind a band of dust that nothing sees through lies **Maffei**, a small spinning disc of a world. It follows two physical laws. **Sound is pressure**: it pushes and it's buoyant. **Silence is mass**: it pools in low places as an invisible dense "hush" that pulls. You can't see hush, only what it bends, because light lenses around it. The disc spins too fast for its visible matter. Its core is an enormous accumulated silence, and it's heating up.

You are **Tempel**, a surveyor who crossed the dust band to map Maffei. The crossing gave you a fever that never breaks. Your body temperature *is* your volume. When you're hot, you're loud whether you like it or not. Loud means buoyant and strong, and everything hears you. When you're cool, you're quiet, and quiet means heavy and invisible. Somewhere deep in the disc, **one voice answers your calls**. Each time you call from a waystone, the answer comes back from a little closer. It's a slow burn across the whole game.

The world is explorable because you only map what *answers*. Calling reveals surfaces by echo, and hush regions stay black on your map until you go into them in person.

### Signature mechanic 1: Diathesis, the three grammatical voices
You switch instantly between three stances, each of which decides *who a sound acts on*:
- **Active:** your sounds act on the world. Your call pushes crates, knocks enemies back and blows out hush fog. Enemy sounds don't move you.
- **Passive:** the world's sounds act on *you*. An enemy's roar, a vent's blast or a cliff colony's chatter becomes your transport, and you ride someone else's scream across a chasm. You can't attack, but you're carried a long way.
- **Middle:** your sounds act on *yourself*. Your call launches you like recoil, your footsteps push you so you sprint, and a shout aimed down gives you a jump.

Moment to moment, it's like Ikaruga's polarity switching applied to *agency*. A turret fires a blast cone, and you go Passive to ride it, Active to throw it back, or Middle to shout-dash out of its path. Bosses are built around combinations of stance switches. Gates are rooms that only open to a specific grammar: a door that only opens when *pushed by something else*, or a gap only a Middle-voice recoil can clear.

### Signature mechanic 2: Fever and Hush
- Your **fever** rises when you make noise or stand in loud areas, and falls when you're in hush.
- **Hot** (loud): your calls are stronger and you float higher, but hunters that track by sound close in, and you can't stay still.
- **Cool** (quiet): you're heavy, you sink through buoyant sound-fog, stealth works and hush wells pull you down. Your calls are weak.
- Much of the traversal is about managing temperature. You heat up in a chattering colony to be light enough for an updraft, then dive into a hush basin to cool down and sink fast to a sunken exit.

### Ability list
1. **Call** (start): echolocation that reveals the map, plus a weak Active push.
2. **Middle Voice**: shout-recoil, which gives a jump boost and a dash in any direction.
3. **Passive Voice**: ride other sources' sounds (vents, creatures, falling rocks' crashes).
4. **Hold Breath**: go completely silent. You become a small hush mass, drop fast, press plates, pass under hunters and cool down fast. This is the ground pound.
5. **Standing Wave**: call between two parallel walls to set up a standing wave. Its nodes become ladder rungs, giving you a wall climb.
6. **Throw Voice**: place your call somewhere else as a lure. In Middle voice it acts on you *from where it landed*, so it works as a grapple that pulls you toward it.
7. **Low Register**: bass calls that shift *hush*, not just matter. You can push silence itself and move gravity wells.

### Biomes
1. **The Rim.** The outer disc spins too fast (the flat rotation curve), so ring-shaped room sections shear past each other and connections line up on a cycle. It's a biome of timed alignments.
2. **The Dust Lanes.** Visibility is close to zero and you navigate by echo alone. The dust also soaks up sound, so every push is weaker and the Passive rides are short.
3. **Hush Basins.** Silence pools with strong gravity and lensing distortion. Fever drops fast and you sink. It's a stealth and route-finding zone.
4. **The Chatter Cliffs.** Colonies of creatures that never stop calling create permanent updrafts of noise. Passive-voice highways run through here, and your fever spikes.
5. **The Lensworks.** Hush wells bend both light and sound. You aim calls along curved paths around masses to hit switches you can't see.
6. **The Core.** The great silence. Gravity turns radial, the answering voice is here, and the fever and the silence meet.

### Encounter or boss: Mouthful
Mouthful is a huge bellows-creature that alternates between two phases. When it **inhales**, it creates a travelling hush well that pulls you in, but you cool down near it. When it **exhales**, it screams a pressure torrent that heats you. You beat it by switching stances through its cycle. During the exhale, go **Passive** to ride the scream up to its dorsal vents. At the top, switch to **Middle** to shout-drop onto them. During the inhale, go **Active** so your call rolls down its throat, so it swallows your voice and chokes. Your fever meter decides how long you can stay close. If you get too hot, you're too light to stay down.

### Why it's not generic (honest critique)
- **Closest games:** Ikaruga and Outland (polarity switching), Mario Galaxy (gravity wells), echolocation games like *Scanner Sombre* and *Perception*, and Celeste-style recoil movement.
- **How it differs:** the switch doesn't change *which* bullets hurt you. It changes the **direction of causality**: whether you're the subject or the object of every force in the room. Silence having gravity gives the lensing shaders a gameplay job, not just a look.
- **Weak spots:** the "one voice that answers" romance could slide into sentimental, generic LLM territory with lines like "the things never said". Fever-equals-volume is elegant, but it adds a second meter on top of the stance system. The setting ("a hidden world") is atmospheric but has less franchise identity than Tallage, and is closer to a lone-wanderer mood piece.

### Greybox test (one room, under an hour)
Build one room with keys 1, 2 and 3 for Active, Passive and Middle. It has a **shouter turret** that fires a pressure cone every two seconds, a **crate**, and a gap too wide to jump. There are three solutions: ride the blast across (Passive), push the crate into the cone to block it and then climb the crate (Active), or shout-recoil across (Middle).
**Question:** do players start switching stances *mid-air* without being told, and does chaining Passive into Middle feel like a skill expression? If they only switch while standing still and have to think about it, the stance system is too abstract.

### Risks
- Three stances could be too many. It may need to be two plus one late unlock.
- Echolocation darkness hurts readability, and we're aiming for Hollow Knight clarity.
- The fever meter may feel like a nagging timer.
- The shearing rooms on the Rim are costly to build with LDtk and deterministic physics.

### Seeds used
- **NGC 1560:** silence as hidden mass, the disc that spins too fast (the Rim), the dust-shrouded Zone of Avoidance (the Dust Lanes, echo mapping) and lensing hush wells. The protagonist is named after the galaxy's discoverer.
- **Diathetic:** the three grammatical voices as the core stance mechanic.
- **"Isa Lang":** "just one" answering voice. Save waystones work as call and answer, and fast travel means riding the answer back in Passive voice. The slow-burn approach is the story's spine.
- **Apobiotic** (lightly): the surveyor's voice keeps living apart from her. Throw Voice places a part of her that acts on its own.
- **Lens and mood:** sound as pressure, silence as mass, and fever as volume.

## Verdict
**DISTRAINT is the stronger concept.** Seize and Levy gives one verb that drives traversal, combat and gating all at once. Removing a sound deletes an obstacle and gives you a tool, so every room is a small economy. The premise (a boxer-bailiff in a loud, living, mortgaged boomtown) has much more franchise identity and much less competition than anything in the genre, and it reads clearly with silhouettes: muted objects turn into dashed ghosts, and coloured noise gets its own particle materials. ZONE OF AVOIDANCE has the more elegant *pure mechanic* (the three stances). It could be moved into DISTRAINT later, for example as a late-game ability, if that concept needs more depth in movement.
