# Critique L1-B: a player's read on the twelve concepts

Independent review. Read only `raw-L1-p1.md` through `raw-L1-p6.md`. Did not read `PLAN.md` §2 or any other critique.

Lens for every judgement below: I am the person who bought Hollow Knight on a whim, finished Ori 2 twice, and dropped three well-reviewed metroidvanias at hour 4 because the "clever" mechanic turned out to be a puzzle layer that kept taking the controller away from me. The questions I ask of each concept: would I buy it from a 90-second trailer, what would I tell a friend in one line, what does it feel like at minute 1 / hour 1 / hour 8, where does it get tedious, does the signature mechanic make *moving and fighting* better or does it interrupt them, and what is it honestly closest to.

Format note: the six packs are strong on world-building. Almost every concept has a good bestiary, a good biome list and a good boss. So I weight those less. What separates them is the verb.

---

## The twelve, one card each

### 1. ONSETTER (p1-A): counterweight rope in a coal pit

- **Buy from trailer?** Yes, on the strength of one shot: rising up a black shaft in a lamp cone while something enormous and blind sinks past you, eye to eye for one second.
- **Friend line:** "Nothing goes up unless something heavier goes down, and the heaviest things are the monsters."
- **Minute 1:** dark, quiet, a tub on a rope. You clip it and rise. Immediately legible.
- **Hour 1:** you have clipped a crawler mid-fight and ridden it up while it climbed its own rope to drag you down. The Tug is a real flinch. You are learning heft by silhouette.
- **Hour 8:** the Goaf, counterweighting against the roof. Riding the Galloway. Good. But you have also spent a lot of minutes in counterweight rooms hunting for the right-weight object, waiting for a shift change, or riding a slow rope.
- **Tedium:** rides that are elevators. Any ride where nothing happens on the rope is dead time, and the concept has a lot of ropes. Shift schedules that make you wait.
- **Moving and fighting:** partly yes. Clip-as-crowd-control (yank the charging thing up and out of the fight) is a first-class combat verb. Pendulum from a sinker is a first-class movement verb. The over-sheave ride is the risk: commit, then wait.
- **Honestly resembles:** Ori's Bash crossed with Limbo/Inside counterweight puzzles, set in Deepnest. The mine is the most generic possible metroidvania backdrop; everything original is in the rope.

### 2. THE DISSOLUTION ROLLS (p1-B): the law lives in the hat

- **Buy from trailer?** Maybe. It would be the trailer people share ("the floor only exists where a bureaucrat's hat says it does") and then I would worry it is a puzzle game.
- **Friend line:** "You have to keep your enemies alive because their hats are the only ground."
- **Minute 1:** knock a porter's hat off, watch him fetch it. Funny.
- **Hour 1:** kicking hats over chasms, following clerks across. Fun for about forty minutes, then I notice I am waiting for AI to walk places.
- **Hour 8:** The Unincorporated, pure void, herding six clerks. This is either the best level in the genre or an escort mission with extra steps, and I think it is the second.
- **Tedium:** waiting for the retrieve animation. Any fight where the correct answer is "don't hit it".
- **Moving and fighting:** no. Brim strike is a good hit, but the mechanic is herding, which is a puzzle layer. The bylaw fields (No Loitering as a forced-dash bridge, Keep Left as a current) are movement modifiers imposed on you, not tools you own.
- **Honestly resembles:** Mario Odyssey's Cappy meets Baba Is You, with a Gilliam tone. Most original *premise* of the twelve, weakest action feel.

### 3. BELLTIDE (p2-A): inside a migrating jellyfish

- **Buy from trailer?** Yes, it would look gorgeous: translucent tissue, light passing through, chain-popping glowing clusters. I would buy it thinking it was Ori 3.
- **Friend line:** "You live inside a jellyfish the size of a country and the whole thing warms up while you play."
- **Minute 1:** cold kelp, standard platforming, pretty.
- **Hour 1:** grape-cluster chain-pops. A decent toy. Ability list is explicitly reskins (the pack says so).
- **Hour 8:** the Eye biome delivers the fever. But the Long Tide has become "go back and check which doors thawed", and I have hit a door I have the key for and been told to wait.
- **Tedium:** waiting for the tide. Revisiting changed rooms as a checklist. Underwater movement everywhere is floaty by nature, which fights the Hollow Knight bar.
- **Moving and fighting:** the cluster is a modest combat/traversal tool; the tide is a world-state gimmick that does nothing for the controller.
- **Honestly resembles:** Ori 2 in a Subnautica coat. Competent, beautiful, least original mechanically.

### 4. LASHLINE (p2-B): relay on the rim of a giant's eye

- **Buy from trailer?** The image (eyelash forest, an eyelid creeping shut on the horizon) is a great poster. The mechanic would not show well.
- **Friend line:** "You live on an eyelash and the eye is about to blink."
- **Minute 1:** climbing lash-trees. Fine.
- **Hour 1:** carry a light pattern from dish A to dish B before it decays. It is a fetch quest with a timer, and the pack admits it.
- **Hour 8:** more dishes, more wind, and the blink has mostly been a lighting change.
- **Tedium:** the relay. Every gate is "go there quickly, sheltered".
- **Moving and fighting:** no. Nothing in the kit changes how a jump or a hit feels.
- **Honestly resembles:** Ico's escort tension applied to a resource, in a GRIS palette. Weakest of the twelve as a game, best as a painting.

### 5. PROBATE (p3-A): depose and cite

- **Buy from trailer?** Yes for the fiction (Gormenghast, a testator who will not die, violet ink on grey), and the shot of an heir lunging off a balcony on command is a real hook.
- **Friend line:** "You win fights by watching them, then making enemies replay their worst moment."
- **Minute 1:** the breathing. The gavel-rod feels fine. Then you meet the first claimant and hitting it does nothing meaningful.
- **Hour 1:** you get it: survive the attack up close to record it, then cite it into a low beam. Genuinely clever. Also: I have spent a lot of hour 1 standing next to danger doing nothing.
- **Hour 8:** perjury, portraits testifying, the Unfiled resolving into violet behind you. This is the best late game on paper of any concept.
- **Tedium:** depose is a waiting tax. "Eat every attack once" is the rule for every enemy. Cite-a-hoist versus a switch is a lateral move.
- **Moving and fighting:** half. Cite is a superb tactical combat verb (make it lunge, now, into that). Depose is the opposite of flow. The rod is explicitly not the win condition, which is the single biggest risk against the stated bar.
- **Honestly resembles:** Tunic/Outer Wilds' "knowledge is progress", instantiated with a Braid/Talos recorder, in a Peake novel. Best writing of the twelve. Best visual rule (violet = on record, grey = not). Most likely to be a puzzle-adventure wearing a metroidvania's coat.

### 6. THE LANDING (p3-B): the stamp decides what a thing is

- **Buy from trailer?** Yes, the stamp-crate-float-ride-stamp-heavy-drop-on-guard chain is a trailer beat on its own.
- **Friend line:** "A crate is whatever your paperwork says it is."
- **Minute 1:** stamp, ride, stamp, drop. Great toy.
- **Hour 1:** allocation juggling, category picking mid-fight. Pointing and choosing from a list while a thing charges is fiddly.
- **Hour 8:** things that are two categories at once. Interesting, but by now your own body has been basic for six hours because Self-Assessment (the whole classic move set) is late.
- **Tedium:** the category menu. Allocation reversion nag. Seven physics rules that must each feel crisp.
- **Moving and fighting:** no, until the late Self-Assessment. It is a gravity-gun puzzle toy. No weapon.
- **Honestly resembles:** Baba Is You meets Scribblenauts meets Viewfinder. Everyone will say Baba and they will be right.

### 7. DISTRAINT (p4-A): the boxer-bailiff who repossesses sounds

- **Buy from trailer?** Yes, and I would pre-order. A boxer jabs a furnace, the roar leaves it as a coloured ribbon into her sack, the furnace goes to a dashed outline and cold, she throws the roar back as a slab that crushes a floor.
- **Friend line:** "Punch the noise out of a wall and the wall stops existing."
- **Minute 1:** the Bourse Stairs. Brokers' shouts are updrafts. You jab-seize a humming wall and walk through it. You have learned the whole game in one input.
- **Hour 1:** seize, levy, weigh-in. Bag weight changes your jump. The Count on a downed enemy. Fights are a loop of disarm, throw it back, put it down, take its voice.
- **Hour 8:** the Static Flats where nothing can be seized and you cross on what you brought. The Auctioneer selling the floor out from under you. The city louder than it was at hour 1.
- **Tedium:** if the bag becomes an inventory screen. If colours, weights, classes and counts all arrive at once. The death loop (chase the clerk who took your bag) is Hollow Knight's shade and the pack knows it.
- **Moving and fighting:** yes, and it is the only concept where the answer is a clean yes. Seize *is* the jab. Slip *is* the dash. The Count *is* a knockdown window. Weight class *is* the moveset. Every seizure changes the room and your kit in the same frame. Nothing here takes the controller away.
- **Honestly resembles:** Kirby's copy ability inside Hollow Knight's structure with Dead Cells' energy, plus "remove the property" from Baba Is You. The living, loud, mortgaged boomtown is the least-crowded setting in the genre.

### 8. ZONE OF AVOIDANCE (p4-B): who pushes and who gets pushed

- **Buy from trailer?** Maybe. Riding an enemy's scream across a chasm and flipping to shout-dash off it is a great clip. The world around it (dust, dark, a lone surveyor, one distant voice) is the mood piece I have played five times.
- **Friend line:** "Switch whether you push the world or the world pushes you."
- **Minute 1:** echolocation darkness. Readability problem from the first screen.
- **Hour 1:** three stances mid-air. When it clicks it is Celeste-level expressive.
- **Hour 8:** managing a fever meter on top of stances. The romance with the voice has said "the things never said" at least once.
- **Tedium:** the fever meter. Darkness. Three stances as a menu rather than a reflex.
- **Moving and fighting:** yes for movement (Passive riding, Middle recoil), decent for combat (Active throws it back). The purest mechanic in the packs.
- **Honestly resembles:** Ikaruga polarity applied to agency, in a Scanner Sombre cave with a Celeste dash. Best movement idea attached to the most generic world. Should be a graft, not a game.

### 9. THE CIRCUIT RIDER (p5-A): itinerant notary on an infinite highway

- **Buy from trailer?** No. The road to infinity is a nice shot. Wax bolas and stamped gates would look like any other kit.
- **Friend line:** "A notary whose spellbook is a filing cabinet." (Friend: "So what do you do?")
- **Minute 1:** walking a highway. Hour 1: grapple, bolas, dash, parry with legal names. Hour 8: Bedpost Hollow, the quietest and best biome, but the mechanics have not changed.
- **Tedium:** the docket as a menu. Puns fighting melancholy. The linear road as a hub.
- **Moving and fighting:** no. Every ability is a named reskin. Deputise-mid-fight is a scripted cutscene of cooperation.
- **Honestly resembles:** Disco Elysium's tone on a stock Hollow Knight kit. The setting is distinctive and would need real care from the people building it; the mechanics do not earn it.

### 10. SLORPWAKE (p5-B): eat the mud, spit a bridge

- **Buy from trailer?** Maybe. A translucent slug glowing on a dry estuary would look lovely in code-drawn light. Then it eats something and spits it out.
- **Friend line:** "You're a slug that eats the world and spits it back as bridges."
- **Minute 1:** eat mud, spit a platform that sinks. Fine.
- **Hour 1:** shellcoat climbing, offering material to a passive creature and waiting for it to accept. Hour 8: consequences of stealing, which is the interesting part, and the part that costs the most content.
- **Tedium:** reservoir management. Timed sinking platforms against precision-platforming expectations. Mandatory cooperation gates that mean "find the NPC and wait".
- **Moving and fighting:** no. A soft-bodied scavenger is the opposite silhouette to a snappy Knight, and combat is deliberately minimal. Ingest/extrude is a builder toy.
- **Honestly resembles:** Rain World's ecology with a Kirby inhale. Body-horror risk is real.

### 11. UREDO (p6-A): a rust with a five-stage life

- **Buy from trailer?** Yes, out of curiosity rather than confidence. An orange fleck ripens, time slows, an arc appears, it lobs itself across a chasm, lands in frost and comes back as a black two-celled coal that sinks through pith. Then wet, and it is four drifting spores threading a stoma. I have never seen that.
- **Friend line:** "Every death is a jump, and where you land decides what you come back as."
- **Minute 1:** hot orange leaf, ripeness fills, first burst. The lob feels like a super-jump. Good.
- **Hour 1:** you are saying "I need cold, then wet, then that green bit" out loud. The gating grammar is genuinely new.
- **Hour 8:** the Autoecious fight, beaten by four intentional deaths. Summer Loop chaining lobs mid-air. That is the game.
- **Tedium:** the Thread state drifting back. Substrate legibility. Half the forms are worse to control by design (Coal's low jump, Dew has no attack). Route logic can become a puzzle you solve on the map screen rather than with your thumbs.
- **Moving and fighting:** yes with an asterisk. The lob is movement and bursting at an enemy is an attack, so the death verb is in the flow. But it asks the team to tune four bodies to Hollow Knight standard instead of one, and a burst every thirty seconds that changes your move set is a rhythm no shipped game has proven.
- **Honestly resembles:** Mushroom 11 (a fungus that moves by dying and regrowing) crossed with Carrion and Rain World, drawn like Ori. Nobody has shipped aim-your-own-death as the core verb. The most original verb in the packs and the best fit for shader-and-particle visuals.

### 12. GALL (p6-B): ink that sets, conducts and eats the page

- **Buy from trailer?** Yes. An ink figure running on paper, hitting zero, setting as a black crust mid-leap that closes a gold line; a lift hums into life; the crust slowly burns a hole. Cheap to draw, expensive-looking.
- **Friend line:** "Your corpses are your bridges, your wires and your slow-burning trapdoors."
- **Minute 1:** cool margins, your trail is the map. Lovely. Hour 1: deliberately walking to a gilt gap to die there, and laughing when the lift starts. Hour 8: the Press, a chain of intentional sets.
- **Tedium:** six-body budget as a lives system (the pack knows). Walking back to drink stains. Soft-locks from corrosion.
- **Moving and fighting:** partly. Stickless (tumbling ink jump) and Nib (cutting dash) are decent feel-carriers. Set is placement, not movement. Combat is thin.
- **Honestly resembles:** Life Goes On (corpse platforming) in Limbo's silhouettes, on paper. Known puzzle genre, admitted by the pack. The trail-as-map is the best single UX idea in all six files.

## Ranking

### 1. DISTRAINT

Reasons a player would care:

- **The verb is a punch.** Seize is a jab. Slip is a boxer's dash with i-frames. The Count is a knockdown. The signature mechanic *is* the combat feel, not a layer over it. This is the only concept where I never worried the game would take the controller away from me.
- **Every seizure changes the room and my kit at once.** Take the hum from a wall: the wall becomes a ghost and my bag gets heavier and my jump gets shorter. That is three consequences from one input, all felt in the body. Subtraction as the main verb is rare and it reads instantly (solid to dashed outline).
- **A protagonist with a face and a city that is alive.** Kid Tallow, a disgraced featherweight with a bailiff's sack, in a boomtown running a fever. That silhouette is drawable in code, cosplayable, and it is not a small sad thing in a ruin. Franchises need a person and a place; this has both.
- **The pitch survives being repeated.** "Punch the noise out of a wall and the wall stops existing" is what your friend says at the pub.

Honest reservations: the pack stacks four systems (colours, weights, classes, the Count). Ship with two colours and three classes and let violet arrive with the Corner Satchel. The death loop is the shade; either own it or replace it (see grafts).

### 2. UREDO

- **I have never done this before.** Aiming my own death as a jump, and choosing where to land so I come back as the thing the next room needs, is the most original verb across all twelve. Players talk about games that give them a new verb.
- **It is the best-looking concept for code-drawn art.** Pustule bursts, spore arcs, frost crystallising on orange, sorrel leaves folding at night: every biome is particles, silhouettes and a shader. The constraint is an asset here, not a cost.
- **The gating grammar comes from the world, not from keys.** "What microclimate is within one lob of here" makes the map a physical argument instead of a lock-and-key checklist.

Why not #1: four bodies to tune to the bar instead of one, two of them deliberately weaker to control, and no shipped precedent that a form change every thirty seconds sustains flow. Sympathy for a fungus is a writing problem the pack is honest about. If the greybox shows the burst-to-form loop feels like a super-jump and not a loading screen, this becomes a serious rival for the top spot.

### 3. ONSETTER

- **The Clip is a two-body verb.** Riding against a living counterweight that climbs its own rope gives combat and traversal a shared tension no grapple has. The Tug, the Passing and the Approach are three distinct beats on one rope, and each one is a flinch.
- **The Galloway is a franchise boss.** Blind, huge, hunting by rope-creak, and beaten by being put to work as the shaft's permanent counterweight. That is the kind of encounter people describe to each other.
- **It fits the tech exactly.** Integer heft, a lookup table, a rope as a length constraint on a sheave graph.

Why #3: a dark coal mine with a lamp is the most generic backdrop in the packs, and the over-sheave ride can become an elevator. The concept lives or dies on whether a ride is ever boring, and the map has a lot of ropes.

Honourable mention: **PROBATE** has the best fiction, the best visual rule and the best late game on paper. It is not in the top three because its signature verb (stand next to danger and watch) is the opposite of Hollow Knight's feel, and the pack itself says hitting is not how you win. It is a puzzle-adventure I would love, wearing a metroidvania's coat.

## The 30-second moment for DISTRAINT

Scene: halfway up the Bourse Stairs. A vertical trading floor, brokers' shouts rendered as upward-streaming pressure currents, the fever ticker climbing on a board behind. Kid Tallow is featherweight (empty bag), riding a shout-updraft upward.

1. **0-4 s.** A furnace-golem steps out onto a landing above and drops. Its roar is visible: a brown, low-frequency ripple around it. It swings. She **slips** through the swing (i-frames, a boxer's lean, no dash trail, just her head moving) and comes up inside its reach.
2. **4-7 s.** She **jabs**. Hitstop. The roar leaves the golem as a brown ribbon that snaps into her sack. The golem goes to a dashed outline mid-swing and freezes, cold. The weigh-in glyph ticks: Featherweight → Heavyweight. Her silhouette drops half a head, the updraft stops holding her, and she lands hard.
3. **7-12 s.** Two clerks with violet whistles (hiss, cutting darts) fire from a ledge. Too heavy to dodge upward, she **levies** the roar straight down onto the auction lot beneath her: it lands as a brown slab, thuds, and the lot floor cracks and gives way. She falls through with it (heavyweight drop-punch), lands on the floor below, and the bag reads Featherweight again.
4. **12-18 s.** The clerks follow over the ledge. She is light: **rope-skip** double-jump, catch the nearest one on the way down with a jab-seize. Its whistle leaves as a violet thread. The clerk, mute, cannot fire. The **Count** ring appears around it: ten ticks. She seizes its footsteps on tick 7 (pink ribbon). It is repossessed: it sits down, outlined, and her ledger flips a page.
5. **18-26 s.** A humming wall blocks the stairwell. She **levies** the pink footsteps at the floor: a springy platform. She bounces, and at the top of the bounce jab-seizes the wall's hum. The wall goes to a dashed outline. She **slips** through it.
6. **26-30 s.** She comes out onto the trading floor as the Exchange Bell rings, the ticker jumps, every sound in the room gets a notch louder and every outline vibrates harder. Title card.

Six inputs (slip, jab-seize, levy, drop, rope-skip, count-seize), each one visibly changes the world and her body, and nothing in it is a menu.

### What must be true in the greybox for this to be fun

1. **Seize is a hit, not an interaction.** Under 100 ms to connect, with hitstop, and the sound leaving as a visible ribbon into the bag on that same frame. If it feels like pressing E on a door, the game is dead.
2. **Muting reads in one frame.** Solid to dashed outline, collision changes in the same tick, and the object's audio cuts. A player must never wonder whether the wall is passable now.
3. **Levy is a throw with a thud.** An arc, a landing, and brown must land heavy in under half a second. Pink must bounce on the first try without a tooltip.
4. **Weight class is felt within one jump.** Three discrete steps, not a slider. Jump height, gravity and knockback change immediately and the silhouette changes with them. If a tester cannot say "I'm heavy now" without looking at the HUD, the classes go.
5. **Losing a sound removes an attack.** The enemy that loses its bark must visibly not be able to lunge, not just take damage. Disarming must be a thing you see the enemy notice.
6. **The room is solvable in two orders and needs both verbs.** If testers only seize (using it as a delete button) and never levy, the economy is dead and the room has to force a put-back.
7. **Base movement stands alone.** A room with nothing to seize must still be fun to cross on slip and rope-skip. The signature verb should make a good body better, not be the only thing carrying it.

Kill signal (from the pack, and I agree): if testers have to read a tooltip to know what seizing will do, it does not work.

## Grafts into DISTRAINT

1. **ZONE OF AVOIDANCE: Passive voice as a mid-game movement ability.** Distraint already has brokers' shouts as updrafts. Give Kid Tallow a stance in which *any* enemy's sound carries her: ride a golem's roar across a chasm, ride the Auctioneer's "going once" up to his stage. It is the best pure movement idea in the packs, it comes free with Distraint's fiction (a sound is pressure), and it gives the second half of the game a new way to move without a new system. The p4 author suggested this and is right.
2. **PROBATE: an enemy is beaten when all of it is on record.** Distraint's bestiary already ties each attack to a sound. Make it explicit: an enemy is fully repossessed when every one of its sounds is in the ledger, and each seizure removes that attack. This gives Probate's "win by complete knowledge" structure a physical, punchy form, turns the bestiary into a ledger of what you have taken from whom, and gives bosses a shape (seize the patter, seize the gavel, seize the bell) without a health bar.
3. **ONSETTER: enlist the boss as machinery.** The Galloway becoming the shaft's permanent counterweight is the best boss reward in the packs. In Distraint, a defeated boss's signature sound becomes a permanent fixture you can levy into the map: the Auctioneer's gavel shockwave as a permanent lift on the Bourse, the Foundry's bellows as a standing updraft. Shortcuts you earn by taking a voice.
4. **BELLTIDE: the fever ticker as a one-way world clock.** Distraint's boomtown "gets louder each week". Make that Belltide's Long Tide: the ticker climbs at story beats, every cleared room gains new sounds (new seizables, new hazards, new updrafts), and nothing ever goes back down. It gives backtracking a reason without a checklist and it makes the city feel like it is running a fever rather than being told it is.
5. **GALL: the map is your trail.** Not as the whole map (Distraint's claims-ledger map is good), but as the fog-of-war rule: rooms you have walked are drawn in the ink of your footsteps, rooms you have only seen are outlines. Cheap, diegetic, and it fits a bailiff who keeps records.

One thing not to graft: THE DISSOLUTION ROLLS' herding. It is delightful and it is a puzzle layer, and the whole point of Distraint is that nothing interrupts the punch.
