# Raw ideation, L1, pack 6

Seed pack: inkpot; *Puccinia sorghi* (maize rust, a heteroecious rust that needs both maize and *Oxalis* to finish its life cycle); Sauterelle (WWI bomb-throwing crossbow, high lob into trenches, initially dismissed); aspersion; stickless; cerebropedal; megmho; atheism; unsaponified. Lens: **death is a mechanic, not a punishment.** Mood: **feverish.**

Worked from the prompt only. PLAN.md and the other concept files were not read.

---

## 1. Ten associations (pushed past the first obvious one)

1. **Rust needs two hosts.** *Puccinia* cannot complete its life on one plant: some spore stages only germinate on maize, others only on wood sorrel. A life that is structurally too long for one body. Every stage transition is a death of the old form. *Structural: ability-set as life-cycle stage; gating by "how many deaths from here".*
2. **Rust launches itself.** Pustules rupture; many fungi ballistically discharge spores. The Sauterelle is the same idea in iron: a body lobbed in a high arc onto a target it cannot walk to. *The player's death is the projectile.*
3. **Iron-gall ink rusts.** Inkpot plus rust: iron-gall ink goes from blue-black to brown and, over decades, eats through the page it was written on. Ink as slow corrosive. *A trail that is a tool now and a hole later.*
4. **Megmho is ohm spelled backwards.** Conductance, the reciprocal of resistance. A world whose stat is "how well does this carry current", where the dead (dry, set) conduct better than the living (wet). *Death raises conductance.*
5. **Cerebropedal: brain in the foot.** In molluscs the cerebral and pedal ganglia are wired together. A creature that thinks with where it walks; whose map is literally its footprint. *The metroidvania map is diegetic: it is the trail.*
6. **Aspersion: a sprinkling.** Basidiospores need a film of water to germinate. Also "casting aspersions": something sprinkled onto you that sticks. *Wet zones as germination gates; being sprinkled as a status.*
7. **Unsaponified: fat that never met the lye.** Wax, tallow, grease. Ink will not take on it (batik, wax resist). Lye turns it to soap: slippery, bubbling, floatable. *Wax as ink-proof material; lye as a key.*
8. **Stickless: a rocket without its guide stick.** Flight that tumbles. Also stickless as "no crutch". *A self-propelled lob that is powerful and uncontrolled.*
9. **Atheism: no author.** Not "gods are dead" (banned territory) but "no one is coming to write this". An unwritten page in a world built to be written on. *The player's traces are the only writing the world will ever get.*
10. **Feverish: heat that ripens.** Uredo (the old name for rust) is Latin for a burning itch. Fever breaks, and the breaking is the death of the fever. Fever as a resource that fills, not drains. *Health that goes UP until you burst.*

Also noted and set aside: "initially dismissed" as a character arc for a weapon nobody believed in; *Oxalis* leaves fold at night (nyctinasty), which is a platform that closes on a clock.

## 2. Five clichés to steer away from

1. Small cute thing with a nail/sword in a fallen bug kingdom, sad ruins, a plague, benches.
2. Spirit of the forest restores light to a dying tree; glowing blue everything.
3. "You are already dead / this is purgatory / a coma dream" twist, with a child's drawings as the world.
4. Paint or ink that *creates* the world (Okami, Chicory): the artist-hero restoring colour.
5. Robot or clone wakes with no memory in the ruins of its makers and discovers "what it means to be human".

Number 4 matters because the inkpot seed pulls straight toward it. The ink concept below is built so that ink *damages* the world rather than restoring it.

## Concept A: UREDO

**Hook:** You are a rust with a five-stage life that no single body can finish. Every death is a metamorphosis and a launch; where you choose to die decides what you come back as.

### Premise and world logic

You are not a creature. You are a life cycle: a heteroecious rust, a lineage that needs two hosts to close its loop. The two hosts are the whole world:

- **The Stalk**: one grass plant, kilometres tall. Leaf blades the size of provinces, a hollow sheath, pith, vascular bundles, a tassel in the hot wind at the top, a root crown in the dark.
- **The Sorrel**: a low sour mat of three-lobed leaves that once grew right up against the Stalk's base, close enough that a single spore lob could cross from one host to the other. It has retreated into scattered patches, and now the loop cannot be closed by anyone who does not know the way.

You are the *uredo*: the repeating summer stage, orange and hot, the one that can reinfect the Stalk over and over without ever going anywhere. It is a fever that has been running for years. The other rusts on the Stalk are autoecious (one host, no second life) and they regard your need for the Sorrel as a birth defect. Your goal is to close the cycle once, all the way round, before the season ends: reach the Sorrel in the only form that can germinate on it, return to the Stalk in the only form that can leave it, and overwinter. The Stalk and the Sorrel are not sick, not in decline, not a kingdom; they are enormous, old, and mostly indifferent to you. Nothing is spreading. The condition to be resolved is *yours*.

Why it is a metroidvania: the world is one connected plant and one patchy mat, and the reason you cannot go everywhere is not walls but *substrate and microclimate*. Each of your forms can only exist, land, or pass in certain conditions (cold, wet, on Sorrel tissue, inside pith). A gate is a place that requires a form you can only reach by dying in the right order nearby.

### Signature mechanic: Burst (death as the jump and the metamorphosis)

There is no health bar. There is **ripeness**. Damage ripens you. Heat ripens you slowly. Sour or cold shock ripens you fast. You can also ripen yourself deliberately (hold a button; a short vulnerable wind-up). At full ripeness you **burst**:

1. Time slows. An aim arc appears: a fixed, high, Sauterelle-style lob. You control power and direction, not the shape.
2. Your body leaves as a spore. It is a projectile: it can hit enemies, pass through gaps too small for the body, ride wind.
3. Where it lands decides what you become. The transition is *fixed by the cycle*, but only if the substrate allows it:
   - Fever on the Stalk, in heat → **Fever** again, at a new position. (This is the "respawn": always a relocation, never a rollback.)
   - Fever landing in cold → **Coal** (teliospore).
   - Coal that has been wetted (aspersed) → **Dew** (basidiospores).
   - Dew landing on Sorrel → **Cup** (aeciospores).
   - Cup lobbing onto the Stalk → **Fever**. Loop closed.
   - Anything landing where its next form cannot germinate → a brief **Thread** state: a mycelial fragment, slow, unarmed, that must reach valid substrate within a few seconds or drift back to the last overwintering site. This is the only cost of a "bad" death, and it is a cost in *position*, not in progress, currency, or upgrades.

Moment to moment: combat is about managing ripeness as a resource you *want* to spend, choosing when to burst *at* an enemy (a Fever burst onto a hyperparasite germinates on it and drains it from inside), and positioning so that the death you are about to have lands somewhere useful. Traversal is routing through microclimates: "I need to be Dew above that gap, so I need a Coal on this ledge, so I need cold within one lob of here, and water within one lob of that."

The forms:

- **Fever** (urediniospore): default. Quick, mid jump, pustule-burst attack (a short fan of spore-lets). Repeats itself on the Stalk indefinitely. Dies of cold.
- **Coal** (teliospore): black, two-celled, heavy. Low jump, high mass. Immune to cold. Sinks through soft tissue (pith, rotten leaf) at a fixed rate. Can **overwinter**: root in place and sleep. That is the save point, and it is a form, not a bench.
- **Dew** (basidiospore): four thin bodies at once, moved as a loose swarm. Glide and drift on wind and sap current. No attack; evades by splitting. Dies in dry air within seconds. Fits through stomata. Can only germinate on Sorrel.
- **Cup** (aeciospore): chained spores. Clings to undersides, grapples by throwing a chain of yourself, bursts in bulk (a wide, weak scatter) and lobs the farthest of any form. Exists only on Sorrel.

### Ability list

1. **Burst** (start): the lob. Upgrades widen the arc window and add mid-air steering.
2. **Overwinter** (find the first frost pocket): unlocks Coal and the save system. Coal's sink-through-soft-tissue is the first "down" gate.
3. **Aspersion** (find the first guttation drip, where the leaf sweats at dawn): unlocks Dew. Later, **Dew Sac**: Coal can carry its own water, so Dew can be made anywhere cold exists.
4. **Aecial Chain** (first Sorrel patch): unlocks Cup, with cling and the chain-grapple.
5. **Ballistic Sterigma**: a second, short burst available to Fever *without* being ripe, which fires a spore-let as a dash-like hop and does not change form. The mid-game mobility tool. Its existence makes the "real" burst feel like the deliberate act it is.
6. **Haustorium**: the plant seals off intruders with walls of its own dead cells (the hypersensitive response). Haustorium lets any form feed through a sealed wall and dissolve it. Opens the Stalk's interior.
7. **Summer Loop** (late): Fever can re-burst into Fever without any substrate check, from mid-air. Chained lobs; the traversal apex.

### Biomes

1. **The Blade**: leaf planes as huge tilted platforms, veins as rails, stomata as doors that only open when the leaf is wet (Dew's routes). Wind bends the whole leaf: platforms move on the sim's fixed cycle. Bright, hot, orange pustule-scatter shaders.
2. **The Sheath and Pith**: the vertical interior. Warm, dim, fever-hot. Pith is soft and Coal sinks through it; xylem carries Dew upward and phloem carries it down, so the swarm form rides elevators the others cannot. Hypersensitive walls everywhere.
3. **The Tassel**: exposed, dry, gusting. Dew dies instantly here; Coal is safe; Fever ripens fast. Pollen as blinding particle weather. The dismissive autoecious rusts live here.
4. **The Sorrel Mat**: low and horizontal. Leaves fold shut at night on the sim clock, so platforms close and routes change twice per cycle. Oxalic acid pools ripen you almost instantly (a burst you did not plan). Cool, green-grey, folding silhouettes.
5. **The Frost Line**: the late-season edge. Brittle, crystalline. Fever cannot survive it, which is the point: it is where Coal is made and where you learn to arrive already dying.
6. **The Root Crown**: wet, dark, underground. Dew lives here longer than anywhere. Hyperparasites hunt. Soil pores as a maze only the swarm can thread.

### An encounter: The Autoecious

A rival rust in the Tassel, one host, no second life, a slab of pustules the size of a room. It has spent the whole game telling you that needing two hosts is a defect. The fight is choreographed around four intentional deaths:

- **Phase 1 (Fever, outside).** It radiates heat, so you are ripening whether you like it or not. Dodge its scatter, then burst *onto* it: its surface is host tissue for you, so you germinate on it.
- **Phase 2 (Fever, on its skin).** Its shaded underside is cold. Get there and burst: you become Coal and sink into its body.
- **Phase 3 (Coal, inside).** Its core is wet. You become Dew, four bodies, and it tries to blow you out through its pores. You want to be blown out, but only toward the Sorrel patch growing at its base.
- **Phase 4 (Cup, on the Sorrel).** Chain-grapple its pustules together and lob into it from below with the bulk burst. It ruptures.

A death in the wrong place drops you to the start of that *phase*, not the fight. The boss is beaten by being better at dying than it is.

### Why it is not generic (self-critique)

Closest existing things: Hollow Knight for shape and feel; Rain World for "you are a small organism in an ecology that does not care"; Carrion for "you are the parasite"; Ori's spirit-in-a-tree for "the world is one plant". The differences: no kingdom, no lore-ruins, no protagonist with a face; the ability set is not a list of powers but a *cycle* you move through by dying, so gating grammar is "what conditions are within one lob of here" rather than "do you have the key". Nobody has shipped self-lob-as-death as the core verb.

Honest weaknesses: the protagonist is unsympathetic on paper (a fungus). The hosts must carry the personality. "You are a parasite" could read, from a distance, as the banned infection trope; it must be clear in every screen that the Stalk is thriving and you are a fleck. Four move sets is real scope.

### Greybox test (one room, one hour)

A wide room with a floor striped in three substrate colours (Stalk, Sorrel, dead), one cold pocket, one wet pocket, a gap wider than any jump, and three dummy enemies. Implement: ripeness meter that fills on hit and slowly on its own; the aim-lob on full; four form stubs with only their traversal verb (Fever: run and jump; Coal: heavy, sinks through a marked soft tile; Dew: float and drift, dies after N seconds; Cup: cling to marked underside). Substrate rule table drives the transition. The question: after ten minutes, does a tester say "I need cold, then wet, then that green bit" unprompted, and do they grin when the lob lands? If bursting feels like a loading screen, the concept is dead.

### Risks

- The death-to-form loop must take under 1.5 seconds or it becomes tedium.
- The Thread (bad landing) state must never feel like the punishment the lens forbids; keep it seconds, not a run-back.
- Four forms times animation, tuning, and enemies is a lot. Ship with three if needed (Cup could fold into Dew).
- Substrate legibility with code-driven visuals: cold, wet, and Sorrel must be readable at a glance. Colour alone is not enough; use texture shaders and particle weather.
- Sympathy. Needs the "dismissed weapon that turns out to be right" arc landed hard.

### Seeds used

- *Puccinia sorghi*: load-bearing. Heteroecism is the entire ability system and the map's two-host structure. Nyctinasty of *Oxalis* gives the folding biome.
- Sauterelle: load-bearing. The high fixed lob is the death verb; "initially dismissed" is the antagonist's stance and the character arc.
- Aspersion: the wet-germination gate and the Dew unlock.
- Feverish: ripeness as health that fills; uredo as a literal burning itch; heat as a ripening pressure.
- Lens: death is the metamorphosis, the jump, the save, and the boss choreography. The only cost is position.

## Concept B: GALL

**Hook:** You are a pot's worth of iron-gall ink in a hot, unwritten paper world. Every death sets a permanent stain that bears weight, carries current, and slowly eats the page.

### Premise and world logic

The world is the **Quire**: a stack of vast paper sheets, bound at one edge, sagging and curling in a heat that rises the deeper you go. The Quire was made to be written on. No one is coming to write it; there is no author and never was. (That is what "atheism" means here: not an absent god, an absent hand.) You are the contents of one inkpot: a body of wet iron-gall ink with a brain in its foot, which is to say you know exactly and only where you have been.

Why you move: heat is drying the Quire and you with it. At the bottom of the stack is the **Press**, a hot-press slowly flattening the whole book, operated by nobody. Your wetness is your life and the Press is taking it. Whether you are going down to stop it or up to get out is a choice the game can leave open for a long time; both routes cross every sheet.

Why it is a metroidvania: the sheets are layers connected by tears, folds, worm-holes, the spine, and holes that *you* burn through. The Quire has **gilt ruling**, lines of gold leaf that carry a current and power its old fittings (lifts, clamps, page-turners). Most are cut. Your body is a conductor, and your dead body is a better one.

### Signature mechanics

**1. Set (death).** Wetness is health. Heat drains it constantly; hits splash it away. At zero you **set**: your body dries where it is, in whatever pose, as a permanent stain. A stain is three things at once:

- **Solid.** A raised crust. It can be stood on, and if you set against a wall you smear upward and leave a climbable streak. Set across a small tear and you have a bridge.
- **Conductive** (megmho: the dead are more conductive than the living). A stain lying across a cut in the gilt ruling closes the circuit. Lifts move, clamps open, page-turners turn.
- **Corrosive.** Iron-gall ink eats paper. Every stain is a slow burn-through. On thick sheets it takes long enough not to matter; on thin sheets it opens a hole to the sheet below in a fixed number of frames. A death here is a door later.

You return at the last **inkpot** (the save point; there is no "life" lost). The pot holds six bodies' worth. Each stain in the world holds one. Walk onto any stain and **drink** to lift it back: the budget comes home and the corrosion under it stops. So the player carries a small, mobile budget of dead selves and places them like a circuit designer. Dying in combat is not a failure; it is a stain you did not choose the position of, and you can go and drink it.

**2. Foot-brain trail (cerebropedal).** You leave a wet line wherever you walk. It dries to a faint mark in seconds. The metroidvania map screen *is* those marks and your stains, nothing else: what you have walked, you know. Heavily re-walked lines darken and start to corrode, so a favourite route eventually gives way and a deliberately paced loop can cut a hole in a floor without dying at all. Feverish reading: you are always sweating yourself onto the world, and the world remembers it as damage.

### Ability list

1. **Set** (start): death as placement.
2. **Drink** (start): reclaim a stain; drink from pots to refill wetness.
3. **Stickless**: expel a gout of ink downward to shove yourself up. A second jump that costs wetness and leaves a small stain that fades unless you set on it. Tumbles slightly, like a rocket without its stick.
4. **Nib**: harden your leading edge. A dash that cuts thin paper in a line; the line corrodes fast and becomes a slot. The combat slash.
5. **Lye Bladder**: carry lye from the Margins' wells. Wax (unsaponified tallow: ink cannot stick to it, and it does not corrode) saponifies on contact into soap: slippery to stand on, but ink takes on it, and in heat it bubbles into short-lived floating platforms.
6. **Gilt Tongue**: be conductive while alive. Stand in a gilt gap and the current flows through you, but it dries you fast. Hold long enough and you set in place as a permanent bridge: the death you walk toward on purpose.
7. **Fold** (late, stretch goal): pull a sheet's corner so two distant points touch. A page-fold shortcut. Expensive to build; cut first if scope demands.

### Biomes

1. **The Margins**: cool, blank, the outer edge and the spine as a vertical hub. Lye wells. Thick paper, so stains last. The tutorial teaches "set on purpose" here.
2. **The Gilt Ruling**: ruled sheets with cut gold lines; lifts, clamps, page-turners. The circuit-building region. Electric hum, thin bright lines, dark ground: made for shaders.
3. **The Waxed Leaves**: batik sheets. Trails do not stick, so the map goes blind; everything slides; wax figures shrug off Nib. Near heat the wax drips into climbable stalactites. Lye changes the rules of the whole biome.
4. **The Foxing**: damp, brown-spotted, soft. Corrosion runs ten times faster: stains burn through in seconds. Set becomes the fastest way *down*, and every floor is temporary. Rust-brown particle bloom.
5. **The Press**: the bottom. Low ceilings, pages compressed. Wetness drains in seconds and trails evaporate before they dry. Progress is a chain of deliberate sets, each one a step. The mood at its most feverish.
6. **The Watermark** (secret): a region visible only when gilt current lights a sheet from behind. Found by standing a stain in the right gap.

### An encounter: The Blotter

In the Foxing. A vast absorbent sheet-creature that drinks anything wet: your trail, your body, your Stickless gouts. It cannot drink a stain. The room has a gilt line with three cuts around its edge and a soft floor. The fight is a chase in which you place three sets across the three cuts, respawning at the room's pot each time with no interruption, while the Blotter reshapes itself to cover the cuts you have not reached. Set in the wrong spot and you have to run back and drink it. When the ring closes, the ruling arcs and stuns it, and by then your three stains have been eating the floor for the whole fight: it drops through into the sheet below, and you follow through the hole you made.

### Why it is not generic (self-critique)

Closest existing things: Life Goes On and other corpse-as-platform puzzle games (mechanically closest; GALL adds conductance, corrosion, the drink budget, and puts it in a combat-forward metroidvania rather than a puzzle sequence); Okami and Chicory (ink worlds, but there the ink heals and here it stains and burns); Limbo and Inside for silhouette-in-heat; Paper Mario and Tearaway for paper (which it resembles the least in tone). Nobody has made a game where the map is your footprint and your dead bodies are the wiring.

Honest weaknesses: "ink hero" is an obvious pull from the seed and the whole concept lives or dies on inverting it. The six-body budget can read as a lives system, which is the punishment the lens forbids; drink must always be available and pots must refill without limit. Corrosion permanence is a soft-lock hazard.

### Greybox test (one room, one hour)

A room with a pot, a paper floor with two thickness values, a gilt line with one cut and a lift it powers, a small tear, and a wax wall. Wetness drains; set at zero; stains are solid, bridge the cut, and burn through thin floor in ~20 s; drink lifts them. The questions: does a tester deliberately walk to the cut and die within ten minutes, and do they laugh when the lift starts? Does a burn-through they set up earlier feel like a gift when it opens? If they treat six bodies as six lives, the budget goes.

### Risks

- Soft-locks from corrosion eating a needed floor: give sheets an un-eatable grain, or refund stains automatically when a floor fully burns.
- Persisting many stains with their corrosion timers in the sim and in saves.
- Legibility of wet versus dry ink with code-driven visuals.
- The premise is quiet ("no author") and could read as nothing. It has to be felt through the world, never said.
- Wax-and-lye chemistry is one more system on top of ink, current, and corrosion; cut if it does not earn itself in the greybox.

### Seeds used

- Inkpot: load-bearing. The pot is the save point and the body budget; iron-gall ink gives corrosion and, with rust, the "ink that eats the page" logic.
- Megmho: load-bearing. Conductance as the world's power system; the dead conduct better than the living.
- Cerebropedal: the trail-as-map; knowledge is where the foot has been.
- Unsaponified: wax as ink-proof material, lye as the key, soap as a traversal state.
- Stickless: the unstable self-propelled second jump.
- Atheism: "no author"; the reason the page is blank and yours are the only marks.
- Feverish: the heat that dries you; the Press; the always-sweating trail.
- Lens: death places a bridge, a wire, and a future hole, and you go back and drink it when you are done.

## Which is stronger

**UREDO.** GALL is easier to explain and easier to greybox, but its closest neighbour (corpse-platforming) is a known genre and its premise is quiet. UREDO's core verb (aim your own death, land as the next thing) is genuinely new for the genre, its gating grammar ("what microclimate is within one lob?") falls out of real biology rather than being bolted on, and it is the more striking to look at in silhouettes, particles and shaders: pustule bursts, spore arcs, folding sorrel, frost crystallising on orange. Its main risk is sympathy for a fungus, which is a writing and hosting problem, not a mechanics problem.
