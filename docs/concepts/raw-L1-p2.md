# Concept pack — L1, seed pack 2

Seeds: Marginella broderickae (margin snail), Blink FM (amateur/youth local radio), choroiditis (inflamed choroid layer of the eye), pulverizator, woodkern, grapewise, citrous, tided, semostomeous (jellyfish order).
Lens: the world changes on a timescale the player can feel over the whole game.
Mood: feverish.
Bans observed: no music/instruments/songs as core theme, no fallen kingdoms/plagues/infections/corrupted gods, no insect/moth protagonist, no light-vs-dark themes, no amnesiac protagonist, no ancient precursor civilisation.

## 1. Ten wild associations (pushed past the obvious)

1. **Margin snail** → not just "snail shell spiral," but *marginalia* — a margin snail lives in the thin unclaimed edge of something bigger, like a note scribbled in the gutter of a page. A world could be that margin: a thin habitable strip on the edge of something enormous and unseen.
2. **Snail shell** → growth rings are a calendar made of architecture. A shell doesn't erase old chambers, it seals them and builds past them — a literal, physical justification for metroidvania backtracking (old rooms still exist, just sealed by the thing that grew).
3. **Blink FM** (youth-run local radio) → not music — the *amateurism* and *intermittency* of it. A signal run by people who aren't professionals, that cuts in and out. Push further: "blink" as an eyelid action, not a station name — an actual, very slow blink as a game-length clock.
4. **Choroiditis** → inflammation of the vascular layer that feeds the retina. Push past "sick eye" (too close to plague/corruption bans) into *healing* — a scar tightening, blood vessels regrowing over a long injury, visible as a spreading/receding red network you can literally watch shrink over the whole campaign.
5. **Citrous** → not flavour, but *preservation*: acid as a pickling/curing agent. A biome that holds its shape because it's acidic, versus one that's collapsing because it isn't.
6. **Pulverizator** → a machine noun for a person/thing that grinds to powder. Push into biology: a gizzard, a mandible-mill, an organ whose job is mechanical grinding rather than a "villain device."
7. **Woodkern** → archaic word for a lightly-armed forest guerrilla. Push past "rebels in a forest" into: a culture of people who live *on* something enormous and treat it the way foresters treat a wood — harvesting sustainably, moving in small light bands, wary of exhausting their home.
8. **Grapewise** → "-wise" as manner-suffix (clockwise, otherwise) fused with "grape": growth *in the manner of a cluster*. Push into a mechanic: nodes that are functionally linked in a bunch, where touching one changes its neighbours — a chain-reaction topology, not just a decoration.
9. **Tided** → the verb form, "to be tided over" (temporarily sustained) sitting right next to ocean tides. Push into: the world isn't cyclical (day/night) but has one enormous, one-directional tide across the whole game — it goes out, or in, exactly once, over dozens of hours.
10. **Semostomeous** (an order of large jellyfish with frilled oral arms) → push past "jellyfish monster" into structure: a creature whose *whole body* is the traversable world, propelled by slow bell-pulses, translucent enough that light and weather pass through it and change how it reads visually over time.

## 2. Five clichés to steer away from

1. Amnesiac chosen one wakes in ruins to eventually face down a corrupted god / dark-lord figure (light vs dark).
2. A kingdom is struck by a plague/blight that turns its people into monsters; you trace it to a source and cure/kill it.
3. An ancient precursor civilisation left behind ability-upgrade relics in buried ruins under a mountain.
4. A small insect protagonist explores the decaying kingdom of a dead insect civilisation.
5. Standard ability reskins (dash/double-jump/wall-climb) dropped into stock biomes (fire, ice, poison, forest) that exist because "that's level 3," with no world-logic reason to be there.

## Concept A — BELLTIDE

**Hook:** You tend the nerve-lanterns of a migrating jellyfish-leviathan swimming from a cold trench to warm spawning shallows — every biome you've already cleared keeps changing, permanently, as its body heats up over the whole game.

### Premise and world logic
The world is the body of a single enormous, living animal — a semostomeous leviathan, built like a scaled-up jellyfish with a vast frilled underside of chambers, canals and gardens of symbiotic life. It is not dead, not a ruin, not divine: it is simply migrating, as it has done every few years of its life, up out of the cold trench toward warm spawning shallows. It is not infected or corrupted — it's healthy and doing something it has always done.

You play a **wick**: a small, soft-bodied tenant creature (not an insect) whose lineage has lived inside the leviathan for generations, tending its bioluminescent nerve-lanterns so the giant can navigate, and harvesting what it needs to live. You have a clear, present goal — reach the spawning shallows and keep the leviathan's nervous system lit well enough to survive the trip — not a mystery about your own past.

The world is explorable in the metroidvania sense because the leviathan's own anatomy is a dungeon: canals, ganglia caverns, a digestive interior, an outer hide. It's interconnected because it's one animal. Backtracking is justified by the margin-snail idea (see Seeds): tissue doesn't so much get replaced as sealed over and grown past, so old chambers remain, just cut off until you find (or force) a new route.

### Signature mechanic(s)

**1. The Long Tide (world clock).** As real playtime passes across the campaign, the leviathan's core temperature rises through a handful of fixed thresholds as it nears the surface. This is one-directional and irreversible — there's no going back to "cold phase" once you've moved on. When a threshold crosses, biomes you've already cleared physically change: frozen kelp-root walls thaw into swamp and open new shortcuts; cold-loving predators die back or migrate away, making old rooms safer; heat-loving predators move into rooms that used to be safe. Some ability-grafts (see below) only function at certain temperature phases, so world-state, not just item pickups, gates progression — you can be standing in front of a door you have the "key" for and still have to wait for the tide to turn.

**2. Grape-clusters (traversal + combat topology).** Zooid pods hang in bunches, literally clustered "grapewise." Popping one (by touch, by a thrown stinger-line, or by impact) triggers a chain reaction through the bunch — each pop can become a platform of light, a burst of thrust, or, near enemies, a discharge of stingers. The same cluster object is a platforming puzzle in a quiet corridor and a screen-clearing tool in a fight, and clusters can be strung between rooms so that combat decisions ("do I pop that cluster now, wasting it, or save it as my escape route") double as traversal decisions.

### Ability list
- **Pulse-Kick** (double-jump reskin): a grafted contractile zooid gives one extra burst of thrust underwater, leaving a brief current that flustered small enemies react to.
- **Umbrella Drift** (glide reskin): open a translucent bell-flap to slow your fall and ride ambient currents — the currents' direction and strength change with the Long Tide, so the same tool plays differently in different chapters.
- **Sting-Line** (grapple reskin): fire a barbed tendril into flesh or a grape-cluster to swing, or to yank-pop a cluster from a distance.
- **Chromatophore Cloak** (stealth): mimic the leviathan's pigment-shift camouflage — only works when your colouring matches the current thermal phase, tying a core ability directly to the world clock.
- **Acid-Gut Membrane** (hazard suit): a secreted lining that lets you enter the acidic digestive interior unharmed.
- **Broadcast Pulse** (scan/map ability, amateur-signal flavour from Blink FM): send a bioluminescent pulse-code that reveals hidden creatures and paths in murky water — and can draw the attention of things that hunt by light.
- **Ballast Shift** (pound/heavy-fall reskin): a grafted stone-gland lets you turn heavy to smash calcified, pulverised barriers and sink fast through water columns.

### Biomes
- **Cold Root Reef** (start): frozen kelp-symbiote gardens at the trench-base; ice blocks that only thaw once the Long Tide advances — mechanically the game's "before" snapshot you'll later watch change.
- **Grape-Vault Ganglia**: nerve-cluster caverns dense with grapewise zooid pods; puzzle-platforming built entirely on chain-pops.
- **Sour Gut Sea**: the acidic digestive interior; corrosive currents and rhythmic peristalsis waves that need to be timed, not just survived.
- **Ashgill Shallows**: as the leviathan nears the surface, exposed gill-slits create a real air/water boundary — platforms that are submerged or exposed depending on the leviathan's own breathing rhythm.
- **The Bloodshot Eye**: a single sensory node, old-scarred and still healing (the choroiditis image, reframed as recovery, not disease) — vascular platforms swell and shrink with a visible heartbeat, parallax distorts, colour bleeds; this is where the "feverish" mood is delivered hardest.
- **Rimwood Hide**: the outer skin, home to the **Kelp-kern** — a small, lightly-armed scavenger culture (the woodkern seed) who harvest the hide sustainably and act as the game's hub/social layer, including margin-snail elders whose shells record earlier migrations as a visible, readable calendar.

### Example encounter/boss
**The Pulverizator**, in the Sour Gut Sea: a building-sized gizzard-organ, a slow rotating mill of grinding stone. It can't be hurt directly — you use Ballast Shift to drop swallowed ballast rocks into its grinding wheels on the beat of the gut's peristalsis waves, jamming it, while dodging the grit it flings out as shrapnel. On defeat, its jammed wheels grind open a wall permanently — a boss fight whose reward is a literal, visible change to the map, reinforcing the Long Tide's "the world doesn't reset" logic.

### Why it's not generic
Closest relative: *Subnautica*'s living-creature-as-environment feel, and Ori's "the world grows with the story" beats. The difference is that Ori's changes are mostly scripted one-offs tied to story triggers, while here the change is a single continuous, legible clock the player can feel ticking throughout — you can watch a specific wall you remember, still frozen, and know it will thaw, and choose to route around waiting for it or not. The honest risk is that if I'm not careful this reads as "generic lava-world reskinned as flesh" — the mood word "feverish" has to be earned with subjective visual/audio distortion (heartbeat-synced screen pulse, bleeding colour, disoriented parallax in the Eye biome), not just literal heat.

### Greybox test
One corridor: a wall of frozen kelp-root blocks the far end; a heat vent midway lets the player channel a "local tide" pulse (a stand-in for the real ability) that visibly raises ambient temperature over ~30 seconds. A cold-only enemy patrols in front of the wall. Build: does thawing the wall (permanent for the play session) and watching the enemy die off/flee as heat rises feel like *earned* progress rather than a cutscene? That tells us in under an hour whether "watch the world change, don't just walk through a door" is fun before we build the full macro-clock system.

### Risks
- Irreversible change conflicts with metroidvania players' instinct to backtrack for 100%-completion; cleared/changed rooms need new content post-change, not just a closed door, or players feel punished for exploring early.
- Simulating gradual, legible change across a large map is a real engineering cost (this is Sim-adjacent work: it must stay deterministic per CLAUDE.md's `src/sim/` rules, since it affects gameplay logic, not just VFX).
- Pacing risk: if the macro clock is time-based rather than progress-based, fast or slow players could hit thresholds "wrong" (too early = area still deadly when they need it; too late = challenge trivialised). Likely needs to be gated by story beats/ability count, not wall-clock time, while still *feeling* like an unstoppable tide.
- "Feverish" is easy to under-deliver — needs deliberate audio/visual direction, not just orange-and-red palette.

### Seeds used
Semostomeous (world = a giant jellyfish-order creature), tided (the Long Tide one-way world clock), grapewise (chain-pop zooid clusters), citrous (acid gut biome), pulverizator (gizzard-mill boss), woodkern (Kelp-kern scavenger culture), margin snail (elders' shells as a readable migration calendar), choroiditis (the healing Bloodshot Eye biome), Blink FM (amateur bioluminescent broadcast-pulse ability).

## Concept B — LASHLINE

**Hook:** You relay pulses of coded light across the eyelash-forest rim of a sleeping giant's open eye, racing a blink that closes the world by degrees over the whole campaign.

### Premise and world logic
The habitable world is a thin ring: the **margin** of an enormous open eye belonging to an unseen, slumbering creature whose body is otherwise buried or submerged — you never see more of it than this. It is not a god, not corrupted, not a ruin of a lost people: it's an animal, alive, and simply hasn't blinked in a very long time. This game covers the length of exactly one blink, from fully open to fully closed, playing out over the whole campaign as one continuous, one-way clock (distinct from Concept A's clock: here it changes light, moisture and access rather than temperature and predators).

You play a **lash-runner** from the **Lash-kern**: a small, scrappy clan (the woodkern image, transposed from forest to eyelash-forest) who live along the giant eyelash-hairs at the rim, harvesting dried tear-salt and light-oil sustainably, the way foresters manage a wood they depend on. You have a known job: keep the relay network running and get a specific message all the way to the tear duct before the lid closes. No amnesia, no chosen-one mystery — a working person with a task and a deadline.

Explorability comes from the eye's real anatomy: the lash-canopy at the rim, the flat dry sclera stretching inward, the tear duct and lacrimal lake, the pupil's dark interior, the vitreous chamber behind it, and a permanently scarred, healing patch of choroid tissue. It's interconnected because it's one eye; old areas seal or flood as the blink advances, not unlike a shell sealing old whorls.

### Signature mechanic(s)

**1. Lashline relay (carrying, not just reading, information).** Scattered relay dishes hold light-pulse codes — semaphore-style patterns, explicitly not music or song — left by other Lash-kern. You "tune in" at a dish and physically carry the captured pulse in your lantern to another dish, where delivering it opens a gate, triggers a bridge, or unlocks a shortcut. The pulse decays under wind, damage and time, shown as a visible flicker/decay meter, so the traversal problem is: pick a route that's fast enough and sheltered enough to deliver the signal intact, not simply "reach the marker." Later upgrades let you carry two signals or hold one far longer, which changes route-planning across the whole map, not just combat options — the same tool that carries lore/hints is the thing that opens doors.

**2. The Long Blink (world clock).** Across the campaign the eyelid ridge on the horizon visibly creeps closed in a small number of large, permanent steps. Each step changes light levels (dimmer, later stages near-dark at the rim but glowing from bioluminescent lash-lanterns), changes what's dry vs. flooded (tear fluid rises before the blink, flooding the Sclera Flats), and sometimes buries or exposes whole rooms. Framing note for whoever builds this: this must stay about wet/dry and open/closed, never about "light is good, dark is safe/evil" — the ban on light-vs-dark themes is a hard rule and this concept sits closest to that line of any idea in this pack.

### Ability list
- **Relay Grip** (wall-cling/climb reskin): sticky lash-oil pads for climbing the giant eyelash-hair "trees" that form the Lash Canopy — the game's basic traversal grammar.
- **Lantern Leap** (double-jump reskin): a bioluminescent bladder puffs for an extra boost; also the physical emitter you use to carry a relay signal.
- **Wick-Dash** (dash reskin): a quick burst that briefly brightens your carried signal, punching through minor wind/static interference.
- **Tear-Coat** (acid/hazard suit): a waxy secretion that lets you swim the acidic Tear Duct Shallows without harm.
- **Grit-Shell** (heavy/pound reskin): a rolled dried-mucus shell (a margin-snail image) that tanks pulverising grit storms and smashes crusted salt barriers.
- **Long Relay** (signature-mechanic upgrade): a bigger lantern rig that lets you carry two signals at once or resist decay far longer — directly expands both which gates you can open and which puzzles are solvable.
- **Vitreous Drift** (low-gravity glide): a grafted eye-floater bladder for slow-fall/drift inside the near-zero-g Vitreous Deep.

### Biomes
- **Lash Canopy** (start): the eyelash-hair forest, climb-based, home of the Lash-kern.
- **Sclera Flats**: a dry, sun-cracked white expanse with long sightlines and wind hazards — the relay-dish network lives here, so this biome is where the signal-carrying puzzle is hardest and most exposed.
- **Tear Duct Shallows**: an acidic saline marsh that floods further as the blink advances, needing Tear-Coat; currents change direction as tear fluid wells up pre-blink.
- **Grit Dunes**: pulverised salt-crust terrain, crumbling underfoot, home to the game's grinding-mite boss.
- **Vitreous Deep**: the near-zero-g interior behind the pupil, full of grapewise clusters of drifting eye-floaters used as chain-pop platforms, disorienting depth cues.
- **Choroid Hollow**: a healing, bloodshot vessel-canyon (the choroiditis image, explicitly framed as an old wound closing, not an infection) — veins visibly regrow and knit closed over the course of the game, a second, slower readable clock layered under the Long Blink.

### Example encounter/boss
**The Grit-Mite**, in the Grit Dunes: a building-sized rolling compactor creature that tumbles through the dunes crushing salt-crust to powder. It can't be damaged head-on; the fight is about luring it onto thin crust so it breaks through and gets stuck, using Grit-Shell to survive its charges in the meantime. Defeating it pulverises a dune into passable powder, permanently opening a shortcut back to the Lash Canopy — an explicit, visible, one-way change to the map, the same "boss fight rewires the world" idea as Concept A's Pulverizator, arrived at independently through a different biome.

### Why it's not generic
Closest relatives: the anatomical/ocular imagery of *GRIS* or *Hyper Light Drifter*'s cryptic light motifs, and the escort/carry tension of games like *Pikmin* or *Ico*. The differentiator is that the "escort" is an abstract resource (a decaying light pattern) rather than an NPC, so it can be split across the whole map as a standing traversal/puzzle grammar instead of a scripted sequence, and the world-changing clock (a single blink) is more legible and more literal than most "seasons pass" metroidvania framing. Honest risk: this concept sits right next to two bans — it must not slide into "light vs dark" morality, and the eye/body-horror imagery must not read as shock value rather than something felt. If the team can't keep the wet/dry, open/closed framing disciplined, this concept is more dangerous to execute than Concept A.

### Greybox test
One room: two relay dishes across a wind-swept chasm. The player receives a light-pulse pattern at dish 1 (shown as a glowing code in the lantern), then must cross the chasm — platforming through wind gusts that visibly degrade a decay meter — and deliver it to dish 2 before it fully decays, which opens a gate. Build: is "protect a fragile thing you're carrying while platforming" actually more interesting than a timer or a health bar would be, within an hour's build? That's the one thing this test needs to answer before committing to the relay system as the spine of the whole game.

### Risks
- The relay mechanic can collapse into a reskinned fetch-quest if route choice doesn't meaningfully trade risk vs. speed vs. range — needs real decision space, not one correct path.
- Sits closest to the light-vs-dark ban of anything in this pack; needs an explicit house rule (physical states only, no moral valence) that any writer/artist joining later must follow.
- The Long Blink primarily reads as a lighting change unless it also gates traversal and combat concretely (flooding, burial, new predators) — risk of feeling like a filter over the same rooms rather than a real change.
- Eye/body anatomy as a setting risks reading as gross-out horror rather than the intended "feverish, intimate, strange-but-natural" tone; needs restraint in creature/gore design.

### Seeds used
Choroiditis (the Long Blink world clock and the healing Choroid Hollow biome), Blink FM (the amateur, non-musical light-code relay network and its "amateur signal that cuts in and out" texture), margin snail (the world as a thin habitable margin on something vast, and the Grit-Shell's shell image), woodkern (the Lash-kern's sustainable-harvest forest-guerrilla culture, transposed to eyelashes), pulverizator (the Grit-Mite boss and the Grit Dunes biome), grapewise (Vitreous Deep floater clusters), citrous (the acidic Tear Duct Shallows).
