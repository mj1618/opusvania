# Critique L1-A: creative director's review of the 12 L1 concepts

Reviewer A (independent; critique-L1-B not read). Inputs: `CLAUDE.md`, `PLAN.md` §1 and §3.5, `raw-L1-p1.md` … `raw-L1-p6.md`. PLAN §2's *Unfinished Opus* is ignored as instructed.

## What I'm judging against

- **The bar is moment-to-moment feel** (PLAN §1: movement and combat pillars). A signature mechanic only counts if it lives *inside* fast movement and the hit-feedback stack. If it lives beside them, as a pause to think, aim or wait, it doesn't count.
- **Small game, HK quality** (PLAN §3.5). A mechanic that multiplies content (world states × rooms, forms × movesets, categories × objects) is a direct threat.
- **Code-drawn visuals for a long time.** The best concepts have a *single visual rule* that shaders, outlines and particles can carry.
- **Agents build it.** Deterministic, table-driven rules beat emergent simulation. Room-local state beats world-global state.

**Pattern across the pack.** Seven of the twelve are "rules as physics" concepts. The ones that work for us make the rule-changing verb **a hit** (DISTRAINT's jab-grab, DISSOLUTION's brim strike). That puts the verb inside the hitstop and knockback stack instead of in a targeting mode. The ones that fail need you to **point, hold or wait** (Stamp, Depose, Set, Relay).

## 1. Ranked shortlist

### #1: DISTRAINT (Kid Tallow)

**Why.**
- **It is the only concept scoring 4 or more on both action feel and franchise.** Seize is a jab-grab, a melee hit that can carry hitstop, sparks and a heavy sound. Levy is a throw. Slip is a boxing dash with i-frames. The Count is a knockdown window. Boxing gives the combat a language of its own that isn't "a nail slash", which is exactly the originality we want where it doesn't cost feel.
- **One verb does three jobs.** Seizing a wall's hum ghosts the wall, seizing a dog's bark removes its lunge, and levying brown noise makes a weight for a pressure plate. Traversal, combat and gating come from one pair of buttons.
- **A poster and a trailer.** A disgraced featherweight champion turned bailiff repossesses the sounds holding a feverish boomtown together, while working up the chain of creditors to take back her own belt. The city of Tallage is alive, loud and in debt: it is not a ruin, not a kingdom and not a plague. It's sequel-friendly (other cities, other things to repossess).
- **It suits code-drawn visuals.** The visual rule is simple: humming things have a vibrating outline shader, and seized things become dashed ghosts. Noise colours become particle materials (heavy slow motes for brown, sharp sparks for violet).
- **It suits an AI-built game's audio.** Brown, pink and violet noise are one-line Web Audio generators, so the game's core sound identity is procedural and free. Seizing a sound is literally cutting a noise source. No other concept gets its audio identity this cheaply.
- **Proven structure where it doesn't matter.** Weigh-in scales are lock-and-key gates, and knockout-then-recover-your-bag is the shade loop with a fictional reason.

**Biggest risk.** It slides into pick-up-and-place puzzling (Kirby meets gravity gun). The pitch stacks five systems (noise colour, bag weight, weight class, the Count, satchel combos), and inventory management is the enemy of flow. A secondary risk: consumable, carried resources make the PLAN §1 graph solver harder, because reachability becomes a planning problem.

**Trim and grafts.**
- **From THE LANDING: allocation as a FIFO queue.** The bag is 3 slots. Levy always throws the newest sound, and seizing a fourth pushes the oldest back to its owner. That removes any inventory screen, bounds the state space, and gives the "which lie do I keep" tension for free.
- **Weight is derived, not managed.** Your weight class is simply the number of brown sounds in the bag (0 is feather, 1 is middle, 2 or more is heavy). There's no separate weigh-in UI.
- **From THE DISSOLUTION ROLLS: owners always chase what you took.** A seized enemy drops everything to get its sound back. That gives seizing aggro and tempo value in combat, and lets you herd: levy a dog's bark across a gap and the dog leaps after it. It also fixes the "enemies must stay aggressive" problem.
- **From ZONE OF AVOIDANCE: Middle-voice recoil.** A downward levy is a recoil hop, the Kid Tallow pogo and second jump. That puts the throw into airborne movement instead of making it a standing action.
- **From ONSETTER: the Galloway principle.** A boss repossessed on the Count becomes permanent world machinery. The Auctioneer's gavel shockwave becomes a hub lift; a boss's roar becomes a permanent updraft shortcut.
- **From PROBATE: one visual rule, no exceptions.** If it hums, it's real. If it's dashed, it's been seized. White static is visually and legally unseizable.
- **Room-local regeneration.** Seized sounds return to their sources when you leave a room. Each room is then self-contained for the solver, and softlocks from a spent resource are impossible.
- **Cut from the first game:** Corner Satchel combos and the Standing Count (bank them for later). Keep the Count for elites and bosses only. Ship with three colours: brown (heavy), pink (spring) and violet (rising or cutting). White is terrain.

### #2: THE DISSOLUTION ROLLS

**Why.** It's the most original premise in the pack. The core verb, the **brim strike**, is an 8-direction attack, so it's combat-native. Law fields are circles with a tint and an icon, which is ideal for shaders. "Keep your enemies alive to have somewhere to stand" is a design sentence nobody has shipped. The Rates Archive, a school of tiny clerks forming a living floor, and the Provost (enumerated, not slain) are strong set pieces.

**Biggest risk.** It becomes an escort game. Waiting for a clerk to walk to his hat is dead time, and "don't kill them" fights the satisfaction of HK-style combat. Hat-kicking also tilts the tone toward comedy.

**Grafts.**
- **From DISTRAINT: a full boxing melee kit.** Players need something satisfying to hit that isn't a hat, so hatless officials can be knocked down and fought properly.
- **Officials sprint for their hats**, faster than the player runs. They become fast-moving platforms you chase and ride, not slow ones you wait for.
- **Cap the bylaws at 4**, the lesson from LANDING's physics matrix: Ground Here, No Falling, Keep Left and Quiet Hours.
- **From ONSETTER:** enlisted bosses become permanent map fixtures (a pinned Provost hat as a permanent floor), and a shift pattern for when officials patrol.
- **From ZONE:** the Marches' flickering disputed zones become the rhythm-platforming biome, already implied.

### #3: ONSETTER

**Why.** It has the best metroidvania skeleton in the pack. Drops are one-way commitments, shortcuts are ropes you permanently balance, and the map is a ledger of balanced and unbalanced ropes. Heft is small integers plus a lookup table, and a rope is a length constraint on a sheave graph, so it's ideal for deterministic integer physics. The Tug, the Passing and the Approach are three distinct events inside one mechanic. The Galloway pony, enlisted as the shaft's permanent counterweight, is the best boss idea in the pack.

**Biggest risk.** Riding against a weight is being carried, and being carried is the opposite of HK control. If rides aren't fast, cancellable and chainable, it's an elevator game. The Tug also takes control away. The setting is the "dark cave" default and carries real-world grief.

**Grafts.**
- **Cut-to-launch.** Releasing the clip mid-ride keeps the rope's velocity, like Ori's Bash out of a ride. Rides become slingshots.
- **From DISTRAINT:** a boxer's melee kit and a protagonist with a face.
- **From UREDO:** "respawn is relocation", so falling down a shaft is never a rollback.
- **From ZONE:** Passive voice, riding an enemy's force, as a second ride type alongside clipping.
- **Lighten the palette:** firedamp blue and blackdamp amber as full biome identities, as the concept already suggests.

**Honourable mentions (not shortlisted).**
- **UREDO** is the most original verb in the pack. Its aimed death-lob should be banked, but a faceless fungus hero with four movesets is a second-game ambition.
- **ZONE OF AVOIDANCE**'s stance switching is the best pure action mechanic. Treat it as a graft donor, not a setting.
- **PROBATE and GALL** are strong, but they're puzzle games in a metroidvania's clothes.

## 2. Recommendation: DISTRAINT, trimmed and grafted

**The argument.** We are a small team of agents chasing *feel*, and the brief's hardest constraints are action feel and franchise identity. DISTRAINT is the only concept where the signature verb *is a hit*, the protagonist *is a character*, and the world *is alive rather than ruined*.

- **The mechanic survives our constraints.** Seizable and ghosted states are just flags on tiles and entities. Levied objects are three entity types. Weight class is three rows in `tuning.ts`. Everything regenerates per room, so the solver stays tractable and nothing needs a world-global clock.
- **It makes our constraints assets.** Code-drawn outlines *are* the visual language, and procedural noise *is* the audio identity.
- **Its main risk is testable in one loop.** Does seize/levy keep you moving? That's measurable. ONSETTER's risk (rides feel slow) is also measurable, but even if its greybox passes, the identity problem remains. DISSOLUTION's risk (herding is waiting) depends more on AI tuning than one loop can settle.

**Fallback.** If DISTRAINT fails the flow gates below, run DISSOLUTION's hat-over-chasm room next in the same harness, then ONSETTER's shaft.

### The greybox experiment: "Lot 7" and "The Pit"

**Prerequisite.** The current `src/sim/player/player.ts` run and jump, plus coyote time and a jump buffer. Everything else below is new, behind a flag. All numbers go in `tuning.ts`. Seize, levy and weight state live in `src/sim/`. Use the existing replay (`src/sim/replay.ts`) and hash (`src/sim/hash.ts`) for tapes.

**What to build (greybox only: rectangles, outlines, particles, jsfxr or Web Audio noise):**
- **Seize.** A short-range jab-grab in 4 directions, usable on the ground and in the air. On a hit against a humming source: 4 frames of hitstop, the source becomes a dashed ghost (non-solid, or its attack disabled), and the sound enters the bag.
- **Levy.** Throw the newest sound in the aimed direction.
  - Brown is a heavy block that falls, anchors and presses plates, and does damage plus hitstop on enemies.
  - Pink lands as a spring pad.
  - A downward levy while airborne gives a recoil hop (the Middle-voice graft).
- **Bag.** A 3-slot FIFO. Weight class is 0, 1, or 2+ brown sounds: feather, middle or heavy. Each class changes jump height, gravity and knockback via three `tuning.ts` rows. Heavy landings press plates.
- **Regeneration.** On room exit, all sounds return to their sources.
- **The owner-chase rule.** A seized enemy switches to *retrieve*, moving toward its sound (in the bag, or where it was levied) until it gets it back.

**Room 1, "Lot 7" (traversal, about 2 × 2 screens).**
- **Layout:** a start ledge; a humming wall (pink, a creaking partition) blocking the corridor; a ledge too high for any jump; a brown furnace; a pressure-plate door to the exit; and a white-static floor strip as the control (it looks like a sound, but can't be seized).
- **Intended route:** seize the wall, pass through, levy pink under the high ledge, bounce up, seize the furnace, then land heavy on the plate *or* levy brown onto it.
- **Also build a control room:** the same dimensions and path length, solved with jumps only.

**Room 2, "The Pit" (combat, one screen, two small platforms).**
- **Enemies:**
  - Two **Barkers**. Their lunge comes from a pink bark and has a telegraph of at least 15 frames.
  - One **Grinder**. Its slam comes from a brown growl and has a telegraph of at least 20 frames.
- **The player also has a plain jab** for damage, so the signature verb is never forced.

**Pass/fail criteria an agent can judge alone.** Run everything headless where possible, with confirming screenshots from `playwright-cli`.

| # | Check | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Responsiveness: frames from seize input to the first sim state change | ≤ 6 (the < 100 ms pillar) |
| A2 | Seize total duration / levy total duration | ≤ 14 / ≤ 10 frames. No action roots the player for more than 12 frames. |

**Overall result.** Pass if every A, C, E and F check passes and at least one of B1/B2 passes. If a kill threshold on B1 or B2 is hit, or C1 finds a bypass, the verdict is **fail: concept slows play**, and the loop moves to the fallback.

**For the human to judge later** (agents can't):
- **The tooltip test from the pitch:** does a new player try to seize *everything* within 2 minutes without being told?
- Does seizing feel **punchy**? The moment a wall goes silent should land like a hit.
- Does the change in weight class feel like **power or encumbrance**?
- Is the bag queue invisible, or do players lose track of what they'll throw?
- Is the coloured-noise soundscape pleasant or grating over 10 minutes?
- Does a Kid Tallow silhouette (boxer's stance, bailiff's coat, the sack) read as a star?
- Does the boomtown tone land as feverish and warm-hearted, not cynical? The redistributor arc is what makes it warm.

## 3. Banked standalone mechanics

One line each, with the source concept. These are worth keeping regardless of which concept wins.

1. **Shared-fate rope** (ONSETTER): a persistent tether to an enemy creates its own events (Tug, Passing), not just a one-off launch.
2. **The Passing** (ONSETTER): a guaranteed one-second, side-by-side encounter window midway through any counterweighted ride.
3. **Danger on approach / overwind** (ONSETTER): arriving too fast hurts, so you brake at the top of a big move.
4. **Enlisted boss** (ONSETTER): a beaten boss becomes permanent world machinery and opens the shortcut it guarded.
5. **Shift pattern** (ONSETTER): enemy populations migrate on a frame-count schedule announced by a distant hooter.
6. **False lights** (ONSETTER): hazards that mimic save points, plus one instrument that tells them apart.
7. **Enemy-carried law field** (DISSOLUTION): a physics rule projected in a radius around a knockable object the enemy always retrieves.
8. **Disputed zone** (DISSOLUTION): two overlapping fields alternate on a learnable rhythm, which gives rhythm platforming without music.
9. **Chain-pop clusters** (BELLTIDE): one object is a platform chain in traversal and an AoE in combat, so you choose to spend it or save it.
10. **Boss kill rewires the map** (BELLTIDE, LASHLINE): the jammed grinder or stuck compactor leaves a permanent, visible new route.
11. **Depose and Cite** (PROBATE): record an entity's current action, then force it to replay that action on demand.
12. **Fully witnessed** (PROBATE): an optional boss or bestiary completion state reached by seeing every move, not by damage.
13. **Perjury** (PROBATE): late enemies visibly deviate from their learned pattern, and the deviation is itself learnable.
14. **Adjournment** (PROBATE): replay your own last few seconds as a ghost for two-switch problems.
15. **Tap to hear weakness** (PROBATE): striking timber reveals breakable structure by sound.
16. **Ungarnish** (PROBATE): strip ornament to expose a machine's cycle or hidden mechanism.
17. **FIFO allocation** (LANDING): hold N transformations, and the (N+1)th reverts the oldest.
18. **Self-application** (LANDING): turn the object verb on yourself to unlock a whole classic move set at once.
19. **Appeal boss** (LANDING): you win by witnessing contradictions mid-fight to overturn an immunity.
20. **Subtraction as the main verb** (DISTRAINT): removing a thing's property removes the obstacle and gives you a tool.
21. **The Count** (DISTRAINT): a knocked-down enemy opens a visible ten-beat window to finish or repossess it.
22. **Inventory mass sets moveset** (DISTRAINT): weight classes with weigh-in gates.
23. **Unclaimable zones** (DISTRAINT): a region where your verb doesn't work and your carried resources leak.
24. **Reverse biome** (DISTRAINT, Mortgaged Row): everything is already removed, and you build the route from a budget.
25. **"Going once, going twice, SOLD"** (DISTRAINT): a three-beat verbal telegraph that deletes a floor section.
26. **Diathesis stances** (ZONE): switch who a force acts on (you, the world, or yourself) mid-air.
27. **Silence as mass** (ZONE): invisible gravity wells shown only by lensing, which gives a shader a gameplay job.
28. **Standing wave** (ZONE): calling between parallel walls creates ladder rungs at the nodes.
29. **Throw Voice** (ZONE): a placed lure that pulls you toward where it landed.
30. **Joint action with a neutral creature** (SLORPWAKE): a two-body structure neither can build alone.
31. **Ripeness** (UREDO): health that fills rather than drains, bursting at full.
32. **Aimed death-lob** (UREDO): death is a projectile, the landing substrate picks your next form, and respawn is relocation, never rollback.
33. **The save point is a form** (UREDO): overwinter by rooting in place, not by sitting at a bench.
34. **Folding platforms on a clock** (UREDO): Oxalis leaves close twice per sim cycle.
35. **Set stain** (GALL): your corpse is a solid, conductive, corrosive object you can later drink back.
36. **Footprint map** (GALL): the map shows only where you've walked, and over-walked lines wear through.
37. **Wax and lye** (GALL): a reagent converts an immune surface into a usable but slippery one.
