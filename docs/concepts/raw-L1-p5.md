# Raw concepts — L1, Pack 5

Seed pack: Bardepur (village, Kaluahi Block, Madhubani District, Bihar, on NH105) · giblets · estamene (twilled woollen dress fabric) · words: preterient, ambiguous, slorp, bedpost, unincumbered, pseudolegal · lens: **cooperation with enemies is required** · mood: **melancholy**

Bans observed: no music/instruments/songs as core theme; no fallen kingdoms/plagues/infections/corrupted gods; no insect/moth protagonist; no light-vs-dark; no amnesiac protagonist; no ancient precursor civilisation.

## 1. Ten wild associations (pushed past the obvious)

1. **Bardepur** — not "quaint village," but a place defined purely by its position in a nested bureaucratic hierarchy (Block → District → Highway number). World structure: geography as administrative address, not place-name.
2. **Giblets** — not "gross innards," but the discarded parts of a body being the *separately prized* part (giblet gravy). An economy built on what's removed, not what's whole.
3. **Estamene** — not "old dress," but the twill weave itself: a diagonal over-under-under lattice. A structural/geometric motif for terrain or a parry/timing pattern.
4. **Preterient** (invented sense, echoing "preterite"/past-tense) — an oracle that only ever reports what has *already* happened, one beat behind — inverted prophecy.
5. **Ambiguous** — not "morally grey villain," but factions with no fixed side at all: allegiance is a live, situational fact, never a label.
6. **Slorp** — not "ew, slime," but a verb: absorb-and-reshape as a traversal/combat mechanic, ingestion as a building material.
7. **Bedpost** — not "sexual conquest tally," but notches as a general interface for recording debt, growth, or passage of time on real world geometry.
8. **Unincumbered** — a legal state (property free of liens) repurposed as a progression gate: rooms/areas are "encumbered" until a debt is cleared, not locked until a key is found.
9. **Pseudolegal** — forged documents that nonetheless *function* as real magic: paperwork as a spell system.
10. **Cooperation-with-enemies lens** — bosses as joint-labour problems, not health-bar duels; "defeat" is sometimes the worse outcome.

## 2. Five clichés to steer away from

1. Amnesiac chosen-one waking in a ruined kingdom (banned anyway, but worth naming).
2. Corrupted mother-goddess/world-tree needing purification (Ori-adjacent).
3. Insect or bug protagonist scuttling through a decayed insect-kingdom (Hollow Knight-adjacent, banned).
4. Elemental fire/ice/lightning abilities gating colour-coded biomes.
5. A shattered-memory/soul-fragment collectathon as the spine of progression.

Both concepts below avoid all five.

## Concept A: *The Circuit Rider* (working title)

**Hook:** You're an itinerant rural notary who travels an infinite highway serving legal papers on a world stuck in bureaucratic purgatory — and your spellbook is a filing cabinet.

### Premise and world logic
You play a **Circuit Rider** — a travelling clerk/notary whose entire jurisdiction is a single road, **National Highway 105**, which no longer connects real places but loops through a telescoping nest of **Blocks** and **Districts** that have detached from geography altogether and now stack inside one another. Villages like **Bardepur** exist only as addresses in this administrative hierarchy; their residents are stuck in a slow, aching limbo, endlessly waiting for a file to be "processed." Nobody is dying of plague or corruption — it's just bureaucratic purgatory, quiet and sad. You're explorable in a metroidvania sense because your literal job is to travel node to node along the Road (the connective spine/hub), and your abilities are notarial tools and legal instruments that double as traversal and combat.

### Signature mechanic(s)
**Filing** — you carry a Docket of legal instruments you can stamp onto the world: a **Writ of Right-of-Way** turns a "Private" wall temporarily public (a marked-tile phase-door), an **Encumbrance Order** attaches a lien to a distant object and yanks it toward you (or you toward it), and forged/pseudolegal paperwork functions as literal reality-altering magic rather than flavour text.

This gates the world through **debt, not keys**: many rooms are "encumbered" and only open once you've cleared a lien somewhere else, tying progression to a light economy of favours and settlements rather than colour-coded items.

Cooperation is built into combat: you can't always just fight. Serving papers on an enemy can **deputise** it — a "settlement" mid-fight turns an adversary into a temporary co-operator who must jointly operate a mechanism with you (one pulls a chain while you throw the lever). Winning a fight the violent way is sometimes strictly worse: it solves your immediate problem but leaves a debt unsettled that surfaces later as a harder, sadder consequence.

### Ability list
1. **Surveyor's Chain** — a grapple/swing tool; you must "survey" (reveal) a plot before its boundary can be swung from.
2. **Wax Seal Bolas** — a thrown wax seal that binds a target or plugs a vent/mechanism (freeze/plug utility).
3. **Writ of Right-of-Way** — stamps a "Private" surface as temporarily public, opening a marked-only phase-passage.
4. **Encumbrance Order** — attaches a remote lien to a heavy object (or an enemy's weapon), yanking it to you or you to it; doubles as a mid-fight disarm.
5. **Right of Appeal** (parry/riposte reskin) — after taking a hit you get one immediate "appeal," a tight counter-window framed as contesting the damage.
6. **Deed Transfer** (dash reskin) — instantly reassigns ownership of the ground ahead, snapping you forward along the new "deed line."
7. **Notary's Lamp** — reveals "unincumbered" (off-the-books, free-and-clear) paths invisible on any official map — the secret-room tool.

### Biomes
1. **The Ledger Fields** — flooded, grid-numbered paddy plots; water level is tied to which plots you've filed claims on (drain/flood puzzles).
2. **The Offal Exchange** (giblets, load-bearing) — a rendering-economy market town; enemies are "Renderers" who won't fight if paid tribute in harvested parts — a barter-combat system replaces straight damage trading.
3. **The Estamene Looms** (estamene, load-bearing) — hill mills whose catwalks are built from giant diagonal twill weaves that shift over-under-over on a timer; enemy attacks move in the same diagonal weave pattern.
4. **Bedpost Hollow** (bedpost, load-bearing) — an abandoned inn district of oversized furniture-as-terrain; every surface is notched with generations of travellers' debts. The quietest, most melancholy biome — empty rooms, only tally marks left. Reading the notches times a late-game puzzle.
5. **The Preterient Archive** — a records vault housing a clockwork oracle-clerk that only ever announces what already happened, one room behind real time; you use its retrospective narration to solve forward puzzles.
6. **NH105, the spine** — the overworld hub, a road to visible infinity in both directions, accumulating toll-checkpoints as you progress.

### Example encounter/boss
**The Repossessor** (end of the Offal Exchange): a butcher-bureaucrat trying to seize machinery you need. It's not a straight duel — you parry/stagger while filing counter-paperwork to redirect the seizure onto a bystander golem. Simply "winning" the naive way lets the bystander take the loss (a worse, guiltier ending state). At 50% HP it can instead offer a truce ("sign here"); accepting turns the second half into a co-op puzzle where you and it jointly haul a seized asset out of a collapsing market while fending off rival creditor-enemies together.

### Why it's not generic
Closest relatives: *Disco Elysium* (bureaucracy, melancholy, paperwork-as-worldbuilding) and *Hollow Knight* (structure/traversal) — but neither combines legal paperwork with ability-gated platforming/combat, and a rural-Indian-highway administrative setting (Bardepur/Kaluahi Block/NH105) is far from the usual gothic-European or insect-decay default. Self-critique: the legal-pun framing risks reading as cute/whimsical in a way that undercuts the melancholy, and "notary powers" could feel like menu busywork rather than physical platforming unless the verbs stay tactile (thrown wax, hauled chains, stamped impacts) rather than UI-driven.

### Greybox test
One room: a flooded Ledger Field plot, a "Private" gate on the far side, a heavy seized cart you need as a bridge, and one static Renderer guarding it. See whether players (a) chain-swing the gap, (b) stamp the gate open, (c) yank the cart across with Encumbrance Order, and — crucially — (d) discover that offering the Renderer a tribute item makes it move the cart for free. An hour tells you whether players even notice the cooperative shortcut, and whether stamp-under-pressure feels good versus being a menu chore.

### Risks
- Legal/bureaucratic theme could read as dry or unheroic against the target "Hollow Knight quality" combat feel.
- Comedy (legal puns) could fight the melancholy tone if not tightly controlled.
- The cooperative alternate-solution risks being invisible to players who default to combat, or feeling like mandatory filler.
- Nested administrative geography (Blocks/Districts) needs strong map UI or it reads as confusing rather than evocative.

### Seeds used
Bardepur/NH105/Block/District (world spine and hub structure), giblets (Offal Exchange economy), estamene (Looms biome geometry/attack pattern), bedpost (Hollow biome, tally marks), preterient (Archive's retrospective oracle), unincumbered (lien-based gating, Notary's Lamp), pseudolegal (the entire Writ/Order magic system), ambiguous (no fixed factions — allegiance is always situational), cooperation lens (Repossessor boss, Offal Exchange barter-combat), melancholy (Bedpost Hollow, purgatorial waiting villages).

## Concept B: *Slorpwake* (working title)

**Hook:** You're a soft-bodied scavenger on a mudflat where the tide left forever, and your only "weapon" is learning what to eat, what to spit back out, and who to share it with.

### Premise and world logic
The setting is a vast estuary that used to be underwater and now, through slow geology rather than any curse, never floods again — a landscape defined by absence, not catastrophe. You play a **Slorp**: a small, translucent, snail/nudibranch-like scavenger (explicitly not an insect) that survives by ingesting loose matter and re-extruding it as something else. The world is explorable metroidvania-style because it's criss-crossed by dry former tide-channels, and your body's own growing repertoire of ingest/extrude tricks is what opens new channels, biomes, and depths.

### Signature mechanic(s)
**Slorp** — you can ingest small terrain objects, dead matter, or parts offered by other creatures into an internal reservoir, then extrude them elsewhere as platforms, plugs, or armour, shaped by what you ate (mud = soft, sinks after a timer; wood = rigid bridge; shell = hard block or wearable plating). This is the core traversal verb.

The **cooperation lens is mechanically literal here**: many "enemies" are non-hostile scavengers competing for the same mudflat resources, not fixed antagonists. Certain gates can *only* open via **Joint Regurgitation** — you offer part of your reservoir to a passive creature, and if it accepts, you both extrude simultaneously to build a structure neither of you could make alone (it holds one end, you shape the other). You can instead fight and steal a creature's half-digested material by force, which works, but permanently sours that colony toward you — a persistent, sad consequence rather than a game-over.

### Ability list
1. **Slorp** (ingest) — absorb loose matter into your gut-reservoir; the core resource-gathering action.
2. **Extrude/Spit** — expel reservoir contents as a shaped platform, plug, or projectile, the physical form determined by what was ingested.
3. **Shellcoat** — permanently bond an ingested shell fragment to your back; unlocks wall-cling on rough vertical surfaces (heavier shells cling better).
4. **Joint Regurgitation** — a two-body cooperative action offered to passive creatures; a hard traversal gate that cannot be solved alone, mechanically enforcing the lens.
5. **Brine Sense** — expel a residue trail that dissolves to reveal what's buried under the mud (secrets/scent-based detection, not sound).
6. **Husk-Split** (dash/double-jump reskin) — briefly shed a lighter outer husk you can fling a short distance as a decoy or extra reach, then reabsorb it.
7. **Old Growth Recall** (the "preterient" ability) — at marked driftwood pilings, ingest a growth-ring sample to trigger a delayed echo: an action you take in the next area repeats itself back at the piling shortly after, letting one body solve a two-trigger puzzle.

### Biomes
1. **The Dry Channels** — the cracked mudflat hub; a "phantom tide" pulses through on a timer, briefly reflooding channels and forcing route changes.
2. **The Piling Fields** (bedpost, load-bearing) — a forest of giant drowned dock pilings, now dry, encrusted with ambiguous colony-creatures whose attitude toward you tracks your prior cooperation choices; vertical Shellcoat-climbing biome.
3. **The Slack Marsh** — reed marsh built almost entirely around Joint Regurgitation puzzles; the lens is most explicit here, with little combat.
4. **The Bonewrack Shoal** — a boneyard of large dead sea creatures where scavengers compete over carcasses; the clearest branch point between "share" (unlocks a trade hub) and "steal" (permanent local hostility).
5. **The Sounding Deep** — the one remaining pocket of standing water; the only real swimming/current-physics biome, requiring mastered Shellcoat and Brine Sense.
6. **The Roostwrack** — clifftop nesting biome at the world's edge; wind physics and Husk-Split gliding culminate in Old Growth Recall revealing the plain, unglamorous geological reason the tide never returned.

### Example encounter/boss
**The Shoal-Warden** (Bonewrack Shoal): a huge scavenger matriarch guarding a carcass field, with no visible health bar. The "fight" is a race to ingest shared carcass material faster than she does; hits knock loose more shared matter rather than dealing conventional damage. Pure aggression drives her off — you get the carcass, but her brood starves and the biome's future cooperative NPCs turn hostile. At a threshold you can instead stop attacking and attempt Joint Regurgitation mid-encounter, splitting the carcass fairly and gaining a permanent ally-piling (fast travel + hints) instead of loot.

### Why it's not generic
Closest relatives: Hollow Knight's absorb-and-spend resource loop, and Rain World's ecosystem-of-competitors-not-villains philosophy for the cooperation lens — but Rain World isn't an ability-gated metroidvania, and no metroidvania builds its traversal tree around literal ingest-and-reshape-terrain. Self-critique: "eating things to make platforms" risks feeling mechanically close to generic gloop/slime-builder games unless the ecological melancholy and the *mandatory* cooperation gating carry real weight; ingestion/regurgitation imagery also risks reading as gross rather than melancholy-beautiful without careful, ceramic/bioluminescent art direction.

### Greybox test
One room: a dry channel gap too wide to jump, loose mud and a floating log nearby, and a passive shelled creature across the gap holding a plank. See whether players work out Slorp-mud → Extrude a stepping platform → approach the creature → trigger Joint Regurgitation to get the plank laid as the rest of the bridge, versus trying to fight/shove past (which should visibly fail). An hour should show whether "offer, don't fight" reads intuitively, and whether ingest/extrude platforming (weight, timing, mud sink-timer) feels good even before cooperation enters.

### Risks
- Ingestion/regurgitation visuals are body-horror-adjacent and could undercut the "beautiful, Ori-adjacent" bar without careful art direction.
- Mandatory (non-optional) cooperation gates may frustrate players used to solving everything by fighting; needs unmistakable tells.
- Timed, sinking mud-platforms could feel unfair if mistuned against precision-platforming expectations.
- The steal-vs-share branching consequence system adds real content cost across every biome for a small team.

### Seeds used
Slorp (the core verb and creature name), bedpost (Piling Fields' driftwood pilings and growth-ring tallies), preterient (Old Growth Recall's delayed-echo puzzle), ambiguous (colony creatures with no fixed faction), giblets (echoed thematically in the Bonewrack Shoal's carcass-economy — organs/parts as a separately valued resource), cooperation lens (Joint Regurgitation as a literal, sometimes mandatory, mechanic; the Shoal-Warden encounter), melancholy (the tide that never returns, explained geologically rather than by catastrophe).
