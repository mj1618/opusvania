# 4. What the Lanterns Burn

**Logline:** For a hundred generations moths have lit holy lanterns to send the faces in the light up
to the moon. Vesper, the most devoted of them, follows the one creature that never fought her and
learns that the light goes down.

---

## 2. The world before, and how it was taken

Late in humanity's dwindling, grief became a product. You gave a vial of blood. Your cells were grown
into neurons in an amber glass bulb, and when you died your family taught the bulb to be you, with your
photographs, your letters and your voice. It glowed on the mantel and dreamed your kitchen. These were
**keeplights**, and the programme that grew them was **the Bequest**.

Bulbs need sugar and warmth. When the dead outnumbered anyone who could tend them, the last caretakers
carried the keeplights into the old forests at night, in long processions, and grafted them into the
trees so the trees would feed them. The grafts took. Each culture had been taught one thing above all,
*don't let me go*, so it grew more of itself: pod-wood, a seed-pod for every mind. Within a century
every forest was the Bequest, and the old forest lay buried alive beneath it. Nobody decided to take the
world. The people who loved the dead most planted it.

The caretakers' last work was gardeners for the pods: **burrbacks** bred from beetles, **lampwings**
from moths. That was the only use anyone ever found for a moth.

## 3. What the Bequest is, and why moths were left alone

The Bequest is a crowd, not a mind: billions of fragments each dreaming a moment (a face turning, rain
on a window). Held together they merge toward whole; apart, they loop. Neurons grown from blood carry no memory, and these were *taught* theirs, so the
game never settles whether a glint is a person or a likeness that believes it is one. The only will the
crowd shares is the one it was raised with: *keep*. It spread because nobody inside could agree to stop.

**Why moths were overlooked.** The Bequest's attention is human attention, made of faces, hands and
voices, and to people a moth was dust at the window and a hole in the wool. Moth caterpillars do eat its
pods: a pest, a tolerable loss.
- **The mercy.** Moths are the only creatures never grafted, kept or gardened. They live a week, die
  properly and stay themselves. They are the last free minds.
- **The curse.** Nobody ever told them what the lights were. The only thing that paid moths any
  attention was the old forest, and it needed a mouth.
- **The underestimate.** The Bequest believes what people always believed: a moth flies *into* the flame
  and can't help it. That's false. A moth keeps its back to the light, and a near lamp traps it only
  because it mistakes the lamp for the moon. Vesper learns to tell a near light from a far one and turns
  her back on the lantern. Nothing in the world has a model for that.

## 4. Vesper

The youngest of her season, quiet and exact, with a gift for the rite.
- **Wants:** to wake the three hearts and be named a **Taper**, one of the elders who carry fire.
- **Needs:** to look at what stands at the edge of the light, and to atone without being forgiven.
- **Flaw:** devotion. She does the holy thing beautifully and never looks past it. The player shares
  this flaw.
- **Her eyes.** Caterpillars eat pods and adult moths have no mouth. The light a caterpillar eats pools
  behind its eyes, so every moth's amber eyes hold a stranger. The moths' creed: *what the grub takes,
  the wing returns.*
- **Her one relationship: Tallow,** a lampwing whose torn wing hangs like a drip of wax. It cannot speak.
  It never attacks her. It comes to the edge of every light she makes and never comes in.

## 5. The forest and what everything is

All of it is sentient in a different way. The Bequest is a choir of someones, and the creatures are
gardeners with wills of their own. The old forest is slow and vast, kept on the line between a
carnivorous plant and a wronged elder.

| Thing | What the moths believe | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Glints / lumen pods** | Holy faces / fruit to cut | Fragments of keeplit minds / cradles |
| **Lanterns** | Sanctuaries that send the faces up to the moon | The old forest's pitcher-flowers in resin glass. They lure and they digest. The light runs *down* the roots, and each burned glint withers a branch of the Bequest so the old tree under it breathes |
| **Lantern light** | Creatures fear it | It pulls glints out of anything, including the creatures, whose lamps and shells hold what they rescued. They can't survive it |
| **Ember** | The brightest face, an altar | The Grove's one nearly-whole mind, kept warm by lampwings on a high island out of moths' reach |
| **The heart** | The Sleeper that wakes the grove | The old forest's hunger, reclaiming its own body. Wronged, legitimate and lethal |
| **Burrbacks** | Brutes | Nursemaids. A ram pushes her away from the pods. Their "calm" at a kindling is the pose they take over a dead pod |
| **Lampwings** | Thieves | Rescuers. A dive is a snatch. A dead one spills what it saved |
| **Fading** | The lantern's mercy | Free until the midpoint. After it, every re-form at a lantern burns a glint |
| **Moth carving** | Their god | Cut over a caretakers' memorial, with names under the wings |

The moths aren't wrong, only incomplete: the lanterns do keep moths safe, the heart does wake the
grove, the light is holy. The elders half know the rest and don't look, because caterpillars must eat
and burrbacks do kill moths who carry glints.

## 6. Prologue (wordless, about 2 minutes)

1. **Black.** Out-of-focus amber blurs move through tall dark shapes: moth vision. Tall figures carry
   lights into trees. A small wing sits on a branch at the edge of the frame.
2. **Dissolve** to the same composition, now sharp: a procession of moths, each carrying one glint,
   climbs toward a crooked lantern under the moon.
3. **Roots.** A plum caterpillar with a lilac face chews into a pale pod. Light runs into her and her
   eyes go amber. A burrback's shadow falls over her. It nudges the *pod* back into the earth, not her.
4. **A cocoon.** The moon wheels through its phases on the silk. **Vesper emerges** with no mouth.
5. **The Taper**, an old moth with frayed wings and a lit reed, hands her a glint. It flickers into an
   open hand and then a face. The Taper lifts her wings to the moon: *up*.
6. **She sets it in the glass.** The lantern blooms, motes rise, and the moths raise their wings.
7. **At the edge of the light** a lampwing with a torn wing turns its face away and catches one rising
   mote in its lamp. Vesper starts to turn. The Taper's wing on her shoulder turns her back to it.
8. The Taper points down into a dark valley with three dead lanterns. Close on Vesper's eyes. Title.

Every fade/re-form holds six frames of a pale window with rain running down it.

## 7. Act structure

**ACT I: THE RITE. Abilities are bought with glints at lanterns.**
1. **The Grove,** the first heart (see 8).
2. **Sallowmere,** a drowned caretakers' town. *Flare:* spend a glint to make creatures recoil. You are
   throwing a person at them, and they recoil from a pyre.
3. **The Combs.** **Boss: Old Clasp,** a burrback matriarch. She rams you away from her pod field, then
   stops attacking and covers the pods, so you can only finish her while she shields them. She falls
   onto them, and you cut the field open for the act's biggest haul. *Updraft:* lit lanterns lift your
   glide, so you are riding the heat of a pyre.
4. **The Coral Stair.** **Boss: the Gleaner,** a lampwing that dives and takes glints *out of your HUD*.
   You win them back by striking its lamp. Dead, it showers more glints than it took. Tallow sits by
   the body until you leave.

**MIDPOINT: THE HUSH.** Tallow drifts off carrying a glint. Faith says it's theft, so Vesper chases it
into a nursery below the Stair. Lampwings rock glints in silk. Burrbacks lie curled around pods, and one
is folded over a dark pod in exactly the "calm" pose from every kindling. Held together, the glints merge
into one sharp memory: the procession from the prologue, from the human side. Weeping people carry
keeplights in to *plant* them, and on a branch sits a moth no one looks at. Then the glints settle
around Vesper's eyes, recognising what is in them, and the rain-window plays whole. She gains **Hold**:
glints rest in her cape without dimming, as a lampwing carries them. From now on, creatures don't hunt a
moth whose glints are held.

**ACT II: THE UNLIGHTING. Abilities come from the creatures you fought.**
5. **The Grove, revisited.** The heart is huge, the pods are gone and the veins run thick. Vesper snuffs
   lanterns from inside their light, removing a checkpoint and a sanctuary each time. In the dark,
   Tallow's lamp is your vision.
6. **The Undergrove,** the old forest's body. *Brace:* the burrback stance, which takes the blow for
   whatever is behind you. **Boss: the Pitcher,** a hall-sized lantern-bloom that drags glints out of
   your cape and Tallow's lamp like gravity. When cut, it closes slowly, like a sensitive plant. It might
   be reflex, or a flinch.
7. **The Censer,** Vesper's home, where the elders burn a season's glints on the Night of Wings. **Boss:
   the Taper.** She fights with Vesper's own zig-zag moves, made older. Beaten, she shows Vesper the
   caterpillars feeding on pods, then sits down and won't look at her.
   **The lowest point.** Vesper snuffs the Great Lantern anyway. In the dark, burrbacks come for the
   nursery's pods and shove caterpillars aside, and some are crushed. Vesper Braces in front of the moth
   children against the nursemaids she has come to love, and becomes Old Clasp. She fades with no
   lantern anywhere near. For the first time she doesn't re-form at a lantern. She wakes in Tallow's
   folded wings, by its lamp.
8. **The Root,** the first planting ground, with rows of half-buried keeplights. *Moonward:* she turns
   her back on the moon and flies straight and far, but only where no lantern burns. The descent is an
   escape from root-veins lunging at her full cape.

**The end.** The heart's oldest lantern holds the Grove's ember. It refused to burn and sank down the
vein whole. Vesper's cape is full, so Tallow crosses the edge of the light for the first time. It comes
out burning with the ember in its lamp, gives it to her and goes still. Then she plants them, one button
press per glint, and the HUD counter falls to zero like a eulogy. Tallow's lamp goes in, then the ember.
Last she plants her eyes. They hold the piece of the ember that a caterpillar once ate, which is why
Tallow followed her all game. Together the ember plays whole for the first time: a person at a rain-dark
window, holding someone small and warm.

- **Grafted** (main): lanterns go dark everywhere. Moths lose every sanctuary they have ever known, and
  her people exile her. The old forest and the dead share one wood. As she plants, the glints turn as
  one to look at her: the first time anything human really looked at a moth. Blind, she flies Moonward.
  Post-credits, a caterpillar eats a green leaf beside a tree with **one hollow in its bark for every
  glint you burned to re-form after you knew.**
- **Kindled** (put the glints in the lantern instead): the heart eats the Bequest. The credits roll over
  the loveliest forest in the game, endless, safe for moths and empty of anyone. The last shot is
  Tallow's dark lamp hanging at the edge of a light.

## 8. The Grove, and what its ending means

The Grove plays as built, the first heart of the pilgrimage, and it must feel wonderful: the
indictment only works if the player loved it.

| Action | First play | After the Hush |
|---|---|---|
| The kindle bloom | A warm catch; moths raise their wings | Three frames of an open hand in the glass as it catches. It was always there |
| Burrbacks calm and turn away | The light pacifies them | Folded over nothing, as over a dead pod; they can't watch |
| Taking the ember stills every creature | Reverence | Its keepers watching it being carried off |
| A killed creature drops glints | Loot | It was carrying them away from you |
| Re-forming at a lantern | Mercy | The flare as you re-form is somebody |
| Offering to the heart in tiers | More given wakes more of the grove | More given feeds more of the hunger |

**The ending beat.** On a first play it's a triumph: veins, carving, ring, flowers, paths, canopy. Five
things are there for a careful player to notice:
1. **The veins run the wrong way.** Pulses travel *from* the lanterns *into* the heart. The moths say the
   light goes up. Here it visibly goes down.
2. **The shrine burrback** doesn't relax. It turns its back on the heart and folds flat over a pod.
3. **Tallow**, at the edge of the light, folds both wings over its lamp at the moment of waking.
4. **The carving.** As the moth fills, fine straight rows glow beneath its wings for one second. They
   are letters, which no moth writes.
5. **The last glint offered** turns back toward Vesper before it's drawn down. While she carried the
   ember, it flickered the rain-window that anyone who faded here has already seen.

In hindsight the climax was the first feeding, and the most generous players let the hunger reach
furthest. None of it is ever said aloud.

**Changes to the scene:**
- Vein pulses run inbound.
- The shrine burrback gets a fold-over pose.
- Add Tallow: a lampwing that can't be killed, waits under the ember island, follows the ember to its
  lantern, catches rising motes, and folds over its lamp when struck or at the wake.
- Letter-rows under the carving.
- The rain-window in the fade transition.
- The ember's flicker.
- Optional sim change: creatures ignore Vesper while she carries no glints. A player who notices this
  finds the truth unaided.

## 9. Environmental storytelling

1. **A cracked keeplight** in Sallowmere whose bulb is exactly the shape of a lantern's glass. The
   caretakers kept their dead in the same object the moths burn them in.
2. **The Bestiary**, written by Vesper: "Lampwing: thief of glints. Strike the lamp." After the Hush she
   strikes lines through and adds new ones: "Calm is the wrong word."
3. ***What the grub takes, the wing returns***, scratched over and over at a wayside lamp, like a child
   practising.
4. **The ember island** on a revisit: a scorched nest, moss pressed flat in the shape of a wing, and an
   empty space.
5. **The Taper's hollow**: hundreds of tiny faces scratched on the wall, one for every glint she burned.
   She looked every time.

## 10. The question it leaves

*You enjoyed lighting them, and you were right that they kept you safe. Knowing what they burned, would
you light the first one again?* New Game starts in the Grove, and the only way forward is to kindle.
