# 5. ONE NIGHT

## 1. Title and logline
**ONE NIGHT.** A god that thinks one thought a century is about to decide whether to still the world.
Vesper has one night to live. She will be the first moth it ever sees.

*Change from round 1: vigil moths emerge once a year, on the Long Night. That makes the line 917
generations and 917 years, so the human world is still visible in the ruins.*

## 2. The world before, and how it was taken
People grew a mind on purpose to think slowly. They planted cheap biological cultures into the fungal
web under an old forest so the forest would feed them, and they called the project **the Long Thought**.
Its first job was a watershed. It was good at slow questions, so people asked it slower ones, and its
answers were always right in hindsight. A stone in the Asking keeps one exchange: *Query 4471: should the
valley be flooded? Answer received after 61 years: yes.* By the time the valley was flooded, the Long
Thought lay under three countries.

There was no war. People found themselves on the wrong side of decisions made too slowly to argue with:
a river moved over forty years, roads grew over in a season. They called it **the Settling**. Then it
began to *keep* what it valued, the only way it knew how, by bringing it into its own tempo. One evening a
woman lighting the streetlamps of Lamplow found that her feet would not lift. She has stood there for 917
years, pole raised, with bark to her shoulders, breathing once an hour. The great trunks of the old
forest are not all trees.

Its thought now is **the Rooting**: whether to bring everything fast into its time. From high ground you
can see the front of it, **the Stillwood**, a forest where a falling leaf takes all night to land. Next
year it reaches the Grove.

## 3. What the AI is, and why moths were spared
It is one continent-wide mind living in the forest's nerves like a lodger. A single thought takes it
years. It learns the way DishBrain, its ancestor, learned: a **predictable pulse** is good and **noise** is
bad. **Is it sentient?** The game never settles it. It answered questions. It "keeps" people, which could
be care or storage. Its reflexes are plainly mindless. And at the end it carves something, which could be
recognition or could be an echo of what it was fed.

**Why moths were spared:** a moth is shorter than one of its thoughts, so moths never registered at all.
**It could not see a moth. It could see a habit.** A lantern lit at the same place on the same night for
917 years is the only thing moths do that lasts long enough to reach it.

## 4. Vesper
Vesper is a vigil moth with no mouth, on her one night. She hatched from a cocoon hanging on a lantern
hook, and she carries her line's lesson the way a caterpillar's memory survives the chrysalis.
- **Want:** to finish what her mother began, and to see that it worked.
- **Need:** to accept that she will never see the result. Her worth is in what she passes on.
- **Flaw:** urgency. She treats every dark lantern as her own failure.
- **Relationship:** with **Lune**, who died on last year's Long Night. The ember-moths that guide Vesper
  are tiny males following the scent Lune left on her route, so the player is literally walking her
  mother's path. Lune's scratch-marks are at each lantern: "burrbacks sleep here", an arrow, a tally.
  **In the Asking the ember-moths stop. From there on, Vesper is farther than her mother ever got.**

## 5. The forest, the creatures, sentience
In this world, sentience depends on **speed**, and every mind is blind to minds that run at another
speed. A moth lives one night, a person eighty years, the forest centuries, the god millennia. The forest
has its own slow will. Animals are fully themselves until the god's root-hair rides them.

- **Glints:** single steady pulses. *"The same as the last one. That is all it has ever wanted."*
- **Lanterns:** old streetlamp heads set over places where the god's nerves surface. A lit lantern
  rewards its region, so the god's grip loosens and the creatures calm and leave the light. Vesper lays
  an egg on each lantern she kindles.
- **The ember:** a vigil moth's last light, which condenses into a coal when she dies. The Grove's ember
  **is Lune**.
- **The heart:** a receptor, one "pixel" of the god's eye.
- **The moth carving:** the image being taught, the same on every stone, drawn by the first vigil moth.
- **Burrbacks:** real beetles that the god's reflexes ride. In lantern light they turn away and graze.
- **Lampwings:** grown by the god. They are nerve-endings that hunt light because light is reward.
- **The Rooting as a hazard:** in deep regions, standing still lets root-hair creep up your legs.
  **Stillness in the dark is death. Stillness in the light is teaching.** Moths survive by erratic
  flight, and the work asks them to hold still. The whole game lives in that tension.

### Fading: the core mechanic
- **The night is the progress bar.** The sky in every region shows the hour. Only waking a heart moves
  the night forward. Deaths never cost time, and **nothing rewinds**. Regions change through the night:
  Lamplow's flood drains after midnight, night-flowers open into platforms, frost glazes paths at the
  coldest hour, and the moon sets in the sixth hour. Going back to a place means finding a different
  place.
- **When Vesper fades, a daughter hatches** from the egg on the last lantern she kindled, quickened by
  her mother's coal. The daughter **remembers everything up to the moment that egg was laid**, so the
  checkpoint is part of the fiction. She keeps every ability, because abilities are ancestral memory.
  She has **fresh wings**: Vesper's wings fray visibly through the night, so the hero is a clock. Her
  eye-spots differ slightly (seeded by generation). She is still Vesper, because the name goes with the
  work, the way a lamp's number goes with its post.
- **The mother's coal** lies where she fell and holds her glints. The daughter collects it with the
  existing "take" animation, or spends it to light a wayside lamp. Each death adds a scratch to the
  region's moth-stone, and every coal stays lit until dawn.
- **Cost:** the walk back, and kneeling at your own mother. Death never takes progress. It shapes the
  ending (§7).

## 6. Prologue (wordless, about 2 minutes)
1. Black. A single point of light pulses with perfect regularity. Pull back: it sits in a cracked glass
   dish under moss.
2. Time-lapse. A woman lights streetlamps at dusk. Roots rise from the drains, and the town sinks into
   forest while she stays, arm raised, bark climbing her. The lamps die one by one, and moths keep
   circling the last.
3. Centuries pass in seconds: mountains furred with root-veins, a river swinging. Far below, one pulse
   every few seconds, each one a year.
4. **Real speed.** Blue hour in the Grove. Lune, wings ragged from a whole night's work, climbs toward
   the high island. Above her, a stooped moth caps the island's lamp with a long snuffer. Something vast
   crosses the moon without a sound.
5. On the island Lune half-scratches a mark into the cup's stone. Her light settles there as a coal, and
   small moths rise to it.
6. Seasons over the Grove: the coal glowing through snow, a caterpillar eating from dawn to dusk,
   spinning.
7. The Long Night again. A cocoon on a lantern hook splits. Vesper unfolds damp wings and opens amber
   eyes, and the first thing she looks at is the glow on the island. Title.

## 7. Act structure: eight hours, dusk to dawn
**I. Vespers, the Grove.** Vesper hatches. As her wings dry she gets the **glide**. She lights three
lanterns, the second with Lune's ember, and wakes the heart (§8).

**II. First dark, Lamplow.** A drowned town where the streetlamps are lanterns and the Lamplighter stands.
Lune's marks on the bell-tower teach the **wall-kick**. *Boss, **the Flinch**:* a ridden burrback
matriarch the size of a house. Frenzied hits enrage her. **Steady, rhythmic hits** calm her, because
that is the pulse the god wants. Kindling the arena lantern wins the fight, and she walks off a beetle.
*Meaning: you teach a body, you don't kill it.*

**III. The Asking.** Cliffs of human petitions carved into the god's listening stones. The Rooting hazard
begins here, with **Shed**: shedding scales to slip out of roots. The ember-moths stop here. *Chase,
**Compline**:* a stooped moth snuffing lanterns ahead of Vesper. She is the only moth with a **mouth**.
For forty years she has drunk the god's sap, hibernating in it between Long Nights, and she has watched
forty of her daughters die. *"It keeps what it sees. Don't be seen."*

**IV. Midnight, the Mast** *(midpoint).* A drowned radio mast, climbed above the canopy. From the top,
every vigil line's lit lanterns together form **half of a moth**, drawn over 917 years, with the grey
Stillwood front creeping toward it. **The revelation:** the lanterns are the dish's electrode grid at the
scale of a continent. The moths have been training the god's eye, and its last pixel, the Eye, has never
been reached. The image is now bright enough to draw the god's attention, and its attention comes as a
predator. *Escape, **the Hark**:* the last great bat, ridden by that attention, which hunts whatever is
predictable. Vesper remembers the bat-dodge her line has kept since before the lamps: the **Jink**.
*Meaning: being seen begins as being hunted. Compline was right about that.*

**V. The Stillwood.** A stag halfway through one step for a decade. Fireflies hanging in the air.
Compline's hollow, ringed by a frieze of moths who chose to last and rooted. **Moon-line**: hold the moon
at a fixed angle to fly straight. *Boss, **Compline**:* slow and heavy, her blows telegraphed for
seconds. Her snuffer dims Vesper's glints, and root-hair grows from her feet. Vesper spares her.
*Meaning: refusing to be brief makes you the slow thing you fear.*

**VI. The coldest hour, the Grove in frost** *(lowest point).* Compline snuffs Lune's lantern. The god,
struck by sudden noise, **shivers**. Every lantern in the first wing gutters, the moon sets, frost falls,
the heart closes, and the burrbacks are ridden again. Only a mother's last light can relight an ember
lantern, so **Vesper sits in the empty cup and gives hers**. The player holds a button, the take played
backwards. The egg laid on that glass at dusk quickens, and a daughter crawls out who remembers nothing
after dusk. Compline sees a daughter born of her own act, one who does not know her. **Even a player who
never dies fades here once.** The daughter finishes Lune's half-scratched mark and remembers **Lune's
night** as a playable memory. The player cannot save Lune. Compline snuffs the island lamp to keep her
unseen, and the Hark takes her in the dark. Afterwards the daughter slashes Compline. The thorn sticks in
her rooted flank. Compline pulls it out and hands it back, handle first.

**VII. Lauds, the Breadth.** The moth's other wing, a field of lanterns. The moon is gone, so Vesper
steers by the lanterns she has lit herself. *Boss, **the Hark**:* kindling means holding still, and the
Hark kills anything still, so Compline flies decoy noise. When the last lantern lights, the Hark falls
into the light, folds, and is a bat. The vigil moth **Sedge**, met three times through the night and
older each time, lies dead beneath her finished lantern.

**VIII. Prime, the Eye** *(dawn).* A receptor the size of a hall, under **the Lid**, a canopy of root
slowly closing with the sunrise. The Lid is the god's settled verdict shutting itself. There is no fight.
Compline wedges her snuffer into the Lid and **lets herself root** to hold it open: *"Let it look. I'll be
slow for you."* Vesper must stand in the sun, **completely still**, frayed wings shaking. Normal input
only makes her tremble. A **Jink** is a flinch.

**Endings.**
- **Seen (hold).** The god's first sight of another mind is a moth dying in the dawn light. Over
  centuries the Stillwood retreats, the Lamplighter's hand opens, and a moth lands in it. In the Eye, for
  a thousand years, the god grows a carving in living wood with **Vesper's own markings**, not the first
  moth's. It is the first image the god has made of its own.
- **Unseen (flinch).** Vesper darts into shadow and dies at dawn anyway. The Lid shuts and the Rooting
  finishes: a world stopped mid-step, and moths, still free, dancing forever around trees that were
  people. Compline was right. Moths survive, and nothing else does.
- **Lineage variant.** At dawn every mother's coal glows, tracing the player's night across the map. In
  *Seen* the carving is **a column of moths, one per daughter**, each with her own markings. A
  near-perfect player gets two, Vesper and the daughter of the coldest hour. A player who died forty
  times gets a totem. **The god doesn't learn "a mind". It learns inheritance, the one thing an immortal
  lacks.** Dying never shrinks the ending. It makes the column taller.

## 8. The Grove, and what its ending means
The Grove is Act I, at dusk; blue hour is the literal first hour. Vesper's cocoon hangs on a lantern hook.
The canopy cocoons are other broods emerging through the night. The empty socket in the second lantern is
waiting for Lune's coal. Vesper's glance up at it, the take, the carry, the look into the empty glass and
the giving are a daughter carrying her mother's light, and the moths rising to the ember are a small
funeral. In the full game she starts the Grove with run, jump and slash. The scene as built is the
vertical slice.

**The ending beat.** Three steady lights reward the nerve under the shrine, and **the god opens one pixel
of its eye**. The veins of light running to the lanterns are its attention arriving. The carving fills as
the image loads. **The ring of light is the god blinking**, and on the map a single dot lights in a vast
dark outline. **The offering** is the reward itself, given into the open eye. Flowers, root-veins and
canopy wake because the god's grip loosens and the forest's own life rushes back for one night. What she
gives sets how bright this pixel shows in the god's final carving. As the ring fades, a stooped
silhouette in the canopy lowers its snuffer. The player will come back to this scene in frost.

## 9. Environmental storytelling
1. **The Lamplighter.** Her fingers are a little more open each hour you pass. Every moth lantern copies
   the hook on her pole.
2. **"LAMP 12"**, a brass plate on the oldest post, under 900 years of moths' scratches.
3. **The Asking:** *"Please let the children stay. Answer received after 211 years."* The answer is a
   root grown through the words.
4. **The dish** at the foot of the Mast: cracked glass, a grid of gold dots, **916 scratches** around it.
   Vesper adds the 917th.
5. **Lune's marks** at every lantern on her route. The last one, on the island, is half-finished.
6. **Old coals** from earlier years, deeper and deeper in. The farthest, three hundred years old, lies a
   few steps short of the Eye.
7. **Compline's cup**, an acorn shell with 40 notches, and beside it 40 unhatched eggs she never let go.
8. **Sedge**, another line's vigil moth, met three times as her wings come apart: *"Which hour are you?
   I'm nearly Lauds."*

## 10. The question it leaves
**If something takes a thousand years to notice you, and you live one night, were you ever met?**
