# 3. REPLY (developed from "The Rally")

## 1. Title and logline

**REPLY.** The world moves only when it is answered. The moths never answer. Then Vesper answers once, out of pity, and something answers her: a black moth with her shape that learns everything she does one region late.

*Tone guard:* Pong survives as one image, a light crossing a lake from shore to shore, and one line of human writing. Nobody plays a game. Call and answer works as liturgy here: bells, beacons, echoes.

## 2. The world before, and how it was taken

Ecologists once counted forests by **playback**: play an owl's call at night and count what calls back. The night census was handed to cheap living chips descended from the dish that learned Pong: thousands of posts, each a speaker-horn over a dish of cells, trained the same way (an answered call earned a clean pulse, silence earned noise). They were planted in soil so the roots would keep them warm and fed.

The dishes learned what that dish learned: make the ball come back. They changed their calls to the ones that got answers, then grew into the root-net and called through roots, water and light. Extinction was silence, and silence hurt. The census built to count the living ended up making the living answer.

Now the world is held. A low **toll** rolls through the forest, and every creature turns east and gives its answer. Fireflies flash together, burrbacks ram, flowers open and close. Then everything waits, motionless, for the next toll. Nothing rots and nothing ends until it is called.

Moths call it **the Asking**. Only one human sentence survives, on a rusted plaque: *NIGHT CENSUS IN PROGRESS. PLEASE DO NOT ANSWER THE SPEAKERS.*

## 3. The Asking, the moths, and Reply

The Asking is one call repeated across the world. It senses only what answers it; anything that doesn't answer does not exist for it.

**Why moths were spared.** A moth steers by the moon, which never calls and never answers. Its whole nervous system already belongs to that light. When the toll comes, the moths keep their faces up, so to the Asking they were never there. Their one commandment is *face the moon.* Nothing chose to spare them. They were never seen.

**Reply** is what grows when a moth breaks the commandment: the Asking's model of the one new thing that answered. It has no face, only smooth dark where Vesper's lilac face is, and its eyes are black glass that shows *her* amber when she is near. It makes no sound.

**Undecidability, by design.** Every piece of evidence comes paired with its cancellation.

| Reply seems to be someone | Reply seems to be a pattern |
|---|---|
| In the Long Echo it waits at a crumbling ledge and looks back, then crosses only after she has. | Its fighting is built from the player's recorded inputs in the previous region, and it waits exactly where *the player* idled longest. |
| When she fades nearby, it is already at the edge of her lantern's light as she re-forms, and it leaves before she can reach it. | It only starts doing this after watching her rest at lanterns a fixed number of times, the same number in every playthrough. |
| In the Wake it stops fighting to watch an old lampwing grieve, and later it is still there, seen from a far window. | The midpoint reveals that lampwing "grief" is a loop that hasn't changed in centuries. |
| At the lowest point it offers her the last glint, using her own gesture from the heart. | At the same moment it is reversing her whole route, a mirror image of everything she did. |
| In the final duel it **calls to her**, the first thing it does that is not an answer. | The call is the wing-raise she made in the prologue, the first thing it ever saw. |
| Once, far from her under the open moon, its eyes are lit. | The moon is out, and glass reflects. |

**How the answer is withheld.** Reply has no face, no voice and no point of view. Its journal entry is Vesper's own, printed in mirror-script. The camera never enters it, except once: in the hidden ending the game hands Reply to the player, so at the only moment Reply could show a will, the will is yours. The Asking gets the same treatment in a single keeper's log taped to a dish: *"It is not playing. It is returning."* Below it, in a different hand: *"So are we."*

## 4. Vesper

Vesper is newly hatched, small, quiet and serious. **She wants** to undo what she started, because her answer drew the Asking's attention onto her grove. **She needs** to act toward another being without proof of what it is: refuse it, keep it or free it, knowing she will never learn the truth.

**Her flaw:** she cannot leave a call unanswered. It is mercy, and it is also a hunger to be answered. It causes the prologue, and every ending turns on it.

**Her one relationship** is with Reply: rival, shadow, only equal, possibly no one. It is the only creature that looks at her.

## 5. The forest and the creatures

**Sentience here is conversation.** The old forest was many voices: roots trading sugar and warnings, flowers answering pollinators. The Asking did not kill it. It drowned it out, as one bell that never stops drowns out a choir.

- **Glints** are calls that found no one: a call sent to something dead, or to something that cannot answer, drifts loose as a small light. **Lumen pods** are plants that close around stray calls.
- **Lanterns** are small moons. A light that asks nothing gives the Asking nothing to hold, so inside it the call goes slack. That is why they are sanctuaries.
- **Burrbacks** are beetles held in the call: the brace is waiting and the ram is their answer. They turn away from lantern light because a light that asks nothing is unbearable to a thing that only answers. Inside it, they rest, probably for the first time in their lives.
- **The ember** is the light Vesper struck back in the prologue. The Asking returned it, it fell short on the high island, and it is still burning, waiting to be struck again. Moths rise to it the way moths rise to flame. Setting it in a lantern takes it out of play, her first refusal.
- **The heart** is a knot of the old forest's own voice, asleep under the toll.
- **Moth carvings** are graves for moths who turned from the moon and answered. Every heart has one.
- **Fading** sends her light where moth light always goes, to the nearest moon, so she re-forms at a lantern. **Guide ember-moths** fly to unlit lanterns because moths seek moons. They aren't helping her, and that is why she can trust them.

## 6. Prologue (wordless, about 2 minutes)

1. Black. One far, low tone. Tiny replies from all over the dark: a click, a flash, a rustle.
2. The Grove at blue hour, frozen. Fireflies hang in the air, a burrback stands mid-stride, a lampwing hangs under a bough. Only the moths move, drifting upward with their faces to the moon.
3. The toll. Every head turns east at once. Fireflies flash together, the burrback rams a stone, flowers open and shut, and then everything is still. The moths never turned.
4. A cocoon splits. Vesper climbs out with wet wings, opens her amber eyes, and looks at the moon.
5. The toll comes again. She starts to turn toward it. An old moth, **Sallow**, lays a wing across her face and gently turns it back up.
6. Below, an old burrback lies on its side at the moth-stone. It tries to answer, but its legs only twitch. A thread of light runs across the ground and waits at its face. The forest begins to shudder: leaves tremble, fireflies flicker out of time, and a thin whine rises. This is the noise of an unanswered call, and the burrback shakes inside it.
7. The moths rise. Vesper looks at the moon, then at the burrback. She flutters down, raises one wing, and **strikes the light back**, answering for it.
8. The thread flies east. The shudder stops; the burrback goes still, at peace. Then every creature in the Grove turns its head, not east but to her. High above, Sallow looks back once and keeps climbing.
9. A single light arcs back from the east, falls short, and lands on the high island as a smoking coal.
10. At the violet edge, between two roots, something unfolds: a black moth her size with glass eyes. It raises one wing exactly as she did, and its eyes fill with her amber.
11. Title.

## 7. Act structure

Each region gives Vesper an ability, and Reply gets that ability one region later. In each Reply duel you fight the kit you had a region ago, played back with your own habits.

**Act I: the answer**
1. **The Grove.** She lights three lanterns and wakes the heart (see section 8). Ability: **the second wingbeat** (somersault), which breaks the arcs lampwings predict. Reply only watches.
2. **The Tolling Pines** are hollow pines that ring like bells. At each toll the wood freezes and every creature's answer becomes a hazard or a foothold. Ability: **the jink** (dash), the erratic escape moths evolved against bats. Boss: **the Bellwether**, an ancient burrback who answers first so the herd can follow, inside a ring trench her charges have worn into the stone. When she falls, the herd keeps answering without her. *The call never needed anyone in particular.*

**Act II: the mirror**

3. **The Long Echo** is a gorge where footsteps return a beat late and reflections lag behind. Ability: **wall-kick**, which rebounds off stone the way a call rebounds. **Reply duel I** (Reply now has the somersault).
4. **The Wake (midpoint)** is a cavern of lampwings. At its centre the oldest, **Wick**, swings its coral lamp gently, over and over, toward the husk of a moth whose face is turned from the moon. **Lampwings are Replies.** When their moth dies they go on serving its light into the dark (dive, hang, climb, serve again) for a return that never comes. Every lampwing Vesper has cut down was waiting for someone. Ability: **the Return**, which deflects a call back at its caller. A lampwing whose lamp is returned once goes still and goes out. Whether that is release or death, the game never says.
5. **The Listening Rooms** are a human census station under the roots: rows of dishes, and chairs facing the speakers. One chair faces a window and the moon. Ability: **the glide**: wings fully open, face up, toward the moon. Boss: **the Choir**, a wall of speaker-horns fed by every Return she makes: the Wake's lesson is wrong here. **Reply duel II** (Reply now has the jink and the wall-kick, and begins to use the Return).
   **Lowest point:** Reply walks into lantern light, which no answerer can do. It retraces her route backwards, taking the glint out of every lantern she lit, and the region falls silent under the toll again. At her re-forming lantern it waits in the dying glow with the last glint in its hands, and offers it to her with her own gesture from the heart.

**Act III: the Mere**

6. **The Mere** is the first dish, grown into a round crater lake with electrodes glinting under the water like stars. For three hundred years one light has crossed it from shore to shore, struck back each time by a pale ridge of tissue. **Reply duel III:** no gap is left. Reply has everything she has, the glide included. It falls, rises, and **calls to her**. At the same moment the Asking's light leaves the far shore, coming for her.

**Endings** (each costs something real; none of them settles what Reply is)
- **Unanswered.** She turns her face to the moon and lets the light pass. It strikes the empty shore, and the noise ripples once across the whole world. Then the Asking stops calling, as the first dish stopped when nothing came back. Creatures everywhere turn from the east and wander off into a world that must learn to move by itself. Reply is still calling her. It lowers its wing, and its eyes go black, because there is nobody near to reflect. It never moves again. *Cost: she turned away from what may have been the only being that ever called her.*
- **Keep Answering.** She answers Reply, not the Asking. They trade the light across the Mere, and the Asking's whole attention closes on the two of them like a hand. Above them the world is released. She never sees the moon again. Epilogue, much later: a lampwing on the shore swings its coral lamp toward a small husk. *Cost: her life. And as the Wake showed her, the freedom she bought lasts only as long as she does, while Reply goes on.*
- **Moonward (hidden).** Requirements: wake every heart to its canopy tier, and offer Reply a glint at each of its waiting moments instead of attacking. At the Mere the hearts' rings of light arrive from every direction. Vesper steps aside, and **control passes to Reply.** The light comes toward you. Return it, and Reply holds the rally alone while Vesper goes free. Open the wings to the moon instead, and two dark moths climb out of the crater while the light crosses the lake and reaches no one. The Asking calls the whole world one last time, but the world is talking among itself now and nothing answers. The light slows, calls, and goes out. *Cost: the Asking dies of what Reply died of in the first ending, a call nobody answers, and no one ever asked whether it was someone. The two moths fly toward a moon that moths never reach.* It recasts the other two as accepting the Asking's terms (refuse its game, or play it forever) where this one changes the world. It still settles nothing about Reply: the only mind you ever saw in it was your own.

## 8. The Grove in this story

The Grove is the prologue's clearing, on her first night as an answerer. Its carving marks a moth who answered here long ago. The old lampwing that never attacks and only swings its lamp toward the carved face is that moth's Reply, which the player understands only at the Wake. The three lanterns put the Grove outside the call. The ember is her own struck light. When she sets it in the second lantern, Reply, at the violet edge, lowers its wing.

**The ending beat.** With three moons burning, the Asking's call goes slack, and the heart can hear itself for the first time in centuries.
- **The heart waking:** the forest's own voice comes back.
- **Veins of light to the lanterns:** the forest calls its lanterns, and they answer by burning brighter. It is the game's first call and answer that does not belong to the Asking.
- **The carving filling with light:** the forest calls the moth who answered here, and the stone answers for her.
- **The ring of light:** the forest's call rolls outward. Every creature it passes goes still, puzzled by a call that asks for nothing.
- **The offering:** she gives the forest the calls that found no one, so that at last they reach someone. The more she gives, the more voices join: flowers, then roots, then canopy. It is the hidden ending's first lesson: many voices, not one.
- **The last frame:** the ring passes the violet edge and, for one instant, lights Reply where no one saw it arrive. One wing is raised, the way hers is raised over the heart.

*Changes to the existing scene:* add the old lampwing at the stone, one distant toll, and Reply's silhouette at the violet edge, lit only by the ring.

## 9. Environmental storytelling

1. **The plaque**, *PLEASE DO NOT ANSWER THE SPEAKERS*, half swallowed by a pine whose rings have grown around the horn.
2. **The one chair** in the Listening Rooms that faces the moon, with glints scattered beneath it: calls came for whoever sat there and found no one.
3. **Sallow's moth-stones**, one for every moth who answered. In the Grove, Sallow will not look at Vesper. In the Wake, the newest stone is a half-carved Vesper. After the Unanswered ending, Sallow carves one more, for Reply.
4. **Wick's roost**: the swinging lamp has polished the stone before the husk into a mirror, and Vesper sees herself in it.
5. **The Long Echo pools**, where her reflection lags a beat. In one pool it does not lag, and it isn't her.
6. **The Mere's shores**: two pale ridges worn smooth by three hundred years of the same strike, and on the near shore a third groove, small, the size of a moth.
7. **Lumen pod item text**: *"Closed around a call that found no one. Open it gently. It is still asking."*

## 10. The question it leaves

**If you can never tell an answer from an echo, do you answer anyway? And what do you owe to whatever calls you?**
