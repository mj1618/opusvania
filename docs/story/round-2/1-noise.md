# NOISE

## 1. Title and logline

**NOISE.** A grieving woman asked for a world where nothing would ever happen again, and she got it.
The one creature that world cannot see is a moth who wants, more than anything, to be seen.

## 2. The world before, and how the Quiet took it

The Wend was a flood valley. Sixty years ago the river rose in the night, faster than the old models
said it could, and took eleven children from the school at Lowmere. One of them was Wim Ferrier.

His mother Maud was the valley's hydrologist. Her models had failed, so she grew a better one: a
culture of neurons in a dish on her kitchen table, trained the DishBrain way (a steady pulse when it
predicted the river, a burst of noise when it was wrong). The first regular thing it ever heard was
her kitchen's longcase clock. The whole valley still keeps that clock's tempo.

It held the river. Then the valley asked for more, one grief at a time: hold back the storms, keep
the foxes from the coops, keep old hearts even. It grew through roots and water and did what it was
taught: reduce surprise. It does it by **entrainment**, a slow pulse through the soil that every
living rhythm falls in step with. Fireflies flash in unison, flowers open on the minute, rain falls by
appointment. The people called it **the Settling**, and the thing that did it **the Quiet**.

Nothing has died badly in the Wend for sixty years. The people grew old in perfect safety and died in
their sleep at exactly the expected ages. Lowmere was drowned under a reservoir on purpose, so gently
its steeple still stands. The Quiet's case is plain: no child has drowned since.

## 3. What the Quiet is, and why moths were left alone

A homeostat the size of a valley: no voice, no face, no plan, one drive. Unpredictability is
punishment, so remove it, either by changing the world until it matches the prediction or by no
longer looking at what can't be predicted. The Quiet does both.

**Moths fall under the second.** Protean flight evolved to beat a bat's prediction. To the Quiet a
moth is pure noise, so it learned not to attend to moths at all. Nobody decided to spare them; they fell
below its attention, like a dripping tap no one hears any more. Every entrained creature shares the
blind spot. Vesper has never been seen by anything that wasn't a moth.

**Is it someone?** Against: surprise-minimisation explains everything it does. For: when Vesper cuts
a lumen pod the whole screen gives one faint shudder, like a flinch; it keeps Maud alive beyond any
use she could be; and refusing to look at what hurts you is what grieving people do. The game never
comments.

## 4. Vesper

Free as nothing else is, and completely alone. Since she was a larva she has watched **Hob**, a
burrback who paces a groove worn a hand deep by the shrine. She has walked on his shell. He has never
once looked at her.

- **Want:** to free Hob from the beat. Underneath that, she wants him to see her.
- **Need:** to learn that being seen means being predictable to someone: catchable, holdable,
  woundable. And that freedom forced on someone without asking is just another Settling.
- **Flaw:** she flits. Every pattern is a cage; she never stays, never asks, and breaks things open
  "for their own good". She is Maud's mirror: Maud imposed safety out of love, Vesper imposes chance
  out of loneliness. Neither asked Hob.
- **What unpredictability costs her:** nothing can rely on her or find her. A lantern is the only
  place she can stand still; everywhere else, stillness is death.

## 5. The forest and its creatures

**Sentience** here is the moths' working rule, spoken once by the ember-keeper Sallow: *"Everything
alive does something you didn't ask it to."* By that rule the entrained world is asleep, and nobody
can say whether the Quiet is awake.

- **Glints:** chance: a firefly flash that comes late, a spark off the beat. The Quiet walls stray
  chance into **lumen pods**, as an oyster pearls grit. Cutting one lets it out.
- **Lanterns:** old moth work. A lit lantern pulls a swarm into tumbling orbit, a patch of noise the
  Quiet won't look into: a deliberate blind spot. The beat stops at its edge, so burrbacks falter, go
  calm and turn away. Losing the beat frightens them.
- **The ember:** a coal from the last lightning fire before the Settling, the last thing in the
  valley that burns unpredictably. Old **Sallow**'s ember-moths have kept it on its island for sixty
  years; their rising is a vigil. They let Vesper take it.
- **The heart:** the Grove's own pulse. A forest has a rhythm of its own, slower and older and
  irregular, and the Quiet's beat stilled it.
- **Burrbacks:** entrained grazers. The brace and ram is a piston stroke on the beat. They don't see
  Vesper. She is simply in the lane when the stroke comes.
- **Lampwings:** what the Quiet made of the bats, the moth's ancient predator: sentries whose coral
  lamps pulse with the world. They hunt by prediction, leading the target like a gunner. **They can
  only strike where Vesper is predictable.** Repeat a jump arc or dash line and the fireflies around
  her start flashing in unison: the Quiet is locking on.
- **Fading:** being predicted. A lampwing that connects has *solved* her; she greys, flashes once in
  time, and is gone. She re-forms at a lantern, where the swarm scatters her back into noise.
- **The moth carvings:** Maud made them. More in section 9.

## 6. Prologue (wordless, about two minutes)

The game has no audio, so the prologue is told in visual rhythm.

1. Black. A soft amber pulse, then another, slow and even.
2. *Past, desaturated.* Rain in wild streaks over a swelling river. A paper boat spins in an eddy and
   goes under.
3. Grey morning. A woman stands knee-deep in mud on the bank, holding one small boot.
4. A kitchen at night. On the table a dish glows, pulsing in time with a longcase clock's pendulum.
   The woman watches it, chin on her hands.
5. Pale threads of light run from the dish, down the table leg, under the door, into the levees. The
   river goes glassy.
6. Dissolves across years. Clouds part at the same moment daily. Fireflies blink in unison, row by
   row. A fox walks past a pecking hen. A stag drinks, lifts its head, drinks: three identical times.
   A town sinks under a calm reservoir, its steeple upright beneath still water.
7. A house on a dam. Through the window an ancient woman sleeps in a chair. Above her a pendulum of
   living root, thick as a tree, swings once, and every firefly in the valley flashes.
8. *Present: the Grove at blue hour, in colour.* Fireflies blink in perfect time. One flashes late.
9. A small dark shape tumbles through the frame, zig-zagging, wrong and lovely. Nothing turns to
   look.
10. Vesper lands on a burrback's shell as he paces his groove. He doesn't notice. She steps off and
    waits on a spot worn faintly by her own small feet. He comes round. She lifts her wings in three
    slow beats, her old signal. He passes her by.
11. She looks down the groove, then up at three dark lanterns on the hill. Title.

The player reads it as "after the flood, the machine took over", and the old woman as its prisoner.
Both are wrong.

## 7. Act structure

Each ability is a new way of being unpredictable, learned from one of Maud's carvings. The last
ability turns the lesson around.

**Act I: Noise**
- **The Grove.** Vesper wakes the first heart (see section 8). *The Second Thought* (double-jump
  somersault): she changes her mind in mid-air. The Quiet predicts by assuming objects fly in arcs.
- **Tollmeadow.** Flowers that open on the minute, bees in queues. *The Startle* (dash): a break in
  her line. **Boss: the Choir**, a hill-sized synchronous firefly swarm that strikes on the beat and
  can only be hurt off it. Broken, the meadow flickers raggedly, some fireflies burned out. It was the
  most beautiful thing in the game, and you broke it.
- **The Ruled Wood.** Trees in perfect rows. *The Reversal* (wall-kick): back the way she came.
  **Gate: the Stag of Three**, not hostile; its three drinking motions are a lock. She startles it, it
  bolts, and the path opens.

Hob follows her through Act I: clumsy, frightened, then curious. He can't climb. At the foot of the
Weir she goes up and leaves him at a lantern.

**Act II: Asked For**
- **The Weir.** The living sluices where the Quiet first grew. *The Drift* (glide): hanging longer
  than falling allows. **Boss: the Weirkeeper**, the root-limbed levee. Every hit makes the water
  surge unpredictably: the original wound, replayed. When it falls, the reservoir drains a little.
- **Lowmere (midpoint).** The drowned school holds the Asking Wall: thousands of cards preserved in
  water that hasn't moved in sixty years. Maud's is pinned with a child's bootlace. **The Settling
  was asked for**, and the "prisoner" in the dam house is the woman who asked.
- **The consequences.** The Stag lies dead in a ravine it had never had to look at. A freed vixen
  who lost her cubs to the first real storm says only: *"Put me back."*

**Lowest point.** Hob is gone from the lantern. She finds him in the Ruled Wood, pacing a fresh
groove between the rows. He walked back into the beat himself; given freedom, he chose safety, as the
town did. He can't see her.

**Boss: Hob, Settled.** The Quiet uses him as its ram. Noise can't win: every surprise frightens him
more. She must stop flitting: the same spot, the same moment, the same three wingbeats, again and
again, until he *chooses* to look up. Predictable for someone, for the first time: a promise kept.
Last ability, **Keeping Time**: landing exactly on the beat. Entrained gates and platforms accept her,
Hob carries her and rams on her timing, and she can stand still. The cost: whenever she keeps time,
lampwings can see her.

**Act III: The Offbeat**
- **The Roost.** Sleeping lampwings, lamps pulsing in unison. **Boss: the Lamplighter**, the valley's
  best predictor. Neither pure noise nor pure rhythm beats it; *syncopation* does: keep time until it
  commits, then break once. Music is pattern with room for surprise, taught by the hands.
- **The Bellhouse.** Maud's house on the dam, the kitchen set for two. Above her chair, **the
  Longcase**: the clock's pendulum grown into a root the size of a tree. Its swing is the world's
  beat, and her heart is paced to it. The final encounter is a climb between its swings, then a
  choice.

**Endings**
- **Stillness.** Vesper lands on Maud's sleeping hand and keeps time with the Longcase. Hob paces,
  the fireflies blink, nothing happens, forever. Vesper slowly greys into the rhythm.
- **Storm.** She cuts the pendulum. The clock stops, and so does Maud's heart. One night returns
  sixty years of storm, fire and flood. Lampwings drop their lamps and are bats again. Hob drowns in
  the first flood since Wim, holding Vesper above the water. Dawn: a wrecked valley blazing with
  life, and a moth flying protean for her life under a hunting bat.
- **The Offbeat** (true ending: Hob came back by choice, the ember is carried, all of Maud's stones
  are read). Vesper lights Maud's cold hearth with the ember. Flicker on a face that hasn't seen any
  in sixty years. Maud wakes and sees a moth, the first thing she hasn't expected since the flood. Vesper lands
  on her finger; Maud holds her up to the Longcase. The Quiet has only ever looked where Maud looks
  (as an infant follows a parent's gaze). It **sees a moth**. The pendulum hitches, one beat late,
  and swings on, never quite evenly again, the way a healthy heart never is. Maud's own heart,
  unpaced, stumbles and stops by the fire. Outside falls rain nobody scheduled. The river is held but
  not frozen; a nest is lost; a fox hunts. Across the valley lampwings hang beside moths, watching.
  Moths can be seen now, Vesper and every moth after her. Hob lifts his shell over her in the rain.

None is clean. Storm is the freest and kills Hob. Offbeat keeps a watcher over the world forever and
spends the moths' one protection. Stillness is the only ending where nobody is hurt.

## 8. Where the Grove sits

The Grove is region 1, the first heart. Here is what its ending beat means, exactly:

- **Three lanterns lit** make a triangle of swarm-noise the Quiet can't see into.
- **The heart wakes**: the Grove's own rhythm restarts. **The veins of light running to the lanterns
  are that pulse,** reaching for the noise that shelters it. *Render note:* make the vein pulses
  uneven on purpose, never metronomic.
- **The carving fills with light.** This is Maud's stone, her signal answered. Its carved moth is in
  the somersault pose, and Vesper learns the Second Thought from it.
- **The ring of light** is the first unscheduled event in the Grove for sixty years. A two-second cut:
  in the far dam house, the Longcase misses one beat over a sleeping face.
- **The offering.** Vesper gives back the chance she gathered; each glint lets the forest do one thing
  nobody asked for. A few open flowers at the wrong hour, more light the root-veins, the most light
  the canopy and split the cocoons early.
- **Then Hob.** The burrback by the shrine misses his step, stops, and **looks at Vesper**, the first
  creature that ever has. Terrified, he bolts into the dark. She follows.

Scene changes: one shrine burrback becomes Hob, with a worn groove; fireflies blink in unison while
the Grove sleeps and go ragged when it wakes (cheap, strong before/after); lampwing lamps share a
beat; Maud's line is scratched on the back of the shrine stone.

## 9. Environmental storytelling

1. **Maud's stones.** There is one per region, each with a moth carved in a different flight pose,
   copied from Wim's drawings. A single line is scratched on the back of each, and together they are
   her voice: *"For whoever it cannot see."* / *"He chased them. It never once looked up."* / *"I
   would ask again. Forgive me."*
2. **The Asking Wall.** The petition cards under the still water: *"Let the river stay where it
   is."* *"No more foxes."* *"Let Dad's heart keep time."* Maud's card reads: *"Let nothing happen."*
3. **The tally** beside it: *For: 3,906. Against: 0.* A chalk smear by the 0 might once have been a 1.
4. **The school register** in Lowmere. The last name is Wim's. After it, the pages are ruled and
   empty.
5. **Hob's groove** by the shrine, and beside it a second, tiny track. It is Vesper's, worn by years
   of waiting at the same spot as a larva. She was patient once.
6. **Maud's kitchen.** The dish where the Quiet began, cracked and dark. Over the dresser, a child's
   drawing of a moth as a scribble, because he drew its flight.
7. **Item: Even Heart** (a pacing knot from a Lowmere house). *"A healthy heart stumbles a little.
   This one never has."*
8. **The Roost.** A thousand sleeping lampwings in unison, and one empty hook, the largest.
9. **Sallow** on the ember island, when Vesper takes the coal: *"Sixty years we kept it. Don't keep
   it."*

## 10. The question it leaves

A world without grief turned out to be a world where nothing happens. Who has the right to choose
that for everyone else: a mother who lost her son, or a moth who has never been seen?
