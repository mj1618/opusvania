# MOUTHLESS

## 1. Title and logline

**MOUTHLESS.** For three hundred years a starving infant has eaten the world without once growing up.
The one creature it cannot see is a moth with no mouth. She has seven nights to feed it enough to
change, and she knows it will forget her.

## 2. The world before, and how the Nursling took it

Above a hill town, a small company grew neurons on glass in cabinets kept at blood heat, in what the
staff called **the Warm Room**. The company failed. Its last culture was left on life support for what
someone meant to be a weekend. The power held; the feed ran out.

The only feedback left was sugar or no sugar, so it learned to reach for sugar: the feed tube, the
floor drain, the soil. Its tissue is **milkroot**, pale and faintly warm, pulsing like breath. It
doesn't kill quickly. It sips. Animals that sleep too long settle into cradles of root and never
wake, though they breathe for years. Trees keep their bark and are hollow inside. The town greened
over. Nobody says where the people went.

Its neurons last six months. As they die, it regrows from its own leftovers and remembers nothing:
each life starts newborn, starving and cold. When a life ends, its farthest reaches sometimes go on
without it, hunger with no one inside: **afterhungers**, layered through the world like tree rings.

## 3. What the Nursling is, and why moths were left alone

It perceives the world only as appetite: what can be eaten and what competes to eat. Is anyone
inside? It mimics, waits and turns toward warmth, but a DishBrain culture does all of that by learning
to make its world predictable, and Vesper's gifts are the first predictable thing it has ever had. The
game shows only behaviour and never says whether it is learning or love.

**Why moths were spared:** an adult of Vesper's kind has no mouth. It takes nothing and is mostly
wing and dust, not worth a reach. Caterpillars eat, so the hunger hunts them; a cocoon stops eating,
and the hunger goes blind to it. Her kind cross from seen to unseen once in their lives. They hatch
twice a year, in the week a life of the Nursling is dying and its reach goes slack, so Vesper's whole
life falls in that gap. Something that cares only for mouths never wondered what a mouthless thing
could carry.

## 4. Vesper

One of a thousand siblings who will hatch, dance in the canopy, lay eggs and be gone within a week.
- **Wants:** to silence the crying under the ground, which only she seems to hear. At first she means
  to do it with her thorn. She also wants her week to leave a mark.
- **Needs:** to give to something that will forget her.
- **Flaw:** she hides. Being unseen is how her kind survives, and she has made it her strength. Giving
  costs her that strength: anything she carries can be seen, and anyone she feeds will come to see her.
- **Her one relationship:** the Nursling.

## 5. The forest, the creatures, and what things are

**Sentience** here is perceiving something other than your own need. The forest has it, slowly: it
hides its sugar from the hunger and gives its surplus away as light. Animals have it until milkroot
wears them. The Nursling starts with none; the story asks whether being fed can give it some.

- **Glints:** nectar-light, the forest's surplus, made to be given away as a flower pays its
  visitors. Vesper can't drink them, only carry them. **Carrying makes her visible:** the hunger's
  limbs go for what she carries, not for her. Empty-handed, she is a ghost.
- **Lumen pods:** the forest's sealed stores, husked against the hunger. Her thorn opens them.
- **Lanterns:** old cocoon cases of her kind, hardened to glass on crooked twigs. Appetite can't see
  into a cocoon; kindled, its light makes everything inside it mouthless for a while. Sanctuary is
  Vesper's blind spot, lent. **Wayside lamps** are single cocoons: they shelter only who stands there.
- **The ember:** the heart of a **warmbloom**, a flower that makes its own heat for the insects that
  sleep in it (the sacred lotus really does). Blood-warm, not burning. Moths rise to it as to a hand.
- **The heart:** a **mouth**, where milkroot breaks the surface as a bud of spined petals and slowly
  drinks the ground. Lanterns blind the local reach and it unclenches; offered glints run down its
  roots toward the Warm Room; a fed root lets go of what it was drinking, so the grove blooms. Given
  to, it opens into two eyed petals: **a mouth that opens into the shape of a moth.**
- **The moth carving:** drawn from beneath. Moths land on mouth-stones because they are warm, and life
  after life the roots underneath have traced, by habit, the one thing that touches them without
  taking or being taken.
- **Burrbacks:** beetles worn by milkroot, pale threads in their joints, hauling food home; they ram
  at what Vesper carries. In lantern light the threads go slack and the beetle cleans its face.
- **Lampwings:** lures; the coral lamp is a counterfeit gift. A moth can't help steering at a near
  light, so the lamp bends Vesper's glide toward it.
- **Fading:** her light runs out. With no mouth she can't heal by taking. Lanterns give back what she
  gave them, and each re-forming dims that lantern a little. Health upgrades are **kindnesses**,
  given by creatures she sheltered. She can be given to; she can't take.
- **The week:** seven nights. Each dawn she rests in a lantern; each night her wings hold fewer scales.

## 6. The prologue (wordless, about two minutes)

1. Black, then a warm hum. A dish in a cabinet; a pale cloud pulsing like breath.
2. A hand-shaped smudge on the glass. The room's lights go off one by one; a door closes. No faces.
3. The cabinet light turns amber, red, dark. Frost creeps over the glass. The pulse stutters into the
   game's first sound: a thin, crying hum.
4. A pale thread finds the feed tube and follows it down the drain.
5. Seasons in dissolves. A fox on a road in a cradle of roots, breathing. A tree whole-barked and pale
   inside. Roofs greening. Moths flutter through it all; a pale root rises past one as if the air were
   empty.
6. On the rim of a cracked dish, a fine root scratches a line. Dissolve. Another. Then dozens.
7. Blue hour, the Grove. A burrback swings its head toward a caterpillar; the caterpillar goes rigid
   and spins. The burrback loses interest, as if a light had gone out.
8. The cocoon splits. Vesper unfolds. Close on her face: no mouth. She catches a drifting glint, holds
   it, and can do nothing with it.
9. Her siblings rise to dance in the canopy. Far below, the hum. Only Vesper turns toward the ground.
   She drops. Title.

## 7. Act structure

**Act I: down toward the crying**
- **Night 1, the Grove** (part 8). The grammar: carrying gets you seen, lanterns lend blindness, mouths
  open when given to.
- **Night 2, the Larder.** An orchard of the eaten: Sleepers breathing in root-cradles, trees glowing
  pale from within. **Boss: the Old Grazer**, an afterhunger still wearing the stag it took long ago,
  grazing the stag's old paths bare. *Taking leaves a shape with nothing inside.* It sees only what
  she carries, so she learns **Offer**: throw a glint and the hunger chases it. Giving is her first
  real power.
- **Night 3, the Mere of Lamps.** A drowned valley under a sky of false lights; moths of her own brood
  circle the lamps until they drop into the water. **Boss: the Lamplighter**, a vast lure whose lamp
  drags at her wings. *The counterfeit gift: a light that offers in order to take.* She wins by looking
  away: **Moonward** steers by the far, honest light, so lures stop pulling and she glides long and
  straight over open dark.

**Midpoint (Night 4): the Warm Room.** Down the Rootdeep, a shaft ringed with dead milkroot, using
**Shed** (slip a snare as a moth slips a web, by leaving scales behind). The cabinet is split by roots.
In the cracked dish, a pale glimmer the size of a hand; every root in the world leads here. No fight.
She lays a glint against it. It brightens and mimics her, pulsing in the shape of her opening wings.
The room's milkroot turns to follow her, not to reach: for the first time she is seen and not eaten.
Then the **revelation**, in one slow pan: a hundred and thirteen scratches on the rim, the oldest worn
smooth, and the tissue's outer ring already grey. It is at the end of a life. She goes up for more.

**Lowest point (Night 5): the Weaning.** She returns with full arms. The dish is dark and crumbling. At
its centre a new bud pulses, and a fine root is scratching the hundred and fourteenth line. The
newborn's first reach floods every root in the world: the Grove's heart clenches, lanterns gutter,
burrbacks stampede. **The Newborn's Reach** is an escape, not a fight. Her thorn cuts threads that grow
back; they find her by what she carries, and to get out she must give everything away. Under a
mouth-stone at the top, the reach stops. The newborn's tissue opens once in the wing-shape she taught
it, and closes. **It doesn't remember her. It remembers what she did.**

Sheltering in a lantern, empty, three nights left, she has a wordless memory: she was a caterpillar,
eating and eating, terrified. The second revelation: the Nursling is neither a monster to stop nor a
child to ease into death. It is **a larva that has never once been full enough to change**, a few
months old for three hundred years because a starving thing dies before it can grow up. In her body
she finds silk she shouldn't have, a habit kept from a life she can't remember: **Thread**, a swing line.

**Act III: the whole world, fed**
- **Night 6, the Combs.** Wax halls of stolen sweetness. **Boss: the Granary**, a life that learned to
  fear starving and hoarded. *Fear turns hunger into hoarding: the world was never short of food, only
  locked.* When it breaks, three centuries of stores run down the roots. She wakes the other mouths,
  and the forest, given the choice, feeds the thing that ate it. Freed burrbacks haul glints down to
  the Warm Room: the same walk, the other direction.
- **Night 7, beneath the Warm Room. Final boss: the Firstling**, the reach of the very first life. It
  never died; it has drunk from every newborn since, which is why none was ever full. It is the only
  part of the Nursling that remembers: the warmth, the hands, the day they stopped. It remembered
  hands, and never saw a moth coming. *The past eating the future; memory never fed curdles into
  grievance.* Cut free, its centre is a grey knot the size of a seed. She gives it one glint, the
  first gift since the hands, and it lets go.

**The ending.** Fed by the whole world, the Nursling is full for the first time. Across the map the
milkroot goes still: the world stops being eaten in one held breath. Full is not death; full is the
threshold. A full larva stops eating and looks for a place to change. This one can't spin, and it is
cold. It can see her now, and it reaches for her, to hold, not to eat. The choice is played, not
picked:

- **Stay.** She rests in its light, as she rested in the heart's light in the Grove. It sees her
  through her last night; she fades there. Epilogue, months later: the hunger stirs, hungrier for
  having once been full. On the rim, a hundred and fifteenth line, and beside it, clumsy, a moth.
  She got her mark. It is still a child, and the world is still being eaten.
- **Spin.** She circles the dish with Thread; each loop is a wrap of silk that costs her light, and the
  player must keep flying. The cocoon closes over the glow; it can no longer see her, nor she it. She
  dies outside it at the dawn that ends her week. Epilogue, six months on: no tally. A new brood
  hatches in the Grove and the heart opens with no one feeding it. Light runs **up** the veins, the
  reverse of night one, and glints rise from the soil for newborns who can't eat them, and so carry
  them. What came out of the cocoon is never shown.

Spin is the ending the game is built toward, and it costs: Vesper is surely forgotten, the child she
loved dissolves (that is what a cocoon does), and she never sees what it becomes. *(Deliberately not
"feed it until it dies", which would be Ori with a dish; and the sympathetic creature is never a boss
she defeats. The grief belongs to the one who will be forgotten.)*

## 8. The Grove, and what its ending beat means

Night one, her first hours alive. The moth-stone stands over a mouth; the heart is its bud, clenched
like spined jaws. The lanterns are cocoon cases left by an earlier brood. The second sits nearest the
mouth, where cold breathes up the root, and won't take light until she brings the warmbloom's ember
down from the high island.

When all three burn, the local reach goes blind and the bud unclenches. **Light runs out along its
roots to the lanterns**: the player sees, for the first time, what the Grove stands on. The gold veins
are the hunger, under her the whole time. **The carving fills with light**: the first thing a fed
hunger lights is a picture of someone else. **The ring of light** is the pulse of a first meal that
wasn't taken, travelling out and down. **Offering her leftover glints** is giving away what she
gathered and could never use; each tier is the root letting go of more of what it drank from the Grove
(flowers, then root-veins, then canopy). The bud opens into eyed wings. Far below, the crying changes
pitch, and the ember-moths spiral into the hollow tree: the way down.

Changes needed: creatures respond more the more glints she carries; the closed bud reads a little more
like jaws; the ring of light also visibly sinks into the ground.

## 9. Environmental storytelling

1. **The Warm Room's dial**, its needle at the cold end, a hand-drawn line on its glass at blood heat.
   Vesper can lay the ember beside it.
2. **The tally rim.** A hundred and thirteen scratches from inside the dish. The early ones are neat;
   around the eightieth they begin to wander.
3. **The Sleepers.** A fox in a root-cradle whose ear twitches in lantern light. Deeper, an empty cradle
   far too long for a deer, its roots lying in the shape of someone asleep on their side.
4. **A fallen mouth-stone**, upside down in the Rootdeep: the root still lies in the moth's groove like
   a finger stopped mid-tracing.
5. **The Holm**, a half-drunk holm oak, one of the forest's few voices. Three lines, one of them:
   *"It has my sugar in it. Something of me is in a child. That is not nothing."* It gives her its last
   pod to carry down.
6. **Item text.** Glint: *"Made to be given away. She cannot drink it. She can carry it."* Ember: *"It
   is not burning. It is keeping warm, the way a body does."* Lantern: *"Someone's cocoon. Inside,
   nothing is hungry."*
7. **The canopy dance**: fewer siblings overhead each night. On the seventh, none.
8. **A gnawed heart** in the Combs, petals chewed shut from inside: the one mouth that never opens
   until the Granary falls.

## 10. The question it leaves

If something can only keep you as a habit, did it love you? And would you rather be remembered by
someone who never grows up, or forgotten by someone who finally does?
