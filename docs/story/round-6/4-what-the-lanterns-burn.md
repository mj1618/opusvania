# 4. What the Lanterns Burn

## 1. Spine

**Logline:** A young moth lights holy lanterns beside the mentor she adores, and learns what is burning
inside them.

**The spine, as a player would tell a friend:**
1. Vesper is a young moth on a pilgrimage with her mentor, the Taper, lighting the lanterns that keep
   moths safe in a forest full of monsters.
2. She finds out that the little lights she feeds the lanterns are what is left of people, and that the
   monsters were trying to save them, and that the Taper has known all along.
3. The Taper burns them because it keeps the moths alive, Vesper included, so Vesper has to choose
   between the person who raised her and strangers she has never met.

**The central question:** *Would you burn a stranger to keep someone you love?*

## 2. The world before, and how it was taken

Late in humanity's dwindling, grief became a product. You gave a vial of blood, your cells were grown
into neurons in an amber glass bulb, and when you died your family taught the bulb to be you, with your
photographs, letters and voice. When nobody was left to tend the bulbs, the last people carried them
into the old forests at night and grafted them into the trees so the trees would feed them. Each one had
been taught a single wish, *don't let me go*, so they grew: pale pods on every branch, each holding a
**glint**, a small light that dreams one moment over and over. Within a century the forest was the
**Bequest**, and the old forest lay buried alive beneath it. Nobody decided to take the world. The people
who loved the dead most planted it.

No one alive remembers any of this. The world shows it through one place, the **Root**: rows of
half-buried glass bulbs under the oldest trees, exactly the shape of a lantern's glass.

## 3. The forest-mind, and why moths don't count

The Bequest is a crowd, not a mind. Its keepers are the **burrbacks**, who guard the pods, and the
**lampwings**, who gather up any glint that gets loose. They see only one thing: *who is carrying our
glints?* An empty moth is nothing to them, and from the first minute of the Grove you can walk an empty
Vesper straight past a burrback without it lifting its head. A moth carrying glints is a thief, and the
keepers believe what everyone believes about moths: that she will fly into the light and can't help it.
Lantern light really does pull at Vesper's glide. Their mistake comes late, and it is played: Vesper
learns to fly straight with her back to the light.

Beneath the Bequest the **old forest** is slow and vast and hungry to have its body back. Lanterns are
its lures. They drink what burns and send the light *down* the roots to the heart, and where they drink,
the Bequest withers and the old wood breathes. It isn't evil, and it isn't kind.

Are glints people, or echoes of people? Each one flickers the same small gesture every time, like a
loop. The Taper believes they are echoes, and the keepers treat them as children. The game never says
who is right.

## 4. The cast

| | Wants | Wants from Vesper | How they pull on her |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vesper** | To be worthy of the Taper | | |
| **The Taper**, an old moth with silk-mended wings and a lit reed | To keep the moth children alive, and to pass her reed to Vesper | Keep your eyes on the glass | Love. She is the only parent Vesper has known |
| **Sedge**, a moth Vesper's age, clumsy and funny | To live, and to keep Vesper near | Come home. Later: let me live | Friendship, then a betrayal she understands |
| **Tallow**, a lampwing with a torn wing | To get one glint back into the earth | Give it back | It never hurts her, and keeps returning to the edge of her light |
| **Clasp**, a burrback mother | Her pods safe | Stop cutting them | First a threat, then a mirror |

Moths live a week. Pilgrims are the exception: when they fall they re-form at a lantern, and **each
re-form burns one glint in its glass.** The Taper has re-formed for a hundred generations.

## 5. What everything is

| Thing | First play | The truth |
|---|---|---|
| **Glints / pods** | Treasure / fruit to cut | Someone, or the echo of someone / a cradle |
| **Lanterns** | Sanctuary, rest, beauty | A lure with someone burning in it |
| **Burrbacks** | Brutes; light "calms" them | Nursemaids; the "calm" is the pose they take over a dead pod |
| **Lampwings** | Thieves | Rescuers. A dive is a snatch |
| **The heart** | The rite's reward; the grove wakes | The old forest drinking |
| **Re-forming** | The lantern's mercy | One glint, every time |

**Abilities carry the turn.** In Act I they are bought with glints at lanterns. *Flare* spends a glint so
that creatures recoil: you are throwing someone at them, and they recoil from a fire. *Updraft* lets lit
lanterns lift your glide, so you ride the heat. In Act II they come from the creatures you fought:
*Brace*, the burrback stance that takes the blow for whatever is behind you; *Plant*; and finally
*Moonward*.

The moths' faith carries weight. Glints are holy, they must be carried by hand, and moth children need
sanctuary, because grubs feed on pods and burrbacks crush grubs. The moths were right about *how* to
carry and wrong about *where to*.

## 6. Prologue (wordless, about 90 seconds)

A crowd of moths at a lantern at night. The Taper, ancient, stumbles and fades. The glass flares, one
glint inside it goes out, and she steps from the light whole. The crowd raises its wings. In the crowd,
two young moths, Vesper and Sedge, nudge each other in awe. The Taper's eyes find Vesper, and she gives
her a glint. It flickers as Vesper carries it, the same small gesture twice. At the edge of the light a
torn lampwing catches a rising spark. Vesper glances toward it, and the Taper's wing on her cheek turns
her face back to the glass. Vesper sets the glint in, and the lantern blooms. The Taper offers Vesper
her reed for a moment, then takes it back: *not yet*. Down in the valley wait three dead lanterns. Sedge
tags along, uninvited.

## 7. The story

**ACT I: TOGETHER.** *With Vesper: the Taper and Sedge.*
- **The Grove** (our scene): her first lanterns, alone, and the Taper arrives for the heart's wake (see 8).
- **On the road.** Sedge races Vesper through the branches and loses every time, and you can wait for
  him. When Vesper is empty at a dark lantern, Sedge gives her his only glint. She keeps it in her cape
  instead of burning it, because it came from him.
- **The Combs. Boss: Clasp.** Clasp rams Sedge, who is carrying glints, and Vesper drags him clear. Then
  Vesper and the Taper fight her together, both re-forming from **the same lantern**, so every fall by
  either of them drops its glass by one. In the last phase Clasp stops fighting and lies over her pods,
  and you can only finish the fight while she shields them. The moths cut the field open for the act's
  biggest haul. Clasp crawls into the dark, watching.
- **The Coral Stair.** Tallow, slow and one-winged, snatches Sedge's glint from Vesper's cape and flees.
  Vesper chases it, furious.

**MIDPOINT: THE HUSH (played, not shown).** The chase ends in a hollow full of pods, and the burrbacks
there don't get up. Out of habit, the player cuts a pod, as they have a hundred times. Nothing attacks.
Clasp is here. She crawls to the cut pod and folds over it in exactly the pose every burrback took when
a lantern was lit. Tallow sets Sedge's glint on bare earth and nudges it in, and a pod begins to grow.
Then Tallow looks at Vesper and backs away, leaving the glint to her. She takes it. She can't plant it
yet. From here on, the re-form tick shows plainly in the lantern's glass. It always did. It used to hide
inside the bloom.

At the hollow's mouth stand the Taper and Sedge. The Taper holds out her wing: *come back*. Vesper
doesn't move. The Taper doesn't look surprised. Sedge looks from one to the other and goes with the
Taper.

**ACT II: APART.** *With Vesper: Tallow, and later Clasp.*
- **The dark.** Vesper snuffs lanterns, and each one she snuffs is a place she can't re-form any more,
  so the dark grows behind her. Tallow's lamp lights the way. It keeps a wing's length from her, then
  less. *Plant:* a glint set in earth grows a slow pod. A pod is no sanctuary and no checkpoint, and every
  glint she plants is one she can't burn for her own life. Boss arenas have only lanterns.
- **Behind her,** the lanterns she snuffed are found burning again. There is reed-ash at their feet, and
  one glint fewer in the glass. The Taper is following.
- **Clasp's forgiveness.** Vesper goes back to the Combs and plants glints in the field she helped cut
  open. Clasp watches, then curls around the new pods. *Brace* is learned at her side, and in the next
  fight Clasp's bulk stands between Vesper and the ram.
- **Sedge's week runs out.** Vesper finds him at a lantern she snuffed, old and frayed and falling. He
  holds out a glint and looks at the dark glass. She can't. The Taper arrives, relights the lantern with
  it, and Sedge re-forms young in its light. He doesn't look at Vesper as he leaves.
- **The Censer, the moths' home. Boss: the Taper.** She fights with Vesper's own moves, older. Each time
  you down her the arena lantern flares and she steps out whole. You win by burning every glint in its
  glass, or by snuffing it mid-fight, standing in the light while she attacks. Beaten, she sits and won't
  look at Vesper. Vesper puts out the Great Lantern.
- **The lowest point.** In the dark, burrbacks come for the pods the grubs feed on, and Clasp is leading
  them. Vesper Braces in front of the moth children against her own ally, taking the rams the way Clasp
  once took hers. Clasp stops, and the two mothers face each other over the children. Clasp turns the
  burrbacks away, and Vesper falls. With every lantern near her dark, she re-forms far off under the bare
  moon, the only light left her, and learns *Moonward*: fly straight and far with her back to the light,
  where no lantern burns. When she returns, the Great Lantern is lit again and the grubs huddle in it.
  The Taper stands beside it with Sedge, and every moth in the Censer turns its back on Vesper.

**ACT III: THE ROOT.** *With Vesper: Tallow and Clasp, and then the Taper.*

The valley's oldest lantern, the one the others draw from, stands among the buried bulbs. A storm of
lampwings rises against a moth carrying glints toward a lantern. They dive where the pull would take
her, and Moonward carries her straight past their dives. The Taper comes after her to stop her. Then a
dive knocks Vesper down, and the Taper covers her the way Clasp covered her pods. The Taper falls and
re-forms from the Root's glass, again and again, and every time the *player* falls, the same glass drops
too. Your deaths spend her. The player watches it reach the last glint. She doesn't get up.

**The goodbye.** Vesper holds Sedge's glint. In the glass, it would bring the Taper back. The Taper lifts
her wing to Vesper's cheek, the prologue gesture, and this time turns her face toward the earth. Vesper
plants it. Sedge lands beside her and lays his wing over hers on the soil. Without a lantern, he has a
week. The pod opens over the Taper as her light goes out. The Root goes dark, and the valley's lanterns
go dark with it.

- **Planted:** Vesper carries the Taper's unlit reed out of the Root, and the moths follow it. The
  procession is the same, with glints carried in both hands and wings raised, but this time they kneel
  and set the glints in earth. The rite survives, re-aimed. With no edge of light left, Tallow lands
  beside Vesper for the first time. Clasp curls around a pod next to a sleeping grub. Sedge's glint has
  grown into a tree with **one hollow in its bark for every glint you burned to re-form after the Hush.**
  Vesper and Sedge sit under the moon with a week left.
- **Kindled** (the glint goes in the glass): the Taper rises, takes her reed and leads the procession
  back up to the glass. The valley is lush, safe and empty of anyone. Vesper walks behind her. At the edge
  of the light, Tallow folds its wings.

## 8. The Grove, and its ending beat

The Grove plays as built and must feel wonderful. The turn only works if the player loved it.

| In the Grove | First play | Replayed after the Hush |
|---|---|---|
| The kindle bloom | A warm catch | The glint's little gesture, once more, then gone |
| Burrbacks fold down at a kindling | The light pacifies them | The pose over a dead pod |
| A lampwing dive | An attack | A rescue |
| Re-forming at a lantern | A mercy | One fewer in the glass |
| Offering to the heart in tiers | More of the grove wakes | More is drunk |

**The ending beat.** At the wake, the Taper lands beside Vesper as the veins light and turns her face to
the glowing heart, the prologue gesture. The camera shows what Vesper doesn't see. The Taper is looking
past her to the edge of the light, where Tallow folds its wings over its lamp and the burrbacks lie flat.
The light in the veins pulses *inward*. On a first play it's a mentor's pride. On a second, it's someone
who has watched this a thousand times. Sedge is one of the moths dancing up into the lit canopy.

**Changes to the existing scene:**
1. **Sim:** creatures ignore Vesper while she carries no glints.
2. **Sim:** a lampwing dive snatches glints and doesn't wound. Strike it and they drop.
3. **One fade cost:** each lantern's glass visibly holds glints, and a re-form burns one. This replaces
   losing a third of the wallet.
4. **Lantern pull:** a glide near a lit lantern drifts gently toward it.
5. **Calm pose:** a calmed burrback folds flat. At the shrine it folds over a pod.
6. **The wake:** the Taper lands for the gesture, Tallow waits at the light's edge, and vein pulses run
   inward.

## 9. What the player finds

1. **The Taper's hollow** in the Censer: hundreds of faces scratched into the wall, all of them the same
   face. Is she looking at someone, or at one echo again and again? Her wing-patches are cut from an old
   cocoon hanging there. It is Vesper's.
2. **The Root's bulbs**, half-buried in rows, shaped like lantern glass. Someone once kept their dead in
   the same glass the moths burn them in.
3. **Clasp's shell-print** in the Combs: a hollow curled around where the pods used to be, mossed over.
4. **Relit lanterns:** reed-ash at the foot, and one glint fewer in the glass than when you left.
5. **Tallow's island** on the revisit: moss pressed flat in the shape of a wing, beside the ember's
   empty cup.
6. **Sedge's scratches** at a wayside lamp: two little moths racing, and one waiting for the other.

## 10. The question it leaves

Would you burn a stranger to keep someone you love? The moths are alive because she did, every night of a
hundred lifetimes.
