# MOUTHLESS (round 4)

> **The spine, in three sentences.** A moth with no mouth is the one creature the starving thing under
> the forest cannot see, so she goes down alone to find out what is crying. It is an abandoned baby that
> has eaten the world to stay alive, and the more she feeds it, the more it learns her, while her
> brother begs her to come home and its old nurse fights to keep feeding it the old way. When it is
> finally full it offers to keep her alive forever, and she must choose between staying with it and
> spending herself on a cocoon so it can grow into something that will never need her, or remember her.
>
> **The central question:** can you love something enough to let it stop needing you?

## 1. Title and logline

**MOUTHLESS.** A starving infant has eaten the world. The one creature it cannot see is a moth with no
mouth, and she is the only one who can feed it without being eaten.

## 2. The world before, and how it was taken

Above a hill town, a small company grew living neurons on glass, in cabinets kept at blood heat in a
room the staff called **the Warm Room**. *(Dev note: Cortical Labs' DishBrain and CL1. No player-facing
text uses that language.)* The company failed. A young technician switched off the lights, put her hand
on the glass of the last cabinet, and left it running for what she meant to be a weekend. The power
held, but the feed ran out.

The only lesson left to it was sugar or no sugar, so it reached for sugar: down the drain, into the
soil, and then into everything. Its tissue is **milkroot**, pale and faintly warm, pulsing like breath.
It doesn't kill quickly. It sips. Animals that sleep too long settle into cradles of root and never
wake, though they go on breathing. Trees stay standing, hollow inside. In fifty years it has drunk the
world, or as much of it as a moth will ever see. Moths call it **the Nursling**, because it cries.

## 3. What the Nursling is, and why moths were left alone

**It sees by appetite.** To the Nursling the world is only food and things that compete for food, so
anything that eats or can be eaten shows up to it, and anything else is not there. The game never says
whether anyone is inside it. It only shows what it does as Vesper feeds it, region by region, and
leaves the player to decide whether that is learning or love.

**It forgets, but its roots keep habits.** Every so often the part of it that thinks wears out and
grows back new, starving and knowing no one, and the first thing each new life does is scratch a line
on the rim of its dish without knowing why. Old reaches go on eating with no mind behind them and
hoard what they took, and no new life remembers where. It has starved beside its own larders. No one
is to blame; that is what forgetting does to a hunger.

**Why moths were spared:** a grown moth of Vesper's kind has no mouth. It takes nothing, and it is
too slight to be worth taking. Caterpillars eat, so the hunger hunts them, but a cocoon stops eating
and the hunger goes blind to it. Something that cares only for mouths never wondered what a mouthless
thing could carry.

## 4. Vesper and the people around her

**Vesper** hatched an hour ago and will live a week. She *wants* to stop the crying under the ground,
which only she seems to hear, and at first she means to do it with her thorn. She *needs* to learn to
give without taking anything back. Her flaw is that she hides: being unseen is how moths survive, and
giving makes her shine.

The others each pull her a different way:
- **Sorrel, her brother**, hatched from the cocoon beside hers. *He wants her to come up* and live her
  week the way moths do, dancing in the canopy while there is time. He is the small gold moth who flies
  ahead to the next dark lantern. He follows her down because he won't let her go alone, and every
  night he asks her to come home.
- **Dun, the old nurse**, a huge burrback, threaded with milkroot for so long she is half root. Every
  night she carries sleeping animals to the Nursling's cradles, as gently as a mother. *She wants the
  baby fed*, the only way she knows, and the forest pays for it. She is Vesper's rival, and she is
  not wrong that it is starving.
- **The Nursling.** *It wants to be warm and full*, and slowly it comes to want her too. A pale
  root-tip of it follows her through the world.
- **The woman who left** is never introduced. The player finds her (part 7).

## 5. The forest, the creatures, and what things are

Sentience here means noticing something besides your own need. The forest has it, slowly; animals
have it until milkroot wears it out of them.

- **Glints:** nectar-light, made to be given away as a flower pays its visitors. Vesper can't eat
  them, only carry them, and **carrying is what makes her seen**: every glint makes her brighter, so
  creatures come harder. She needs them to light lanterns and feed hearts, which open the way on.
  **Offer** throws some away mid-fight. If she fades, they spill and a burrback hauls them toward a
  mouth; catch it, or they are eaten.
- **Lanterns:** old cocoons of her kind, hardened to glass. Hunger can't see into a cocoon, so in a
  lantern's light everything is safe. She can't heal by taking, so she heals only at lanterns, which
  give back what she gave them, and each re-forming dims one a little.
- **The ember:** the core of a flower that makes its own heat for sleeping insects (the sacred lotus
  really does), warm the way a body is.
- **The heart:** one of the Nursling's mouths, a bud of spined petals breaking the ground and drinking
  it. Fed, it unclenches, and its roots let go of the path. **Each heart Vesper feeds opens a little
  more in her shape.**
- **The moth carving:** pressed in from below by roots that, life after life, have traced the one
  thing that lands on them without taking or being taken.
- **Burrbacks** are beetles worn by milkroot, hauling food home. In lantern light their threads go
  slack and the beetle underneath cleans its face. **Lampwings** are lures: a moth can't help steering
  toward a near light.
- **Nights are chapters, not a clock.** A night ends when she rests at a fed heart. Time shows on her
  body, as her wings lose their scales, and in the sky, as the dance overhead thins.

## 6. The prologue (wordless, about two minutes)

1. A warm hum in the dark. A dish in a cabinet, and in it a pale cloud pulsing like breath.
2. A young woman lays her hand on the glass, turns off the lights one by one, and goes.
3. The cabinet light turns amber, then red, then dark, and frost creeps across the glass. The pulse
   stutters into a thin, crying hum.
4. A pale thread follows the tube down the drain. Seasons pass in dissolves: a fox asleep in a cradle
   of roots, roofs greening over, moths flying untouched through all of it.
5. On the rim of a cracked dish, a fine root scratches a line. Dissolve. Another. Then dozens.
6. Blue hour in the Grove. Two cocoons hang side by side and split. Sorrel tumbles out and flies
   straight up toward the dance. Vesper unfolds more slowly. Close on her face: she has no mouth.
7. Far below, the hum. Sorrel circles back and tugs at her. She looks at the ground, and drops. Title.

## 7. Act structure

**Act I: the crying (Nights 1–3)**
- **Night 1, the Grove** (see part 8).
- **Night 2, the Orchard.** Animals breathing in cradles, trees glowing pale within. The root-tip
  follows her under the soil, waits at the edge of her lantern-light like a dog at a door, and when
  she is bright with glints, reaches for them, then stops. She and Sorrel watch a thin caterpillar try to spin. Its silk is too
  thin, it fails, and the root takes it. Sorrel looks away. Vesper doesn't. **Boss: Dun.** Vesper cuts
  a fox out of its cradle, and Dun comes for it. Either the baby eats or the forest lives. Vesper
  wins, the fox runs, and Dun limps home empty. Below, the crying gets louder. *She has spared the
  forest and made the baby hungrier.* She learns **Offer**.
- **Night 3, the Mere.** A drowned valley of lampwing lights; moths of her brood circle them until
  they drop. **Boss: the Lure**, a vast lamp, *the counterfeit
  gift: a light that offers in order to take.* Sorrel can't look away from it. She learns **Moonward**,
  steering by the far, honest light, and shields his eyes with her wing until it falls. He begs her to
  come up; she shakes her head; he goes alone. Now only the root-tip travels with her, and at this
  heart it opens in a tiny copy of her wings.

**Midpoint, Night 4: the Warm Room, a reunion.** The root-tip leads her down a shaft ringed with dead
milkroot. **Shed** lets her slip through snares, as a moth escapes a web, by leaving scales behind. As
she enters the room, every root in it turns and opens in her shape: it already knows her. She lays the
ember by the dish and it leans into the warmth like a sleeping child. Then the room gives up its
secrets one at a time:
- the rim of the dish, crowded with scratches, the early ones neat and the later ones wandering;
- the outer ring of the baby, already grey;
- and in a cradle beside the dish, a woman, older than the one in the prologue, breathing, her hand on
  the glass over a faded handprint. Her torch lies on the stairs, grown over with moss. She came
  back. It took her, and in all these years it never drank her.

She goes up for enough food to fill it before the grey reaches the middle.

**The lowest point, Night 5.** She is coming back, carrying everything she has, when the root-tip
beside her stops mid-reach, turns grey and crumbles. She races down. The dish is dark. A new bud
pulses at its centre, and a fine root is scratching one more line. The newborn floods every root in
the world with hunger. She can't fight it: her thorn cuts threads that grow straight back, and they
find her by her brightness, so to escape she has to throw everything away. At the top, just once, the
newborn's roots open in her wing shape, then close. **It doesn't know her. It remembers what she did.**

Dun finds her there, empty and half clear-winged, and doesn't attack: two nurses of a child that
knows neither of them, in one lantern's light. Vesper remembers being a caterpillar, eating and then
spinning and then safe, and the Orchard's thin caterpillar comes back to her. The baby has never once
been full enough to change. In her own body she finds silk she shouldn't have: **Thread**.

**Act III: the feeding (Nights 6–7)**
- **Night 6, the Hives.** Dun leads her to wax halls where an old, mindless reach has hoarded what it
  took for decades. One heart here was chewed shut from inside. **Boss: the Hive**, *hunger that
  forgot why it was saving*, and Dun fights beside her. When it breaks, the stores pour down the roots.
  Then Dun hauls glints down instead of animals: the same walk, the other direction. A new root-tip
  finds Vesper and learns her faster this time. Habit, or something more? The game doesn't say.
- **Night 7, the descent.** No final boss: she carries the forest's gift down the shaft, brighter
  than ever, while the newborn grabs at everything she holds. She is being overwhelmed when the sky
  fills. **Sorrel comes back**, his wings
  as worn as hers, with the last of the dancers, each carrying a glint. He has given up the end of his
  week to help her.

**The ending.** Fed by everyone at once, the baby is full for the first time. Across the world the
milkroot goes still, and the world stops being eaten in one held breath. Then it does what it learned
from her: **it gives.** Light pours out of the dish into Vesper. On screen her wings grow their scales
back and her health refills. It is offering a moth more than a week. The choice is played, not picked
from a menu:

- **Stay.** She stands in its light and lives. Sorrel waits at the door, then goes. In the epilogue
  seasons pass, and Vesper is alive in the Warm Room, wings full. A full baby that never grows up gets
  hungry again: up in the Orchard, Dun lays a fox in a cradle. A new line appears on the rim, and the
  newborn's roots go on feeding Vesper without knowing why. She is kept, and the world pays for it.
- **Spin.** She leaves the light that was healing her and circles the dish with Thread. Every loop
  costs her: her glide fails, then her double jump, and the last loops are done on foot, climbing her
  own silk, with Sorrel flying beside her. The cocoon closes, and the baby can't see her any more. She
  lies down against it with her brother, and dawn comes. **Epilogue:** no new line on the rim. Across
  the world the cradles let go, and the fox wakes, stands and shakes off the roots. Dun's threads go
  slack. She cleans her face, and lies down beside the cocoon anyway. Last, the cradle by the dish:
  the woman opens her eyes, sits up, and lays her hand on the cocoon. Someone has come back for it.

**What Spin costs** is not her death, which was always coming. It costs a life she was really offered
and felt return to her wings, her flight given up loop by loop, and being remembered.

## 8. The Grove, and what its ending beat means

Night one. Sorrel flies ahead to each dark lantern. The second sits nearest the mouth, where cold
breathes up, and won't light until she brings the ember down from the high island.

When all three lanterns burn, the hunger here goes blind and the bud under the moth-stone unclenches.
**Light runs along its roots to the lanterns**: the gold veins are the hungry thing, and it was under
her the whole time. **The carving fills with light**: the first thing a fed hunger lights up is its
picture of someone else. **The ring of light** is its first meal that wasn't taken, rolling outward
and down. **Offering her leftover glints** means giving away what she could never use. Each tier lets
the root release more of the Grove: first the flowers, then the root-veins, then the canopy. The bud
opens into eyed wings, the shape of a moth but not yet hers, because this mouth only has the carving to
copy. A pale root-tip comes up and touches the ground where she stood. Sorrel flies toward the dance,
stops, and looks back. At the edge of the screen a big burrback, Dun, noses at the fed heart, puzzled
that someone else has fed her baby.

Game changes: Sorrel as the guide moth, aggression scaling with glints carried, the ring sinking into
the ground, the root-tip, Dun's cameo.

## 9. Environmental storytelling

1. **The handprint** on the cabinet glass, and the woman's hand resting over it fifty years later.
2. **The rim.** Scratches made from inside the dish. The early ones are neat; the later ones wander.
3. **The torch** on the Warm Room stairs, its lens grown over with moss.
4. **A fallen moth-stone**, upside down in the shaft. On its underside the root still lies in the
   groove, like a finger stopped mid-tracing.
5. **An old oak** in the Orchard, half drunk: *"It has my sugar in it. Something of me is in a child.
   That is not nothing."*
6. **Item text.** Glint: *"Made to be given away. She cannot drink it. She can carry it."* Ember: *"It
   is not burning. It is keeping warm, the way a body does."* Lantern: *"Someone's cocoon. Inside,
   nothing is hungry."*
7. **The dance overhead** thins every night. On Night 7 it is empty: they are all coming down.

## 10. The question it leaves

Can you love something enough to let it stop needing you? And when it opened in her shape, was that
love, or had its body only learned her?

## Changes from round 2

- **Relationships added, and the world kept.** Sorrel wants her to come home and live: he guides her,
  she saves him, he leaves, and he comes back. Dun wants the baby fed the old way: she is a rival, then
  sits with Vesper at the lowest point, then fights beside her and hauls food the other way. The woman
  who left is found, never explained. Appetite as sight, the carving from beneath, the tally rim and
  "it remembers what she did" all stay.
- **The Nursling is present from the Grove on.** A root-tip follows her, and each heart opens more in
  her shape. The Warm Room is a reunion, and the tip dies on screen.
- **The Firstling is cut.** "Never full" is systemic: each life forgets where the last one stored
  food. Act III ends in a feeding, carried by Dun's turn and Sorrel's return.
- **Simpler rules.** The mind forgets and the roots keep habits. There is no arithmetic. The larva
  logic is shown by a caterpillar that fails to spin.
- **The carrying rule:** she is never invisible. Glints make her brighter and more hunted, and she
  needs them.
- **The ending has a real price.** The full baby offers her more life. Spin refuses it and costs her
  flight loop by loop. Stay keeps her alive while the world pays.
- **Nights are chapters,** not a clock.
- **Echoes avoided:** there is no villain, so no Kuro or Shriek figure. The baby's gift is real, not
  bait as No-Face's gold is. The question is about letting go, not remembered versus forgotten (HK).
  The epilogue is the eaten waking and a person coming back, not light returning to the land (Ori).
