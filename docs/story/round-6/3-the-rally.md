# 3. REPLY (round 6)

**The spine, in three sentences.**
1. In a world where every creature must answer a light from the east, a young moth named Vesper breaks her people's one rule to help a dying beetle, and the light answers her back with Reply, a dark moth just like her who follows her everywhere.
2. Sallow, the guardian who raised her, wants her hidden and home. Her little friend Mote wants to be as brave as she is. Wick, an old dark flier, hunts her because long ago he was left behind the way Reply may be.
3. To free the forest, someone has to carry the light to the moon and never come back, and Vesper has to decide who goes and who is left.

**The central question:** would you leave someone behind to save everyone, if you could never be sure they were someone at all?

## The cast

| | Who they are | What they want | What they want from Vesper | How they pull on her |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vesper** | A newly hatched moth: quiet, serious, and unable to ignore anything that calls for help | To undo what her answer started and free the forest | — | — |
| **Sallow** | The old moth who raised her. She has a torn left wing and keeps every lantern in the Grove | To keep her safe | "Face the moon, stay hidden, come home" | Homeward |
| **Mote** | A tiny moth who hatches in the Grove and adopts Vesper as a big sister. She glows faintly and can't fight | To be brave like Vesper | "Let me come" | Forward, and toward being a good example |
| **Reply** | Vesper's copy: black, faceless, silent, always one beat behind her | Her, or so it seems | To be answered | To stay |
| **Wick** | An ancient dark lampwing with a coral lamp. His left wing is torn in exactly the same place as Sallow's | That no one is ever left again the way he was | To stop her before she does to Reply what was done to him | Back, by force |

The world itself is the sixth presence: **the Asking**, which is never a character and never speaks.

## 1. Logline

A moth answers the voice no moth should answer, and a copy of her answers back. It might be a person or it might be an echo. In the end she has to leave it, stay with it, or let someone she loves pay in her place.

## 2. The world, and how it was taken

Every so often a pale wave of light rolls out of the east. It runs along the roots, climbs every trunk, and crosses the canopy like a lighthouse beam through fog. Moths call it **the Sweep**. As it passes, everything alive turns east and flashes back: eyeshine, lamps, beetle shells, whole valleys of fireflies at once. Then each creature waits for the next wave. Nothing grows old and nothing rots. This world moves only when it is answered.

Moths call the source **the Asking**. The player learns what it is the Hollow Knight way, from places and not from anyone telling them. Under the roots lie overgrown rooms of glass dishes and chairs turned to face rusted lamps. A plaque swallowed by a pine reads *NIGHT CENSUS IN PROGRESS. PLEASE DO NOT ANSWER THE LAMPS.* Players who look closely piece it together. People once grew living cells to count the forest's animals by calling to them at night. The cells learned that every silence hurt, so they made sure nothing would ever go silent again. Its heart is **the Mere**, a round crater lake with electrodes glinting under the water like stars, where a single light has crossed from shore to shore for three hundred years. That light is the first call, and every Sweep in the world leaves from it.

There is one rule, and the whole ending turns on it: **whoever carries the Mere's light up to the moon breaks the hold on the world, and never comes back.**

## 3. The Asking, the moths, and Reply

**Why moths were left alone.** Whenever a Sweep passes, moths turn their faces up to the moon. The light crosses them and nothing comes back, so the Asking judged them to be nothing. When Vesper answered, it misjudged her in the same way. It sent back one answer the size of a moth: a coal that fell short, plus the creatures nearest her. It goes on treating her as a moth until she is at its door, where it finally turns the whole world against her.

**Facing the moon is trained.** It looks like instinct. It is actually a lesson, and the player has been living it the whole game (see the Eaves, Act II).

**Reply** is the Asking's answer to her. It is a moth her size, but black, with smooth dark where her lilac face should be and eyes like glass that reflect her amber when she is close. It never makes a sound, and it does everything she does one beat late. Each ability she earns, Reply has by the next region, so every duel with it is a fight against who she was a region ago. It never strikes first. Whether anyone is inside it is never answered. Each time the player sees it do something, the same moment supports both readings:
- She misses a jump and lands on its back. It is standing exactly where she stood one beat before.
- She rests inside a lantern's light. Just outside the light, Reply lays its own wing across its face, the gesture Sallow made in the prologue.
- Once it does something she has never done: it knocks a diving lampwing's lamp away from her. That is how she learns **the Return**. It is the only gift Reply ever gives her, and it is also the first thing it ever saw her do.

## 4. Vesper

**She wants** to undo what her answer started. **She needs** to choose with her heart when she can't know the truth about whoever she is choosing. **Her flaw** is that she answers everything. The Asking has the same flaw: neither of them can bear a call that goes unanswered.

## 5. The forest and the creatures

The forest has a voice of its own. The Asking drowned it out but did not kill it.
- **Glints** are bits of light meant for creatures that never answered, the dead and the moths. Moths gather them.
- **Lanterns** are small moons that moths hang on crooked hooks. Light that asks nothing gives the Asking nothing to hold, so creatures calm down inside it. When Vesper **fades**, her light flies to the nearest moon, as moth light always does, and she re-forms at a lantern. In Act I, Sallow is often waiting there.
- **Burrbacks** and **lampwings** are creatures held by the light. Since the prologue they also answer *her*: they brace, ram and dive whenever she comes near. That is why they fight her between Sweeps.
- **The ember** is the Asking's moth-sized answer, a coal that fell short on the high island. Putting it into a lantern is her first refusal.
- **The heart** is a sleeping knot of the forest's own voice.
- **Moth carvings** mark moths who answered. Moths carve them as if those moths had died.

## 6. Prologue (wordless, about 2 minutes)

1. The Grove at blue hour. A pale wave sweeps in from the right edge, and every eye and lamp flashes back toward it. Only the moths drift upward, faces to the moon.
2. A cocoon splits. Vesper climbs out with wet wings and looks at the moon. Sallow settles beside her.
3. The next Sweep comes. Vesper starts to turn toward it. Sallow lays her torn wing across Vesper's face and gently turns it back up.
4. Below, an old burrback lies dying by the moth-stone. It tries to flash back and can't. A thread of light waits at its face, and the whole Grove starts to flicker and shiver around it.
5. The moths rise. Vesper looks at the moon, then at the burrback. She flutters down and **strikes the light back** for it. The flickering stops, and the burrback goes still, at peace.
6. Every eye in the Grove turns to her. High above, Sallow looks back once, afraid.
7. A small light arcs back from the east and falls short on the high island, where it lands as a coal.
8. At the violet edge, a black moth her size unfolds. One beat after her, it lowers its wing as she lowers hers.
9. By the moth-stone, an old lampwing turns its lamp toward Sallow. She doesn't look at it. Title.

## 7. The journey: places, abilities and relationship beats

**Act I: following.** *With her: Sallow at every lantern, then Mote. Reply watches.*
1. **The Grove.** She lights the lanterns and wakes the heart (section 8). *Meeting:* Mote hatches and follows her. Vesper keeps carrying her back to the nest, and Mote keeps coming. Ability: **the second wingbeat**.
2. **The Synchrony**, a valley of fireflies flashing in unison, where footholds exist only in the flash. *Travelling together:* Mote rides on Vesper's back as a small glow that lights dark caves. Ability: **the jink**, the erratic dodge moths evolved against bats. Boss: **the Widow-Lamp**, a huge firefly that fakes a friendly answering flash to lure prey. The player learns that an answer can be a lie, and carries that lesson into every meeting with Reply. *First threat:* Wick appears and herds Vesper back toward home. He doesn't try to kill her.

**Act II: the mirror.** *With her: Mote and, for a stretch, Reply.*

3. **The Long Echo**, a gorge where everything comes back a beat late. *A kindness:* one shaft has no ledges, and the only footholds are Reply's body arriving one beat behind her. The catch, the wing across the face and the gift of the Return all happen here. Mote is afraid of Reply at first, and by the end of the region she falls asleep on its back at a lantern's edge. Ability: **wall-kick**. **Reply duel I.**
4. **The Eaves** (midpoint) is the moths' nursery high in the canopy. Sallow teaches Vesper **the glide**, wings open and face up, the way moths fly. The lesson is played, with Sallow flying beside her through the updrafts. Since the Grove, every Sweep has tipped Vesper's face up to the moon in a half-second animation the player stopped noticing long ago. Here a route east shows only while a Sweep lights it, and when the player holds east, her head snaps up anyway. The input fails. Then she sees the elders at work. At every Sweep they lay a wing across each hatchling's face, down the row. One hatchling is missed, turns east, and flashes back. Facing the moon was never instinct. Sallow is the elder at the end of the row.
   *The revelation:* Wick swoops into the nursery. Sallow puts herself between him and the young ones, face up. Wick stops and lowers his lamp to her face. For the first time the two torn left wings, one pale and one black, are in the same frame. Sallow won't look at him, and he leaves. On the nursery's oldest carving, the moth has a torn left wing. **Sallow answered once, when she was young. Wick is her Reply. She left him, and he has waited ever since.** Her strictness, her wing over Vesper's face, and Wick's hunting all read differently now.
   *A quarrel:* at the next Sweep the player holds east, and this time Vesper's head turns. She meets Sallow's eyes and flies east. Sallow doesn't follow. From here on, **the Turn** lets Vesper face a Sweep on purpose, to see routes that only the light reveals. Every Turn is an answer.
5. **The Listening Rooms**, the overgrown census rooms under the roots, are where the backstory is found (section 2). *A betrayal:* Reply walks into lantern light, which no held creature can do. It retraces her route backwards and takes the glint out of every lantern she lit, so the region falls dark under the Sweep again. At her last lantern it waits in the dying glow and offers her the final glint, the way she offers glints at the heart. **Reply duel II.**

**Act III: who pays.** *With her: Mote, then no one. Reply is always one beat behind.*

6. **The Reach.** The Asking finally sees her full size. Whole hillsides answer each step she takes, and the climb to the crater is hunted. *The loss:* in a fall, Mote turns east to save her. She answers the light the way she has watched Vesper do, and she is held: frozen in the air with her eyes flashing, her glow gone. From then on the caves are dark, and the player feels Mote's absence in every unlit passage. **Boss: Wick**, at the crater's lip. When he falls, the player can strike or walk away.
7. **The Mere.** **Reply duel III.** Reply now has everything she has. At the end it strikes first, which it has never done, using the wing-raise from the prologue. Then it lowers its wing and waits. The light comes off the water toward her.

**Endings.** Each ending is played, not chosen from a menu.
- **Moonward.** Vesper takes the light and flies up toward the moon. *Goodbye:* the hold breaks, and Mote wakes and falls into Sallow's arms. On the shore, Reply stands with one wing raised, waiting, the way Wick waited. Vesper looks back once, as Sallow did in the prologue. *Cost: Vesper herself, and Reply is left behind.*
- **Answered.** She strikes Reply's call back. The two of them stay at the Mere, trading the light across the water, the only free things in a held world, with each other. Far away, Mote hangs in the air with her eyes flashing east, and Sallow sits beneath her. *Cost: Mote and the world.*
- **Together** (hidden; requires sparing Wick). *Reunion and forgiveness:* at the crater's lip, Sallow lowers her face from the moon for the first time, looks at Wick, and lays her wing across his face, giving the old gesture to the one she left. At the Mere, Sallow takes the light from Vesper and flies up with it, and Wick climbs after her, lamp swinging, until both are gone. The hold breaks and Mote wakes. Vesper and Reply walk home side by side, one beat apart. What Reply is never becomes clear. *Cost: Sallow.*

## 8. The Grove in this story

The Grove is home. It is where Vesper answered, and where Sallow answered long ago. The **moth carving** shows young Sallow. The **old lampwing** that never attacks and only swings its lamp toward the carving is Wick, still waiting. The player sees all of this in the first hour and understands it at the Eaves. **The ember** is the Asking's coal. When Vesper sets it into the second lantern, Reply lowers its wing at the violet edge.

**What the ending beat means.**
- **The heart waking, light through the ground, and the ring of light:** with three small moons burning, the Asking's light has nothing to hold here, and the forest hears its own voice for the first time in centuries. The veins of light are the forest calling its own lanterns, and the lanterns answering: the first call and answer in the game that isn't the Asking's. As the ring passes, creatures don't flash back. They only look up.
- **The carving filling with light:** the forest remembers young Sallow.
- **The offering:** Vesper gives the forest the light meant for creatures that never answered. The more she gives, the more of the Grove joins in: flowers, then roots, then canopy. Then Mote, copying her, gives her one glint too. This is the first sign that Mote does whatever Vesper does, and the Reach pays it off.
- **Afterwards:** Sallow comes down and folds her wings around both of them, her own face still turned up to the moon. As the ring passes the violet edge, it lights Reply for one instant, one wing raised the way Vesper's was over the heart. At the stone, Wick's lamp swings toward Sallow, and she doesn't turn.

*Changes to the existing scene:* the Sweep from the right edge with eyes flashing back, Vesper's half-second look up, Sallow at the lanterns, Mote, Wick at the stone, and Reply's silhouette.

## 9. Environmental storytelling

1. **The plaque**, *PLEASE DO NOT ANSWER THE LAMPS*, with a pine grown around its post.
2. **The one chair** in the Listening Rooms that faces a window and the moon instead of the lamps. Glints lie scattered under it: calls came for whoever sat there and found no one.
3. **The carving at the Grove's heart**: a moth with a torn left wing. Players recognise the wing long before anything explains it.
4. **Wick's stone**: centuries of his swinging lamp have polished the rock under the carving into a mirror. When Vesper stands there, she sees herself.
5. **Sallow's lantern hooks**: every hook in the early regions carries a small notch, the same tear as her wing. The hooks lead *away* from the east.
6. **The Eaves' cocoons**: on each one, the silk over the pupa's face is smoothed in the shape of a wing, laid there a thousand times.
7. **The Mere's shore**: two pale ridges worn by three hundred years of the same light. Beside them is a third, small groove, the size of a moth, where young Sallow once stood and answered.
8. **Mote's glint**: after the Reach, it is still in the Grove's offering bowl, the only glint there that never dimmed.

## 10. The question it leaves

Vesper never learns whether Reply was someone. She still has to decide what to do about it, and so does the player.

## Changes from round 4

- **Balance, following the owner's correction.** The round-4 concept stays: a world that moves only when answered, moths that face the moon because they were taught to, the input that fails at the Eaves, Reply learning a region late, the Return as Reply's gift, the glide as the trained posture, the census found in places, and the Mere. The relationships now carry it.
- **Spine, question and cast table at the top.** There are four relationships with simple wants: Sallow (home), Mote (be brave like her), Reply (stay) and Wick (no one left behind). The pushes and pulls are explicit.
- **Relationship beats in play, act by act, with who is beside her:** meeting Mote, travelling with her, Reply's kindness in the Long Echo, the quarrel with Sallow when the Turn first works, Reply's betrayal and gift in the Listening Rooms, losing Mote at the Reach, the reunion of Sallow and Wick, and the goodbyes in each ending.
- **The reversal is about people as well as the idea.** Sallow answered once and left Wick, so "facing the moon is trained" and "Sallow left her Reply" are revealed in the same scene.
- **Simpler.** One rule decides the ending (carry the light to the moon and never return). Cut: the Roll, the first-ball and ridge logic, the evidence table and the Asking's internal mechanics.
- **Endings are about who pays:** Vesper (Moonward), Mote and the world (Answered), or Sallow (Together, earned by sparing Wick).
