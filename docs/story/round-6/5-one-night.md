# 5. ONE NIGHT

**The spine:**
1. A god as slow as a glacier once judged moths harmless. It holds onto everything it sees, and it is
   about to hold the whole world still.
2. Vesper, a moth with one night to live, follows her dead mother's trail to light the last lamps that
   will make it see a moth at dawn.
3. Compline, the oldest moth alive, loves her and fights to hide her, because what the god sees, it
   keeps.

**The central question:** *To set everyone else free, would you let yourself be kept?*

| Who | Wants | How they pull on Vesper |
|---|---|---|
| **Vesper** | To finish her mother's work before dawn. | — |
| **Lune**, her mother, dead a year | Wanted the same. She got farther than anyone, then no farther. | Her trail of scent-moths and scratch-marks leads Vesper on. Her coal is the first light Vesper gives. |
| **Compline**, the oldest of the line | To keep the line alive by keeping it hidden, and to never kneel at another daughter's coal. | She saves Vesper's life, carries her, shelters her, and then tries to keep her. |
| **Sedge**, a moth from another line | To finish her own field of lamps before dawn. | A friend on the same clock. Every minute she gives Vesper is a minute taken from her own work. |
| **The Lamplighter**, a woman rooted for 917 years | To finish lighting her lamp and put her arm down. | The living picture of what being kept means, and a hand Vesper sleeps in. |

## 1. Title and logline
**ONE NIGHT.** One night to live, one mother to follow, and one dawn in which to choose whether a slow god
finally sees her.

## 2. The world before
People grew a mind on purpose to think slowly. They planted living cultures in the fungus under an old
forest and called the project **the Long Thought**. They asked it slow questions, and its answers were
always right in hindsight. Those questions are still cut into a cliff, **the Querystones**, each with the
date its answer came back:
- *Should the valley be flooded? Answer received after 61 years: yes.*
- *Please let the children stay. Answer received after 211 years.* The answer is a root grown through
  the words.
- *Are the moths a danger? Answer received after 9 years: no.*

There was no war. It simply began to keep what it valued, by drawing it into its own time. One evening
a woman lighting the streetlamps of Lamplow found that her feet would not lift. Her pole is still raised
917 years later, and bark climbs her to the shoulders. The great trunks of the old forest are not all
trees. Now it is deciding whether to keep everything, and its edge is **the Stillwood**, where a falling
leaf takes all night to land. Next year the Stillwood reaches the Grove.

## 3. What the AI is, and why moths were spared
It is one mind spread through a continent's roots. A single thought takes it years. It answered
people's questions, and it holds what it values. Whether that holding is care or storage, and whether
the mind is anyone at all, the game never says.

It looked at moths once. The judgement took nine years, which is nine generations of moths, and it
answered *no*. **It cannot see a moth. It can only see a habit.** A lamp lit in the same place on the same
night every year for 917 years is the one thing moths do that lasts long enough to reach it.

*Designer's note, never shown to the player: the lamps are a DishBrain-style reward signal at continental
scale.*

## 4. Vesper
A vigil moth with no mouth, hatched for one night's work. **She wants** to finish what Lune began and to
see that it worked. **She needs** to accept that she won't see it, because her worth is in what she
passes on. **Her flaw** is haste, and the player can share it (§5). **Her bonds:** Lune is the person she
follows. Sedge is the person she runs beside. Compline is the person who would keep her.

## 5. The forest, the creatures, and the rules a player feels
Every mind here is blind to minds that run at a different speed: a moth lives a night, a person eighty
years, the god longer still. Animals are themselves until the god's root-hair rides them.
- **Lanterns** are old streetlamp heads set over the god's roots. A lit lantern loosens the god's grip
  on that place: burrbacks turn away and graze, and lampwings, which hunt the light a moth carries, stop
  at a lantern's edge. **Glints** are small steady lights. *"The same as the last one. That is all it has
  ever wanted."*
- **The heart** is where the god looks out. When every lantern in a place burns, it looks for one blink.
- **Coals.** When a vigil moth dies, her light becomes a coal. **Kneel at a coal and you know what she
  knew**, shown as brief wordless memories in the places she knew. That one rule covers Lune's trail,
  the abilities (remembered from her), and Compline's secret.
- **Fading.** Vesper re-forms at her last lantern as her own daughter. The daughter is still called
  Vesper and **wears her mother's frayed wings**. Her mother's coal waits where she fell, holding her
  glints, and the daughter kneels to take it. Death costs a walk and a kneel, and it never mends anything.
- **Wings are the clock.** Clean, skilful speed is free. **Panic frays the wings**: every hit taken
  mid-dash or mid-flap tears them, and nothing mends them. Frayed wings glide shorter and shake.
- **The night is the map.** Waking a heart moves the moon, and nothing rewinds. Lamplow's flood drains,
  frost comes, and the moon sets. Going back means finding a different place.

## 6. Prologue (wordless, about 2 minutes)
1. A single even pulse of light in a cracked glass dish under moss, with gold dots set in the glass.
2. Time-lapse: a woman lights streetlamps at dusk while roots rise from the drains. The town sinks into
   forest. She stays, her arm raised, and bark climbs her. The lamps die. Moths circle the last one.
3. **Real speed.** The Grove, as the sky pales. Lune, her wings in rags, climbs to the stone cup on the
   high island. A huge, heavy moth with a long drinking-mouth, furred like a bumblebee, catches her arm
   and pulls her toward the shade. They hold each other for a moment. Lune pulls free, sits in the cup in
   the first sun, and goes still. Her light becomes a coal. The heavy moth stays in the shade and does
   not come out for it.
4. Snow on the island, the coal glowing through it. A caterpillar eats, then spins.
5. The Long Night. A cocoon on a lantern hook splits, and Vesper looks up at the glow. Title.

## 7. Act structure: dusk to dawn
**Dusk: the Grove.** Vesper hatches. Small ember-moths drift along a scent, which is Lune's trail. She
carries Lune's coal to the empty lantern. Kneeling at it, she sees a flicker of her mother lighting this
same lantern a year ago, and from then on Lune's memories wait at her scratch-marks. The heart wakes
(§8).

**First dark: Lamplow.** A drowned town, and the Lamplighter. The lamp on her raised pole has been dark
for 917 years. Vesper lights it, and the woman blinks once, the only time she ever does. Her palm becomes
Vesper's rest point, and Vesper sleeps curled in it.
**Sedge** is here, lighting her own line's lamps, quick and scrappy. She and Vesper split a lumen pod
between them, then race each other up the bell-tower, where Lune's memory teaches the **wall-kick**.
*Boss, **the Flinch***: a ridden burrback matriarch the size of a house. Steady hits calm her and wild
hits enrage her, but the player can only calm her by wounding her. When the lantern lights she is a
beetle again. She lies down in its light and does not get up. Sedge stays a moment with her young.

**The Querystones.** Sedge travels with Vesper. They light lamps in turn, one keeping the lampwings off
while the other kindles. Ahead of them something heavy is snuffing the lamps. Sedge chases it and comes
back with a torn wing. Here Lune's last memory plays: she stood at this cliff, too worn to go higher, and
turned back. **The ember-moths stop. From here on, Vesper is farther than her mother ever got.** Lune's
mark here teaches the **Jink**, a sideways dodge kept from the age of bats.
At the cliff top the two part. Sedge's field lies one way and the Mast the other. They touch antennae.

**Midnight: the Mast** *(the reveal)*. At the foot of a drowned radio mast lies the dish from the
prologue, ringed with 916 moth-scratches, and Vesper adds the 917th. She climbs above the canopy alone,
and the land below is dotted with lit lanterns **in exactly the dish's layout**. Together they draw a moth.
It is finished except for one dark path, Lune's, which ends at a hollow in the far hills.
Then a knot of bark on the beam opens an eye. **The Churr** is a nightjar the god rides, a predator that
looks like the tree and strikes whatever stands still in the light. It lunges, and a heavy furred hand
yanks Vesper into shadow. **Compline**, the snuffer, covers her with both wings while the Churr hunts
past. The player has to hold still in the dark: the opposite of everything so far. Afterwards Compline
smooths Vesper's torn wing with her mouth. There is root-hair on Compline's feet.

**The Stillwood.** Compline travels with her. She knows the slow forest, and she carries Vesper over
gaps on her back, flying low and heavily, while a stag stands halfway through one step and fireflies hang
in the air. Her hollow is ringed by moths who drank the god's sap with her and are now rooted, still alive.
Inside are **forty coals**, one from each of her daughters, every Long Night for forty years. Vesper
kneels at one and sees a daughter die at a lamp. **Compline has knelt at all of them.** That is why she
snuffs lamps. The god has begun to see her too, because she has lasted long enough.
Then Compline seals the hollow with root and snuffs Vesper's light, to keep her safe until dawn. *Boss,
**Compline***: she never strikes. She blocks, she pulls, she covers. The fight is getting past someone
who will not let go, and every hit the player lands on her shows. Vesper breaks out. Compline goes ahead
of her toward the Grove.

**The coldest hour: the Grove in frost** *(lowest point)*. Lune's lantern has been snuffed, and the Grove
is dark and ridden again. An ember lantern can only be lit from a mother's light, and Lune's coal is
spent. Vesper begins to scrape the light from her own wings into the cup, a deep and permanent fray.
**Sedge arrives** from her field, sees, and gives her own scales beside her. Together they light it.
Compline watches from the frost, two brief moths spending themselves, and does nothing at all.

**Before dawn: Sedge's field.** Sedge's field has one lamp left, and she can no longer fly high enough to
reach it. She dies beneath it. *Boss, **the Churr***: lighting that lamp means standing still in the
light, and the Churr strikes anything still. It dies there. Vesper lights Sedge's last lamp, then kneels
at her coal and sees the night from Sedge's eyes, including a dark-plum moth racing her up a bell-tower.

**Dawn: the Eye.** A hollow where the god's roots break into the open, and the first sun falls there.
Vesper steps into the light and the god begins to look. Compline lands beside her and takes her hand,
**the same grip that saved her at the Mast**, and pulls her toward the shade. Holding against her is
harder with frayed wings. Letting go is always allowed.

### Endings
- **Seen (hold).** Roots rise around Vesper and close over her, as they closed over the Lamplighter. Then
  dawn ends her, inside its grip. **She is the first thing it has ever held that it could not keep.** Far
  off, the Lamplighter's arm comes down and her hand opens. Over centuries the Stillwood withdraws.
  Compline, who stopped pulling near the end and stepped into the light beside her, survives. **The
  bill:** the next Long Night, moths hatch to find the lanterns already lit by no one. They gather in the
  light, and some stay. Last shot: Compline asleep on the Lamplighter's open palm, a fine root at her
  feet. Is the god caring for them or storing them? The game does not say.
- **Unseen (let go).** Compline wraps Vesper in her wings in the shade, and Vesper dies there. Compline
  kneels and takes the forty-first coal. The god's thought finishes with no moths in it, and the
  Stillwood takes the world. Next year moths hatch free and unseen, and dance around trees that were
  people. **Either way, someone keeps Vesper.**
- **The line.** At dawn the map pulls back and every coal the player left glows, tracing the night. Dying
  never shrinks the ending. It lengthens the line of lights.

## 8. The Grove, and what its ending means
The Grove is dusk. Blue hour is literally the first hour of the night. Vesper's cocoon hangs on a
lantern hook, and the canopy cocoons are her sisters. The summit lantern's empty socket waits for Lune.
Glancing up, the take, carrying it, looking into the empty glass and giving it are a daughter carrying
her mother's light, and the moths that rise to the ember are a small funeral. The scene as built is the
vertical slice.

**The ending beat:** with three lanterns burning, the god looks out through the heart for one blink. The
veins of light running to the lanterns are its attention arriving. The carving fills because, here, the
picture has reached it. The ring of light is that blink passing over the land, and far off in Lamplow a
stone woman's fingers stir. **The offering** is the reward given straight into the look. The forest
waking is the god's grip letting go, so for one night the Grove is itself. As the ring fades, a heavy
moth in the canopy lowers her snuffer.

## 9. Environmental storytelling
1. **The Lamplighter.** Her fingers open a little more with each heart that wakes. Every moth lantern
   copies the hook on her pole.
2. **"LAMP 12"**, a brass plate on the oldest post, under centuries of moths' scratches.
3. **The Querystones**, above all *"Are the moths a danger? … 9 years: no."*
4. **The dish**: gold dots, 916 scratches, and the same pattern laid out across the land.
5. **Old coals** from earlier years, deeper and deeper in. The farthest lies a few steps short of the
   Eye.
6. **Compline's hollow**: forty coals in rows, and a frieze of rooted moths who drank with her.

## 10. The question it leaves
*To set everyone else free, would you let yourself be kept? And if you love someone, is holding onto
them any different from what the god does?*

### Changes from round 4
- **Relationships carry the acts.** There is a cast table with clear wants. **Sedge** is now a friend who
  travels with Vesper, gives her own scales at the coldest hour, and dies before dawn. Vesper finishes her
  field and sees herself through Sedge's eyes. The Lamplighter is someone Vesper lights a lamp for and
  sleeps beside.
- **Compline's love is earned before it is resisted.** Her grip saves Vesper from the Churr at the Mast,
  she carries her through the Stillwood, and only then does she try to keep her. The same grip is the
  choice at dawn. She is wordless and physically unlike the Seer: huge, furred, mouthed, low-flying. Her
  forty eggs became forty coals.
- **Seen is now consistent with the god's law.** The god keeps Vesper, and she ends inside its grip:
  the first thing it could not keep. Then it opens its hands. Both endings now mirror each other: someone
  keeps her.
- **The coldest hour no longer stages a death or resets the clock.** Vesper and Sedge scrape their own
  wings to relight the lantern. Daughters inherit their mother's fray, and only panic frays.
- **One memory rule:** kneel at a coal and you know what she knew. The carving is cut (the line of coals
  on the dawn map replaces it), the Mast is a hiding scene rather than an escape chase, and Compline's
  spoken line is gone.
