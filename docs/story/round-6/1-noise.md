# NOISE

## The spine (tell it to a friend)

1. After her little boy drowned, a mother wished that nothing bad would ever happen again. The living
   machine she grew made the whole world calm, safe and still.
2. It can't see moths, because since that night she can't bear to look at them: her son drowned
   chasing one.
3. A young moth called Vesper sets out to wake her only friend, an old beetle stuck walking in
   circles. To finish what she started, she has to make the mother look.

**The central question:** is it love to keep someone safe, or to let them go?

## The cast

| | Who they are | What they want | What they want from Vesper | How they pull on her |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vesper** | A young moth. Nothing in the settled world has ever seen her. | To be seen by someone. | | |
| **Hob** | An old burrback who has paced the same circle by the shrine for as long as Vesper has been alive. | To be safe. Later, a life of his own. | To be left alone, then to be protected, then for her to stay in the circle with him. In the end, to be let go. | He is why she starts, and why she keeps wanting to stop. |
| **Sallow** | The oldest moth, keeper of the last wild fire. She more or less raised Vesper. | For the moths to survive the way they always have: by never being seen. | To come home and hide. | She is home and safety, set against Vesper's need to be seen. |
| **Maud** | A very old woman who walks the top of the dam with a lamp every evening. Wim's mother. | For nothing to happen to anyone, ever again. | Nothing. She cannot see her. | Her grief is so understandable that undoing her wish feels cruel. |
| **The Lamplighter** | The great lampwing that flies Maud's walk with her. | To keep Maud undisturbed. | For Vesper to stay away from Maud. | It is Hob's mirror: Maud's only companion. Vesper fears it, then comes to respect it. |

**Wim**, the drowned boy, is never seen. He is present in what he left behind: moths scratched into
stones, a drawing, a jar lamp.

## 1. The world before, and how the Quiet took it

The Wend was a flood valley. Sixty years ago, one night, Wim Ferrier took a jar lamp down to the
flood stone to catch moths. The river rose faster than anyone had said it could, and it took him and
ten other children.

His mother Maud kept the valley's water. She grew a better keeper: living nerve cells in a dish on her
kitchen table. They got a steady pulse when they predicted the river and static when they were wrong.
*(Dev note: grounded in Cortical Labs' DishBrain and CL1. No player-facing text uses these words.)*
The dish learned the river. The first regular thing it ever felt was the kitchen's longcase clock,
and it learned to keep that time.

It held the river, and the town asked for cuttings. Every household in Lowmere grew a dish on its
windowsill, like a candle left out. Each asked for one thing more: hold back the storms, keep the fox
from the hens, keep Dad's heart steady. The dishes rooted together. Then the valleys beyond asked for
cuttings too. Now one soft swell a second runs through the ground of the world, and living things fall
into step with it. Fireflies blink in rows, flowers open on the minute, a stag drinks in the same
three motions, and rain falls when it is due. People called this the **Settling**, and the thing that
did it the **Quiet**. Nothing has died badly since. The people grew old in perfect safety and died in
their sleep at the expected ages.

## 2. What the Quiet is, and why moths were left alone

**How it sees.** The Quiet sees through the lamps of its lampwings, and the lamps look where Maud
looks. It learned to see from her, and after the flood she never once looked at a moth, not even in
the pictures she fed the dish. So it has no shape for a moth. It can't see Vesper. It *can* see a
habit: if she repeats a move, she stops being a moth and becomes a pattern, and the lamps find
patterns at once. Fading means being caught as a habit. The player feels this simply: do the same
thing twice and they find you.

**Underestimated.** Early in the Grove, a lampwing hangs in front of Vesper with its lamp full on her
face for a long beat. It weighs her: small, mouthless, no habit, no harm. Then it swings its lamp away
towards the distant dam. It saw her, and it decided she was nothing.

**Is it someone?** The game shows both answers and never chooses.
- *For:* the screen gives one faint shudder when a lumen pod is cut. When Maud nods in her chair,
  every lamp dips with her, the way a child's eyes follow a parent's. And the Lamplighter changes its
  mind.
- *Against:* everything else the Quiet does, Maud did first: her walk, her clock, her looking away.
  A minute after it weighs Vesper, the same lampwing gives a falling leaf exactly the same long hang.

Does the blindness belong to the Quiet, or only to her? The game leaves that open.

## 3. Vesper

Free, as nothing else is, and alone. Since she was a larva she has waited at the end of Hob's loop,
on a spot her own small feet have worn, and lifted her wings three times, their hello. He has never
looked up.

- **Want:** for Hob to see her.
- **Need:** to let others choose, even when what they choose is to leave her.
- **Flaw:** she flits, and she forces. Every pattern looks to her like a cage, so she breaks things
  open "for their own good" without asking. In this she mirrors Maud: Maud forced safety on the world
  out of love, and Vesper forces chance on it out of loneliness. Neither of them asked Hob.
- **What being unpredictable costs her:** nothing can rely on her or find her, and she has never been
  held. A lantern is the only place she can be still.

## 4. The forest and its creatures

Sallow's rule for what is alive: *"Everything alive does something you didn't ask it to."*

- **Glints:** chance. A firefly flash that comes late, or a spark off the beat. The Quiet walls stray
  chance up in **lumen pods**, the way an oyster pearls a speck of grit, and cutting a pod open lets it
  out.
- **Lanterns:** old moth work. A lit lantern draws a swarm into a tumbling orbit, and a swarm has no
  habit for the lamps to find. Burrbacks lose the beat at the edge of the light, stop like a clock run
  down, and turn back.
- **The ember:** a coal from the last lightning fire before the Settling. Sallow has kept it alive on
  her island for sixty years.
- **The heart:** a forest's own pulse. The Quiet beats as one even swell. A living heart flickers
  unevenly, lub-dub, and the player learns to tell the two apart at a glance.
- **Burrbacks:** settled grazers. Their brace-and-ram is a stroke on the beat.
- **Lampwings:** bats the Quiet remade into eyes.
- **Fading and re-forming:** she greys, flashes once on the beat, and is gone. She re-forms at a
  lantern, where the swarm scatters her back into noise.
- **The moth-stones:** the valley's old flood stones. Wim scratched a moth into each one at a child's
  height, every one in a different pose of flight. The lamps slide off them. Each one teaches Vesper
  a move, so she learns to fly the way a dead boy drew moths flying.

**How the beat reads in a game with no sound.** Every settled thing swells together once a second:
fireflies, lamps, pods, the dust of a burrback's footfall, and a lift in the fog. When Vesper lands
on the swell, an amber ring opens at her feet, and when she lands off it she leaves a lilac scatter.
Lock-on is shown by the fireflies near her flashing with her repeated move, and then falling onto the
world's swell. The bosses' openings are the dark gaps between swells.

## 5. Prologue (wordless, about two minutes)

1. Night rain on a rising river. In the reeds, moths circle a jar lamp. The water climbs a stone with
   a child's moth scratched into it.
2. Grey morning. A woman on the bank holds one small boot. A moth lands on it. She brushes it away and
   turns her face.
3. A glow on every windowsill of a town, all swelling together. Then farther towns, linked like a
   pulse along a vein. The river goes glassy. *(It reads as an invasion. It isn't one.)*
4. Years pass. Fireflies blink in rows. A fox walks past a hen. A steeple stands upright under a calm
   reservoir.
5. Blue hour. A very old woman walks the top of a dam with a lamp, step for step, and a great lampwing
   flies beside her. Moths cross her light and she doesn't look down. She reads as a sleepwalker, a
   prisoner.
6. The Grove today. A burrback paces his worn circle. A small moth rides one lap on his shell, then
   steps off onto her worn spot and lifts her wings three times. He walks past.
7. A lampwing drops in front of her and holds its lamp on her face. Then it turns away. Title.

## 6. The story

### Act I: Noise. *With her: Sallow at first, then Hob.*

- **The Grove** (see section 8). Sallow teaches the moth way: never the same path twice, never be
  seen. When Vesper climbs to take the ember, Sallow blocks her with her wings spread, then steps
  aside. It is the first time she has given in to her. Vesper lights the three lanterns and the
  heart wakes. **Hob misses a step, stops, and looks at her**, the first creature who ever has. He
  panics and bolts. Vesper finds him stuck in a hollow, shaking. She lights a wayside lamp beside him
  and sits with him through the night. In the morning he follows her. *Move:* **Tumble**
  (double-jump), changing her mind in mid-air.
- **Tollmeadow.** Flowers open on the minute. *Move:* **Dart** (dash). Far behind the meadow at blue
  hour, the old woman walks the levee with her lamp, at the same moment every evening.
  **Boss: the Choir**, a hill of fireflies that strikes on the swell and can only be hurt in the
  gaps. When it falls, the meadow flickers raggedly and some fireflies have burned out. It was the
  most beautiful thing in the game, and you broke it.
- **The Ruled Wood.** Trees in perfect rows. *Move:* **Turn** (wall-kick). Hob and Vesper work as a
  pair here:
  - He can't climb, so she goes up and drops glints down to him.
  - He can break walls, so she rides his back while he charges.
  - When lampwings come, he crouches and she hides under his shell.
  - He starts to wait for her at the foot of every climb.

  **The Stag of Three.** It has drunk the same three sips for sixty years, and its drinking blocks
  the path. She startles it, it bolts, and the way opens. Hob watches it run.

### Act II: Asked For. *With her: Hob, then no one.*

- **The Weir.** The first sluices. *Move:* **Drift** (glide). **Boss: the Weirkeeper.** Every hit
  makes the water surge, a replay of the original wound. When it falls, the reservoir begins to
  drain.
- **The Levee.** Vesper meets Maud. She lands on her lamp, then her sleeve, then the back of her hand.
  Maud's eyes slide off her, and at the same instant every lamp in the valley slides away. The
  Lamplighter drives Vesper off. Hob waits below in the dark.
- **The quarrel.** The weather Vesper has been loosening sends its first real storm, and a falling
  branch cracks Hob's shell. She wants to push on, and he won't move. She leaves him at a lantern and
  goes on alone, as she always has.
- **Lowmere (midpoint), shown and not told.** The town is drained now. Every window has a cracked dish
  on its sill, with a root running from each into the street. The roots braid down the high street
  and up the dam to one lit window: hers. In the school, eleven small coats hang on their pegs. By the
  door is Wim's stone, with a jar lamp in the reeds below it. The old woman was never a prisoner, and
  nobody conquered this world. A whole town asked for it.
- **The loss.** Vesper goes home and finds the storm has reached the ember island. Sallow lies on the
  ember stone with her wings still, curled around the coal, and it is still warm. She kept it alive
  until Vesper came back. Back in the Ruled Wood, the Stag lies dead in a ravine it never had to look
  at before.
- **The betrayal.** Hob is gone from the lantern. Vesper finds him walking a new circle between the
  rows. He walked back into the Quiet because it doesn't hurt there. He can't see her any more.

### Lowest point and reunion. *With her: no one.*

**Boss: Hob, Settled.** The Quiet drives him at her like a ram. There are two ways to win:
- **By force.** She dodges and strikes until he is knocked out of step. He follows her afterwards,
  but at a distance, afraid of her.
- **By staying,** the one thing she has never done for anyone. She goes to the same spot, at the same
  swell, and gives the same three wingbeats, again and again. She lets him charge past her until he
  chooses to stop and look up. The moth who never flew the same path twice keeps a promise. He lowers
  his head and she climbs on.

Either way she learns **Keeping Time**: landing exactly on the swell. Settled gates and platforms
accept her, and lamps can see her while she does it. Only a Hob who chose her will carry her and
charge on her signal.

### Act III: The Mother. *With her: Hob, later the Lamplighter too.*

- **The Roost.** A cave of sleeping lampwings under the dam, every lamp turned towards Maud's window.
- **The Spillway.** A climb up the dam face in a storm nobody scheduled. She calls the moment and Hob
  charges.
- **Boss: the Lamplighter.** It is the only lamp that doesn't look where Maud looks. It looks at
  whatever comes towards her, so it tracks Vesper herself and not her habits. Pure noise can't beat it
  and neither can pure rhythm. What works is keeping time until it commits, then breaking once. At the
  end Hob pins it, and Vesper stands over its lamp and doesn't strike. The Lamplighter lowers its lamp
  and flies behind them from then on.
- **The Bellhouse.** Maud's kitchen has a table set for two and a cold hearth. A child's drawing lies
  face down on the dresser. Above her chair, the clock's pendulum has grown into a root as thick as a
  tree. Each swing is the world's beat, and her heart keeps time with it. The kitchen door has been
  shut for sixty years, and it opens only to Hob's charge on Vesper's signal.

## 7. Endings

- **The Offbeat** (needs a Hob who came back by choice).
  1. Vesper lights the hearth with Sallow's coal, and firelight flickers across Maud's face for the
     first time in sixty years.
  2. Maud turns from it, as she always does, and her hand knocks the drawing over. It is Wim's moth,
     drawn as a scribble because he drew it flying.
  3. Vesper flies that scribble in front of her: the Tumble, Dart and Turn the stones taught her.
  4. **Maud looks.** She holds out a finger, and Vesper lands on it and keeps still.
  5. The Lamplighter's lamp turns to the moth, and so does every lamp in the world. The Quiet sees a
     moth.
  6. The pendulum hitches one beat late and swings on, never quite even again, the way a healthy
     heart never is.
  7. Outside falls rain that nobody scheduled. Hob stands in the doorway and Vesper gives him three
     wingbeats. He sees her completely. Then, free of the Quiet and free of her, he turns and walks
     into the wet hills. She doesn't follow.

  Maud lives on, unevenly, beside the fire. Storms and hunting foxes come back, and some things will
  die badly again. After the credits there is no ride and no charge. Lanterns no longer hide her,
  because nothing needs to. Lampwings watch every moth. Grass has grown in Hob's circle, and her small
  worn spot is still beside it.
- **Storm.** She cuts the pendulum, and Maud's heart stops. One night brings back sixty years of
  storm, fire and flood. The lamps go out and the lampwings are bats again. Hob drowns holding her
  above the first flood since Wim's. At dawn the Lamplighter, now a bat, hunts her through a blazing
  green valley.
- **Stillness.** She sits on the pendulum and keeps its time until she is a habit and greys into it.
  Hob paces for ever, and nothing happens.

Every ending costs her Hob. The endings only choose how.

## 8. The Grove (the scene we have)

The Grove is the first chapter, and its ending is the first time Vesper is seen.
- **Three lit lanterns** make a ring of swarms the Quiet can't see into, so the forest's own heart can
  beat again.
- **The veins of light** running out to the lanterns are that heartbeat, reaching for the swarms that
  shelter it. They should pulse unevenly, never like a metronome.
- **The moth carving** is Wim's. It fills with light and teaches her the Tumble.
- **The ring of light** is the first unplanned event in sixty years. A two-second cut follows: on the
  dam, the old woman misses one step. Her head turns away from the Grove, and every lamp in the valley
  turns with it.
- **The offering** gives the forest its chance back. Each glint lets it do one thing nobody asked
  for: flowers open at the wrong hour, the root-veins light, and the cocoons in the canopy split
  early.
- **Then Hob looks at her.**

Changes this needs in the scene:
- One burrback by the shrine becomes Hob, with his worn circle and her worn spot beside it.
- Fireflies, lamps and pods swell together while the Grove sleeps, and flicker out of time once it
  wakes.
- The ember island is Sallow's.
- The lampwing weighing beat happens near the first lantern.

## 9. What the player finds

1. **Wim's stones.** A child's moth on each, at a child's height, with dated water-marks above it.
2. **The dishes** on every windowsill of Lowmere, their roots braided down the street.
3. **Eleven small coats** on the school pegs.
4. **The jar lamp** in the reeds, with air holes punched in its lid.
5. **Hob's circle** and Vesper's little worn spot beside it.
6. **Maud's first dish** on the kitchen table, cracked and dark. A strip of tape on it, in her hand:
   *"Let nothing happen."* These are the only human words in the game.
7. **Item: Even Heart**, a pacing knot found in a Lowmere house. *"A healthy heart stumbles a little.
   This one never has."*
8. **Sallow**, as she steps aside from the ember: *"Sixty years I kept it. Don't keep it."*
9. **The leaf.** A lampwing gives a falling leaf the same long, weighing hang it gave Vesper.

## 10. What it leaves the player thinking

Maud kept the whole world safe because she couldn't bear to lose anyone again. Vesper saved it by
letting go of the one friend who ever saw her. Which of them loved more? And if the Quiet only ever
looked where Maud looked, whose grief was it?
