# Review: Mouthless (round 31)

## 1. Anchor gate: PASS

All three readers' first sentence puts a moth against the thing that swallowed the world, and all three found, unprompted, that it is "a lab-grown brain" people abandoned. That truth is found in play (the poster, the coat, the cracked dish, the memory) and never stated in jargon. The Hush is on screen at world scale in every act: in Act I the breathing mountain, the orchard of cases and the tower root; in Act II it turns on moths everywhere; in Act III its picture of Umber covers the city walls and the final fight is inside it. It still spreads, and it escalates.

Owner's ideas:
- Biological AI has taken over the world: **central**.
- Umber, a moth, saves the world: **central**.
- Moths beneath its notice, and that proves wrong: **central**. "Notices only things that eat" ties the blind spot to having no mouth.
- Sentience ambiguity: **central**. Is its love real, or did it only learn to stop the static? The forest is a mind that acts, and Dun wakes.

## 2. Verdict: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES

No foundation problem. The spine, cast and central question survived three cold readings almost word for word.

## 3. At the bar

- **Sorrel's shiver.** "a dark knot round a pale case… Then he shivers: he is alive, and keeping the case warm." Two readers named it as what they would remember.
- **One gesture carries the relationship.** The folded wing and the tap on the wingtip start at Umber's birth, are handed between Umber, Sorrel and Dun, and end the true ending.
- **The antagonist is wrong in a way that shows something.** "Each leaving hisses like a missed ball", and the first root goes to a hungry child. The heart reader "would grieve for" it.
- **Mechanics carry the theme.** Giving warmth beats fighting, shivering spends yourself, and each lap of the Spin "costs her glide, then somersault, then dash." The final cocoon is "mouthless, like her."
- **Dun's turn.** "pulls against its own root until it snaps, and lies across her lantern's door, facing out." A defection told by an action, not a speech.

## 4. Problems, ranked

1. **The cases' effect on time contradicts itself (POLISH, but all three readers tripped on it).** The partner wakes "no older than the night she was taken". People wake "old". In Burn, "only those kept days" survive, yet the wood ends with "nothing else moves" even though its moths were cased days earlier. *Fix:* cut "no older than…", since she was only taken days ago. In Burn, let the wood's cased moths wake and the human world stay dark.
2. **Tallow lies down off-screen (POLISH).** All three readers flagged it. It is the hinge of his arc and it falls between two nights. *Fix:* after he lays the torch down on Night 6, he follows Umber. On Night 7 the camera holds while he walks past her to his sister's case, and a case opens for him. One shot, no new rule.
3. **"No one has ever warmed it" contradicts the sister's warm hand (POLISH, on the spine line).** *Fix:* change the spine to "since they left, nothing has warmed it."
4. **The ball game in the memory reads as random (POLISH, though it touches the anchor's revelation).** The casual reader asked "why is a brain playing ball?", and the heart reader could not picture how a dish feeds dinner tables. *Fix:* make the ball the job. The cloud keeps a grow-light on a sprout, and a miss means the sprout wilts and static follows. The same image explains "it feeds us".
5. **Umber's own want is generic (POLISH).** Heart reader: "wants something a bit generic." *Fix:* use what is already there. On Night 1 she watches the summit dance from the dark, so give her that want: to dance once. Spin's circling becomes the only dance she gets, and Burn's dawn dance a temptation that costs the world.
6. **Small legibility snags (POLISH).** Tallow sees "grey stone" where Umber sees a face; the outline needs to show that the skin turns clear only to eyes pressed against it. When Sorrel "loses every scale", make it explicit that he lives. Spin's "stalks spun into threads" needs one concrete image of what the Hush becomes; the last image already half-delivers it.

## 5. Scores

Anchor 9 · Relationships 9 · Clarity 8 · Idea 9 · World 9 · Mechanics-as-theme 9 · Antagonist 9 · Ending 8 · Originality 8 · Grove fit 8 · Balance 9

Clarity: every confusion was a continuity detail, never the story. Grove fit: lanterns, carving, thorn blade and lampwings are re-meant well, but burrbacks becoming badgers is a stretch; give Dun a beetle-like shell or the burrback name.

## 6. At the bar? Yes

I would put this beside Ori, Hollow Knight and Nine Sols: a fable surface, a technical truth found in a lab, a relationship told through a gesture, a pitiable antagonist, and an ending paid for in abilities. All three readers would finish it. What remains is continuity polish, mainly how time works in the cases.
