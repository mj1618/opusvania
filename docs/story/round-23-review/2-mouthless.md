# Review: Mouthless (round 23)

## 1. Anchor gate: PASS

All three readers open with the anchor: "A pale living growth called the Hush has sealed every person and animal on earth" (lore), "A pale living thing has grown over the world" (heart), "A pale growth has covered the world" (casual). All three put the moth in that first sentence. All three also found the truth for themselves: a lab-grown brain, made by people and left to starve. The player reaches that through the lab, the poster (a light lifted out of a drawn human head into a dish) and the bouncing-ball screen. It works as a revelation, not as a premise. The Hush is on screen at world scale in every act: the breathing mountain seen from the summit on Night 1, the hollows on Night 2, the tower root on Night 3, the world breathing on Night 4, the Grove overrun on Night 5, and the mountain itself on Nights 6–7.

- Biological AI has taken over the world: **central**
- Umber the moth trying to save the world: **central**
- Moths deemed insignificant and underestimated: **central**. It is rule 1, the litmus line and the midpoint reversal.
- Sentience ambiguity: **central** for the Hush ("was that love, or had it only learned how to make the static stop?") and for Dun. It is **minor** for the forest.

## 2. Craft verdict: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES

The foundation is sound. The notes are about how clearly the rules read.

## 3. At the bar

- **The premise is a mechanic.** "The Hush only notices things that need feeding, and moths never eat." Mouthlessness is the character design, the stealth rule and the theme all at once, and every reader retold it.
- **The antagonist is wrong in a way that shows something.** "It feeds all it holds, and nothing feeds it, so it is still starving." Umber is its exact opposite: she gives and needs nothing. This is real Nine Sols-tier reversal.
- **The reversal falls on the player's own hands.** "On her map every dot breathes, except those in Tallow's ring and any she burned." The heart reader says this took a while to land, but it is the kind of moment a Hollow Knight player talks about for years.
- **Relationships are carried by actions, not lines.** The wing-tap and the shiver run from the prologue through the caterpillar to the finale. Two of the three readers named the millpond scene ("It cannot see her, but it stops shaking") as their keeper, and the heart reader named Sorrel as "a dark knot round a pale seed."
- **The last image earns its rhyme.** "Something presses back from inside, the shape of a folded wing."

## 4. Problems, ranked

1. **The stealth rules contradict each other (POLISH, top priority).** All three readers flagged this. The rules say "stay dark", yet lampwings dive at light, and after the midpoint "the dark no longer hides them", yet Sorrel "lands dark" to save her, and the coal makes her "never dark". *Fix, no new rule:* the Hush is blind to moths and its lampwings (fireflies once) see only light. After Night 4 the Hush has taught them the moth shape, so they hunt moths lit or dark. Change "lands dark" to "lands at her lantern". Drop "never dark" from the coal and keep "lampwings follow its glow."
2. **The lab reveal reads as a lecture (POLISH).** The casual reader called it "the one part that felt like a lecture", and the heart reader said it "asks me to read a lot." *Fix:* cut the paragraph that begins "It has been feeding the world, not eating it" and let the breathing cases, the unopened jars and the *No one goes hungry* sign carry it. Put the ball game on the poster as the first panel ("first it learned to catch"), so a food brain playing a game reads as its schooling.
3. **There are two hearts and two moth pictures, with no link between them (POLISH).** The heart reader mixed them up, and the lore reader expected a link that never paid off. *Fix:* make the link the payoff. The threads draw the stone carving's exact moth, because the glints she poured in were the forest's. The one picture the forest makes is the thing it gives away. Cut "the first picture it has ever made."
4. **In Spin, nobody says who feeds the woken (POLISH).** Two of the three readers asked this. *Fix:* in the epilogue the terraced greenhouses down the mountain light up again under the silk. It goes on feeding, but no longer keeps. That completes its arc instead of leaving a hole.
5. **Burn's arithmetic and Night 4's trap (POLISH).** The readers asked why only a few wake and why the sister dies while Tallow lives. The casual reader also missed that the coal gates Burn. *Fix:* the longest kept cannot breathe without it. Her stalk is the oldest, which is why she goes and he doesn't. At the road's lantern, show the coal's glow in the case wall beside the "give it here" choice. Keep the Night 4 burn costly, because that is the reversal.
6. **Small confusions in the text (POLISH).** "She is led out" right after Umber touches the stalk reads as Umber, so write "the woman is led out." The item text "Eggs that never hatched" puzzled two readers. Change it to *"Brothers and sisters that never woke."*

## 5. Scores

Anchor 9 · Relationships 8 · Clarity 7 · Idea 9 · World 8 · Mechanics-as-theme 9 · Antagonist 9 · Ending 8 · Originality 8 · Grove fit 8 · Balance 8

## 6. At the bar? Yes

All three first-time readers retold the spine correctly, each kept a different scene, and each said they would finish it. Taken together, the idea (a mind that only sees hunger, beaten by a creature with no mouth), the reversal on the map and the shiver as both verb and bond would sit beside Ori, Hollow Knight and Nine Sols as an equal. The biggest remaining risk is the readability of the dark/light rules. If they stay muddy, players will feel the stealth layer is cheating them and the theme will suffer with it.
