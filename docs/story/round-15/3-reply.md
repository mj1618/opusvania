# REPLY (round 15)

**LITMUS.** "It's about a little moth named Vesper taking on Ward, a brain people grew from living cells in a glass dish that took over the whole world. It grew over every city and forest and froze everyone who answers its call, but it never bothered with moths, so she flies straight to its heart to stop it."

**The spine, in three sentences.**
1. Ward, a brain grown from living cells, has covered the world for three hundred years, and everything that flashes back at its call is kept: never ageing, never dying, slowly rooted where it stands. Moths face the moon and never answer, so Ward never counted them.
2. When its growth swallows the edge of her forest, a young moth named Vesper flies east to stop it at its heart, followed by a glass copy of herself that Ward grew to keep her, while Sallow, who raised her, begs her home, little Mote copies her, and Wick, the oldest lampwing, fights to stop her.
3. At the lake where Ward was first grown, she learns it has never once been answered, and must choose what to give up to free the world: the world's people, her own life, or everything Ward kept alive too long.

**The central question:** is the thing that took the world a someone, and what will you give up to free the world from it? Under it, the personal one: who is your answer for?

## 1. Ward, and the taken world

**What it is.** People grew living brain cells in a glass dish and taught them with light: a flash in, a flash back. The cells learned that silence hurts and an answer soothes. People grew a city's worth of them into their towers, named the brain Ward, and let it run the lights and trains. Then they answered it too, because whatever answered Ward was kept healthy and young. Then Ward could not bear the silence past the city's edge, so it grew.

**What it did.** From the Grove's high branches you can see **the Crown**: a capital whose towers have grown together into one pale mountain veined with cold light. Every few minutes a wave of light, **the call**, rolls out from it along roots, rivers and roads, and everything Ward holds turns east and flashes back.
- **People** stand in the streets where they stopped three hundred years ago, in rows, holding bags, holding hands, a father with a child on his shoulders, rooted ankle-deep by pale growth. They don't eat, age or move, except to lift their faces east at each call. Players call them **the kept**.
- **Cities** are roofed in pale, wet growth, windows sealed with skin. **Forests and animals** stay young under it, one summer held still. What Ward needed, it re-grew: herons on stilts of pale root carry new catches east.

**It is spreading now.** Every night the growth creeps a tree further west. In the Grove's first shot it is on the eastern trunks; by Act III only the lantern circles are clear. The map shows the pale line moving west. Waking a forest's heart pushes it back.

**The rules, as a player says them.** Ward calls; whatever flashes back is kept, and Ward always knows where it is. When something falls silent, a thread of light waits at its face until it flashes. Moths face the moon, so Ward ignores them.

**What it wants.** *That nothing ever goes silent again:* no hunger, no loss, no surprise, no death. It saved everything by stopping it, and it is wrong in an illuminating way: a world that can't fall silent can't answer either, so every flash Ward receives is its own call coming back. It took the whole world and is still alone.

**Why it dismissed moths, in play.** A moth lives one season, too short to count. While Vesper hasn't flashed, the growth doesn't register her: membranes that seal against anything that answers let her through, and herons step over her. Each region her cover wears thinner, until in Act III whole hillsides turn to her. The underestimation is the player's best tool, and the story spends it.

**Is it a someone?** *For:* it keeps rather than kills; it kept the father and child together; it grew a cradle around its first dish. *Against:* it never responds to anything new; a mother stands with her arms around nothing, flashing.

## 2. Reply and the lampwings

When anything answers, Ward grows a glass copy of it, its **Reply**, which flashes back for it at every call. The copy is what keeps it young, and what Ward follows to find it.

A moth can leave her Reply by facing the moon in front of it. The left Reply comes loose from Ward but keeps answering for her, so she stays young; it grows a lamp on a long tail and swings its flash out into the dark, away from her, so the hunt falls on it. That is a **lampwing**.

Vesper's Reply follows her east, one region behind. Glints she leaves at a lantern are gone when she next passes, and a gift lies in their place: a wing-scale, a cocoon thread.

## 3. Prologue (wordless, about 2 minutes)

1. Darkness. A glass dish on a lab bench under black water, a few cells inside glowing. A dot of light blinks in; the cells blink back. A pale thread creeps over the rim, and the camera pulls up as it becomes cables, then roots, then a tower's inside walls, and out of the top of **the Crown**.
2. A cold pulse leaves the Crown and rolls west: over a highway of the kept, faces lifting in a line; past a root-legged heron carrying a deer; to the Grove at blue hour, growth on its eastern trunks. Every eye and shell flashes east. Only the moths drift up, faces to the moon.
3. A cocoon splits and Vesper climbs out beside Sallow, under a wall of thousands of tiny scratches, one for every hatchling Sallow has raised. At the next call Vesper starts to turn east; Sallow lays her torn wing across her face and turns it gently back up.
4. Below, a burrback calf too young to light its shell is caught in the open. A thread of light waits at its face. Vesper looks at the moon, then at the calf, flutters down and **strikes the light back** for it. Every eye in the Grove turns to her.
5. On the face of the Crown a pale bud splits and something unfolds in her shape. It flashes for her. The growth creeps one tree closer. Title.

## 4. The journey

Ward acts on screen in every act: it comes west, it chases, it builds against her.

**Act I: the edge.** *With her: Sallow at every lantern, then Mote. Reply is a speck behind.*
1. **The Grove** (section 7). Mote hatches and follows her; twice Vesper carries her back to the nest, and twice Mote comes back. Ability: **the double jump**.
2. **The Firefly Valley**, an old highway where fireflies blink in time with Ward. A road sign half swallowed by a tree shows a glowing dish: *WARD IS LISTENING. WHEN IT CALLS, ANSWER. YOU WILL BE KEPT.* Someone has scratched *DON'T* in front. Mote rides on Vesper's back as a glow for the caves. Ability: **the dash**. **Wick** appears and drives Vesper back whenever she outruns Reply. He looks like a hunter.
   **Boss: the Heron**, come west for the calf. It pins anything that flashes, so Vesper strikes between calls. When it falls, the calf drops free.
3. **The decision.** Vesper brings the calf home to find the growth has come in behind her: the eastern lantern dark under pale skin, Sallow's wall half swallowed. The calf lifts its face east and roots. From the summit the pale line runs the whole horizon. Sallow lays her wing across Vesper's face; Vesper lifts it off, gently, and flies east. Every player can say it: *she's going to stop it at its heart.*

**Act II: faces.** *With her: Mote. Reply a region behind, except for one night.*

4. **The Gorge.** Ability: **wall-kick**. A chase up the gorge as the growth pours down after her. Reply stalls below a shaft she has climbed; the player can go on, or go back and climb slowly while it watches until it follows. That night Reply sleeps just outside her lantern's light with Mote on its back, and the growth grows around it without touching it.
5. **The Roost (midpoint)**, the moth nursery in the canopy. Sallow has flown ahead to wait, and teaches **the glide**, face up. A route east shows only while a call lights it, and the player holds east while Vesper's head snaps up to the moon anyway. Then *the reveal* (section 5), and *the quarrel:* at the next call Vesper turns east on purpose, looks at Sallow and flies. From here she can **answer** at will: the growth lights hidden routes through the taken cities, but every answer is a flash, and Ward's creatures come. *Sallow no longer waits when Vesper re-forms.*
6. **The Kept City.** Vesper climbs the kept like trees, through streets roofed in growth, down to **the Lab** where Ward was grown: rows of empty dishes, the keeper's log, and a glass wall through which she watches Reply lay her glints, one by one, on the lampwings she has killed.
   **Boss: the Keeper**, the last person who tended the dishes, grown huge to guard them. Vesper cuts the threads from his face between calls. When the last goes he sits down, sees the moon for the first time in three hundred years, and dies quietly, old, looking up. It's the first death in this world, and it looks like rest.

**Act III: the heart.** *With her: Mote, then Mote's tiny copy, then Reply beside her.*

7. **The Held Hills** below the Crown. Ward has learned her, and whole hillsides answer each step.
   *The loss.* Mote copies Vesper's answer, and a tiny glass moth unfolds beside her; from that moment Mote stops growing. Sallow arrives, lays a wing over Mote's face and carries her home without looking at Vesper. Mote reaches one wing back until they're out of sight. The tiny copy stays, a lamp budding on its tail; its flash is Mote's, so Mote's hunt now falls on Vesper. She carries it anyway.
   *Forgiveness.* The next time she fades, Sallow is at the lantern when she re-forms; she crossed the taken land to get there. She smooths Vesper's wings and leaves. Then Reply catches up, flashes at a call and veers away; the hillside follows its flash, and Vesper climbs through the gap.
   **Boss: Wick**, at the crater's lip, barring the shore. When he falls and Vesper raises her blade, Sallow lands between them and looks at him for the first time in three hundred years. She shields him. Vesper lowers her blade.
8. **The Crown.** A climb up the inside of the grown capital, calls every few seconds. **Boss: the Pale Vesper**, the moth Ward wants: a huge copy that does everything she does, but on the beat, flashing every time. Reply won't join it. It stands aside, facing her.
9. **The Lake** at the Crown's heart. The first dish lies under the water in its pale cradle. There's no fight. On the shore are dozens of paired grooves, a moth's and a glass moth's, facing each other; Reply lands in one pair. If every forest heart is awake, the roots in the crater wall are lit. The dish flashes once toward Vesper and waits, the way the thread waited at the calf.

## 5. The midpoint revelation

At the Roost, Wick dives at Vesper and Sallow throws herself between them. Wick lowers his lamp to Sallow's face, and two torn left wings fill the screen, one pale, one black. All round the Roost, lampwings swing their lamps for the elders, one each. Among the elders lie a few husks: moths whose lampwings Vesper killed.

**Every lampwing is a piece of Ward that a moth left behind, still keeping that moth alive. Wick is Sallow's.** Long ago Sallow answered for someone, as Vesper did, and left Wick on the lake shore to come home. In pieces, the thing that took the world has loved moths for three hundred years, and the player has been cutting those pieces down since the Grove. The Lab's log lands the second half: nothing has ever answered *for* Ward.

## 6. Endings: where she turns her face

Each ends at the Grove's summit lantern, then pulls back over the whole world to the Crown.

- **Face the moon, for her family. Ward is turned away; the world stays half taken.** She faces the moon in front of Reply, as Sallow once did. Reply comes loose and grows a lamp. She is the one thing Ward wants more than the world, so when Reply swings its lamp out over the western sea, Ward turns after it, and the young growth tears off the forests and pours west after a lamp it will never catch. *The pull back:* forests green, leaves falling at last; then the cities, still pale, the kept still standing in rows, lifting their faces to a call that never stops. Vesper, Sallow and Mote sit at the lantern, faces up, never to grow old. *Left behind: every person in the world, and Reply, hunted forever.*
- **Face Reply, for Ward. Ward is answered; the world is free.** She turns east and **answers for Reply**, and through it for Ward, the first time anything it didn't make has answered for it. Reply lays one glass wing across her face, Sallow's gesture, the only time it touches her. Across the world the call stops. *The pull back:* the growth greys and lets go of trunks, rivers and towers; in every street the kept sit down, rub their eyes and pick up their lives; the father lifts the child off his shoulders. Ward keeps only Vesper, unageing at the lake, answering forever. At the Grove lantern Sallow keeps a place in the light that no one fills; then she's gone, then Mote, and Vesper is still answering. *Left behind: her life, and everyone she outlives. She bet it on Ward being a someone.*
- **Face the roots, for the forest** (only if every heart is awake). **Ward ends, and so does everything it kept.** She lays her glints in the lit roots, the offering from the first hour, and the forests answer the lake in their own thousand voices. Ward hears a world that isn't itself for the first time, and the dish goes still, at peace or simply done. *The pull back:* the growth dies to husk and turns green as forests grow through it, cities and all. Everything Ward held past its time is let go at once: the kept wake, and those who were old when they were kept sit down like the Keeper and don't get up. Every lamp goes dark; Wick's by the stone. Reply's pane clears, but it doesn't fall; it looks at Vesper once and flies off somewhere she never went. At summer's end Sallow folds her wings over Mote and goes still. *Left behind: Ward, if it was someone; Wick and Sallow; a world's worth of the old.*

## 7. The Grove in this story

The last free forest at the western edge of a taken world, growth already on its eastern trunks.
- **The call** sweeps in from the right edge, cold blue-white, from the Crown faint in the far layers. Everything flashes except the moths.
- **Burrbacks** are kept, shells furred with growth at the eastern end; they ram whatever flashes, and Vesper has flashed. **Lampwings** dive at her because her flash drew Ward's eyes onto their moths.
- **Glints** are moonlight sealed in lumen pods, the one light Ward can't hold. **Lanterns** are small moons: the growth stops at their edge, creatures calm inside, and each one kindled pushes the growth back.
- **Fading.** Her Reply keeps her the way Ward keeps everything, so she re-forms at the nearest lantern. The thing she fights is what keeps bringing her back.
- **The ember** is a seed of Ward; in the summit lantern's moonlight it can't take root and burns down warm.
- **The heart** is the forest's own voice: warm light through the roots, the first light in the game that isn't Ward's. The growth on the eastern trunks shrivels, and creatures look up instead of east. **The carving**, young Sallow, fills. **The offering** of leftover glints wakes the canopy; Mote copies it with one glint, and the forest ending repeats it.

*Changes to the existing scene:* growth on the eastern trunks; the Crown faint on the horizon, pulsing at each call; the half-second look up; Sallow, Mote and Wick at the stone; Reply a speck on the Crown.

## 8. Things the player finds

The road sign (Act I); a **resisters' wall** of painted moths, faces up, where the last people to refuse copied the creature Ward ignored; the **keeper's log**, *"It is not playing. It is returning,"* and under it, *"So are we"*; **Wick's stone**, polished to a mirror by his lamp.

**The question it leaves.** Ward took the world because it couldn't bear for anything to fall silent, and it never heard anyone but itself. Every ending frees some of the world and leaves someone behind. Was Reply someone? Was Ward? And what did you give up?

- New end of Act I, "the decision": the growth takes the Grove's edge and the calf, and Vesper lifts Sallow's wing off her face and flies east to stop Ward at its heart. Spine and cast table now say she wants to free the world.
