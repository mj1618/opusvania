# Review: Mouthless (round 32)

## 1. Anchor gate: PASS

All three first-time readers' first sentences name a grey living thing that sealed the world asleep, and a moth. All three also name the Hush as a brain grown in a dish, unprompted. The truth is found in play: the poster on the old road (*Grown from us*), the greenhouse coat, the dish, the empty sister-dishes (*"Only one lived."*). It arrives as a revelation and uses no technical words. The Hush acts at world scale in every act. It owns the prologue (root spreading from the mountain, town lights going out), Nights 2–3 (the hollows, the orchard of cases, the tower root), the midpoint (every root on earth turns toward moths), Night 5 (the wood overrun) and the finale.

- Biological AI took the world: **central**
- Umber saves the world: **central** (all three endings settle the takeover, and each costs something)
- Moths ignored as insignificant, then underestimated: **central** (it notices only things that eat; she has no mouth; warming it is what ends her invisibility)
- Sentience (the AI, the forest, the animals): **central** for the Hush (its first picture is a moth, and the closing question asks whether that was love). The forest and Dun count as minds in what they do, which is enough.

## 2. Verdict: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES

Nothing below is a foundation problem. The spine, the antagonist, the rules and the endings hold. Every note is about clarity, and three readers tripped on the same few lines.

## 3. At the bar

- **Theme comes from the body.** "She lifts it to her face as the vole did its seed, but she has no mouth. She holds it out instead." The gameplay rule, the stealth rule and the ending all come from it.
- **An antagonist who is wrong in a way that shows something.** It was trained to flinch at every miss, then left: "each leaving hisses like a missed ball." All three readers pitied it, and none called it evil.- **The relationships play out in actions.** The wingtip tap, the shared shivering, Sorrel wrapped round his partner's case: "Then he shivers, keeping the case warm." The heart reader carried this away, and it needs no dialogue.
- **The midpoint image.** "The cloud's threads draw a moth with open wings ... the first picture the Hush has ever made." Then a seed-sized case closes "warm as a folded wing." The temptation lands before the choice does.
- **Every ending costs something.** In Spin the world wakes "thin and hungry into a world grown old." Burn buys one dance with nearly every sleeper's death. Stay is a gentle apocalypse.

## 4. Problems, ranked

1. **The sister's torch (POLISH, but all three readers stumbled).** The spine says "no one ever came back to warm it," yet she comes back with a torch and pinches it out. No reader could tell whether she meant to kill it or comfort it. *Fix:* make each action read on its own. She raises the torch, the cloud flinches the way it did at a missed ball, she sees the flinch and pinches the flame out, and the case closes before her hand reaches the dish. In the spine, change the line to "no one's warmth ever reached it."
2. **Sorrel "bare of every scale" (POLISH, two readers).** The text never shows it happen. *Fix:* on Night 6, each time he draws the lampwings off they tear away some of his coral scales. Show it once on screen and he arrives bare on Night 7.
3. **Tallow's arc has gaps (POLISH, two readers).** Night 3 says he sees only grey stone, but Night 6 says the face is "like the one on his street", and his lying down on Night 7 is sudden. *Fix:* on Night 6, write "he sees what Umber saw on his street." On Night 7, he sets the coal's red glow to his sister's case and sees her stalk grown into the dish: cutting her out would kill her. Only then does he lie down.
4. **Who wakes in Burn (POLISH).** "Only those taken days ago wake" clashes with "nothing else wakes." *Fix:* give the reason in one image. Long sleepers have grown into their stalks, as the sister has. Change the last line to "above a world where almost nothing else wakes."
5. **Too many names for one antagonist (POLISH).** The heart reader lost track among cloud, dish, root, mountain and Hush. *Fix:* use "the Hush" when the whole thing acts, "the dish" for its heart, and "root" for its reach, and drop "cloud" after the memory.
6. **The lab memory is crowded (POLISH).** It covers the ball game, the greenhouses, the closing, the hunger, the first case and the sister. The lore reader also asked why the lab closed. *Fix:* move the sister's return out of the flashback and into the room: her pinched torch lies on the floor by her case, so the player finds it rather than being told. Answer "why closed" with one more poster, pasted over the first: the greenhouses dark and the town lights on, *We grow our own now*. The town traded it away. No new rule is needed.

Smaller issues: in Spin, "mouthless, like her" slips, because the Hush feeds others and never eats. Change it to "with nothing left to hold." Take the dev note out of the player-facing outline.

## 5. Scores

Grove fit: lanterns as cocoons, glints as the forest's warmth, the carving, the shrine's thorn blade, the ember re-meant as Tallow's coal. Originality risk: a faint Hollow Knight echo (a sleeping god sealed in a vessel).

## 6. At the bar? Yes

I would put this outline next to Ori, Hollow Knight and Nine Sols as an equal. Every reader could retell it and would finish it. Each remembered a different beat. The biggest remaining gap is the lab: the one scene carrying the revelation is also the one all three readers struggled with. Lighten it and make the sister's choice clear.
