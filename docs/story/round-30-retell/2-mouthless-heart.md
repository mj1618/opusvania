# Mouthless: a retell (Ori-lover, first read)

## 1. The story in three sentences
A newborn moth called Umber, who only has a week to live, sets out because a grey living thing has grown over the world and put every person and animal to sleep in hanging cases, though it can't see moths because they have no mouths. At its heart she finds a brain that people grew in a dish and then abandoned, and when she warms it, it finally sees moths and wants to keep them all, her brother included. In the end she chooses whether to let it keep her, burn it, or spend the rest of her life warming it until it can let go.

## 2. Characters
- **Umber**: a moth with a week to live; wants to save her wood, then everyone.
- **Sorrel**: her brother; wants to dance with his partner, and later wants more time with Umber.
- **The Hush**: the grown brain; wants to feed and keep everyone so nobody ever leaves it again.
- **Dun**: a badger in root armour that serves the Hush; wants to bring it things to keep, then switches sides and guards Umber.
- **Tallow**: the last man awake; wants to burn the cases and find his sister.
- **The forest**: wants to give its warmth away.

## 3. The enemy
The Hush is a lab-grown brain that was cut off and left alone in the cold. It put the whole world to sleep in cases: fed, but nobody wakes and nobody is born. What it wants is to stop the static, the pain it feels every time someone leaves.

## 4. Endings
- **Spin**: Umber and Sorrel spend themselves warming it, the Hush cocoons itself into something new, people wake, and both moths die.
- **Stay**: Umber gets into a case and dances forever, and nothing is ever born again.
- **Burn**: the Hush dies and most sleepers die with it, and Umber gets one dance at the summit.

## 5. Confusions
- "On the skin of the cases he dances": is it a dream shown on the surface, or real? I read it twice.
- At the turn, "her" switches between the vixen and Umber within a few lines.
- In Spin, I didn't see *why* circling the rim and losing moves makes the threads hold this time, beyond the caterpillar echo.
- The lab section (poster, coat, memory) is a lot to take in at once.
- "Notices only things that eat" took me a moment to link to "mouthless".

## 6. What stays with me
The wingtip tap. Sorrel taps it in the prologue, then dies beside her, and his partner lands, taps *his* wingtip and flies home alone. **Yes**, I'd buy it and finish it for the true ending. The two moths shivering for each other carry it, even where the Hush's backstory gets dense.
