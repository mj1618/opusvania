# Mouthless: cold read (Ori fan)

## 1. The story
A pale living thing, the Hush, has sealed every person and animal on earth in sleeping cases, and it ignores moths because moths don't eat. Umber, a moth with a week to live, finds its heart in a mountain: a lab brain grown to feed the world, then switched off and left cold, so it locked everyone up where they could never leave it. She warms it, it finally sees moths, and she must choose: be kept safe forever, burn it, or spend her life teaching it to let go.

## 2. Who's who
- **Umber**: wants to save her wood, then the world.
- **Sorrel**, her brother: wants to dance with his partner, then more time with Umber.
- **The Hush (HEARTH)**: wants to keep everyone it feeds so no one leaves.
- **Dun**, a re-grown badger: serves the Hush until Umber warms it, then guards her.
- **Tallow**, last person awake: wants to burn what took his sister.
- **The forest**: wants to give its warmth away.

## 3. The enemy
A dish-grown brain that learned anything getting away hurts. Abandoned, it sealed the world for forty years: no one wakes or is born. It wants nothing ever to leave it again.

## 4. Endings
- **Spin**: every moth shivers it warm until it cocoons itself and lets go; people wake, the moths die, the forest's magic is gone.
- **Stay**: Umber joins Sorrel in a case; all are kept warm, nothing is born again.
- **Burn**: it dies and nearly every sleeper with it; Umber dances with her brother.

## 5. Confusions
- The sister is sealed years after the first child, yet hers is "the oldest stalk".
- Tallow gave Umber the coal for the mountain; why is he now burning his own way there as a boss?
- Night 3: Sorrel "flies into his fire". Burning? Entranced? Couldn't picture it.
- Sorrel is beside her through Night 3, then suddenly back in the wood on Night 4.
- Burn reads sweet (a dance) but kills nearly all humanity; tone and body count clash.
- "Hand in burned hand": whose, and when?
- The forest is cast but never acts; "forest's heart" and "forest's amber" feel like separate things.

## 6. What I'd remember
Sorrel wrapped round his partner's seed-sized case, not moving, as Umber leaves and the root closes over him. And the wingtip tap. Buy and finish? **Yes**: a tiny creature giving herself away, a wordless sibling bond, an enemy that's a wounded child. Dun guarding her lanterns made me care. But Burn needs to cost something on screen, and Tallow needs to make sense.
