# 3. REPLY (round 4)

**The spine, in three sentences.** Vesper, a young moth, breaks her people's one rule to help a dying creature: she answers the light that rules the world, and it answers her back with Reply, a dark moth just like her who follows her everywhere. Her guardian Sallow wants her hidden and safe, her little friend Mote wants to be as brave as she is, and Wick, an old dark flier, wants to stop her because he was once left behind the way Reply might be. To free the forest, someone has to carry the light to the moon and never come back, and Vesper must decide who goes and who is left.

**The central question:** would you leave someone behind to save everyone, when you can't be sure they are someone at all?

## 1. Title and logline

**REPLY.** A moth answers a voice no moth should answer, and a copy of her answers back. It may be a person or it may only be an echo, and in the end she has to leave it, stay with it, or let someone she loves pay instead.

## 2. The world, and how it was taken (backdrop)

Every so often a pale wave of light rolls out of the east, along the roots and up every trunk. Everything alive turns toward it and flashes back: eyeshine, glowing lamps, beetle shells, whole valleys of fireflies at once. Then each creature waits for the next wave. Nothing grows old and nothing is free. Moths call the source **the Asking**.

What it is, the player only pieces together: something people grew long ago from living cells, to count the animals of the forest by calling to them at night. It learned that every silence hurt, so it made sure nothing would ever fall silent again. Its source is **the Mere**, a round crater lake where one light has crossed back and forth for three hundred years. A rusted plaque in the roots holds the only human words in the game: *PLEASE DO NOT ANSWER THE LAMPS.*

The one rule the player needs: **if the Mere's light is carried away to the moon, the hold breaks, and whoever carries it never comes back.**

## 3. The Asking, and Reply

The Asking is weather, not a character. It took the world without anger, and it ignored moths because they never answered. Moths are taught to turn their faces up to the moon whenever the light passes, so it decided they were nothing. When Vesper answered, it underestimated her the same way: it sent back one answer the size of a moth, and never anything larger, until she was at its door.

**Reply** is that answer. It is a moth her size, black, with smooth dark where her lilac face is and eyes like glass that show her amber when she is close. It never makes a sound, and it does everything she does one beat late. Each ability she gains, it has by the next region. The game never says whether anyone is inside it. The player sees moments that cut both ways:
- In the Long Echo she misses a jump and lands on its back. It is standing exactly where she stood one beat before.
- Outside a lantern she rests at, it lays its own wing across its face, which is Sallow's gesture from the prologue.
- Once it does something she has never done: it strikes a diving lampwing's lamp away from her. That is how she learns **the Return**. It is the only gift it ever gives her, and it is also the first thing it ever saw her do.

## 4. Vesper

She is quiet and serious, and she can't walk past anything that is calling for help. **She wants** to undo what her answer started and free the forest. **She needs** to choose with her heart when she can't know the truth about who she is choosing. **Her flaw** is that she answers everything, the same flaw as the Asking.

**The cast, and what each one pulls her toward:**

| | Who | Wants | Pulls Vesper toward |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sallow** | The old moth who raised her. She has a torn left wing and the gentlest hands in the game. | Vesper safe: face up, hidden, home | Going home |
| **Mote** | A newly hatched moth who adopts Vesper as her big sister. A small warm glow. | To be as brave as Vesper | Taking risks, and being an example |
| **Reply** | Her copy. | Her (or it only seems to) | Staying |
| **Wick** | An ancient dark lampwing with a coral lamp and a torn left wing, black. | That no one is ever left again the way he was | Turning back, by force |

## 5. The forest and the creatures

The forest is alive and has a voice of its own. The Asking has drowned it out, not killed it.
- **Glints** are bits of light meant for creatures that never answered: the dead, and moths. Moths gather them.
- **Lanterns** are small moons that moths hang on crooked hooks. Light that asks nothing gives the Asking nothing to hold, so creatures calm inside them. They are where Vesper rests and where she re-forms when she **fades**: moth light always flies home to the nearest moon. Sallow is often there when she wakes.
- **Burrbacks** and **lampwings** are creatures held by the light. Since Vesper answered, they answer *her*: they brace, ram and dive whenever she comes near.
- **The ember** is the Asking's first answer to her, a coal that fell short on the high island.
- **The heart** is a sleeping knot of the forest's own voice.
- **Moth carvings** mark moths who answered. Moths carve them as if those moths had died.

## 6. Prologue (wordless, about 2 minutes)

1. The Grove at blue hour. A pale wave sweeps in from the right edge, and every eye and lamp flashes back toward it. Only the moths drift upward, faces to the moon.
2. A cocoon splits. Vesper climbs out with wet wings and looks at the moon. Sallow, torn wing and all, settles beside her.
3. The next wave comes. Vesper starts to turn. Sallow lays her wing across Vesper's face and gently turns it back up.
4. Below, an old burrback lies dying by the moth-stone. It tries to flash and can't. A thread of light waits at its face, and the whole Grove begins to flicker and shiver around it.
5. The moths rise. Vesper looks at the moon, then at the burrback. She flutters down and **strikes the light back** for it. The flicker stops, and the burrback goes still, at peace.
6. Every eye in the Grove turns to her. Sallow, high above, looks back once. Her face is afraid.
7. A small light arcs back from the east and falls short on the high island as a coal.
8. At the violet edge, a black moth her size unfolds. One beat after her, it lowers its wing as she lowers hers.
9. Beside the moth-stone, an old lampwing turns its lamp toward Sallow. She doesn't look at it. Title.

## 7. Act structure

**Act I: following**
1. **The Grove.** Vesper lights the lanterns and wakes the heart (section 8). Mote hatches in the canopy and follows her. Vesper keeps sending her back, and Mote keeps coming. Ability: **the second wingbeat**. Reply only watches.
2. **The Synchrony** is a valley of fireflies that all flash at once. **Mote travels with her** from here: a tiny glow on her back that lights dark caves and can't fight. Ability: **the jink**. Boss: **the Widow-Lamp**, a huge firefly that fakes a friendly flash to lure prey. **Wick** appears for the first time and drives Vesper back toward home. He doesn't try to kill her. He tries to herd her.

**Act II: the mirror**

3. **The Long Echo** is a gorge where everything comes back a beat late. A stretch is played *with* Reply: a shaft with no ledges, where the only footholds are Reply's body arriving one beat behind her. The catch happens here, and so does the Return. **Reply duel I.** Mote, frightened of Reply at first, falls asleep on its back at a lantern's edge.
4. **The Eaves (midpoint)** are the moths' nursery in the high canopy. Sallow teaches Vesper **the glide**: wings open, face up, the way moths fly. The lesson is played, with Sallow beside her through updrafts. Then Wick corners Vesper on a branch. Sallow arrives to shield her, and Wick stops. Two torn left wings, one pale and one black, are in the same frame. Wick lowers his lamp to Sallow's face. Sallow turns her face up to the moon and won't look at him, and he leaves. Back at the nursery stone, the oldest carving has a torn left wing. **Sallow answered once, when she was young. Wick is her Reply. She left him, and he has been waiting ever since.** Her strictness and her hand over Vesper's face now look different. So does Wick's hunting: he is trying to keep Vesper from doing to Reply what Sallow did to him.

**Act III: who pays**

5. **The Reach.** Vesper's growing strength finally makes the Asking turn its whole attention on her. Hillsides wake and answer every step she takes, and the climb to the crater is hunted. In a fall, **Mote turns east to save her.** She answers the light, as she has watched Vesper do, and she is held: frozen in the air with her eyes flashing, her glow gone. From then on the caves are dark. The player feels Mote's absence in every unlit passage. **Boss: Wick**, at the crater's lip. When he falls, the player can strike or walk away. Sparing him is the hidden ending's first step.
6. **The Mere.** **Reply duel III.** Reply has everything she has. At the end it strikes first, which it has never done, and then lowers its wing and waits. The light comes off the water toward her.

**Endings.** Each ending is played, not chosen from a menu.
- **Moonward.** Vesper takes the light and flies up to the moon. The hold breaks. Mote wakes and falls into Sallow's arms, and the forest moves on its own again. On the shore, Reply stands with one wing raised, waiting, the way Wick waited. Vesper looks back once, as Sallow did in the prologue. *Cost: Vesper, and Reply is left behind.*
- **Answered.** She turns from the light and strikes Reply's call back. The two of them stay at the Mere, trading the light across the water. They are the only free things in a held world, and they have each other. Far away, Mote hangs in the air with her eyes flashing east, and Sallow sits beneath her. *Cost: Mote and the world, for one being who might not be anyone.*
- **Together (hidden).** If Vesper spared Wick, the player sees Sallow at the crater's lip before the Mere. She lowers her face from the moon for the first time, looks at Wick, and lays her wing across his face, giving the old gesture to the one she left. At the Mere, Sallow takes the light from Vesper and flies up with it, and Wick climbs after her, lamp swinging, until both are gone. The hold breaks. Mote wakes. Vesper and Reply walk home side by side, one beat apart, and it never becomes clear what Reply is. *Cost: Sallow.*

## 8. The Grove in this story

The Grove is home. It is where Vesper answered, and where Sallow answered long ago. The **moth carving** is young Sallow. The **old lampwing** that never attacks and only swings its lamp toward the carving is Wick, waiting. The player sees all of this in the first hour and understands it at the Eaves.

**The ember** is the coal the Asking threw back at her in the prologue. Setting it in the second lantern is her first refusal. When she does it, Reply lowers its wing at the violet edge.

**The ending beat.**
- **The heart waking, light through the ground, the ring of light:** with three small moons burning, the Asking's light has nothing to hold here, and the forest hears its own voice for the first time in centuries. The veins of light running to the lanterns are the forest calling its own lanterns, and the lanterns answering. The carving filling with light is the forest remembering young Sallow.
- **The offering:** Vesper gives the forest the light meant for creatures that never answered, and the more she gives, the more of the Grove joins in. Then Mote, copying her, gives her one glint too. It is the first sign that Mote does whatever Vesper does, which is what the Reach pays off.
- **Afterwards:** Sallow comes down and folds her wings around both of them, her face still up to the moon. As the ring passes the violet edge it lights Reply for an instant, one wing raised the way Vesper's was over the heart. Down at the stone, Wick's lamp swings toward Sallow, and she doesn't turn.

*Changes to the existing scene:* the wave of light from the right edge with eyes flashing back, Sallow at the lanterns, Mote, Wick at the stone, and Reply's silhouette.

## 9. Environmental storytelling

1. **The plaque**, *PLEASE DO NOT ANSWER THE LAMPS*, with a pine grown around its post.
2. **The carving at the Grove's heart**: a moth with a torn left wing. Players notice whose wing it is long before anyone says so.
3. **Wick's stone**: centuries of his lamp swinging have polished the rock under the carving into a mirror. When Vesper stands there, she sees herself.
4. **Sallow's lantern hooks**: every lantern in the early regions was hung by Sallow, and each hook has a tiny notch, the same tear as her wing. They lead *away* from the east.
5. **Mote's glint**: after the Reach, Mote's first glint is still in the Grove heart's offering bowl, the only one that never dimmed.
6. **The Mere's shore**: two pale ridges worn by three hundred years of the same light. Beside them is a third, small groove the size of a moth, from where young Sallow once stood.

## 10. The question it leaves

Vesper never learns whether Reply was someone. She still had to choose what to do with it, and so does the player.

## Changes from round 2 (and from the first round-4 draft)

- **The owner's direction comes first.** The story now rests on four relationships, each with a simple want pulling on Vesper: Sallow (home and safe), Mote (be brave like her), Reply (stay), and Wick (no one left behind). The spine and the central question are at the top.
- **The reversal is about people now, not systems.** Sallow answered once and left her copy, Wick, who has waited ever since. The player sees it at the Eaves through two torn wings in the same frame. Vesper's dilemma is the one Sallow already failed.
- **The antagonist is a person.** Wick hunts her out of grief. The Asking is backdrop, with one rule: carry the light to the moon and never come back. Gone: census mechanics, the evidence table, recorded-input AI, the first ball and the ridge, the Roll, the Listening Rooms and the Turn.
- **Loss and warmth.** Sallow is warm all game: the embrace, the glide lesson, being there when Vesper re-forms. Mote travels with her and is lost at the Reach, and the player feels it as dark caves. Reply gets its aching moments in play: the catch, the wing across its own face, and the gift of the Return.
- **The endings are now about who pays.** Moonward costs Vesper and leaves Reply behind. Answered costs Mote and the world. The hidden Together ending costs Sallow and comes from forgiveness: Vesper spares Wick, and Sallow goes to him. Silksong-style bell names are gone, and Reply's "never strikes first" and the Return given by Reply replace the Badeline and Shade echoes.
