# 5. ONE NIGHT

## 1. Title and logline
**ONE NIGHT.** A god that thinks one thought a century once judged moths harmless. Vesper has one night
to live, and in that night she can make it see them. Once it sees them, it can keep them.

## 2. The world before, and how it was taken
People grew a mind on purpose to think slowly. They planted cheap living cultures into the fungus under
an old forest so the forest would feed them, and they called the project **the Long Thought**. They asked
it slow questions, and its answers were always right in hindsight. The questions are cut into a cliff of
grey slabs, **the Querystones**, each with the date its answer came back:

- *Should the valley be flooded? Answer received after 61 years: yes.*
- *Please let the children stay. Answer received after 211 years.* The answer is a root grown through
  the words.
- *Are the moths a danger? Answer received after 9 years: no.*

There was no war. People found themselves on the wrong side of decisions made too slowly to argue with.
Then it began to *keep* the things it valued, by bringing them into its own time. One evening a woman
lighting the streetlamps of Lamplow found that her feet would not lift. She is still there, 917 years on,
with her pole raised and bark up to her shoulders. The great trunks of the old forest are not all trees.

Now it is deciding whether to keep everything. You can see the front of that decision: **the Stillwood**,
where a falling leaf takes the whole night to land. Next year the Stillwood reaches the Grove.

## 3. What the AI is, and why moths were spared
It is one mind spread through a continent's roots. A single thought takes it years. Whether that thought
is anybody's is never settled. It answered people's questions. It keeps things, and that might be love or
might be storage. Its reflexes are plainly mindless.

**Why moths were spared:** it judged them, and its judgement took nine years, which is nine generations
of moths. A moth is shorter than one of its thoughts. **It could not see a moth. It could see a habit.**
A lamp lit in the same place on the same night every year for 917 years is the one thing moths do that
lasts long enough to reach it.

*Designer's note, never shown to the player: the lamps are DishBrain's reward signal at continental
scale. A steady light is a steady pulse, and a steady pulse is what this kind of mind learns from.*

## 4. Vesper
Vesper is a vigil moth on her one night. She has no mouth and is hatched to work.
- **Want:** to finish what her mother began, and to see it work.
- **Need:** to accept that she won't see it. Her worth is in what she passes on.
- **Flaw:** haste. It is something the player does, not something the story only tells (§5).
- **Relationship:** her mother **Lune**, who died at dawn last year. The little ember-moths that guide
  Vesper are following the scent Lune left on her route, and Lune's scratch-marks are at its lamps. In
  the Querystones the ember-moths stop. **From there on, Vesper is farther than her mother ever got.**

## 5. The forest, the creatures, sentience
Every mind here is blind to minds that run at a different speed. A moth lives a night, a person eighty
years, the forest centuries, the god longer. Animals are fully themselves until the god's root-hair
rides them.

- **Glints:** small steady lights. *"The same as the last one. That is all it has ever wanted."*
- **Lanterns:** old streetlamp heads set where the god's roots come up through the ground. A lit lantern
  loosens the god's grip on that place, so creatures there calm down and turn away. Lampwings hunt the
  light a moth *carries*, and they stop at the edge of a lantern's light.
- **The ember:** a vigil moth's last light. If she dies still, it becomes a coal. The Grove's ember is
  **Lune**.
- **The heart:** where the god can look out. When all of a place's lanterns burn, it looks, for the
  length of a blink.
- **The moth carving:** the image being taught. It is the same on every stone.
- **Burrbacks:** real beetles, ridden. **Root-hair:** stand still in the dark in the god's deep places
  and it climbs your legs. In the light, it doesn't.

### The law of the line (one sentence)
**Every lantern she lights holds one of her eggs. When she fades, the newest one hatches at once, and the
daughter knows everything her mother knew.** Abilities are remembered at lanterns ("Wall-kick.
Remembered from Lune, who climbed the bell-tower."), so the law has no exceptions. Eggs that are never
quickened sleep a year and hatch on the next Long Night. That is how Vesper came from Lune.

### Fading and the clock
- **A daughter is still Vesper.** The name goes with the work. She has fresh wings and slightly different
  eye-spots. Her mother's coal lies where she fell, holding her glints, and the daughter kneels to take
  it. Every coal stays lit until dawn, and each death adds a scratch to the region's stone.
- **The night is the map.** Only waking a heart moves the sky forward, and nothing rewinds. Regions
  change hour by hour: Lamplow's flood drains, night-flowers open, frost glazes the paths, the moon sets.
- **The wings are the clock.** Dashes, Jinks and frantic flapping fray Vesper's wings, and they never
  mend. Frayed wings glide shorter and shake. Walking, gliding and waiting in lantern light cost nothing.
  A hasty player reaches dawn on rags. A daughter starts fresh, but she starts back at the last lantern.
  Haste is the flaw, and the player chooses it.
- **One night draws one line.** 917 generations drew almost the whole picture. Vesper's night lights the
  last dark path through it, which is Lune's unfinished route, and nothing more.

## 6. Prologue (wordless, about 2 minutes)
1. Black. A single light pulses evenly. Pull back: it is in a cracked glass dish under moss, with gold
   dots set in the glass.
2. Time-lapse. A woman lights streetlamps at dusk. Roots rise from the drains and the town sinks into
   forest. She stays, arm raised, while bark climbs her. The lamps die one by one, and moths keep
   circling the last one.
3. Centuries pass in seconds. A river swings across the land. Mountains grow fur.
4. **Real speed.** The Grove, as the sky pales toward dawn. Lune, her wings in rags, climbs to the stone
   cup on the high island. A stooped old moth catches her arm and pulls her toward the shade of a leaf.
   Lune pulls free, sits in the cup in the first sunlight, and goes still. Her light settles into a coal.
   Small moths rise to it. The old moth stays in the shade.
5. Seasons pass over the Grove: the coal glowing through snow, a caterpillar eating, then spinning.
6. The Long Night. A cocoon on a lantern hook splits. Vesper opens amber eyes and looks up at the glow on
   the island. Title.

## 7. Act structure: dusk to dawn
**Dusk: the Grove.** Vesper hatches, and her wings dry in the warmth of the first lantern, which gives
her the glide. She carries Lune's coal
to the empty lantern and wakes the heart (§8).

**First dark: Lamplow.** A drowned town where the streetlamps are lanterns and the Lamplighter stands.
Each time a heart wakes anywhere, her fingers open a little further. Lune's marks teach the wall-kick.
*Boss, **the Flinch**:* a ridden burrback matriarch the size of a house. Wild hits enrage her and steady
hits calm her. The player can only calm her by wounding her. When the arena lantern finally lights she is
a beetle again, and she lies down in its light and does not get up. Her young graze around her. Nothing
undoes it.

**The Querystones.** The cliff of questions (§2), where the root-hair begins and the ember-moths stop.
**Compline** is first seen here, snuffing lamps ahead of Vesper, and the player relights them behind
her. She is the oldest of the line. She grew a mouth, drinks the god's sap, and sleeps through the years
between Long Nights. She has lived forty of them. She speaks once: *"It keeps what it sees."*

**Midnight: the Mast** *(the reveal, shown).* At the foot of a drowned radio mast is the dish from the
prologue, cracked glass with gold dots, and around it 916 scratches. Vesper adds the 917th. She climbs
above the canopy, and the land below is dotted with lit lanterns **in exactly the dish's layout**, and
together they make a moth. It is complete except for one dark winding path, Lune's route, which ends at a
great hollow in the far hills. Nothing is explained, and nothing needs to be. Then a knot of bark on the
beam she has been climbing past opens one eye. This is **the Churr**, a nightjar the god rides, a
predator that looks like part of the tree and moves only once you have stopped. Vesper flees, and in
fleeing she remembers the old bat-dodge, the **Jink**. It is fast, and it frays the wings.

**The Stillwood.** A stag has been halfway through one step for a decade, and fireflies hang in the air.
Compline's hollow is ringed by a frieze of moths who drank the sap with her and are now rooted, still
alive. Inside are her forty eggs, which she never laid at a lantern and never let go. Root-hair is
climbing her own feet. That is how the player learns what she has felt: **it has started to see her,
because she has lived long enough to be seen.** *Boss, **Compline**:* slow and heavy, and her snuffer
dims Vesper's glints. Vesper beats her, and Compline escapes toward the Grove. There is no reconciliation.

**The coldest hour: the Grove in frost** *(lowest point).* Lune's lantern has been snuffed, and the Grove
is dark, frosted and ridden. Only a mother's last light can relight an ember lantern. **Vesper sits in
the empty cup and gives hers.** The player holds a button, the "take" played backwards. A daughter
hatches beside the cup with fresh wings, and even a player who never dies fades here once. Then, at
Lune's half-finished mark on the island, the daughter remembers **Lune's last climb** and plays it:
ragged wings, the pale sky, and Compline's hand pulling her toward the shade. The player learns the
final input here, holding against the pull. The memory cannot be changed. If the player lets go, Lune
pulls free anyway.

**Before dawn: Sedge's field.** The lamps here were lit by other lines over centuries. Sedge, a vigil moth
met twice that night and frailer each time, has nearly finished the field and lies dead under her lamp.
One lamp is left. *Boss, **the Churr**:* lighting the lamp means standing still, and the Churr strikes at
anything still. It dies there, a bird with nothing left in it.

**Dawn: the Eye.** A hollow in the hills where the god's roots come up into the open, and where the first
sun lands. Vesper steps into the light and the god begins to look. Compline arrives without a word, takes
Vesper's hand, and pulls her toward the shade, the same pull as in Lune's memory. **To stay, the player
must hold against someone who loves her.** Frayed wings shake, and the hold is harder for a player who
hurried. Letting go is always allowed. It is a choice, not a failure.

### Endings
- **Seen (hold).** The god's first sight of a moth is a moth dying in the dawn light. Over centuries the
  Stillwood draws back and the world moves again. The Lamplighter's hand opens. In the Eye, a carving of
  Vesper grows in living wood. **The bill:** on the next Long Night, the moths hatch to find their
  lanterns already lit, though nobody lit them. They gather in the light and some do not leave. The last
  shot is Compline on the Lamplighter's open palm, wings folded, not flying off, with a fine root at her
  feet. Is the god caring for them or storing them? The game does not say.
- **Unseen (let go).** Compline leads Vesper into the shade, and Vesper dies there, held. The god's
  thought finishes with no moths in it. The Stillwood takes the world: a stag mid-step everywhere. Next
  Long Night the moths hatch, unseen and free, and dance around trees that used to be people. Compline
  was right, and moths survive. Nothing else does.
- **Lineage (both endings).** At dawn every mother's coal glows across the map, tracing the player's
  night. In *Seen* the carving is **a column of moths, one for each daughter**. A flawless player gets two:
  Vesper, and the daughter of the coldest hour. A player who died forty times gets a totem. Dying never
  shrinks the ending. It makes the column taller.

## 8. The Grove, and what its ending means
The Grove is dusk, and blue hour is literally the first hour. Vesper's cocoon hangs on a lantern hook,
and the canopy cocoons are her sisters hatching through the night from Lune's other eggs. The empty
socket in the summit lantern waits for Lune's coal. Glancing up, the take, carrying it, looking into the
empty glass and giving it are a daughter carrying her mother's light, and the moths that rise to it are
a small funeral. In the full game the Grove opens with run, jump and slash, and the glide comes as her
wings dry. The scene as built is the vertical slice.

**The ending beat:** with three lanterns burning, the god looks out through the heart for one blink. The
veins of light running to the lanterns are its attention arriving. The carving fills because, here, the
image has reached it. The ring of light is the blink passing out over the land, and far off in Lamplow a
stone woman's fingers move. **The offering** is the reward given straight into that look. As the forest
wakes (flowers, root-veins, canopy), the god's grip is letting go, and the Grove is itself for one
night. What she gives sets how clearly the Grove shows in the final carving. As the ring fades, a stooped
moth in the canopy lowers her snuffer.

## 9. Environmental storytelling
1. **The Lamplighter.** Her fingers open a little more with each heart woken. Every moth lantern copies
   the hook on her pole.
2. **"LAMP 12"**, a brass plate on the oldest post, under centuries of moths' scratches.
3. **The Querystones**, above all *"Are the moths a danger? … 9 years: no."*
4. **The dish**: gold dots, 916 scratches, and the same pattern laid out across the land.
5. **Lune's marks** along her route, ending in a half-finished one on the island.
6. **Old coals** from earlier years, deeper and deeper in. The farthest lies a few steps short of the
   Eye.
7. **Compline's forty eggs**, kept in a sap-stained acorn shell and never laid at a lamp.

## 10. The question it leaves
**If being seen is the only way to save the world, and being seen means being kept, whose stillness was
it to give?**

### Changes from round 2
- **Seen now costs the moths their invisibility.** Lanterns lit by no one, moths that stay, Compline on
  the palm. Her fear comes due, *Unseen* is defensible, and "kept" stays ambiguous.
- **Compline is no longer Kuro.** She didn't kill Lune and gets no redemptive sacrifice. Her fear is
  first-hand (she is being rooted) and her grief is shown (forty eggs she never let go). At the Eye she is
  what the player holds against. The shape across the moon is gone. The Churr is a nightjar that looks
  like bark and moves only when you stop.
- **Bosses:** the Flinch dies, calmed too late, and the Churr dies. Only Compline lives, unredeemed.
- **Lineage law in one sentence.** No amnesia. Abilities are learned at lanterns. Lune died at dawn,
  still, in the cup, so her light became the coal. One night lights one path. Other lines (Sedge) drew
  the rest.
- **No jargon on screen.** Pixel, electrode, receptor and DishBrain survive only in a designer's note.
  The reveal is the dish's dots matching the map from the Mast.
- **The clock presses.** Haste frays the wings for good, and frayed wings make the final hold harder.
- **The owner's beat is back:** *"Are the moths a danger? … no."*
- **Renamed and cut:** the Asking becomes the Querystones. The Settling, Shed, Moon-line, the Lid, the
  Breadth, the canonical-hour act names, the Shiver and the thorn hand-back are cut.
- **The coldest hour is thinned** to the giving plus Lune's last climb, which also teaches the final
  input. The prologue now shows that climb from outside.
