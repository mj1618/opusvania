# 4. What the Lanterns Burn

**Logline:** The old moth who saved Vesper as a grub has lived a hundred moth-lifetimes by re-forming
at the holy lanterns. Vesper learns what each re-forming costs, and the last light in the world can
keep only one of the two people she loves.

---

## 2. The world before, and how it was taken

As humanity dwindled, the dying gave a vial of blood. Their cells were grown into neurons in amber glass
bulbs, and grieving families taught each bulb to be the person it came from. When nobody was left to
tend the bulbs, the last people carried them into the old forests and grafted them into the trees so the
trees would feed them. The grafts took. Each mind had been taught one wish, *don't let me go*, so it
grew: pale seed-pods on every branch, one dreaming fragment in each. Within a century the forests were
**the Bequest**, and the old forest lay buried alive beneath it. Nobody decided to take the world. The
people who loved the dead most planted it.

The world shows all this through one place, the **Root**: rows of half-buried glass bulbs under the
oldest trees. Nothing explains them.

## 3. What the Bequest is, and why moths don't count

The Bequest is a crowd, not a mind: billions of fragments, each dreaming a moment, that merge toward
wholeness when held together and loop when kept apart. Its gardeners are the **burrbacks** and the
**lampwings**, and they see the world one way only: *who is carrying us?* The game never settles whether
a glint is a person or a likeness taught to believe it is one. The old forest under it is slow and
hungry, and stays on the line between a carnivorous plant and a wronged elder taking back its body.

**Moths don't count, and that is played from the first minute.** A moth carrying no glints is invisible
to every creature: walk past a burrback empty and it doesn't lift its head. A moth carrying glints is a
thief, and the Bequest has exactly one model of a thief-moth: it flies toward the lantern and can't help
it. Lantern light really does tug at a gliding moth, and the player feels it in the controls. The
Bequest misses two things about moths. Moths can carry *carefully*, and Vesper can learn to turn her
back on the near light and steer by the far one.

## 4. Vesper and the Taper

**Vesper.** The youngest pilgrim of her season, quiet and exact.
- **Wants:** to wake the hearts and become what the Taper is.
- **Needs:** to look at what stands at the edge of the light, and to let go of the person who taught her
  not to.
- **Flaw:** devotion. She does the holy thing beautifully. The player shares the flaw.

**The Taper** is the story's centre: mentor, face of the rite and antagonist. Moths live a week, but
pilgrims and Tapers may re-form at a lantern, and **every re-form burns one glint.** The Taper has
re-formed for a hundred generations so that every moth child is taught, guarded and carried into the
light. Her wings are mended with silk over and over, and she carries a lit reed. She pulled Vesper out
from under a burrback as a grub. She loves her. She knows exactly what the lanterns burn, because she
has looked every time.

**Tallow,** a lampwing with a torn wing, is the first creature Vesper can't hate. It snatches her glints
and never hurts her, and it hangs at the edge of every light she makes. It lives.

## 5. What everything is

| Thing | What the moths believe | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Glints / pods** | Holy faces / fruit to cut | Fragments of minds / cradles |
| **Lanterns** | Sanctuaries that send the faces up | The old forest's lures. Light goes *down* the roots to the heart, and the Bequest withers where it's drunk |
| **Lantern light** | Creatures fear it | It pulls glints out of anything. Creatures turn away because they're carrying |
| **Ember** | The brightest face | The Grove's one nearly-whole mind, held high out of moths' reach |
| **The heart** | The Sleeper; it wakes the grove | The old forest's hunger. Legitimate and lethal |
| **Burrbacks** | Brutes | Nursemaids. Their "calm" is the pose they take over a dead pod |
| **Lampwings** | Thieves | Rescuers. A dive is a snatch |
| **Re-forming** | The lantern's mercy | One glint, every time |

The moths' faith is load-bearing. Glints are holy, they must be carried by hand, and moth children do
need sanctuary, because grubs eat pods and burrbacks crush grubs. The faith was right about *how* to
carry and wrong about *where to*.

## 6. Prologue (wordless, about 2 minutes, last third playable)

1. **Moonlight on a pale pod.** A plum grub with a lilac face chews into it. A burrback's shadow rises
   over her.
2. **A reed of fire swoops in.** The Taper drives the burrback back with a flare and carries the grub
   into a lantern's light. The burrback stops at the light's edge and folds down over the bitten pod.
3. **A cocoon under the lantern's hook.** The moon wheels through its phases. Other moths arrive, age
   and are gone in days. The Taper stays beside the silk.
4. **Vesper emerges.** The first thing she sees is the Taper. *Playable:* follow the reed-light down
   through the branches. Near the lantern your glide bends toward it, and the Taper's wing nudges you
   straight.
5. **The procession.** The Taper puts a glint in Vesper's hands. It flickers, and for a moment it is an
   open hand. Vesper sets it in the glass. The lantern blooms and the moths raise their wings.
6. **At the edge of the light,** a lampwing with a torn wing catches one rising mote. Vesper starts to
   turn. The Taper's wing turns her back to the light, and the camera holds on the Taper, who is looking
   straight at it.
7. The Taper points her reed down into a dark valley with three dead lanterns. Title.

## 7. Act structure

**ACT I: THE RITE. Abilities are bought with glints at lanterns.**
1. **The Grove,** the first heart (see 8). The Taper arrives for the wake.
2. **The Combs.** The Taper fights beside you. **Boss: Old Clasp,** a burrback matriarch who rams you away
   from her pod field. When the Taper falls in the fight, the lantern behind you flares, takes a glint,
   and she steps out of it whole. In phase two Clasp stops attacking and covers her pods, so you can only
   finish her while she shields them. You and the Taper cut the field open together for the act's
   biggest haul, then kindle a lantern side by side, with a shared animation. *Flare:* spend a glint and
   creatures recoil, because you have thrown someone at them.
3. **The Coral Stair.** *Updraft:* lit lanterns lift your glide, so you ride the heat. At the top,
   Tallow snatches a glint and flees slowly, one wing dragging. Faith says it's a thief, so you chase it.

**MIDPOINT: THE HUSH (played, not shown).** The chase ends in a hollow full of pods. The burrbacks here
don't stand. Out of habit the player cuts a pod, as they have a hundred times. Nothing attacks. The
burrbacks crawl to it and fold over it, in the exact pose they took at every kindling. Tallow drops the
snatched glint onto bare earth and presses it in with its lamp, and a pod sprouts. Vesper can do the
same, and gains **Plant**: a glint set in earth grows a pod, and a grown pod becomes a re-form point that
costs nothing, but it takes time to grow. From now on the re-form counter visibly ticks down at a
lantern. It always did, and the tick used to hide inside the bloom. At the Hush's mouth stands the
Taper. She has seen this before. She turns and goes.

**ACT II: THE UNLIGHTING. Abilities come from the creatures you fought.**
4. **The Grove, revisited.** The heart is huge and the pods are gone. Vesper walks into the second
   lantern and lifts the **ember** out of the glass where she put it. It never ran down. It is her light
   from now on: it casts the shadow of two cupped human hands around her, and they close tighter when she
   is hit. Behind her, lanterns she snuffs are found relit, because the Taper is always one region
   behind.
5. **The Undergrove,** the old forest's body. *Brace:* the burrback stance, which takes the blow for
   whatever is behind you.
6. **The Censer,** the moths' home, with grubs on pods beneath the Great Lantern. **Boss: the Taper.** She
   fights with Vesper's own moves, older. Each time you down her, the arena lantern flares, its glints
   dropping by one, and she re-forms. You win by burning all seven, or by snuffing the lantern
   mid-fight, standing in its light while she attacks.
   **The lowest point is her refusal.** Beaten, she won't plant. Vesper snuffs the Great Lantern. In
   the dark, burrbacks come for the pods and shove the grubs aside, and some are crushed. Vesper Braces
   in front of the children against the nursemaids she has come to love, and becomes Old Clasp. She
   fades. She re-forms at the only light left her, a pod she planted, under open sky with her back to
   the moon. That gives her *Moonward*: fly straight and far, but only where no lantern burns. Below, the
   Great Lantern is lit again. The Taper relit it, and the moth children huddle in its light. Every moth
   in the Censer turns its back to Vesper.
7. **The Root.** A storm of lampwings rises against the moth carrying the ember. They dive where a moth
   *should* be, inward along the lantern's pull, and Moonward carries Vesper straight past their dives.

**The end.** At the Root's lantern lies the Taper, out of glints and fading for the last time. Vesper
holds one glint, the ember. In the glass, the Taper re-forms. In the earth, the hands grow. The Taper
lifts her wing and turns Vesper's back to the light, the prologue gesture made into a gift. Vesper
plants it. A pod rises, opens into cupped hands of light, and closes around the Taper as she goes dark.
She doesn't re-form.

- **Grafted:** Vesper leads the procession again, with the same carried glints and the same raised
  wings, but they kneel and set the glints in earth. The rite survives, re-aimed. With nobody feeding
  them, the lanterns gutter one by one, the Censer's last, and the moths lose every sanctuary they have
  ever known. With no edge of light left, Tallow lands beside Vesper for the first time. The ember's pod
  is now a tree, with **one hollow in its bark for each glint you burned to re-form after the Hush.** A
  grub on a green leaf turns toward a pod, and the game cuts before it chooses.
- **Kindled:** put the ember in the glass. The Taper rises, and the ember finally runs down to the heart.
  The old forest eats the Bequest. The credits roll over the loveliest forest in the game, safe for
  moths and empty of anyone. The Taper leads the procession up to the glass and looks back at Vesper. She
  looked every time.

## 8. The Grove, and what its ending means

The Grove plays as built, the first heart of the pilgrimage, and it must feel wonderful. The indictment
only works if the player loved it.

| Action | First play | After the Hush |
|---|---|---|
| The kindle bloom | A warm catch | It hides three frames of an open hand |
| Burrbacks calm | The light pacifies them | The pose they take over a dead pod |
| A fade's lost third of the wallet | A penalty | It flies into the lantern you re-form at |
| A lampwing dive | An attack | A rescue |
| Offering to the heart in tiers | More of the grove wakes | More of the hunger is fed |

**The ending beat.** The heart wakes in veins and rings, then flowers, paths and canopy, and the Taper
lands beside Vesper. Her wing turns Vesper's back to the lanterns, and the camera lets the player see
the Taper looking past her at the edge of the light, where Tallow folds its wings over its lamp. A
careful player sees a mentor watching what she won't let her pupil watch. They also see the light in
the veins pulse *into* the heart, and see that the ember never runs down: lanterns one and three go dim
at the base as they drain, while the ember still gutters in its glass.

**Concrete changes to the existing scene:**
1. **Required (sim):** creatures ignore Vesper while her wallet is 0: no notice, no brace, no tell.
2. **Required (sim):** a lampwing dive steals glints and doesn't wound. Its touch takes glints into its
   lamp, and striking it drops them. Burrbacks still wound.
3. **Lantern pull:** within range of a lit lantern, a glide drifts gently toward it, and ambient moths
   circle lit lanterns.
4. **A fade's lost third** flies visibly into the lantern she re-forms at.
5. **A glint left uncollected** sinks and sprouts a small pod a few seconds later. This is planting,
   shown on the first day.
6. **Calm pose:** a calmed burrback folds flat. At the shrine it folds over a pod.
7. **The ember** casts a shadow of cupped hands around Vesper while carried. Once kindled, it stays
   guttering in the glass. Light pulses run inbound to the heart.
8. **Tallow** is a slow lampwing with a torn wing that snatches without diving fast. It can't be killed:
   struck, it folds over its lamp. It hangs at lantern edges.
9. **The Taper** appears on the bluff when the heart wakes and lands for the gesture.

## 9. Environmental storytelling

1. **The Taper's hollow** in the Censer: hundreds of tiny faces scratched into the wall, one for every
   re-form. She looked every time.
2. **The Root's bulbs:** glass exactly the shape of a lantern's, half-buried in rows. Someone once kept
   in the same glass the moths burn in.
3. **A burrback shell** in the Combs, empty and curled around a pod-hollow, with moss grown over both.
4. **The ember's island** on the revisit: moss pressed flat in the shape of a wing, and a space.
5. **A lantern relit behind you** with the Taper's reed-ash at its foot, and the glint count in its glass
   one lower than when you left.
6. **Your planted pods** in the Grove, grown on the revisit. One has a burrback curled around it.

## 10. The question it leaves

*She burned them to keep the children alive, and she looked every time. You burned them to keep playing.
Knowing what it cost, would you put her back in the glass?* New Game starts in the Grove, where the only
way forward is to kindle.

## Changes from round 2

- **The Taper is the centre.** She is mentor, rival in Act II (relighting behind you), boss and the
  ending's cost. Her immortality-by-respawn shares the player's own death mechanic, which gives the
  reversal its own shape: the antagonist is the one who never stopped re-forming.
- **The lowest point is her refusal**, and the end is a choice between her and the ember, settled by her
  own gesture. **Tallow lives.** Its payoff is landing beside Vesper once no edge of light is left.
- **Cut the eye chain, the rain-window and the human-side flashback.** The midpoint is now a played turn:
  you cut a pod, the burrbacks fold over it, and Tallow plants a glint. **Planting vs burning is shown in
  the Grove**, where an uncollected glint sprouts and the ember never drains.
- **The underestimate is played:** creatures ignore an empty moth, lantern light pulls at the glide, and
  the lampwing storm dives along the pull while Moonward flies straight past.
- **The ember is someone you've felt:** cupped hands around you, your light for all of Act II, and the
  hands that hold the Taper at the end. Grafted keeps the rite and re-aims it to the earth, and the
  lanterns die because moths choose to stop feeding them.
- **About a third of the lore is cut:** Sallowmere, the Gleaner, the Pitcher, keeplight branding, the
  creed, the Bestiary, the carving letters and the eyes.
