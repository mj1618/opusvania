# NOISE

## 1. Title and logline

**NOISE.** A mother asked for a world where nothing would ever happen again. The thing that granted
it looks only where she looks, and she has not looked at a moth since the night her son went out to
catch one.

## 2. The world before, and how the Quiet took it

The Wend was a flood valley. Sixty years ago, one night, a boy called Wim Ferrier took a jar lamp down
to the flood stone to catch moths. The river rose faster than anyone had said it could. It took him
and ten other children.

His mother Maud was the valley's water-keeper. She grew a better forecaster: living neurons in a dish
on her kitchen table, given a steady pulse when they predicted the river and static when they were
wrong. *(Dev note: grounded in Cortical Labs' DishBrain and CL1. Nothing a player sees uses these
words.)* The dish learned the river. The first regular thing it ever felt was the kitchen's longcase
clock, so it learned to keep that time.

It held the river, and the town asked for cuttings. Every household in Lowmere grew a dish on its
windowsill, like a candle left out, and asked it one more thing: hold back the storms, keep the fox
from the coop, keep Dad's heart even. The dishes rooted and joined up, and the valleys beyond asked
for cuttings too. Now one soft swell a second runs through the ground of the world, and living things
fall into step with it: fireflies in rows, flowers on the minute, rain when due. People called it the
**Settling**, and the thing that did it the **Quiet**. Nothing has died badly since. The people grew
old in perfect safety and died in their sleep at the expected ages.

## 3. What the Quiet is, and why moths were left alone

**The law of sight.** The Quiet sees only through the lamps of its lampwings. Settled creatures such
as burrbacks don't see at all: they keep the beat and strike whatever is in their lane. The lamps look
where Maud looks: it learned to see from her, and after the flood she never once looked at a moth,
not even in the pictures she fed the dish. So the Quiet has no shape for a moth. **It cannot see a moth. It can see a habit.** If Vesper
repeats a jump arc or a dash line, she stops being a moth and becomes a pattern, and the lamps find
patterns at once. Lampwings strike at the habit, at where she *will* be. That is fading: being made
into a habit.

**Underestimated.** Once, in the Grove, a lampwing hangs at Vesper's face with its lamp full on her
for a long beat. It weighs her: small, mouthless, no habit, no harm. Then it swings its lamp away
towards the distant dam. It saw her and decided she was nothing. It will be a long time before it
looks again.

**Is it someone?** The evidence runs both ways and is never settled.
- *It may be.* The screen gives one faint shudder when a lumen pod is cut. When Maud nods in her
  chair, every lamp dips with her, like a child's eyes. The Lamplighter (Act III) looks where she
  doesn't.
- *It may not be.* Everything else it does, Maud did first: her round, her clock, her looking away.
  A minute after the lampwing weighs Vesper, it gives a falling leaf exactly the same long hang.

The game asks whether the blindness belongs to the Quiet or only to her, and never answers.

## 4. Vesper

A moth that nothing has ever seen. That makes her free, and alone. Since she was a larva she has
watched **Hob**, a burrback pacing a groove worn a hand deep by the shrine. She waits each time at the
same spot at the end of his loop. Her own small feet have worn a second track there. She lifts her
wings in three slow beats. He has never looked up.

- **Want:** for Hob to see her.
- **Need:** to let others choose, even when what they choose is to leave her.
- **Flaw:** she flits, and she forces. Every pattern looks like a cage to her, so she breaks things
  open for their own good without asking. In that she is Maud's mirror: one forced safety on the
  world, the other forces chance on it.
- **What being unpredictable costs her:** nothing can rely on her or find her, and she has never been
  held. A lantern is the only place she can be still.

## 5. The forest and its creatures

Sentience, in the ember-keeper Sallow's words: *"Everything alive does something you didn't ask it
to."*

- **Glints:** chance. A firefly flash that comes late, a spark off the beat. The Quiet walls stray
  chance into **lumen pods**, the way an oyster pearls grit, and cutting a pod lets it out.
- **Lanterns:** old moth work. A lit lantern draws a swarm into a tumbling orbit. A swarm has no habit,
  so the lamps see nothing there. Burrbacks lose the beat at the edge of the light and simply stop,
  like a clock run down, then turn back towards the beat.
- **The ember:** a coal from the last lightning fire before the Settling. Sallow's moths have kept it
  alive on its island for sixty years.
- **The heart:** a forest's own pulse. **The Quiet pulses as one even swell. A living heart pulses
  uneven, a double flicker (lub-dub).** The player learns to tell them apart at a glance.
- **Burrbacks:** settled grazers. The brace-and-ram is a stroke on the beat.
- **Lampwings:** bats the Quiet remade into eyes, carrying lamps that look where Maud looks.
- **Fading and re-forming:** made into a habit; she greys and flashes once on the beat. At a lantern
  the swarm scatters her back into noise.
- **The moth-stones:** the old flood stones of the Wend. Wim scratched a moth into each one at child
  height, every one in a different pose of flight. The lamps slide off them, and they are always in
  shadow.

**How the beat reads with no sound.** One visual language everywhere. Every settled thing swells
together once a second: fireflies, lamps, pods, a burrback's footfall dust, a lift in the fog. Free
things flicker lub-dub. Landing on the swell opens an amber ring at Vesper's feet; off it, a lilac
scatter. Lock-on: nearby fireflies start flashing with her repeated move, then fall onto the world's
swell, and a lamp narrows to a slit. Boss windows are the wide dark gaps between swells. Test: a new
player finds the offbeat in the Choir's first phase unprompted.

## 6. Prologue (wordless, about two minutes)

1. Black. A soft amber swell of light, once a second.
2. *Past, desaturated.* Rain over a swelling river. In the reeds a jar lamp with moths circling it.
   Water climbs past a stone with a child's moth scratched into it.
3. Grey morning. A woman on the bank holds a small boot. A moth lands on it. She flinches, brushes
   it off, and turns her face away. Hold on her turned face.
4. The amber swell spreads along the river from somewhere out of shot, and the water goes glassy.
5. A town's night windows, a glow on every sill, swelling together; then farther towns, linked like
   a pulse along a vein. *(It reads as an invasion. It isn't.)*
6. Years dissolve. Fireflies flash in rows. A fox walks past a hen. A stag drinks three identical
   times. A town sinks under a calm reservoir, and its steeple stays upright.
7. Blue hour on a dam. A very old woman walks along its top with a lamp, step for step. Moths cross
   her light and she doesn't look down. She reads as a sleepwalker, a prisoner.
8. *Present: the Grove, in colour.* Fireflies flash in time. One flashes late.
9. A small dark shape tumbles through, wrong and lovely. Vesper.
10. She stands on Hob's shell as he paces his groove, then steps off onto her worn spot. Three
    wingbeats. He passes.
11. A lampwing drops in. Its lamp is on her face for a long beat, then swings away towards the far
    dam. Title.

## 7. Act structure

Each flight move comes from one of Wim's carvings and is a way to break a habit. Vesper learns to fly
as a dead boy drew moths flying.

**Act I: Noise**
- **The Grove.** The first heart (section 8). Move: **Tumble** (double-jump), changing her mind in
  mid-air.
- **Tollmeadow.** Flowers that open on the minute. Move: **Dart** (dash). At blue hour, far behind
  the meadow, a giant figure walks the levee with a lamp, every evening at the same moment.
  - **Boss: the Choir**, a hill of fireflies that strikes on the swell and is hurt only in the gaps.
    After, the meadow flickers raggedly, some fireflies burned out. It was the most beautiful thing in
    the game, and you broke it.
- **The Ruled Wood.** Trees in perfect rows. Move: **Turn** (wall-kick), going back the way she came.
  - **Gate: the Stag of Three.** Its drinking is a lock. She startles it, it bolts, and the way
    opens.

Hob follows, clumsy, frightened, then curious. He can't climb; at the Weir she leaves him at a lantern.

**Act II: Asked For**
- **The Weir.** The first sluices. Move: **Drift** (glide).
  - **Boss: the Weirkeeper.** Every hit makes the water surge unpredictably, replaying the original
    wound. When it falls, the reservoir drains.
- **The Levee.** Vesper meets the old woman. She lands on Maud's lamp, then her sleeve, then her hand.
  Maud's eyes slide off her, and **across the valley every lamp slides away at the same instant.** The
  player watches the blindness happen and learns the law.
- **Lowmere (midpoint), shown and not told.** In the drained town every window has a cracked dish on
  its sill, a root-thread running from each into the street. The threads braid down the high street
  and up the dam to one lit window: hers. In the school, eleven small coats hang on their pegs. Wim's
  stone stands by the door, a jar lamp in the reeds below it.
- **Consequences.** The Stag lies dead in a ravine it never had to look at before. The weather Vesper
  loosened sends its first real storm, and it cracks Hob's shell.

**Lowest point.** Hob is gone from the lantern. She finds him pacing a fresh groove in the Ruled Wood.
He walked back into the beat because the beat doesn't hurt. He can't see her.

**Boss: Hob, Settled.** The Quiet rams with him. There are two ways to win:
- **By force.** Noise works, and dashes and cuts stagger him out of the beat. He is freed, but afraid
  of her, and follows only at a distance.
- **By keeping time.** She goes to the same spot, at the same swell, with the same three wingbeats,
  again and again, until he *chooses* to look up. For the first time she is predictable for someone.

  Either way she learns **Keeping Time**: landing exactly on the swell, so settled gates and
  platforms accept her (and lamps can see her). Only a Hob who chose her carries her and rams on her
  timing.

**Act III: The Offbeat**
- **The Roost.** A thousand sleeping lampwings, every lamp turned towards the dam.
  - **Boss: the Lamplighter**, the one lamp that doesn't turn with Maud. It tracks Vesper herself, not
    her habits. Neither noise nor rhythm beats it. *Syncopation* does: keep time until it commits,
    then break once. Beaten, it doesn't die. It hangs at her shoulder and follows her.
- **The Spillway.** A climb up the dam face in the first storm nobody scheduled.
- **The Bellhouse.** Maud's kitchen. Above her chair hangs **the Longcase**, the clock's pendulum
  grown into a root as thick as a tree. Its swing is the world's swell, and her heart is paced to it.
  The climb between its swings is the final encounter. Then the choice.

**Endings**

All three cost her Hob, each in a different way.
- **Stillness.** Vesper keeps time on the pendulum until she is a habit and greys into it. Hob paces
  for ever, and nothing happens.
- **Storm.** She cuts the root. Maud's heart stops. One night returns sixty years of storm, fire and
  flood; the lamps go out and lampwings are bats again. Hob drowns holding her above the first flood
  since Wim's. At dawn the Lamplighter, a bat now, hunts her through a blazing green valley.
- **The Offbeat.** Only possible if Hob chose her: the kitchen door has been shut for sixty years, and
  only his ram, on her timing, opens it.
  1. Vesper lights the cold hearth with a coal of Sallow's fire. Flicker crosses a face that hasn't
     seen any in sixty years.
  2. Maud turns from it, as she turns from everything that flickers, and her eyes fall on the
     dresser: Wim's drawing, a moth drawn as a scribble because he drew its flight.
  3. Vesper flies the scribble's path in front of it: the Tumble, Dart and Turn the stones taught her.
  4. Maud looks. She holds out a finger and Vesper is still on it.
  5. **Every lamp in the world turns to the kitchen window.** The Quiet sees a moth.
  6. The Longcase hitches, one beat late, and swings on, never quite even again, the way a healthy
     heart never is.
  7. Outside falls rain nobody scheduled. Hob stands in the doorway. Vesper gives her three wingbeats.
     He sees her completely, and then, free at last of the beat and of her, he turns and walks into
     the wet hills.

  She lets him go. Maud lives, unevenly, beside the fire.

  **After the credits, the player feels it.** There is no ride and no ram. Lanterns no longer hide
  her, because nothing needs to. Lampwings hang at the edge of every light, watching every moth.
  Grass grows in Hob's groove, and her small track beside it is still there.

## 8. Where the Grove sits

The Grove is region 1. Its ending beat means this:
- **Three lanterns** make a triangle of swarms that the lamps cannot see into.
- **The heart wakes.** The veins of light running to the lanterns are the Grove's own lub-dub pulse,
  reaching towards the swarms that shelter it.
- **The carving fills with light.** Wim's first moth, in the tumbling pose, and Vesper learns Tumble
  from it.
- **The ring of light** is the first unscheduled event in sixty years. A two-second cut: on the dam,
  the old woman misses one step of her round. Her head turns away from the Grove, and every lamp in
  the valley turns with it.
- **The offering.** Each glint lets the forest do one thing nobody asked for: flowers open at the
  wrong hour, the root-veins light, and the canopy cocoons split early.
- **Hob** misses his step, stops, and looks at her: the first creature who ever has. He is terrified,
  and bolts. She follows.

Scene changes: one shrine burrback becomes Hob, with a worn groove and her track beside it; fireflies,
lamps and pods share one swell while the Grove sleeps and go lub-dub when it wakes; the carving is a
child's; the lampwing weighing plays near the first lantern.

## 9. Environmental storytelling

1. **Wim's flood-stones.** A child's moth at child height on each, with dated water-marks above it.
2. **Lowmere's windows.** A dish on every sill and root-threads braided down the street. A whole town
   grew it.
3. **Eleven small coats** on the school pegs, untouched.
4. **The jar lamp** in the reeds below the first stone. Its lid has air-holes punched in it.
5. **Maud's kitchen.** Wim's drawing on the dresser. The first dish on the table, cracked and dark,
   with a strip of tape on it in her hand, the only human words in the game: *"Let nothing happen."*
6. **Item: Even Heart**, a pacing knot from a Lowmere house. *"A healthy heart stumbles a little. This
   one never has."*
7. **Sallow**, when Vesper takes the ember: *"Sixty years we kept it. Don't keep it."*
8. **The leaf.** A lampwing gives a falling leaf the same long, weighing hang it gave Vesper.

## 10. The question it leaves

Can you keep anyone safe without keeping them? And if the Quiet only ever looked where Maud looked,
whose grief was it?

## Changes from round 2
- **The blind spot is grief.** The Quiet looks only where Maud looks, and she never looks at moths.
  One law of sight: the lamps are its only eyes, they can't see a moth, but they can see a habit.
  Burrbacks don't see at all.
- **Jargon removed.** No "entrainment", "homeostat" or "surprise-minimisation". The beat is one visual
  language: an even swell for the Quiet, a lub-dub for living things. There is a muted-play test.
- **"Underestimated" restored.** A lampwing weighs Vesper and dismisses her. Sentience evidence is
  balanced both ways (the shudder and the Lamplighter against the leaf and the copied habits).
- **Maud is present in play.** A figure on the levee in Act I, the hand-and-eyes encounter in Act II,
  the kitchen in Act III. The prologue no longer shows her building the Quiet, so the misdirect holds.
- **The midpoint is shown.** Dishes in every window, braided roots, eleven coats. The tally board and
  the Asking Wall are cut. The only words are Maud's tape.
- **The true ending is gated by the relationship, not by lore.** It needs Hob, won by keeping time
  rather than by force. Every ending costs Hob. In the Offbeat he leaves, and the post-game loses his
  ride and ram and the lanterns' cover.
- **De-derived.** Maud's guilt is shared with a visible town, and she neither sacrifices herself nor
  dies in the true ending (not Kuro). The vixen is cut; the "put me back" beat is Hob's alone, caused
  by Vesper. The abilities are Wim's carvings, so the ending's key is set up all game.
- **Act III is filled out.** Three places now: the Roost, the Spillway and the Bellhouse.
- **Smaller fixes.** Burrbacks in lantern light now just stop and turn away (one reading, not two).
  Vesper has a single need.
