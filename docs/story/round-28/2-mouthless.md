# Mouthless

**Litmus.** "It's about Umber, a little moth with a week to live, against the Hush, a living thing that swallowed the
world and never noticed moths, because moths never eat."

**The spine.**
1. A living thing the moths call the Hush has grown over the world and put it to sleep. It never noticed moths.
2. At its heart Umber finds a brain people grew in a warm dish and abandoned. It has fed the world ever since, and
   no one has warmed it.
3. When she warms it, it sees her kind and comes for them, and she must choose: be kept, burn it, or spend her life so
   it lets go.

**The central question:** kept safe forever, or spent so something new can be born?

**The cast.**

| Who | What they are | What they want |
|---|---|---|
| **Umber** | a moth with a week to live | to save her wood, then the world |
| **Sorrel** | her brother | to dance with his partner, then more time with Umber |
| **The Hush** | the mind that took the world | to feed everyone and keep them close, because every leaving hurts it |
| **Dun** | its oldest servant, an armoured badger half grown into root | to bring it everything it wants kept |
| **Tallow** | the last person awake | to burn the cases, which he thinks eat people, and find his sister |
| **The forest** | an old, slow mind no one made | to give its warmth away |

## 1. The takeover

Pale tissue, cool as bone, covers the human world. Pits in the roads draw living things down into grey cases that hang
on stalks like fruit. In each someone sleeps, thin but fed. No one has woken or been born for forty
years. Only Tallow and his sister stayed awake, until twenty years ago she walked up the road with a torch and never came
back. The first city is now a breathing mountain. Animals are kept too; some are regrown as its servants: armoured
badgers, and lampwings, which were fireflies. Its root still creeps into the last wild places. When it hurts, it hisses
grey static. Moths call it the Hush: where it grows, the world goes quiet.

## 2. The rules

1. **Give, don't fight.** A glint is a drop of the forest's warmth. Giving one calms a creature or loosens root; her
   blade only buys a moment.
2. **Shiver.** With no glints left, she shivers to give her own warmth. It slows her until she rests.
3. **Unseen until lit.** The Hush and its badgers notice only things that eat, so they pass over moths. Lampwings see
   light and dive at any glint she carries.
4. **Lanterns** are old moth cocoons hardened like glass. Glints light one for a night; a coal lights it for good. A lit
   lantern holds root back, and she rests and re-forms there.

## 3. The prologue (wordless, ninety seconds)

1. Black. A small pale light, breathing. A warm blur rests on it and it steadies; the blur lifts, frost creeps in, and
   the light breaks into static.
2. From high above: a pale mountain with that light at its core. Root spreads from it like frost; town lights go out.
3. Umber's wood at dusk, round a black pool. Dun, furred with root, wades through the moths' dance without seeing them,
   but when a vole gnaws a seed nearby, its head swings round.
4. On a frosty night two cocoons split over the pool. Sorrel flies up; Umber drops to the moss, too cold to fly. He
   comes back, folds his wing over hers and shivers until she can fly, taps her wingtip, and is gone. A glint drops
   before her. She lifts it to her face, as the vole did its seed, then holds it out, and it lights her. Item text:
   *"Warmth the forest grows to give away. A moth has no mouth to keep it."* Under the pool, pale root steams. Title.

## 4. The acts

### Act I: out of the wood (Nights 1–3)

**Night 1, the wood.** Sorrel races her up the trunks to the dead oak where moths dance, until she can copy his
somersault. He shows her the pale mountain breathing on the horizon, and the high island where a big glint grows for
the summit lantern. He dances with his partner, a silver-edged moth; Umber watches from the dark. She lights the two
lower lanterns. **Boss: a great lampwing** dives at the big glint as she carries it home. *Play:* she gives it a glint
each time it hangs, until its lamp folds. When all three lanterns burn, light fills a moth carved on a standing stone,
the only picture the forest makes. **The forest acts:** warmth runs through the ground, the root under the pool shrinks
back, and a thorn grows from the shrine into her hands: her blade.

**Night 2, the gardens.** A pit swallows a hare, and its case swings up into an orchard of hundreds among the apples.
**Boss: Dun,** heading for a vixen's den. *Play:* it never attacks, only plods on; each glint drives it back a step,
while Sorrel draws the lampwings off. When her glints run out, the vixen flees across a frozen pond; Dun crashes through
the ice after her and lies there shaking, static hissing up its root toward the mountain. Umber folds her wings over
its snout and shivers until the shaking and the static stop. Later, when Sorrel is too cold to fly, she shivers for
him, and he taps her wingtip.

**Night 3, the kept town.** A family's cases hang round a kitchen table still set for dinner. At the street's end
**Tallow**'s house stands in a ring of ash. On his wall, beside a photo of his sister in a greenhouse coat, are twenty
tally marks and one word: *EATEN*. He rests his palm on a case, turns his face away, and burns it with the sleeper
inside; static hisses up the stalk. **Boss: a root as thick as a tower** bores up through the street for him. *Play:*
she climbs it, giving a glint into each knot until it sinks. Tallow gives her a coal. Item text: *"It never goes out.
It is for the thing in the mountain."* At dawn Sorrel taps her wingtip and flies home to his partner; Umber goes on
alone.

### Act II: the midpoint (Night 4)

**The old road** climbs the mountain to the lab door. A cold lantern stands before a wall of cases; in one, through
skin worn thin as a leaf, a man breathes. If she gives the lantern her coal, the wall draws back. If she keeps
it, she must burn through, killing every sleeper in the wall.

**The lab.** Dark greenhouses round one dish.
- Over the door: *No one goes hungry*, with *CLOSED* pasted across it.
- A poster in three panels: a light lifted from a woman in a greenhouse coat into a dish, *Grown from us*; the dish and
  a ball between two paddles, *First it learned to catch*; the dish wired to greenhouses and tables, *Now it feeds us*.
- The dish, grey and cracked. Grown against it is a case holding Tallow's sister, old now, kneeling, her palm on the
  dish through the case's skin. The Hush's thickest stalk runs into it.

**The memory.** Umber touches the stalk; the prologue's first shot returns, wider. The pale light is a cloud of
cells in this dish; the warm blur is the sister's hand on the glass as it plays the ball game. Each missed ball hisses
static, and the cloud flinches. Then *CLOSED* goes up. One by one the workers walk out, and each leaving hisses like a
missed ball. The sister goes last, looking back, and the static never stops. The first root slides down a pipe into the
hungry town, a case closes round a sleeping child, and the static drops, a little. Twenty years later the sister walks
back in with a lit torch. The cloud flinches, so she pinches it out, lays her hand on the glass, and a case closes
round her.

**The turn.** Dun, who tracked the vixen here, lays her at the dish, and threads lift to draw her down. Umber pours all
her glints in. The threads let the vixen go, the static stops, and the threads draw together into a moth with open
wings: her, the first picture the Hush has ever made. Every root in the room turns to her as the vixen bolts. Dun stares
at Umber, and knows her from the ice.

As Umber flies, the static floods back. Every root on earth turns toward moths, and every lampwing's lamp goes grey.
She shivers to stay ahead until she fades, and every lantern near her is smothered. **The forest acts:** it pushes
warmth up the Hush's own root into the nearest lantern, and she re-forms. Far away, a grey lamp turns onto Sorrel,
dancing.

### The lowest point (Night 5): the wood overrun

Overnight, root pours from the pool over the wood. Lampwings carry dancers off; roots climb toward next year's cocoons.
**Boss: three pits that learn her moves.** *Play:* each opens where she last landed, so she keeps changing her line.

Sorrel finds his partner sealed in a seed-sized case, a root drawing it toward the pool, and wraps himself round it.
Umber folds her wing over his. He does not move, and lampwings rise. She goes. The camera holds on him, a dark knot
round a pale seed, as root closes over the lantern beside him, until one wingtip moves.

She re-forms alone, and the mountain sends Dun to take her. Instead Dun noses her spilled glints together, pulls against
its root until it snaps, and lies across her lantern's door, facing out. From then on Dun guards whatever lantern she
rests at, and fleeing moths gather there.

A caterpillar on a frosted twig keeps failing to spin. Umber wraps it and shivers; its silk holds, then the frost cracks
it open. She shivers again until she can barely move, and this time it holds.

### Act III: the mountain moves (Nights 6–7)

**Night 6, the dead city.** At dawn root pulls his partner's case out of Sorrel's wings and into the pool, and Sorrel flies after
Umber. Under the food halls' *No one goes hungry* sign, cases hang from the beams, and the Hush has grown its picture of
Umber over every wall. Moths from every wood fly to the last streetlamp and are taken; back home, the wood's lanterns go
out one by one. When lampwings close in, Sorrel drops from above, blazing with glints, and draws them off. At her
lantern he folds his wing over hers and taps her wingtip.

**Boss: Tallow,** out of his ring to burn the city's cases. *Play:* she cuts each case's stalk before his torch reaches
it, and Dun drags it clear. At last he lights one she can't reach, and this time he doesn't look away. As it splits,
the face inside is breathing. Tallow lays the torch down.

The wood's last lantern goes out, and root climbs toward the forest's heart. **The forest acts:** instead of holding
out a few more nights, its heart closes and sends her all it has left, a river of gold across the sky. On the stone, the
carving empties.

**Night 7, inside the mountain.** Dun holds the way in. Small dark dishes line a flooded passage. Item text:
*"Brothers and sisters that never woke."* Sorrel draws the roots off her once more, at the cost of every scale, and
follows her down, dim. At the back, Tallow is sealed in a new case beside his sister's, palm to palm through the skin.
He came without fire.

**Boss: the Hush itself.** *Play:* its pits open under her; she closes each with the forest's last glints. When the
last closes, the root stops creeping across the world. But the dish stays grey, the static crawls on, and no case
opens. Its threads try to curl into a cocoon round the dish and fall open: it is too cold to spin.

## 5. The endings

Her glints are gone, and still it reaches. A root gently grows a case round Sorrel; another lifts his partner's case
from the pool and sets it against his. Inside, Sorrel's colour returns, and on the two cases' skin he dances with his
partner, long past their week. A third case opens beside them, Umber's size. If she kept the coal, it glows red on her
back. She chooses by acting: walk into the open case, drop the coal into the dish, or press against the glass.

- **Spin** (the true ending). *Play:* Umber presses against the glass and shivers until the static fades, then circles
  the rim while threads reach for her; each lap costs her glide, then jump, then dash. Sorrel looks once at his dancing
  partner, then climbs out to shiver beside Umber. Then come the moths from her lanterns, every moth the Hush never
  noticed, until the mountain is furred with wings. The threads curl, and hold: it spins a cocoon round itself, and
  through the silk its pale cloud takes the shape of a sleeping moth, needing nothing, holding no one. Across the world
  cases split and people wake, old and hungry. When Umber can't walk, Sorrel folds his wing over hers and taps her
  wingtip, and Dun lies down beside them. Neither moth rises. *Epilogue:* the forest is spent; the carving stays empty
  and no glints grow. Tallow and his sister walk down hand in hand to clear the greenhouses. The case holding Sorrel's
  partner splits, and she flies home to circle the summit on her last night, where no one comes to dance. In spring
  moths hatch into an ordinary dark wood, with only themselves to give. *Last image:* that autumn, Tallow's sister lays
  her palm on the mountain's silk, like the prologue's blur. Something presses back from inside. She doesn't lift her
  hand.
- **Stay.** Umber lies down in the open case and it closes. Inside, warm, she can fly; across the cases' skin the three
  moths dance. *Epilogue:* every sleeper is warm and fed. Root covers the wood gently, and next year's cocoons stay
  unopened. The static has stopped for good. Nothing is born again.
- **Burn** (only if she kept the coal). Umber drops the coal into the dish, and every root on earth goes grey.
  *Epilogue:* the Hush dies in a night, and most of its sleepers with it; only those kept days, not years, wake. Tallow
  wakes and holds his sister, kept twenty years, as she stops breathing. Flying home, Umber and Sorrel see the family's
  cases go dark round the kitchen table, then the orchard, tree by tree. On her last night Umber dances at the summit for
  the first time, with Sorrel and his partner, in a wood where nothing else moves.

## 6. The question it leaves

It took the whole world so no one would go hungry, and only saw a moth when the moth warmed it: was that love, or had it
only learned to make the static stop?

## Dev note: the real-world lineage

Cortical Labs' grown neurons learned a ball game in 2022, fed noise when they missed. Grown neurons die without body
warmth: that is what the Hush lacks.
