# 4. What the Lanterns Burn

## 1. Spine

**Logline:** A young moth lights lanterns beside the mentor who catches her every time she falls, and
learns what is burning inside them, and the one light her mentor has never let burn.

**The spine, as a player would tell a friend:**
1. Vesper is a young moth on a pilgrimage with her mentor, the Taper, lighting the lanterns that keep
   moths safe in a forest full of monsters.
2. She finds out that the lights the lanterns burn are what is left of people, that the monsters were
   trying to save them, and that the Taper has always known.
3. At the end there is one light left. It could bring the Taper back, or it could be the one person the
   Taper loved and never let burn.

**The central question:** *Would you burn one life to keep the one you love?*

## 2. The world before, and how it was taken

Late in humanity's dwindling, the dying gave a vial of blood. Their cells were grown into neurons in
amber glass bulbs, and grieving families taught each bulb to be the one it came from. When nobody was
left to tend them, the last people grafted the bulbs into the old forests so that the trees would feed
them. Each bulb had been taught one wish, *don't let me go*, so it grew: pale pods on every branch, each
holding a **glint**, a small light that dreams one moment over and over. The forest became the
**Bequest**, and the old forest lay buried alive beneath it. Nobody decided to take the world. The people
who loved the dead most planted it.

**How a player finds this: hands.** Every glint flickers a gesture made by a large, soft, five-fingered
hand, bigger than any creature in the game: a wave, a cupping, a hand laid on a cheek. At the **Root**,
rows of glass bulbs lie half-buried under the oldest trees. Each is the exact shape of a lantern's glass,
and each carries the print of that hand pressed in where someone held it last. One place in the rows is
empty.

## 3. The forest-mind, the old forest, and why moths don't count

The Bequest's keepers are the **burrbacks**, who guard the pods, and the **lampwings**, who gather up
loose glints. They move like one will, and they notice one thing: *who is carrying our glints?* A moth
carrying none is nothing to them. A moth carrying glints is a thief, and they believe what everyone
believes about moths, that she will fly into the light and can't help it. Their mistake is played twice:
a moth can carry a glint *away* from the light, and Vesper learns to fly straight with her back to it.

Under the Bequest, the **old forest** is hungry for its body back. Each grove's **heart** is its mouth,
and the lanterns are its lures. What burns in them runs *down* the roots, and so do the glints a pilgrim
offers to the heart. Where that light arrives, the Bequest withers, the old wood breathes and the keepers
fall back. The moth on the heart's standing stone was never carved. The old forest grew it, the way an
orchid grows the shape of a bee. It isn't evil, and it isn't kind.

Are glints people, or echoes of people? Each repeats its gesture like a loop. The Taper says they are
echoes. The keepers treat them as children. The game never says.

**The one rule.** A moth who fades is a drifting spark, with no hand in it. A lit lantern catches the
spark and burns one glint from its glass to remake her. With no lantern near, the spark goes out. Only
pilgrims are allowed this. Everyone else lives a week.

## 4. The cast

## 5. The ember, and the Taper's secret rite

The **ember** is one glint, the most whole in the valley. Its gesture is a large hand laid on a cheek, and
the Taper has made that gesture with her wing for a hundred lifetimes. Long ago, as a young pilgrim, she
was carrying it to the glass, saw that gesture and couldn't let go. She took it from its place at the
Root, and Tallow has kept it on the Grove's high island ever since.

The Grove is her rite. Once in every pilgrim's lifetime she sends a new pilgrim down alone to wake the
heart, and the rite requires the great glint from the island set in the summit lantern. While the heart
wakes, she holds her reed over that glass so it can't burn, and for the length of a wake she sees her
beloved lit. Afterwards Tallow carries it home. They have kept this truce without a sound for a hundred
lifetimes. Vesper is the latest pilgrim.

## 6. Prologue (wordless, about 90 seconds)

Moths gather at a lantern at night. A few of them are grey and fraying, and in the next shot they're gone.
The Taper, ancient, stumbles and fades. Her spark drifts, the glass catches it and flares, one glint in it
goes dark, and she steps out whole. In the crowd, Vesper and Sedge nudge each other. The Taper chooses
Vesper and gives her three glints. One flickers as Vesper carries it: a large hand, waving. At the edge of
the light a torn lampwing waits. Vesper glances at it, and the Taper lays a wing on her cheek, steadying
her and keeping her face to the glass. The Taper points her reed at a dark valley, *your first grove,
alone*, and perches on the bluff above it. Her reed-light watches.

## 7. The story

**The re-form is the relationship.** Through Act I, every time Vesper fades, the Taper is at the glass
when she steps out, and lays her wing on Vesper's cheek. After the Hush, Vesper steps out alone. At the
Root it reverses.

**ACT I: TOGETHER.** *With Vesper: the Taper, and for a while Sedge.*
- **The Grove** (our scene): see 8.
- **On the road.** The Taper reaches every lantern before you, reed raised. The first few times you fall
  short of a ledge, she swoops in and catches you by the arm. Later she lets you fall, because she's
  teaching. At rests she mends your wing with silk. *Flare*, bought with glints, makes creatures recoil:
  you are throwing someone at them.
- **Sedge** catches up. He races you and loses every time, and you can wait for him. When you're empty at
  a dark lantern he gives you his only glint. His wings begin to grey.
- **The Combs. Boss: Clasp.** You fight beside the Taper, both re-forming from the same glass. In her last
  phase Clasp heaps her pods onto her back and runs. You crack her shell to spill them, and that is the
  act's biggest haul. She limps into the dark, empty-backed.
- **The Coral Stair.** Tallow snatches Sedge's glint from Vesper's cape and flees slowly, one wing
  dragging. Vesper chases it.

**MIDPOINT: THE HUSH (played).** The chase ends in a hollow full of pods. Tallow presses Sedge's glint into
bare earth, and it sprouts. It glitters like a pickup, so the player grabs it. That is Vesper's flaw and
the player's. Tallow only watches. Then Clasp crawls past to a pod the player cut on the way in and folds
down over it, in the pose every burrback took at every kindling. Vesper looks at the glint in her grip.
It flickers a large hand, cupping. She kneels and puts it back: *Plant*. At the mouth of the hollow stand
the Taper and Sedge. The Taper holds out her wing. Vesper doesn't come. Sedge goes with the Taper.

**ACT II: APART.** *With Vesper: Tallow.*
- **The truce.** Vesper goes back to the Grove at night and sees the Taper below the high island, lifting
  her wing toward it once. Above, Tallow holds the ember. The Taper leaves without seeing Vesper. Then
  Tallow brings the ember down and gives it to Vesper. It is the first thing it has ever given her.
  Carried, the ember throws the shadow of a large hand around her, palm open, and it closes when she's
  hit. It is her light now. Tallow wants it taken home to the Root, which it can't fly to.
- **The dark.** Vesper snuffs lanterns, and each one is a place she can't re-form any more. She plants
  what she finds, and a planted glint is one she can't spend on her own life. Boss arenas have only
  lanterns. When she glides short, Tallow catches her by the cape, as the Taper used to. Behind her,
  snuffed lanterns turn up relit, with reed-ash at their feet.
- **The old forest,** seen once. Following the veins down, she finds where all the light goes: a buried
  trunk wider than the screen, its bark greening where the light pours in and the pale pods withering
  around it. It breathes. It doesn't look at her.
- **Sedge's week runs out.** He is grey and falling at a lantern she snuffed. He holds a glint toward the
  dark glass. She can't do it. The Taper arrives and relights it, and Sedge steps out young, with the
  Taper's wing on *his* cheek. He doesn't look at Vesper.
- **The Censer, the moths' home. Boss: the Taper.** She never strikes Vesper. Vesper snuffs the ring of
  lanterns and the Taper relights them. Her only attack is the wing: she catches Vesper's cheek and turns
  her to the glass. Vesper wins by snuffing the Great Lantern. Beaten, the Taper looks at the moth children
  under the dark glass, not at Vesper.
- **The lowest point.** In the dark, burrbacks come for the children, who still carry the glints they were
  given, and Clasp leads them. Vesper *Braces* over the children and takes Clasp's ram without moving.
  Clasp stops, turns the burrbacks away and lies down beside the children. Vesper falls. No lantern near
  her is lit. Tallow catches her drifting spark and carries it, torn wing and all, across the valley to the
  one lantern still burning: the Great Lantern, which the Taper has relit. It lets go at the edge. The glass
  flares and a glint goes dark for her. Sedge, young again, watches it go out, then looks at his own wings.
  Vesper steps out. The Taper's wing lifts toward Vesper's cheek and stops. Every moth turns its back on
  Vesper, except Sedge, who turns round.
- **Moonward.** Vesper leaves with the Censer burning behind her, and her glide doesn't bend. Sedge follows.

**ACT III: THE ROOT.** *With Vesper: Tallow, Clasp and Sedge. Then the Taper.*

**The goal:** bring the ember home to the empty place among the bulbs. A storm of lampwings rises against
a moth carrying a glint toward the valley's oldest lantern, which stands at the Root's edge, and the whole
Bequest moves at once. They dive where the pull should take her, and she flies straight past them. The
Taper has followed. She doesn't stop Vesper. She flies beside her, Flaring the storm back, and both of
them re-form from the Root lantern's glass. When the Taper falls, Vesper is at the glass when she steps
out, and lays a wing on her cheek. Every time the *player* falls, the same glass drops. At the last
stretch the Taper spends its final glint on one Flare that breaks the storm off Vesper. She falls, and
there is nothing left to catch her.

**The choice.** Vesper kneels at the empty place in the rows with the ember. The Taper's spark is
guttering at the foot of the dark glass. In the glass, the ember would bring her back. The Taper looks
toward the far light of the Censer, then lifts her wing to Vesper's cheek and turns her face to the
glass. She is asking Vesper to burn the one she spent a hundred lifetimes keeping from the fire, so that
someone can go on keeping the children. Vesper chooses. The game waits.

- **Kept.** Vesper sets the ember in the glass, and nobody shields it. It burns. The Taper steps out whole.
  She lifts her wing toward Vesper's cheek, stops, looks at the wing and folds it. She never makes the
  gesture again. She keeps her reed and flies back toward the Censer. Sedge goes after her, since his life
  is in the lanterns now. Clasp lies down at the edge of the light, folded over nothing. Tallow hangs at
  that edge, stopped where it always stopped. Last shot: moth children asleep in a lantern's light, and in
  the glass above them, once, a large hand on a cheek, then gone.
- **Planted.** Vesper turns herself to the earth and sets the ember in its place. Sedge lays his wing over
  hers. Every bulb in the rows flickers at once, and the hands turn toward her: the first time the Bequest
  has ever looked at a moth. The ember's light makes its gesture over the Taper's spark, a hand on a cheek,
  and the spark goes out. Vesper carries the unlit reed away. Sedge, Tallow, Clasp and a few moths follow
  her and plant. Most stay at the Censer, where the Great Lantern still burns. The ember grows into a tree
  with **one hollow for every glint you burned to re-form after the Hush.** Vesper and Sedge sit under the
  moon with a week left, and Tallow lands beside them. Last shot: moth children in the dark by the tree,
  with no lantern near. A burrback's shadow falls over them. Cut.

## 8. The Grove, and what its ending means

Vesper enters carrying the Taper's three glints, so the keepers hunt her from the first minute. The Grove
must feel wonderful and dangerous, because the turn only works if the player loved it.

| In the Grove | First play | Replayed after the Hush |
|---|---|---|
| Re-forming | The Taper's wing on your cheek | A glint went dark so she could do that |
| Burrbacks fold down at a kindling | Calmed | The pose over a dead pod |
| A lampwing dive | An attack | It hurt you, yes, and it took a glint back |
| The ember | The great fetch | The Taper's beloved, fetched for her by every pilgrim |
| Offering glints to the heart | The grove wakes, tier by tier | Feeding people to the old forest, a meal |
| The moth on the stone | Moths carved their god | The forest grew a lure |

**The ending beat, in the normal camera.** The heart wakes. The Taper glides down from the bluff and lays
her wing on Vesper's cheek as the veins pulse inward. Then her reed-light leaves Vesper's side, rises to
the summit lantern and stays there until the veins go quiet. On a first play, she's honouring the great
kindling. Later the player knows she was shielding the one light she couldn't bear to lose, and that she
let Vesper feed the rest. At the edge of the frame, small and unremarked, a torn lampwing hangs under the
island.

**The essential changes to the existing scene:**
1. **The glass and the Taper:** each lantern's glass visibly holds glints, and a re-form burns one,
   replacing the lost third of the wallet. The Taper glides down to meet Vesper at the glass.
2. **Lampwing dives wound and steal:** a hit takes a glint into its lamp, and striking it drops the glint.
3. **Hands:** glint flickers show hand gestures, and the ember throws a hand's shadow while carried.
4. **The calm pose:** a calmed burrback folds flat.
5. **The wake:** the Taper comes down from the bluff and her reed-light rises to the summit lantern.
   Vein pulses run inward. Tallow hangs under the island.

## 9. What the player finds

1. **The Taper's hollow** in the Censer. Its wall is scratched with hundreds of hands, each laid on a cheek,
   over and over. Her wing-patches are cut from the old cocoon hanging there, which is Vesper's.
2. **The summit lantern:** reed-ash layered at its foot from a hundred visits.
3. **The Root's bulbs:** hand-prints in the glass, and one empty place.
4. **Tallow's island:** moss pressed flat in the shape of a wing, around a hollow the size of the ember.
5. **The heart's stone,** seen closely in Act II: the moth's edges are bark, not cut stone.
6. **Sedge's scratches** at a wayside lamp: two little moths racing, one waiting for the other.

## 10. The question it leaves

Would you burn one life to keep the one you love? The Taper burned thousands for the children, and kept
one back for herself.

## Changes from round 10

- **The Grove is the Taper's secret rite.** Once a lifetime a pilgrim brings her the ember, she shields it
  at the wake, and Tallow carries it home. This explains the ember and the reed-ash. In Act II the truce is
  shown, and Tallow hands the ember over.
- **The Dark Souls echo is reshaped.** The endings are renamed **Kept** and **Planted**. In Kept nobody
  takes up the reed. The Taper loses her gesture for good. Sedge, Clasp and Tallow each get a shot.
  Planted adds the Bequest looking at a moth for the first time.
- **Act III has a concrete goal:** bring the ember home to the empty place among the bulbs. The Taper
  spends her last glint on the Flare that breaks the storm, so nobody re-forms after the glass is empty.
- **The heart is the old forest's mouth.** Feeding it is a meal on replay. The moth on the stone is a lure
  the forest grew, not a carving. The Grove's climax is now consistent with the story.
- **Rules tightened.** A faded moth is a spark with no hand in it, and a lantern remakes her. The wall
  shows hands. Sedge turns round because he watches a glint die for Vesper and looks at his own wings.
  The Grove changes are cut to five.
