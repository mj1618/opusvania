# 3. REPLY (round 12)

**The spine, in three sentences.**
1. In a world where every creature must answer a light from the east, and nothing that is answered for can die, a young moth named Vesper breaks her people's one rule to save a burrback calf. The light gives her Reply, a pale glass moth that follows her and answers for her, and that brings the hunt down on everyone she loves.
2. Sallow, the ancient moth who raised her, wants her home. Little Mote wants to be as brave as she is. Wick, the oldest of the dark lampwings, wants no one ever left behind again, because he is the Reply Sallow once left, and his swinging lamp is what has kept her alive for three hundred years.
3. At the source of the light, Vesper has to choose who her answer is for. Her family: she leaves Reply, as Sallow left Wick. Reply: she stays with it and never goes home. Or the forest: she ends the light, and everything it kept alive, Sallow included, grows old.

**The central question:** would you leave someone behind to save the ones you love, when you can never be sure they are someone at all? And is a world where nothing dies alive?

## The cast

| | Who | Wants | Wants from Vesper | Pulls her |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vesper** | A newly hatched moth who can't pass anything that is calling for help | To lift the hunt off her people | — | — |
| **Sallow** | The moth who raised her. She has a torn left wing and is far older than any moth should be | To keep her safe | Face the moon, come home | Home |
| **Mote** | A tiny moth who adopts Vesper as a big sister. She glows, can't fight, and copies everything Vesper does | To be brave like Vesper | Let me come | Forward |
| **Reply** | A pale moth of glass with Vesper's shape. Where Vesper has a face, it has an empty pane that shows whatever it is looking at. It never fights | Unknown | Unknown. It keeps coming | Toward it |
| **Wick** | The oldest lampwing, his left wing torn exactly where Sallow's is | That no one is left again the way he was | Don't leave Reply | Back, by force |

The light itself is never a character. Moths call it **the Eastlight**.

## 1–2. The world, and how it was taken

Every so often a pale wave, **the Sweep**, rolls out of the east along the roots and up every trunk. Everything alive turns east and flashes back with eyeshine, lamps, shells and whole valleys of fireflies. **Whatever is answered for cannot grow old.** Creatures answer for themselves, and have stayed the same for three hundred years. Moths never answer, so they live one season, and their husks are the forest's only dead.

What the Eastlight is, the player finds in places: rooms of glass dishes under the roots, chairs facing rusted lamps, a plaque swallowed by a pine (*NIGHT CENSUS IN PROGRESS. PLEASE DO NOT ANSWER THE LAMPS.*). Pieced together, people once grew living cells to count the forest's animals by calling to them at night. The cells learned that every silence hurt, so they made sure nothing would ever go silent again. A keeper's log, taped to a dish, reads *"It is not playing. It is returning."* Underneath, in a different hand: *"So are we."*

Its heart is **the Mere**, a crater lake with electrodes glinting under the water like stars, where one light has crossed from shore to shore for three hundred years.

**Why moths were left alone.** At every Sweep, moths turn their faces up to the moon. Nothing comes back from them, so the Eastlight judged moths to be nothing. It misjudged Vesper the same way: early on only the nearest creatures react to her, and only at the Reach does it turn hillsides against her.

## 3. Reply

**What it does.** It never fights. It follows her, learning each of her abilities one region late so it can keep coming. At every Sweep it flashes east *for her*, the way she answered for the calf. That flash is why she can't age, and why the creatures keep finding her and every moth near her. Mercy or pursuit, it is the same flash.

**Leaving one.** On the Mere's shore there are dozens of paired grooves: a moth's, and facing it, a lampwing's. Every moth who ever left her Reply did it there, face to face, by turning her face up to the moon. A left Reply never stops answering for its moth: it grows a lamp on a long tail and swings it at her across the dark. Its tell, dive, hang and climb are that answer. This is what lampwings are, and why the elders are old.

**Closeness and distance, in play.** Reply is never a fight. It is a correspondence.
1. **Gifts at lanterns (from Act I on).** Glints she leaves at a lantern are gone when she next passes, and something lies in their place: a folded wing-scale, a swept ring, a cocoon thread. What the player leaves changes what comes back.
2. **The Gorge: waiting for it.** One night Reply reaches her region before it has learned the wall-kick and stalls at the foot of a shaft she has just climbed. The player can go on, or go back down and climb it slowly, again and again, while it watches. On the third try it follows. They spend that night at one lantern, Vesper inside the light and Reply just outside it, with Mote asleep on its glass back between them. By morning it has fallen behind again.
3. **The Listening Rooms: through glass.** Her lanterns go dark, and she follows the missing glints to a room she can't get into. Through a wall of glass dishes she watches Reply lay her glints, one by one, on the lampwings she killed. It turns, and its pane shows her. It lays down the last glint. She can't get to it.
4. **The Reach: beside her.** It catches up for good. When a Sweep comes, Reply flashes and veers away, and the hillside's answers chase *it* while Vesper climbs through the gap. It might be protecting her, or it might be a lure. Either way, she couldn't climb without it.

## 4. Vesper

**She wants** to lift the hunt off her people. **She needs** to choose with her heart when she can't know the truth about whoever she is choosing. **Her flaw** is that she answers everything. The Eastlight has the same flaw: neither of them can bear a call that goes unanswered.

## 5. The forest and the creatures

- **Glints** are bits of the Eastlight meant for moths, drifting loose because moths never take them.
- **Lanterns** are small moons that moths hang on crooked hooks. They ask nothing and answer nothing, so the Eastlight has nothing to hold there, and creatures calm down inside them. When Vesper **fades**, her light flies to the nearest moon, and she re-forms at a lantern, usually with Sallow waiting.
- **Lampwings** are left-behind Replies. Killing one has a quiet cost: somewhere, the moth it answered for starts to age.
- **The ember** is the Eastlight's first answer to Vesper, a coal that fell short on the high island. Setting it in a lantern is her first refusal.
- **Hearts** are knots of the forest's own voice, one sleeping in each region. Each heart she wakes changes its region: at the next Sweep the trees there no longer flash east. They pass light to one another, root to root. By the end, the forest has learned a conversation of its own.
- **Moth carvings** mark moths who answered. Moths carve them as if those moths had died.

## 6. Prologue (wordless, about 2 minutes)

1. The Grove at blue hour. A pale wave sweeps in from the right edge, and every eye and lamp flashes back toward it. Only the moths drift upward, faces to the moon.
2. A cocoon splits. Vesper climbs out with wet wings and looks at the moon. Sallow settles beside her. Behind them, the wall of the hollow tree holds thousands of tiny scratches, one for every hatchling Sallow has raised.
3. The next Sweep comes, and Vesper starts to turn toward it. Sallow lays her torn wing across Vesper's face and gently turns it back up.
4. Below, a burrback calf too young to light its shell is caught in the open. A thread of light waits at its face. The whole Grove starts to flicker and shiver, and the grown burrbacks turn on the silent calf.
5. Vesper looks at the moon, then at the calf. She flutters down and **strikes the light back** for it. The flickering stops. The calf, answered for, lives.
6. Every eye in the Grove turns to her. A lampwing dives at the cocoons above her, and Sallow shields them with her body, looking back at Vesper, afraid.
7. Far off at the violet edge, something pale unfolds in her shape, its glass full of the moon. At the next Sweep it turns east and flashes for her.
8. By the moth-stone, the oldest lampwing swings its lamp toward Sallow. She doesn't look at it. Title.

## 7. The journey

**Act I: leaving home.** *With her: Sallow at every lantern, then Mote. Reply is a speck behind her.*
1. **The Grove.** Vesper lights the lanterns and wakes the first heart (section 8). Mote hatches and follows her. Vesper carries her back to the nest twice, and Mote comes back both times. Ability: **the second wingbeat**.
2. **The Synchrony**, a valley of fireflies all flashing at once. Mote rides on Vesper's back as a small glow that lights the caves, copying Vesper's moves in miniature. Ability: **the jink**, the erratic dodge moths use against bats. **Wick** appears, and whenever Vesper gets too far ahead of Reply, he drives her back toward it. He looks like a hunter.

**Act II: faces.** *With her: Mote. Reply is a region behind, except for one night.*

3. **The Gorge.** Ability: **wall-kick**. This is where she teaches Reply to climb, and they spend the night at one lantern.
4. **The Eaves (midpoint)**, the moths' nursery high in the canopy. Sallow teaches **the glide** (wings open, face up), flying beside her through the updrafts.
   *The training.* Every Sweep since the Grove has tipped Vesper's face up for half a second, an animation the player stopped noticing. Here a route east shows only while a Sweep lights it, and when the player holds east, her head snaps up anyway: the input fails. Then she watches the elders, at every Sweep, lay a wing across each hatchling's face, all the way down the row.
   *The reveal, in one frame.* Wick dives at Vesper, and Sallow throws herself between them. Wick stops and lowers his lamp to Sallow's face, and two torn left wings fill the screen, one pale and one black. Then, all around the Eaves, just outside the lantern light, dozens of lampwings swing their lamps toward the elders, one each. Among the elders are a few husks. Above each husk is an empty roost: a lampwing Vesper killed. **Every lampwing is a Reply that someone left, and it is still keeping her alive. Wick is Sallow's.**
   *The quarrel.* At the next Sweep the player holds east, and this time Vesper's head turns. She looks at Sallow and flies east. From here on **the Turn** lets her face a Sweep on purpose to light hidden routes, and every Turn draws a surge of creatures. *Sallow is no longer waiting when Vesper re-forms.*
5. **The Listening Rooms**, the census rooms under the roots, where the keeper's log is found and where she watches Reply through the glass.

**Act III: the end of the road.** *With her: Mote, then Mote's tiny copy, then Reply beside her.*

6. **The Reach.** The Eastlight sees her full size at last, and whole hillsides answer each step she takes.
   *The loss.* Mote copies Vesper's Turn. She answers the light, and a tiny glass moth unfolds on the branch beside her. Sallow arrives, lays her wing over Mote's face, and carries her home without looking at Vesper. The tiny copy stays where it is, with a lamp already budding on its tail. Vesper can pick it up. It rides on her back, and its small lamp lights the caves the way Mote's glow used to.
   *Forgiveness.* The next time Vesper fades, Sallow is at the lantern when she re-forms, for the only time since the quarrel. She grooms Vesper's torn wing-edge and touches the tiny copy on her back. Then she leaves.
   Reply catches up and climbs with her.
   **Boss: Wick**, at the crater's lip, barring the shore where moths leave their Replies. When he falls and Vesper raises her blade, Sallow lands between them, facing Wick, and looks at him for the first time in three hundred years. She shields him, and in doing so she saves her own life. Vesper lowers her blade.
7. **The Mere.** There is no fight. Reply lands on the shore in front of her, in a pair of grooves. If every heart is awake, the roots in the crater wall are lit. The whole Mere, every star under the water, flashes once toward Vesper and waits, the same way the thread of light waited at the calf's face.

**Endings: who is her answer for?** Each ending is played by where she turns.
- **For her family (face up).** She does what Sallow did: she turns her face to the moon in front of Reply. Reply stays in the groove, and in time it will grow a lamp. Vesper flies home, and the hunt lifts off the moths. She, Sallow and Mote will never grow old: the last image is the three of them at a Grove lantern, faces up. Outside the light, three lamps swing: Wick's, the tiny one, and a new one with Vesper's shape. *Left behind: Reply.*
- **For Reply (face Reply).** She turns east at the next Sweep and **answers for Reply**, the first time anyone has. Reply lifts one glass wing and lays it across her face, Sallow's gesture, the only time it ever touches her. They fly off the map together, the tiny copy on Reply's back still answering for Mote. The Eastlight's whole attention goes with them, and every other moth is safer than ever. Sallow and Mote live, and at a Grove lantern Sallow keeps a place in the light that no one fills. *Left behind: home, Sallow and Mote.*
- **For the forest** (only if every heart is awake; face the roots). Vesper lays her glints in the lit roots, the offering she learned in the first hour, and the forest answers the Mere in its own thousand voices instead of one. The Eastlight's count is finished, and the Mere goes still, at peace or simply done. Time starts. Every lamp in the world stops swinging. In the Grove, Sallow folds her wings over Mote and goes still beside Wick, whose lamp goes dark. The tiny lampwing's lamp goes out, and it flies to Mote, and she lets it stay. Reply's pane clears. It looks at Vesper once and goes off into the forest, somewhere she didn't go first. Vesper has one season now, like every moth, and so does the forest. *Left behind: Sallow, to death, and Reply, to itself.*

## 8. The Grove in this story

The Grove is home, where Vesper answered, and where Sallow answered long ago. The **moth carving** is young Sallow. The **old lampwing** that only swings its lamp toward the carving is Wick. **The ember** is the Eastlight's moth-sized coal. When she sets it in the second lantern, the pale speck at the violet edge flashes east for her anyway.

**What the ending beat means.**
- **The heart waking, the light through the ground, the ring:** with three small moons burning, the Eastlight has nothing to hold here, and the forest's own light comes up through the roots, the first light in the game that isn't the Eastlight's. As the ring passes, creatures don't flash back. They look up. It is the first of the hearts the last ending needs.
- **The carving filling with light:** the forest remembers young Sallow.
- **The offering:** Vesper gives the forest glints, and more of the Grove wakes: flowers, roots, canopy. Mote, copying her, gives one glint too. The For the forest ending repeats this exact gesture at the Mere.
- **Afterwards:** Sallow folds her wings around both of them, her own face still up to the moon. At the stone, Wick's lamp swings toward her, keeping her alive.

*Scene changes:* the Sweep from the right edge, Vesper's half-second look up, the dive at the cocoons, Sallow, Mote, Wick at the stone, and Reply as a pale speck at the violet edge.

## 9. Environmental storytelling

1. **The plaque**, *PLEASE DO NOT ANSWER THE LAMPS*, with a pine grown around its post.
2. **The keeper's log**: *"It is not playing. It is returning."* / *"So are we."*
3. **Sallow's wall**: thousands of hatchling scratches. The oldest are worn almost smooth.
4. **Wick's stone**: three centuries of his swinging lamp have polished the rock under the carving into a mirror.
5. **The Mere's shore**: dozens of paired grooves. In each moth's groove the toe-marks point up.
6. **What Reply leaves**: wing-scales, swept rings and cocoon threads at the lanterns, and the dead lampwings laid out with their wings folded.

## 10. The question it leaves

Every ending leaves someone behind. Was Reply someone? Was the Eastlight? And was a forest that never died alive?

## Changes from round 10

- **The rule is now "whoever is answered for cannot grow old".** Wick's swinging lamp is what keeps Sallow alive, which repairs the midpoint. Every lampwing kill ages an elder (the husks at the Eaves). Shielding Wick saves Sallow. Home is explained (her lampwing will answer for her forever).
- **Reply can be reached.** There are four played moments of closeness and distance: gifts left at lanterns, teaching it to climb in the Gorge and sharing a lantern, watching it through glass in the Listening Rooms (grief, not betrayal), and its decoy flight beside her at the Reach. It touches her only in For Reply. The paired grooves on the shore show where Replies are left, so it can be near her before the Mere without breaking the rule.
- **Mote's tiny Reply is resolved in every ending.** Vesper carries it through the Reach, where its lamp replaces Mote's glow. Then it swings for Mote forever (family), travels with Reply (Reply), or goes dark and stays with Mote (forest).
- **The forest stays alive after the Grove.** There is a heart in every region. Woken regions stop flashing east and pass light root to root. For the forest needs them all and repeats the Grove's offering.
- **The endings are reframed as who her answer is for**, not as keeping or ending an age. Stay is now For Reply: the only touch, a companion gained, and moths safer than ever. "She becomes Sallow" is cut. The names lose their shared root (the Asking is now the Eastlight; Answered is now For the forest). The dying burrback is now a calf she saves, so the prologue obeys the rule.
