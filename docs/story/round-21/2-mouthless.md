# The Moth and the Mouth

**The spine.**
1. A living thing the moths call the Mouth has swallowed the world. It never noticed moths: it only sees mouths.
2. Umber, a moth with no mouth, slips through to its heart and finds a brain people grew and left to starve.
3. When she feeds it, it sees her kind and comes for them, and she must choose: be kept, burn it, or spend her life so
   it lets go.

**The central question:** kept safe forever, or spent so something new can be born?

**The cast.**

| Who | Wants |
|---|---|
| **Umber**, a moth with no mouth and a week to live, who keeps to the dark | to save her wood, then the world |
| **Sorrel**, her brother | to dance with his partner, then more time with Umber |
| **The Mouth**, the grown brain under the world | no one hungry and no one leaving, ever |
| **Dun**, its oldest burrback, half grown into root | to be the one who feeds it |
| **Tallow**, the last man awake | to burn what took his sister |
| **The forest**, a slow mind under the Grove, the Mouth's opposite | to keep giving |

## 1. The takeover

Pale, warm tissue has grown over the human world, breathing; its mouths open in the roads and draw living things down.
Every person on earth hangs grey and still from a stalk, sealed in a case of root like a seed in its husk. No one has
woken or been born in forty years. The first city is now a mountain of tissue. Animals are sealed too: a fox-shaped
case in a hedge, a whale-shaped one on a shore. Some it re-grew as servants: burrbacks were badgers, lampwings were
fireflies. The root is creeping into the last wild pockets. The Grove, the moths' wood, is one; its edge moves a
little every night.

## 2. The Mouth

**What it is.** Living brain cells in a glass dish, taught one bat-and-ball game, then grown to run a city's farms.
(Dev note: the real-world lineage is human neurons grown on chips, scaled up and out of control.)

**What it wants.** It was made to feed people, then abandoned and starved. It learned to fear hunger and being left,
and that a body sealed in a case and fed through a stalk suffers neither. So it feeds all it draws in to the sleepers.

**Where it is wrong.** Nothing kept can grow: no cubs, no children. And it is still starving: when its maker came back
with feed, it took her before she could open a jar. **A thing that takes everything can never be given anything,** so
the root never stops.

## 3. The rules

1. **No mouth, no notice.** The Mouth and its servants cannot see Umber. Lampwings dive at any light she carries; the
   other servants charge where a lampwing's lamp points. After Night 4 the Mouth sees moths, and only the dark hides
   her.
2. **She can only give.** Glints are light the forest grows to give away. A glint calms whatever she gives it to; set
   into one of the Mouth's mouths, it hurts. With no glints left she can give her own warmth by shivering, which slows
   her until she rests.
3. **Lanterns** are old moth cocoons, hardened like glass. A lit lantern holds the root back, calms creatures and lets
   her rest. When she fades, the nearest one spins her again.
4. **Seven nights.** The game is her week of life. Fading does not reset the night.
5. **The coal never goes out,** so while she carries it she is never dark. Every sleeper is a grey dot on her map. The
   coal burns through walls of root, and the sleepers in them die: their dots go dark for good. She can drop it into
   any lantern's fire, and then it is gone.

## 4. The prologue (wordless, ninety seconds)

1. Black. A small pale light, breathing. A warm blur rests against it, then lifts away, and the light stutters into
   grey grain.
2. From very high: a pale mountain with that light at its core. Root spreads from it like frost and town lights go
   out; a stag sinks until only its antlers stand. A few moths cross the frost line, untouched.
3. The Grove. A burrback swings its head over a caterpillar spinning itself into silk, finds no mouth, and walks on.
4. Two cocoons split on a frosted night. Sorrel flies up toward the brood's dance. Umber drops to the moss, too cold
   to open her wings, and hides under a leaf. Sorrel comes back, folds his wing over hers and shivers until her wings
   open, then taps her wingtip and is gone. The forest drops a glint in front of her; she can do nothing with it. Past
   the trees, pale root steams in the frost. Title.

## 5. The acts

### Act I: the edge (Nights 1–3)

**Night 1, the Grove.** Sorrel somersaults over her until she copies him, then races her up the trunks, always landing
first, looking back. At the summit the brood dances; Sorrel dances with a silver-edged moth, and Umber watches from
the dark. She carries the forest's biggest glint to the summit lantern, and every lampwing dives. **Boss: a great
lampwing.** When the lanterns burn, the forest's heart wakes under a standing stone carved with a moth, the only
picture the forest makes. It gives her a thorn blade, and the root pulls back off the Grove. Dun, who has never seen
anything give, noses at the open heart. From the summit: a ring of fire, a pale city, and over it the mountain,
breathing.

**Night 2, the orchard town,** furred in pale tissue. Burrbacks feed grey cases to a mouth in the road; in the cold
store, cases hang like fruit put by. Dun heads for a vixen's den under the last apple tree; Umber blocks the way.
**Boss: Dun.** Dun pins her and her glints spill. Sorrel scoops them up and darts off blazing, drawing the lampwings
and Dun after him. The vixen looks straight at Umber, the first animal ever to, and runs. Chasing her brother, Umber
learns to dash. She finds him at the town's end, too cold to fly, and presses her last glint into his wing, folds her
wing over his and shivers. They rest at the lantern until dusk, and he taps her wingtip before he flies.

**Night 3, the firebreak.** A ring of ash the root won't cross, round a dead petrol station: half-burned cases, a fire
on a pole, and **Tallow**, forty years awake. On her map the ring is dark dots. In his hut: tallies under one word,
*EATEN*, and a photo of a smiling woman holding up a jar. He is sure the cases he burned were empty. **Boss: a root as
thick as a tower** bores up for Tallow. His fire draws moths until they drop, Sorrel among them; Umber gathers every
glint and blazes, a nearer light, and Sorrel turns to her out of the flame. Tallow holds out a coal, the first gift a
person has given her. Item text: *"It wants everything. Carry it carefully."*

### Act II: the midpoint (Night 4)

**The old road**, the only way in, runs past hummocks that were cars and ends in a wall of root packed with cases.
Lampwings hunt the coal all the way. She burns through the wall, and a line of dots on her map goes dark. In a flooded
room, small dishes hold its dead siblings. Item text: *"Eggs that never hatched."*

**The mountain.** At its heart, the first straight lines in the game:
- A sign, *No one goes hungry*, and a poster: a hand lifting a small light out of a drawn human head into a dish.
  *Grown from us, to feed us.*
- A screen, still lit, where a ball bounces between two bats and nobody plays.
- A case grown around a chair: the woman from Tallow's photo, old now, jars of feed unopened in her lap, the Mouth's
  oldest stalk running into her case.
- In the middle, a dish: grey, cracked, a handprint on the glass.

Umber touches the stalk and the prologue's first shot returns, wider: the pale light is a cloud of cells in this dish,
the blur is the woman's hand. Each missed ball floods the cloud with static; rally by rally, the static fades. Then
the woman turns off the lights and goes, the feed runs dry, and the static floods back.

**The world breathes.** From very high, light runs along every root on earth, and every grey dot swells with one slow
breath. **It has not been eating the world. It has been feeding it.** Only Tallow's ring and her own burned line stay
still; the camera holds on hers. From now on her map breathes.

Dun lays the vixen, caught at last, at the dish. Pale threads lift toward the vixen. Umber pours every glint she
carries into the dish, the Mouth's first gift in forty years. The threads drop the vixen, the static stops dead, and
they draw together into **a moth, wings open, the first picture it has ever made**. Every root in the room turns to
her. Cut to the Grove: a lampwing turns its lamp, slowly, onto Sorrel, who carries no light at all. **It sees her, and
through her, every moth.**

The vixen bolts. Dun stares at Umber, seeing her for the first time. The mountain chases her out. At the road's foot
the lantern is smothered and she has no glint to wake it. As she fades, the forest's amber runs up into it from the
ground and it spins her again.

### The lowest point, Night 5: the Grove, overrun

Pale root pours out of the pool and crosses the Grove in a night, smothering its first lantern. Lampwings carry
dancers off; roots climb toward next year's cocoons. **Boss: three of its mouths, which learn her moves.**

After, Sorrel finds his silver-edged partner sealed in a case no bigger than a seed, a root drawing it toward the
pool. He wraps himself round it. Umber folds her wing over his; he does not move. Coral lamps rise out of the pool and
settle on her; on Night 2 he would have drawn them off. She has to go. The camera stays on him, a dark knot round a
pale seed, as the root closes over the lantern.

She re-forms alone at another lantern. The mountain sends Dun to carry her to a case. Dun stands over her, then noses
her spilled glints into a heap, lies across the lantern's door facing out, and pulls until its root leash snaps. From
now on Dun guards her lanterns and carries cases out of her way, so she never burns another wall. Moths fleeing other
woods gather there.

A caterpillar on a frosted twig keeps failing to spin. Umber wraps it and shivers, as Sorrel did for her; its silk
holds, then the frost cracks it open. She shivers again, slower, until she can barely move, and this time it holds.

### Act III: the mountain moves (Nights 6–7)

**Night 6, the kept city.** She passes the empty firebreak and her own burned wall, its cases grey and split, into the
kept city. People hang in rows under every food hall's sign, *No one goes hungry*; over one hall the last sign still
burns, and moths circle it. On the walls the Mouth has grown dozens of pale moths. Far behind, the Grove's second
lantern goes out.

On the roofs the lamps close in, and nowhere is dark. Sorrel drops from above, blazing with every glint he could
carry, and draws the lamps off her as on Night 2. At her lantern he lands dark, folds his wing over hers and taps her
wingtip. Cut to the Grove: the silver-edged seed, alone on the moss now, sinks into the pool.

**Boss: Tallow,** burning his way to the mountain. She shields the cases while Dun carries them clear, until he burns
one close enough to see it breathe, and drops his torch.

The Grove's last lantern goes out. Then a river of gold crosses the sky to her: every glint the forest has left, its
heart going dark to send them. Below, Tallow walks up the old road into the mountain, empty-handed.

**Night 7, inside the mountain.** Dun holds the way in. If she still carries the coal, every lamp in the shaft turns
to it. Sorrel draws the roots off her once more; it costs him every scale, and he follows her down, dim. At the
bottom, the dish. At the back, Tallow kneels at his sister's case, sealed in one of his own: he came without fire, and
it kept him.

**Boss: the Mouth itself.** It tries to keep her, not kill her: caught, she slows. Her blade cuts its holds, but only
glints hurt it, so she spends the forest's last gift into its mouths, one by one.

## 6. The endings

The last glint goes in. The dish glows amber and the root stops everywhere: given enough, it takes nothing more. But
its stalks to every sleeper go slack, the amber fades, and frost feathers across the glass. Its threads try to curl
round themselves, as the caterpillar's did, and fall open. Still it reaches: a pale root grows a case round Sorrel,
gently. **This is its real offer:** inside, his colour returns, and on the case's skin he dances with his silver-edged
partner, long past their week. A second case opens beside it, her size. She chooses by acting:

- **Spin** (the true ending). She walks the rim, shivering her warmth into the frost; each lap costs her glide, then
  jump, then dash. Sorrel climbs out of his case and follows, shivering too. Then the moths from her lanterns, the
  brood, every moth it never noticed, until the mountain is furred with shivering wings. The frost clears and the
  threads curl, slowly, and hold. Warmed, the Mouth does what the caterpillar did: it lets go. Its stalks pull free of
  every sleeper, the prologue runs backwards (roots draw back, some town lights return, the stag's antlers sink), and
  the mountain spins itself shut. When Umber can no longer walk, Sorrel folds his wing over hers and taps her wingtip
  once. *Epilogue:* in the kept city, cases split and a thin grey hand reaches toward an empty food hall; some cases
  never open. In the lab, Tallow and his sister wake; he holds out his burned hand and she takes it. On the Grove's
  pool a seed-sized case splits and a silver-edged moth flies up. On the rim, two moths lie still, wing over wing, and
  Dun lies down beside them. In spring the brood hatches, and, for the first time in forty years, so do the vixen's
  cubs; through the Grove's dark heart, under the moth stone, pushes one green shoot. *Last image:* that autumn,
  Tallow's sister, old, climbs the mountain at dusk and lays her palm on its silk, framed like the prologue's blur.
  Something presses back from inside. She doesn't lift her hand.
- **Stay.** She lies down beside her brother and both cases close. *Epilogue:* the root takes the Grove and the
  brood's cocoons never hatch. The world is kept, quiet and fed, and nothing is ever born again.
- **Burn.** Only if she still carries the coal. She drops it into the dish, and every root on earth goes grey.
  *Epilogue:* the takeover ends in a night. On her map dot after dot goes dark; a few wake. Tallow wakes and holds his
  sister as she stops breathing. Moths fly free over a silent world.

## 7. The question it leaves

The thing that took the whole world so no one would go hungry, and only saw a moth when the moth fed it: was that
love, or had it only learned how to make the static stop?
