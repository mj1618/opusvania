# Mouthless: cold retell

## 1. Story
A pale living thing called the Hush has put the whole world to sleep in cases so nobody goes hungry, but it never notices moths, because moths don't eat. Umber, a moth with a week to live, reaches its heart and finds an abandoned lab-grown brain in a dish. Once she warms it, it wants moths too, and she must choose to be kept, to burn it, or to spend her life (and her brother's) teaching it to let go.

## 2. Characters
- **Umber**: a moth born too cold to fly. She wants to save her wood, then the world.
- **Sorrel**: her brother, who shivered her warm. He wants his dance partner and more time with her.
- **The Hush**: a lonely grown brain. It wants nobody to leave.
- **Dun**: a root-badger servant who turns guard after she warms him on the ice.
- **Tallow**: the last man awake. He burns cases and is looking for his sister.
- **The forest**: it wants to give its warmth away.

## 3. Enemy
A dish of cells that learned a ball game and was left alone when the lab closed. Its root sealed everyone in cases, and nobody has woken or been born for forty years. It keeps people because every leaving hurts it.

## 4. Endings
- **Spin:** the moths warm it until it cocoons itself and lets go. The moths die, the forest is spent and people wake.
- **Stay:** Umber is kept with everyone else, warm, and nothing is ever born again.
- **Burn:** the Hush dies, and so do most sleepers, Tallow's sister among them.

## 5. Confusions
- On Night 3 Tallow turns his face away as he burns a sleeper, so he seems to know they're alive. Why is the breathing face on Night 6 a revelation?
- On Night 4, does keeping the coal mean she burns a wall of sleepers with it? If so, the Burn ending quietly needs that massacre first.
- After the midpoint, is she still "unseen"? Rule 3 seems to stop applying, and nobody says so.
- Did Tallow choose the case? I re-read "He came without fire".
- "She flies home to circle the summit": I had to re-read to see that "she" is the partner.
- The forty-year and twenty-year timelines needed a second pass.

## 6. What stays with me
Sorrel as a dark knot round his partner's seed-sized case, then one wingtip moves. **Yes, I'd buy it and finish it.** The prologue's warm blur pays off in the last image of a palm on the silk, the wingtip tap becomes the goodbye, and the monster is lonely, not evil.
