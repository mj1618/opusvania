# Mouthless

**In one sentence.** Umber, a little moth with a week to live, against the Hush, a living thing that swallowed the
world and never noticed moths.

**The spine.**
1. A living thing the moths call the Hush has grown over the world and put everyone to sleep.
2. At its heart Umber finds what it is: a brain people grew in a dish from their own cells, then abandoned. It has fed
   the world ever since, but no one ever came back to warm it.
3. When she warms it, it sees moths for the first time and comes for them. She must choose: be kept, burn it, or spend
   her life so it lets go.

**The central question:** kept safe forever, or spent so something new can be born?

**The cast.**

| Who | What they are | What they want |
|---|---|---|
| **Umber** | a moth with a week to live, who hides | to dance once at the summit with her brother; then to save the world |
| **Sorrel** | her brother | to dance with his partner, then more time with Umber |
| **The Hush** | the mind that took the world | to feed everyone and keep them close, because every leaving hurts it |
| **Dun** | its oldest burrback | to bring it everything it wants kept |
| **Tallow** | the last man awake | to burn the cases, which he thinks eat people, and find his sister |
| **The forest** | an old, slow mind no one made | to give its warmth away |

## 1. The takeover

Pale tissue, cool as bone, covers the human world. Hollows open in the roads and swallow living things; each comes up
sealed in a grey case that hangs on a stalk like fruit. Inside, a sleeper is fed through the stalk and does not age,
but grows thinner every year. No one wakes, and no one is born. The first city is now a breathing mountain. Some
animals are regrown as its servants: burrbacks (badgers under shells of root) and lampwings (once fireflies). Its root
still creeps into the last wild places. When it hurts, it hisses grey static. One man, Tallow, has stayed awake behind
a ring of fire.

## 2. The rules

1. **Give warmth, don't fight.** A glint is a drop of the forest's warmth. It calms a creature or makes root let go. Her blade only buys a moment.
2. **Shiver when the glints run out.** Shivering gives her own warmth instead, but slows her until she rests.
3. **Moths go unseen.** The Hush and its burrbacks notice only things that eat, and moths have no mouths. Once she warms
   one of them, it sees her.
4. **Lampwings see light.** They dive at any light a moth carries.
5. **Light and fire hold root back.** Lanterns are old moth cocoons hardened like glass, and a glint lights one. She
   rests at a lit lantern, and when the cold takes her, she re-forms there.

## 3. The prologue (wordless)

1. Black. A small pale light, breathing. A warm blur rests on it, and it steadies. The blur lifts, frost creeps in, and
   the light breaks into static.
2. From high above: a pale mountain with that light at its core. Root spreads from it like frost, and town lights go
   out.
3. Umber's wood at dusk: a black pool below, a summit above where the moths dance. Dun, shelled in root, wades through
   the dance without seeing them. Nearby a vole gnaws a seed, and Dun's head swings round.
4. On a frosty night two cocoons split over the pool. Sorrel flies up; Umber drops to the moss, too cold to fly. He
   comes back, folds his wing over hers and shivers until she can fly, taps her wingtip, and is gone. A glint drops in
   front of her. She lifts it to her face as the vole did its seed, but she has no mouth. She holds it out instead,
   and it lights her. Item text: *"Warmth the forest grows to give away. A moth has no mouth to keep it."* Under the
   pool, pale root steams. Title.

## 4. The acts

### Act I: out of the wood (Nights 1–3)

**Night 1, the wood.** Racing Sorrel up the trunks, she learns his somersault, dash and glide. He shows her the high
island where a big glint grows for the summit lantern. At the summit
he dances with his partner, a silver-edged moth, and beckons Umber in; she stays in the dark, watching. She lights the
two lower lanterns. **Boss: a great lampwing** dives at the big glint as she carries it home. *Play:* each time it
hangs in the air, she gives it a glint, until its lamp folds. When all three lanterns burn, light fills a moth carved
on a standing stone, the only picture the forest makes. **The forest acts:** warmth runs through the ground, the root
under the pool shrinks back, and a thorn grows from the shrine into her grasp: her blade.

**Night 2, the gardens.** A hollow swallows a hare, and its case swings up into an orchard where hundreds of cases hang
among the apples. **Boss: Dun,** heading for a vixen's den. *Play:* Dun never attacks. Each glint she drops makes it
stumble back a step, while Sorrel draws the lampwings off. When her glints run out, the vixen bolts across a frozen
pond, and Dun crashes through the ice after her. It lies there shaking, hissing static. Umber folds her wings over its
snout and shivers until the shaking stops, and its eyes find her for the first time. Later, when Sorrel is too cold to
fly, she shivers for him, and he taps her wingtip.

**Night 3, the kept town.** A family's cases hang round a kitchen table set for dinner. Tallow's house stands in a
ring of ash. On his wall, beside a photo of his sister in a greenhouse coat, are tally marks for twenty winters and one
word: *EATEN*. He holds his torch to a case. To him it is grey stone; only Umber, pressed to its skin, sees the sleeping
face inside. The camera stays on the face as the flame takes it. **Boss: a root as thick as a tower** bores up through
the street for him. *Play:* she climbs it, giving a glint into each knot until it sinks. Tallow gives her a red coal
from his fire. Item text: *"It never goes out. It is for the thing in the mountain."* At dawn Sorrel flies home to his
partner, and Umber goes on alone.

### Act II: the midpoint (Night 4)

**The old road** climbs the mountain past a peeling poster: a light lifted from a woman's hand into a dish
(*Grown from us*), then the dish wired to greenhouses and dinner tables (*Now it feeds us*). At
the top, the lab's door is grown shut. Its sign reads *No one goes hungry*, with *CLOSED* pasted across it, and cases
hang beneath. She slips in through a crack only a moth would fit.

**The lab.** Dark greenhouses surround one dish. The greenhouse coat from Tallow's photo hangs on a hook. The dish is grey and cracked, and the Hush's thickest
stalk runs into it. Tallow's sister, grey-haired, kneels in a case grown against it, her palm pressed to the inside of
the case a finger's width from the dish.

**The memory.** Umber touches the stalk, and the prologue's first shot returns. The pale light is a cloud of cells in
this dish; the warm blur is the sister's hand. Beside it a screen plays a ball game: the cloud moves a bar, and each
missed ball hisses static that makes it flinch. Then the workers wire the same screen to the greenhouses, and every
catch sends water down a pipe to the sprouts. *CLOSED* goes up. The workers walk out one by one, and each leaving
hisses like a missed ball. The sister goes last, looking back. The screen goes dark, the pipes run dry, the town goes
hungry, and the static never stops. Then the first root slides down a dry pipe to a hungry child. A case closes round
her, she is fed, and the static drops a little. Years later the sister walks back in carrying a lit torch. The cloud flinches from the flame. She pinches it out and reaches for the dish, but a case closes round her
first.

**The turn.** Dun lays a sleeping hare at the dish, and root lifts to draw it down. Umber pours all her glints into
the dish. The root lets the hare go, the static stops, and the cloud's threads draw a moth with open wings: Umber, the
first picture the Hush has ever made. Dun looks from the picture to her. A root cups her, and a case no bigger than a
seed starts to close round her, warm as a folded wing. She almost stays. Then she wriggles free, and as she flies the
static floods back, like every leaving before. The Hush comes after her: every root on earth turns toward moths. Root
smothers every lantern near her, and she flees until the cold takes her. **The forest acts:** it pushes warmth up the
Hush's own root into the nearest lantern, and she re-forms. Far away, a lampwing lands on Sorrel as he dances and
leaves its coral glow on his wings, a light he cannot put down.

### The lowest point (Night 5): the wood overrun

Overnight, root pours from the pool over the wood. Burrbacks wade through the dance, and this time every head swings
round. Lampwings lift moths gently into little cases on the root, and roots climb toward next year's cocoons.
**Boss: three hollows that learn her moves.** *Play:* each one opens where she last landed.

Sorrel finds his partner sealed in a seed-sized case on a root, her silver edge showing through. He wraps himself round
it. Umber folds her wing over his. He does not move, and lampwings rise toward his glowing wings. She leaves him. The
camera holds on him, a dark knot round a pale case, as root closes over the lantern beside him. Then he shivers,
keeping the case warm. At dawn the root tears it from his wings and drags it toward the mountain.

She re-forms alone, and the mountain sends Dun to take her. Instead Dun noses her spilled glints together, pulls
against its own root until it snaps, and lies across her lantern's door, facing out. From then on Dun guards her lantern,
and fleeing moths gather there.

A caterpillar on a frosted twig keeps trying to spin a cocoon; each time its threads curl round it, then fall open.
Umber wraps it and shivers until she can barely move, and at last the silk holds.

### Act III: the mountain moves (Nights 6–7)

**Night 6, the dead city.** Sorrel follows his partner's root toward the mountain and catches up with Umber. The
Hush's picture of Umber covers every wall of the food halls. Moths fleeing the woods crowd round the city's last
streetlamp, and lampwings fold them into cases. Sorrel, still glowing coral, draws the lampwings off her, and at her
lantern he folds his wing over hers.

**Boss: Tallow,** out of his ring of ash to burn the city's cases. *Play:* she cuts each case's stalk before his torch
reaches it, and Dun drags it clear. At last he lights one she can't reach, worn thin as a leaf. Through the flame he
sees a face inside, breathing, like the one on his street. He lays the torch down and follows her.

The wood's last lantern goes out, and root climbs toward the forest's heart. **The forest acts:** it sends her
everything it has left, a river of gold across the sky. On the stone, the carving
empties.

**Night 7, inside the mountain.** Dun holds the door. A flooded passage is lined with smaller dishes, cold and empty.
Item text: *"Grown beside it. Only one lived."* Sorrel draws the roots off her once more and follows her down, bare of
every scale but alive. At the back, his partner's little case hangs beside the dish. Tallow walks past
Umber, lies down in a case that opens beside his sister's, and lays his palm to hers through the skin.

**Boss: the Hush itself.** *Play:* its hollows open under her, and she closes each with the forest's last glints. When
the last one closes, the root stops creeping across the world. But the dish stays grey, the static goes on, and no case
opens. Its threads curl round the dish and fall open, like the caterpillar's.

## 5. The endings

Her glints are gone, and still it reaches. A root grows a case round Sorrel and sets it against his partner's. Inside,
his colour returns, and two shadows dance on the lit skin. A third case opens beside them, Umber's size. She chooses by
acting.

- **Spin** (the true ending). *Play:* Umber presses herself to the dish and shivers until the static fades. Then she
  circles the rim, and its threads follow her round. Each lap costs her glide, then somersault, then dash. Sorrel
  climbs out of his case and circles beside her: the dance she never joined. Moths come from every wood until the
  mountain is furred with wings. Every stalk on earth draws back to the mountain, and the moths spin them into a white
  cocoon round the dish, with nothing left to feed through: mouthless, like her. Across the world the cases go slack,
  and people wake thin and hungry into a world grown old. Through the silk, the pale cloud takes the shape of a
  sleeping moth. When Umber can't walk, Sorrel folds his wing over hers and taps her wingtip, and Dun lies down beside
  them. Neither moth rises. *Epilogue:* the forest is spent; the carving stays empty and no glints grow. Tallow and his
  sister walk down hand in hand to clear the greenhouses, Dun padding behind. The partner's case opens with the rest.
  She lands beside the two still moths, taps Sorrel's wingtip, and flies home to circle the summit alone for the rest
  of her week. In spring the caterpillar's cocoon splits on its twig, and moths hatch into an ordinary dark wood, with
  only themselves to give. *Last image:* that autumn, Tallow's sister lays her palm on the mountain's silk, like the
  blur in the prologue. Something presses back from inside. She doesn't lift her hand.
- **Stay.** Umber lies down in the open case, and it closes beside the other two. Inside, warm, she can fly; three
  shadows dance on the lit skin, and Dun lies across the cases, facing out. *Epilogue:* every sleeper is warm and fed.
  Root covers the wood gently, and next year's cocoons never open. The static has stopped for good. Nothing is born
  again.
- **Burn.** Umber drops the coal into the dish, and every root on earth goes grey. The two small cases split, and
  Sorrel and his partner climb out. *Epilogue:* the Hush dies in a night, and of its sleepers only those taken days ago
  wake. Tallow wakes and holds his sister as she stops breathing. Flying home, the three moths watch the cases go dark
  round the family's kitchen table, then tree by tree through the orchard, while Dun, a badger again, pads below
  them. At dawn, her last, Umber dances at the summit for the first time, with Sorrel and every waking moth of her
  wood, above a world where nothing else wakes.

## 6. The question it leaves

It took the whole world so no one would go hungry, and it only saw a moth when the moth warmed it. Was that love, or
had it only learned to make the static stop?

## Dev note: the real-world lineage

Cortical Labs' grown neurons learned a ball game in 2022, fed noise when they missed. Grown neurons die without body
warmth: that is what the Hush lacks.
