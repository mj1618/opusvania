# Review: The Moth and the Mouth (round 20, draft 2)

## 1. Anchor gate: PASS

All three readers open with the litmus without being prompted. The casual reader wrote "A pale root-thing called the Mouth has sealed every person and animal into pods, and you're Umber, a moth with no mouth". All three also found the lab-grown brain and put it at the centre of their account of the enemy. It is found through objects, not told: the bat-and-ball screen, "*Grown from us, to feed us*", the dish with a handprint. Nothing on screen is technical.

The Mouth is on screen and acting in every act. It feeds the orchard mouths and sends a tower root (Act I), breathes through the whole world at the reveal (Act II), then overruns the Grove, grows pale moths to search for her and fights as the final boss (Act III). Its scale is always the world.

**The owner's four ideas**
- Biological AI takeover: **central**.
- A moth saves the world: **central**.
- Moths judged insignificant and underestimated: **central**. "It only sees mouths" is the stealth rule, and it flips at the midpoint. It is a blind spot rather than a judgement, but it does the same job in play.
- Sentience: **central** for the Mouth (the closing question, and the moth picture it draws). **Minor** for the forest, which every reader listed and none saw act before the river of gold.

## 2. Verdict: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES

There is no foundation problem. All three retellings get the spine right in three sentences, and all three readers would finish the game.

## 3. At the bar

- **The hook is the mechanic.** "It never noticed moths: it only sees mouths." The title, the hero's body, the stealth and the antagonist's blind spot all come from one idea.
- **A real reversal, carried by the UI.** "It has not been eating the world. It has been feeding it." The breathing map beads rewrite every wall she burned; the reveal "would make me go back and look at what I burned" (lore reader).
- **Dun.** Every reader kept the same image: Dun "lies across the lantern's door, facing out, and pulls until its root-leash snaps." Rival to guard, wordless.
- **The antagonist is wrong in a way that teaches something.** "A thing that takes everything can never be given anything." Its maker sits sealed with "jars of feed unopened in her lap", and Tallow's photo shows the same woman holding up a jar.
- **The ending costs you in controls.** In Spin, "each lap costs her glide, then jump, then dash". The player gives up the moveset that got them there.

## 4. Problems, ranked

1. **Sorrel's exit on Night 5 cannot be read (POLISH, top priority).** All three readers asked why he leaves. The emotional payoff on the rim depends on this pair. *Fix:* make the sealed dancer the partner he was dancing with, and show him wrapping himself round her seed-sized case. The lamp then stops on Umber, and he chooses the case over his sister. He is not leaving her out of blame; he is staying with someone. His return on Night 6 then means he has chosen Umber. Add one or two wordless companion beats earlier, such as resting wing to wing at a lantern.
2. **The black-seed choice does nothing (POLISH).** All three readers caught it. *Fix:* cut the step. The Burn ending stays open while she still carries the coal, and carrying it costs her through a rule the game already has: lampwings dive at any light she carries. Keeping it means being hunted through Nights 5 to 7.
3. **Burning the wall is morally silent (POLISH).** Before the reveal, Umber burns a line of sleepers and nothing in the game reacts. *Fix:* after the breath, the camera holds on her dark line on the map. On Night 6 the path takes her back past it, still dark. This needs no words.
4. **The Spin ending's physics are unclear (POLISH).** "Taking nothing, it has no warmth" confused the heart reader, and the lore reader found the caterpillar parallel "too neat". *Fix:* show it. Its feed stalks go slack, frost forms on the dish, and its threads try to curl and cannot. Make the caterpillar scene a little less of a rehearsal by letting it fail once more before it holds.
5. **The forest is billed as a character but barely acts (POLISH).** *Fix:* give it one visible act in each act, such as glints pushed up at her feet beside a cut root, or a lantern relit from below. Also answer whether it survives Spin: one green shoot through the Grove's dark heart in the epilogue.
6. **Small clarity snags (POLISH).** Cut the unshown flaw "she hides". Write "a case of his own" and "Umber's lantern". Give the maker's waking one shot. Show the firebreak, the kept city and the mountain together from one summit.

## 6. At the bar? No, narrowly.

The idea, the antagonist, the reversal and the way the mechanics carry the theme already stand next to Hollow Knight and Nine Sols. **The single biggest gap is the central relationship.** The outline sets up Umber and Sorrel as its heart, a bond to rank with Yi and Shuanshuan or Ori and Naru, but their middle is one unexplained exit. "Two moths lie still" on the rim will not land until the player has seen why Sorrel left and what it cost him to come back. Fix problem 1 and this is at the bar.
