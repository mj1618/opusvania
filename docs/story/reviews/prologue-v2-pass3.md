# Prologue v2, pass 3: a blind viewing, then a director's review (2026-10-14)

Source: the sheet, the frames in `shots/prologue/frames/`, and frames from `clips/prologue-small.mp4` taken every 1 s
from 10 to 131 s, every 0.5 s at the key beats, and at 5 fps through 82-85. The cut runs 131 s (target: about 172 s).

## 1. Blind

**Retelling:** A tall, pale moth hatches by a sleeping fox, waits, and wraps the small dark moth that drops from the
second cocoon. They play under a cloud of glowing moths, ride the fox, and watch the ground swallow it and seal it in a
glass pod. A red thing on a thread lights up and they hide. On a dark height she offers him her light; he pushes it
back and leaps into the swarm, leaving her alone with the pod.

- **Who:** a showy older brother and a smaller, darker sister. I could tell them apart from 20 s. The fox reads as
  their bed or guardian.
- **Wants:** he wants the swarm (he looks up at 40-52 and leaves at 113). She wants him to stay (108-110). I couldn't
  tell that she wants the fox.
- **Threat:** the ground (65-70), then a red spider on a thread (86), which I read as a second monster. A veiled
  mound with one glowing eye sits on the horizon (102+).
- **Felt:** the wrap (21-29), the fox sinking (66-70), the offer refused (108-112, the best beat), and her alone
  (116-118).
- **Bored:** 30-37 (nothing happens); 42-52 (a light passed back and forth on one platform); 74-83 (she stands on
  the pod); 92-100 (a static huddle; the pink glow never comes for them).
- **Confused:** the split husks have fang prongs. The hard cut at 58. At 67-69 the white column rises to her glint,
  and it reads as "it's drinking her light". At 84 the camera whips up so fast it plays as a cut. At 102, sea or land?
  The dot on a line looks like a fishing float. Sorrel's face at the summit is blown out white: is he possessed?

**Reading wrong:** the root column (it takes her light), the lamp (a separate spider), and Sorrel's exit.

**Guardrails.** *Never orange:* pass (bone case, coral lamp). *No mouths or teeth:* the hollow passes. The moths' own
husks show fangs in every forest shot, and the mound reads as a face with one glowing eye ("infected eyes" territory).
*Moths never worship light:* **at risk.** At 102-116 Sorrel's face is a white blaze and he leaps into a ring of
lights and vanishes. That is moth-to-flame.

**Against the bar.** It's better. The relationship now reads without help: a big pale brother, a small dark sister, a
wrap, play, an offer refused, a departure. That is Ori's engine. But Ori's loss has a visible cause, and here the
threat reads as three unrelated things (a hole, a spider, a ghost mound) with no rule. Hollow Knight carries its threat
with atmosphere and text, Nine Sols with explicit panels. This cut has neither, so it must act the rule, and it doesn't
yet. **Verdict: not at the bar. It's a good animatic with one great beat (the summit offer).** About half the beats
land, up from a third.

## 3. Fixes, ranked

1. **STORY (74-83 s): her fold on the case.** She lands, kneels, and wraps both wings round the case, in the same
   silhouette as his wrap at 22-27. Push in to 3x for 3 s: her eyes dim, she shivers, the case stays pale. This
   replaces 8 s of standing.
2. **STORY (102-116 s): he leaves for someone, not into light.** Drop the white blowout on his face. Turn the pair
   three-quarter-back. Draw the silver-edged moth as a moth, not goggles, and have it swoop to him at 104-106 so he
   follows it up. Add the tap at 112.5.
3. **STORY (60-70 s): show the rule.** The fox's muzzle goes into the pool twice, with ripples. The root tip is thin,
   bone-coloured and curling. It rises to her *face* (not her glint), pauses 1 s, and sinks.
4. **STORY (83-100 s): one threat, one eye.** Slow the tilt to 2.5 s, with the pulse climbing the case's stalk. Draw
   a pale lamp that kindles coral, not a red spider. At 92-100 the wash crawls down the bark, stops over the ferns,
   and leaves.
5. **CRAFT (16-58 s): earn the fox.** A pale thread steams in the moss from 16 s. Cut the hug to 5 s. She looks back at
   the fox at 34. Replace the cut at 58 with the glint landing on the fox's nose and the sneeze (2 s at 2x).
6. **CRAFT (14-131 s): fix the silhouettes.** Round off the husks' fang prongs. Break the mound's face-and-eye
   reading. Put the case's pale point in the canopy right below the twig, with its line running to the root front,
   and add dark town shapes so the land reads as land.

**The one thing that would most raise caring:** fix 1. His wrap at 21-29 now half-lands, but the case half of the
wing-fold rhyme is missing. When she does to the fox's case what he did for her, and it doesn't work, we learn who she
is, what she wants and what was lost.
