# Prologue v2, pass 4: a blind viewing, then a director's review (2026-10-15)

Sources: the sheet, the frames in `shots/prologue/frames/`, and `clips/prologue.mp4` (142 s) at 480x270: 1 fps
throughout, plus 2 fps at 30-34, 56-61, 72-77, 89-93 and 114-122. Times are clip seconds.

## 1. Blind

**Retelling:** A pale moth hatches by a sleeping beast and warms the small dark moth who falls into the frost. They
play under a ring of glowing moths. The fox they ride is taken by the ground and sealed in a glass pod. She drapes
her wing over the pod, a cocoon overhead turns into a red eye, and they hide. On a night height she offers him her
light; he refuses it and follows a white moth up into the ring, leaving her alone with it.

- **Who and want:** a tall pale brother who wants the swarm and the white moth (115-124), and a small dark sister who
  wants the fox (82-90) and wants him to stay (116-118). I could tell them apart at once.
- **Threat:** the ground that takes the fox (71-74), and a red eye (93-108). The tilt up the stalk joined them. I
  never learned why the moths were spared.
- **Felt:** the wrap (25-29), her wing over the pod (82-88), the refusal (116-119), and him leaving (122-124).
- **Bored:** 45-56 (one platform, a light passed back and forth) and 97-108 (a static huddle).
- **Confused:** the sleeping beast (12-38: domed, spiny, cat-faced) and the drinking fox (59-72: lean, flat, with a
  red stripe) look like two animals. The fox vanishes in about a second (72.5-73.5). The stalk rises to her *light*
  (74-76). The red lamp is shaped like a cocoon. The summit cloud holds a sleeping face, and the dot on a line looks
  like a shooting star. The tap is a ring floating between them (120). Her face is a white blaze under the title
  (126-135).

**Guardrails.**
- *Never orange:* passes, narrowly. The case is bone and the wash is pink, but the lamp measures hue 10-12° (coral)
  and reads orange-red at phone size, close to her amber glint (~20°).
- *No mouths or teeth:* pass.
- *Moths never worship light:* at risk. He rises into a ring of lights (eased because he follows the moth), and her

**Against the bar.** The relationship engine finally turns: he warms her, she warms the case, and it fails. That's
Ori's feeding rhyme, and it's ours. About two thirds of the beats land, up from half. But Ori's loss has a cause you
can state, Hollow Knight's threat a mood you can name, and Nine Sols explains itself. Here a viewer can't say why the
moths are spared, isn't sure the taken fox is *her* fox, and misses the summit split, which is the game's premise.
**AT THE BAR: no. It's close, three story fixes away.**

## 3. Fixes, ranked

1. **STORY, blocking: one fox, and a take we see (12-75 s).** Draw the hatching fox as the pool fox, curled, with its
   ember tail tip. Replace the cut at 58 with the glint landing on the drinking fox's nose and a 1 s sneeze at 2x.
   Slow the sinking at 72-75 to 3 s, with the camera low on the fox as its eyes close.
2. **STORY, blocking: the rule (73-77 s).** She holds the glint low, under her wing. The root tip rises past it to
   her *face*, pauses 1.5 s, turns to Sorrel, pauses, and sinks. Shoot it at 2.5x. That reads as "it looked and did
   not see", not "it wants light".
3. **STORY, blocking: the summit split (110-125 s).** Turn the pair three-quarter-back. At 113-115 he tilts up to the
   ring, and she tilts down to a small bone-white oval steaming in the canopy at the twig's foot (it replaces the star
   line). Make the mound a faceless, breathing mountain. Make the tap at 120 a wingtip touch. At 123-125 she looks up
   after him.
4. **CRAFT, polish: the title shot (126-135 s).** Cap the glint's bloom so her eyes stay readable. She lowers it to
   her chest and looks out at the mountain, not into the light.
5. **CRAFT, polish: the eye and the hide (92-108 s).** Give the lamp its own silhouette: a bone bulb with root fibres,
   not a cocoon. Make it cold rose (~350°). At 100-103 the wash stops on the ferns, one amber thread leaks, she folds
   tighter, and the wash moves on. Trim 45-56 by 4 s.

**What would most raise caring:** fix 1. The fold rhyme already works. Once we know the taken fox is the one they
were born against, we'll grieve it.
