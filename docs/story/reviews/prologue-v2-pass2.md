# Prologue v2, pass 2: a blind viewing, then a director's review (2026-10-14)

Source: `shots/prologue/sheet.jpg`, all 67 frames in `shots/prologue/frames/`, and about 30 extra frames from
`clips/prologue-small.mp4` (60-119 s). The cut runs 131 s; the target is about 172 s.

## 1. Blind (watched before reading any doc)

**Retelling:** Two identical purple moth-children hatch beside a sleeping spiny fox, huddle against it, and play in
the canopy while a ring of glowing eyes watches them. The fox wakes, the ground opens like a toothed jaw and swallows
it, and it comes back sealed in an amber pod. A red lantern-creature on a thread hunts the clearing while one moth
hides in the ferns. On a moonlit height one moth leaps up into a swarm of lights and the other is left behind, alone
by the pod.

- **Who:** two siblings (twin cocoons). I could not tell them apart, so I didn't know whose story it was until 116 s,
  when one leaves. The fox reads as a guardian or parent, but its glowing muzzle looks like a nose-lamp, not a tail
  ember.
- **Wants:** the leaver wants the swarm. I only learned that at 114 s, and the swarm had looked hostile at 44-58 s.
  The one who stays wants... I don't know. Nothing she does says "fox".
- **Threat:** the ground with teeth (68-72 s), then the red lamp (88 s). I read them as two separate monsters. The
  pod looked warm, like amber, so it might be protecting the fox.
- **Felt something:** at 70 s the fox going under (the first jolt in the film), and at 116-118 s the moth left alone
  on the twig. Bored at 0-10 s (no context yet, which is fine), at 22-32 s (ten seconds of identical moths hugging),
  and at 40-60 s (characters about 30 px tall; I couldn't see "his" or "bumps").
- **Confused:** at 46 s the dance lights have slit-shaped eyes like owls glaring. At 86 s the cut to empty platforms
  left me asking where the moths went. At 92-102 s only one moth is in the ferns: where is the other? At 104 s the
  "sea" with white veins, the veiled mound with a light inside, and the point on a line at bottom right meant nothing
  to me. At 131 s the pod is gone in gameplay.

## 2. Informed: the intent against the screen

| Intended beat | Reads? |
|---|---|
| Breath (0-10) | Yes, as mood. Fine. |
| Sorrel waits for her; frost; her wings stuck; his wing-fold warms her; the tap | **No.** No frost, no failed wings, no distinct fold or tap. It reads as two hatchlings cuddling. |
| She looks at the fox (34-38) | No. |
| He shows, she copies; the race; she wins while he stares at the dance | No. It's too wide, and the dance reads as eyes (**reads wrong**). |
| Glint keep-up; the sneeze | No. The glint is a pixel, and the fox isn't in shot for the sneeze. |
| Fox drinks twice; the pale thread; the moss turns | No. The fox "drinks" over a ledge into air, and there's no thread. |
| The hollow takes the fox | **Yes**, strongly. But the ring of pale root lips reads as a toothed mouth, which breaks the de-mouthing rule. |
| Root tip looks at her and passes (the rule) | Missing. |
| Case, bone and breathing; ember greys | **Reads wrong.** The case is amber and warm, so it looks protective (and the Hush must never be orange). |
| She lands on the case and folds her wing over it | No. She stands beside it, holding the glint up. Sorrel hovering reads. |
| A pulse up the stalk lights the lamp: the Hush's eye | No. A hard cut to another place, so it reads as a separate hunter. |
| The hide; he drags her; light leaks | Half. She hides and the wash sweeps, but Sorrel is absent. |
| Summit: they look up and down; pale point and line; towns dark | No. Both face the camera, so their gazes can't be read, and the point looks like a fishing line. |
| Offer, fold back, tap, he goes without looking back | Half. He goes and she stays, which reads. The offer and the tap don't. |
| Alone; husks; case; item text | Yes, until the case vanishes at 131.4 s. |

About a third of the beats land. Every beat that fails, fails for the same reason: the acting is at play scale (moths
30-60 px tall, side-on, in wide shots). Each one is a three-second action that needs a 3x close-up and a face turned
toward its object.

**Against the bar.** Ori's Nibel prologue works because the two characters can't be confused (huge dark Naru, tiny
white Ori). Every beat is one readable action in a medium shot, and a repeated habit (feeding) breaks in front of you.
This script has the same engine: the wing-fold habit that he gives and she repeats. But neither fold reads, so the
engine doesn't turn. Hollow Knight's opening is atmosphere, not character, and the Grove frames already compete with it
there. Nine Sols leans on text, so this is more ambitious. **Verdict: not at the bar as storytelling. It's a promising
animatic.** The bones are right (siblings, a fox taken, the eye that hunts light, one leaves for love), but a first-time
viewer gets the plot outline without the who or the why, so they watch it rather than feel it.

## 3. Fixes, ranked

1. **STORY (10-38 s): make the siblings two people.** Give them different silhouettes and colours (Sorrel lighter,
   wings always half open, antennae swept back; Wing darker, smaller, wings shut). Shoot the hatch at 3x and hold
   each action: he looks up, he waits under her cocoon, she drops and can't open her wings, he folds over her, her
   eyes brighten, the tap. Cut the hug to about 4 s.
2. **STORY (44-58 s, 104-124 s): the dance must look desirable, not watchful.** Remove the eye slits. Draw soft wings
   turning in a moving ring with trails. Have the silver-edged moth swoop within 100 px of Sorrel, close to camera.
3. **STORY (62-86 s): make the hollow and the case one cause.** Put the fox's muzzle in water and have it drink
   twice. Make the root lips steam and curl, not show teeth. Make the case **bone-white**, with the ember greying
   inside. Add the root tip that rises to her face and sinks back.
4. **STORY (78-102 s): she warms the case, and the case calls the lamp.** Land Wing on the case with Sorrel's exact
   fold from 24-31 s. Replace the cut at 86 s with a tilt up the stalk following a pulse to the lamp lighting coral.
   Put Sorrel in the ferns at 92 s, dragging her in.
5. **CRAFT (104-120 s): the summit.** Turn the moths' backs to camera, three-quarter, so "up" and "down" read. Push
   in for the offer, the fold-back and the tap. Make the case's pale point and its line to the root front bright
   enough to find. Hold 2 s on her alone before the title.
6. **CRAFT (40-60 s, 131 s): the play stretch and continuity.** Frame the race and the keep-up at 1.3-1.5x so the
   glint and the sneeze can be seen. Keep the case standing in gameplay after the wake.

**The one thing that would most raise caring:** make the wing-fold rhyme read. Show him warming her at the hatching
(close, held, her eyes brightening), then show her doing the same to the fox's case, and it doesn't work. That single
rhyme tells us who she is, what she wants and what was lost, and it's the game's ending in miniature.
