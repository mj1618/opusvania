# MOUTHLESS

**LITMUS.** "It's about Vesper, a little moth with no mouth, against the Mouth, a living thing that swallowed the
whole world and never bothered to notice moths. Halfway through you find out what it really is: a brain people grew
from their own cells, starving, and it's been feeding everyone but itself."

**The spine.**
1. A living thing the moths call the Mouth has swallowed the world into its roots. It never noticed moths, because it
   only sees mouths and light.
2. Vesper, a moth with no mouth, slips through it to its heart and finds a brain grown by people and left to starve,
   feeding the world and never fed.
3. When she feeds it, it sees her kind and hunts them, and she must choose: be kept, burn it, or spend her life so it
   lets go.

**The central question:** kept safe forever, or spent so something new can be born?

| Who | Wants | Pull on Vesper |
|---|---|---|
| **Vesper**, a moth with no mouth and a week to live | to keep the root out of her brood's wood, then to free the world | can't eat, only carry and give; her flaw is that she hides |
| **Sorrel**, her brother | their week together: the dance, then more time with her | draws danger off her by shining; leaves when her gift sets the Mouth on the moths; comes back |
| **The Mouth** | no one hungry, no one leaving, ever | ignores her, then sees her, then wants to keep her more than anything |
| **Dun**, its oldest burrback, half grown into root | to be the one who feeds it | rival, then the first of its servants to refuse it |
| **Tallow**, the last man awake | to burn it and get back his sister | gives her a coal, and his war |
| **The forest** | to keep giving | a slow mind under the Grove that makes light to give away: the Mouth's opposite, and losing |

## 1. The takeover

What the player meets is a myth: a pale, warm, rooted thing that swallowed the world. Its mouths open in the ground
and draw living things down. It steams in the cold, because all its warmth is taken. Every person on earth lies grey
and still in a **case** of root, like a seed pod, hanging from a stalk; no one has woken or been born in forty years.
Towers are wrapped like cocoons, and the first city is now **the mountain**, a glowing peak of folded tissue on every
horizon. Deer, foxes, a whale on a white shore are sealed away too. Some animals it re-grew as servants:
**burrbacks** were badgers, shelled in root to haul bodies to the mouths; **lampwings** were fireflies, now its eyes.
Its root is creeping through the last wild places, the moths' wood among them.

The game runs over seven nights. Within a night the world is open, and old places change as the root advances.

## 2. The Mouth

**What it is.** Living brain cells on glass, taught one bat-and-ball game, then grown across a building to run a
city's farms. (Dev note: this is the real-world lineage of Cortical Labs' dish-grown neurons. No player-facing text
uses technical words; the player learns it from objects in the lab.)

**What it wants.** It was made to feed people, and its maker, Tallow's sister, fed it by hand. When the company failed
she switched off the lights and left, meaning to come back. Its feed ran out. It learned two wrongs, hunger and being
left, and found that a body sealed in a case and fed through a stalk suffers neither. So it followed its feed tubes
into the city, the soil and every tree, and sends everything it draws from the earth down the stalks to the sleepers.

**Where it is wrong.** Nothing kept can grow: no cubs, no children. And it is starving; its own dish has had nothing
in forty years. When its maker came back with food, it took her before she could give it. **A thing that takes
everything can never be given anything,** so the root never stops.

**Is it a someone?** Both readings stay on screen. *For:* once it sees Vesper it raises her shape on every wall, as
the forest carved a moth into the Grove's stone, and at the end it gives. *Against:* it learned its first game only
to make the static stop, and a world kept still is the quietest world there is.

## 3. The rules

1. **It sees only mouths and light.** Grown moths have no mouths, so it never noticed them. Dark and empty-handed,
   Vesper is invisible to it and its servants; anything glowing she carries shows her. Once she has fed it, it sees
   her anyway.
2. **She can't take, only give.** Every power she has is a gift. She can set glints down for whatever is in front of
   her, and a gift calms what it is given to.
3. **Lanterns** hold the root back and calm its creatures. She rests there, re-forms there when she fades, and can
   leave things there.
4. **Root walls** are packed with cases, each a grey bead on her map. She can burn a wall with the coal, or, from
   Night 5, Dun lifts it and carries the cases aside.
5. **Shiver.** She can spend her own light to warm something cold, which leaves her dim until she rests.

## 4. The gifts

She hatches able to run, wall-kick, glide and set glints down. The rest is given: the **somersault** (Sorrel, Night 1,
doing it over and over until she can); the **thorn blade** (the forest, Night 1); the **dash** (Sorrel, Night 2,
learned chasing him); **the coal** (Tallow, Night 3, the one power that takes); **Dun's help** (Night 5, the coal's job
done by giving); and **shiver** (a caterpillar, Night 5).

## 5. The prologue (wordless, about ninety seconds)

1. Black. A small pale light, pulsing like breath. Something warm rests against it, then lifts away. The light
   flickers. *(A seed? An egg? A heart?)*
2. Night from very high: a dark mountain with that light at its core. Root spreads from its foot like frost across
   glass; town lights go out; a herd of deer is drawn into the ground. Moths fly through it all untouched.
3. The Grove. A burrback swings toward a caterpillar, which spins fast; the burrback loses interest.
4. Two cocoons split. Sorrel flies straight up to the brood's dance. Vesper catches a glint, raises it to her face,
   and can do nothing with it. Beyond the trees the root steams in the frost. Title.

## 6. The acts

### Act I: the edge (Nights 1–3). Mythic, almost wordless

**Night 1, the Grove.** The last wild wood near the mountain, root steaming under its soil. Sorrel is her guide.
**Glints** are light the forest makes to give away; **lanterns** are old cocoons of her kind, hardened like glass.
Carrying the **ember**, the forest's own flame, up to the summit lantern, she is seen for the first time. **Boss: a
great lampwing** dives at the glow in her hands. When the lanterns burn, the forest's heart, whose stone carving is
its picture of a moth, its oldest friend, pushes the root back and offers her a thorn. Dun noses at the open heart,
puzzled: she has never seen anything give. From the summit, Vesper sees the mountain's glow.

**Night 2, the orchards.** Burrbacks carry grey bodies to a mouth in the ground; a root slides past Vesper's wing,
unseen. Dun lays a hare in the mouth, and it stays clenched, still hungry.
In the cellar below, grey cases hang in rows like fruit put by for winter. Back above, Dun heads for a den where a
vixen, one of the last animals awake, is hiding, and Vesper blocks the way. **Boss: Dun.** Dun pins her. Sorrel
snatches her glints and darts off blazing, and Dun turns after him: *he saves her by making himself the brightest
thing in the field.* The vixen runs.

**Night 3, the firebreak.** A ring of ash the root can't cross, a beacon fire, and **Tallow**: burned hands, forty
years awake. Tallies cover his walls under one word, *EATEN*, beside a photo of a smiling woman. **Boss: a
tower-thick root** bores up under the ash for him. The beacon draws moths until they drop, Sorrel among them. Vesper
fights unseen until she gathers every glint and blazes, a nearer light; Sorrel turns, and so does the root. *Her
hiding cost him; her shining saves him.* Tallow, who has watched roots part for moths, sets a coal in a drilled seed
and holds it out: the first gift anyone has given her. Item text: *"It wants everything. Carry it carefully."*

### Act II, the midpoint, Night 4: the old road and the mountain

**The old road** to the mountain is a wall of root packed with cases. She burns through with the coal, and beads on
her map go out. Where the root has rotted, the mouths' throats are old metal pipe. In a flooded room of small glass
dishes, each holds a dead grey cloud: its failed siblings. Item text: *"Eggs that never hatched."*

**The mountain.** Up a dry pipe, past a torch with its lens grown over, to a room at its heart:
- A sign half grown over: *No one goes hungry.* Beside it a poster: a hand lifting a small light out of a drawn human
  head into a dish. *Grown from us, to feed us.*
- A screen, still lit, where a ball bounces between two bats and nobody plays.
- A case grown around a chair, holding the woman from Tallow's photo, old, with unopened jars of feed in her lap. The
  Mouth's oldest stalk, worn smooth, runs into her case.
- In the middle, a dish: grey, cracked, its tubes dry, a handprint on the glass.

Vesper touches the stalk, and the prologue's first frame returns, wider: the pale light is a cloud in this dish, the
warm thing the woman's hand. Each miss in the ball game floods the cloud with static; rally after rally, the static
stops. She turns off the lights and goes. The feed light goes red, and the static floods back.

Then light runs along every root on earth, and every bead on Vesper's map swells with one slow breath. **It has not
been eating the world. It has been feeding it.** The beads she burned stay dark.

Dun arrives with the vixen, caught at last, and lays her at the dish. Vesper sets down every glint she carries: the
first gift it has had in forty years. The root drops the vixen, Dun's offering thrown away for a moth's. Then every
root in the room turns toward Vesper, and her shape rises out of every wall. **It sees her, and through her, every
moth.** The mountain chases her down its own veins, and the roots no longer part. At the lantern where she re-forms,
she sets the coal down.

### The lowest point, Night 5: the Grove, overrun

The Grove's first lantern goes out under a tide of root. Lampwings dive into the brood's dance; roots climb toward
next year's cocoons, the last things on earth still being born. **Boss: three mouths**, learning her: the first grabs
and misses, the second waits where she will land, the third closes the path behind her.

Sorrel looks at her across the scattering dance: she fed it, and now it hunts them. He goes. She fades and re-forms at
a lantern, alone. Dun, sent by the mountain, stands over her a long moment, then noses Vesper's spilled glints into a
heap and lies across the lantern's mouth, facing out. From now on Dun waits at Vesper's lanterns, and moths fleeing
the root from other woods gather there, a few more each night.

Outside, a caterpillar keeps failing to spin on a frosted twig. Vesper wraps it and shivers until its silk holds.
Warmed, it lets go and spins: the ending, small.

### Act III: the mountain moves (Nights 6–7)

**Night 6, the kept city.** Every food hall wears the sign *No one goes hungry*, and under it people hang in rows.
The Grove's second lantern goes out. Sorrel is in what is left of the dance, with a partner, the thing his week is
for. Far below he sees her small lantern with strangers round it; he drops, and the dance follows him down. Tallow is
burning every wall of cases in his way. **Boss: Tallow**, fighting its taking by taking. She can barely hurt him; the
fight is shielding the cases while Dun carries them clear. It ends when he burns one in front of her and the old man
inside stops breathing. Tallow lifts him out, too late, and follows them up the mountain empty-handed.

**Night 7, inside the mountain.** The Grove's last lantern dims as the forest pours every glint it has left toward
her, its own heart going dark to send it. **Boss: the Mouth itself.** The burrbacks of the world converge, and Dun
holds the way in against her own kind. In the shaft, Sorrel draws the grabbing off Vesper, as on Night 2, and it
costs him every scale. In a cavern of folded tissue around the grey dish, the Mouth tries not to kill her but to keep
her: caught, she slows, warm and still. Her blade cuts her free, but gifts win; each glint she offers a reaching root
slackens it. At the back, Tallow kneels at his sister's case, and when the roots reach for him, he lets them.

## 7. The endings

Given everything, the Mouth is full for the first time in forty years. It stops taking, and the root stops
everywhere. With nothing taken, nothing keeps it warm, and frost creeps over the dish; it tries to wrap itself, but
its threads are too thin. Then it grows a case around Sorrel. **This is its real offer:**
his scales grow back, and through the case's skin the dance plays on overhead, long past the end of their week. Kept
moths don't die. A second case opens for her. The choice is played, not picked:

- **Spin** (the true ending). She walks the dish's rim, shivering her warmth into the threads. Each circuit costs her
  glide, then jump, then dash. Sorrel climbs out of his case and walks behind her, shivering too. Then come the moths
  from her lanterns, the dance, and the moths of the world it never noticed, until the mountain is furred with
  shivering wings. Warmed, the Mouth does what the caterpillar did: it lets go. **Every root on earth draws back into
  the mountain, and the mountain spins itself shut.**
  *Epilogue:* people stand up in ruined streets, old and hungry, with no one to feed them; not everyone wakes.
  Tallow's sister opens her eyes in his arms. Dun lies against the cocoon to keep it warm. On its rim, two moths lie
  still. In spring the brood hatches, and so, for the first time in forty years, do fox cubs. Last image: something
  presses outward against the mountain's silk.
  **The cost:** a forever, handed back; both moths' lives; the forest's heart; a world that must wake hungry.
- **Stay.** She lies down beside her brother and both cases close. *Epilogue:* the root takes the Grove and the
  brood's cocoons never hatch. Tallow sleeps beside his sister. The world is kept, quiet and fed, and nothing will be
  born again.
- **Burn.** Only if she went back to the lantern for the coal. She drops it into the dish, and every root on earth
  goes grey. *Epilogue:* the takeover ends in a night. The strongest wake; most never do. Tallow's sister stops
  breathing in his arms. Moths fly free over a silent world.

## 8. The question it leaves

The thing that took the whole world so no one would go hungry, and only saw a moth when the moth fed it: was that
love, or had it only learned how to make the static stop?
