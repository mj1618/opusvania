# Mouthless: a cold read (the Ori fan who cried at the prologue)

## 1. The story in three sentences
Umber is a moth with one week to live, and a pale, breathing growth called the Hush has put the whole world to sleep in hanging cases; it ignores moths because moths don't eat. She travels from her wood to the mountain at its centre and finds it was a lab-grown brain, built to feed people, that was abandoned and starved, and it only grabbed the world to make its own pain stop. In the end she can let it keep her and her brother, burn it and kill most of the sleepers, or spend her last warmth with every moth to help it cocoon into something new, which wakes the world.

## 2. Characters
- **Umber:** wants to save her wood, then everyone.
- **Sorrel**, her brother: wants to dance with his partner, then more time with her.
- **The Hush / HEARTH:** wants to keep everything, forever, so nothing goes hungry and the static stops.
- **Dun**, a badger turned root-servant: wants to be the one who feeds it. It switches to guarding Umber.
- **Tallow**, the last man awake: wants to burn whatever took his sister.
- **The forest:** wants to keep giving.

## 3. The enemy
It's a starving lab brain. It sealed every person and animal in cases, stopped all births for forty years and turned beasts into its servants. It wants to keep everything fed and still. Once Umber feeds it, it wants moths too.

## 4. Endings
- **Spin:** the moths shiver it into a cocoon, the world wakes, and Umber and Sorrel die side by side.
- **Stay:** she lies down in a case, the world stays kept and nothing is ever born again.
- **Burn:** the Hush dies, most sleepers die with it, and Tallow's sister dies in his arms.

## 5. Confusions
- On Night 2, "darts off with every lamp after him": are the lamps the lampwings? I re-read it. "It never fights her… until it tramples over her" also confused me.
- Night 1, "Dun noses at the open heart": why? It's never explained.
- Night 6, "people hang from the beams." My first reading was a mass hanging. I think it means cases.
- The coal: if I burned the wall with it, do I still have it? The rule only says a lantern uses it up. I had to work that out.
- At the midpoint it's glints that feed it, but in the ending it's warmth. Does it eat light or warmth?
- In Spin, does the Hush die, or is it reborn? "Wings closed, never needs feeding" is lovely, but I couldn't say what happens to it.
- Tallow "believes it eats" while it actually feeds the sleepers. I only got that from the Stay epilogue.

## 6. The moment, and would I buy it
Sorrel wrapped round his partner's seed-sized case while the root closes over the lantern and the camera stays on him. Then, at the very end, he folds his wing over hers and taps her wingtip, and "Neither moth rises." **Yes.** The shiver-for-each-other motif starts in the prologue and pays off three times. Two siblings are a relationship I'd cry over, and the starving-dish reveal turns the monster into something I pity.
