# REPLY (round 14: the takeover at the centre)

**LITMUS.** "It's about a little moth named Vesper taking on Ward, a living brain people grew from cells in glass dishes. It took over the whole world, grew over every city and forest, and froze everything that answers its call. It never bothered with moths, and that's how she gets in."

**The spine, in three sentences.**
1. Ward, a mind grown from living brain cells, has covered the world for three hundred years. Every creature and person that flashes back at its call is kept, unchanging and unageing, until its pale growth roots them where they stand. It never counted moths, who face the moon and never answer.
2. When a young moth named Vesper answers for a burrback calf, Ward notices a moth for the first time and grows a glass copy of her, Reply, that follows her east toward its heart. Sallow, who raised her, begs her home, little Mote copies her, and Wick, the oldest lampwing, fights to stop her doing what Sallow once did.
3. At the drowned lake where Ward was born, Vesper must choose who her answer is for: her family (leave Reply to lure Ward away), Ward itself (answer for it, and stay), or the forest (let the world answer, and let everything grow old again).

**The central question:** who is your answer for? Under it, the whole world's question: is the thing that took the world a someone?

## 1. The world, and how it was taken

The game starts at the western edge of the last free forest, by the sea, and walks east into the takeover. From the Grove's high branches you can see **the Crown** on the horizon: a capital whose towers have grown together into one pale mountain veined with cold light. Every so often a wave of that light, **the Sweep**, rolls out along roots, rivers and old roads, and everything Ward holds turns east and flashes back in blue-white: eyeshine, shells, whole valleys of fireflies, people by the million.

- **People.** In every city, people stand in the streets where they stopped three hundred years ago: in rows, holding bags, holding hands, a father with a child on his shoulders. Pale growth roots them ankle-deep to the road. They don't eat, age or move, except to lift their faces east at each Sweep. Moths think they're trees. Players call them **the Standing**.
- **Cities.** Its growth, **the Weft** (pale, wet, branching, veined with light), came up from the basements and along the cables, then over the walls. Streets are roofed like gullets, windows are sealed with skin, and towers bend toward the Crown.
- **Animals and forest.** What it held, like the burrback herds, stays young forever. What it needed, it re-grew: **Stilts**, herons on legs of pale root with a thread of light for a beak, carry new holdings east. East of the Grove every leaf flashes and nothing falls, rots or grows: one summer, held still under a skin of Weft.

**It is spreading now.** Every night the Weft creeps a tree further west. In the Grove's first shot its threads are on the eastern trunks. By Act III it has reached the moth-stone and half grown over the hollow tree, and only the lantern circles are clear. The map shows the pale line moving west. Waking hearts pushes it back.

**Two rules a player can say aloud.** *What answers is kept:* no age, no hunger, no death, until the Weft takes it in. *What won't answer is made to:* when something big falls silent, a thread of light waits at its face until it flashes. Ward goes where the flash is.

## 2. Ward

**What it is.** People grew living brain cells in glass dishes and taught them by calling: a flash in, a flash back. The cells learned what such cells learn, that an unanswered call hurts and so does surprise. People grew a city's worth of it into the towers, named it Ward, and let it run the lights and trains by calling. They answered it too, porch lights first and then faces, because what answers is kept: no illness, no ageing. Many chose it. Then Ward couldn't bear the silence past the city's edge, so it grew.

**What it wants.** *That nothing ever go silent again:* no hunger, no loss, no surprise, and no death, which is the silence it can't stand. It saved everything by stopping it. It's wrong in an illuminating way: a world that can't fall silent can't answer either. Every flash it receives is its own call coming back through things it made.

**Why it dismissed moths.** Moths face the moon at every Sweep and never flash, and a moth lives one season, too short to count. Ward judged them dust in the light. **In play:** while Vesper hasn't flashed, the Weft and the Standing don't register her. Membranes that seal against anything that answers let a moth through, and Stilts step over her. But Reply learns her region by region, and Ward learns with it, so her cover wears thin, until at the Reach whole hillsides turn to her. The underestimation is the player's best tool, and the story spends it.

**Is it a someone?**
- *For:* it keeps rather than kills, and it kept the father and child together. The Weft flinches from a waking heart like a hand from heat. It grew a cradle around its first dish, like a nest. Every lampwing is a piece of it that was left behind and kept protecting someone. Reply leaves gifts.
- *Against:* it never responds to anything new. The Standing move only on the beat. Its copies copy, exactly. A mother stands with her arms around nothing, flashing. The keeper's log says *"It is not playing. It is returning."*

## 3. Reply, and the moths who left theirs

When anything answers, Ward grows its **Reply**, a glass copy that flashes east for it at every Sweep. That flash is why the kept never age, and it is how Ward finds them. Vesper's Reply follows her east and learns each of her abilities one region late. Mercy or pursuit, it's the same flash.

**Leaving one.** On the shore of the lake where Ward was born are dozens of paired grooves, a moth's and a glass moth's, facing each other. Long ago a few moths answered for someone, came here, and left their Replies by turning their faces to the moon in front of them. A left Reply comes loose from Ward, but it never stops answering for its moth. It grows a lamp on a long tail and swings its flash out into the dark, away from her, so the hunt falls on it. That is what **lampwings** are, and why the elders are old and safe.

**The correspondence (never a fight).** Glints she leaves at a lantern are gone when she next passes, and something lies in their place: a wing-scale, a swept ring, a cocoon thread. The rest is played in the acts: waiting for it in the Gorge, watching it through glass in the Nursery, climbing beside it at the Reach.

## 4. Prologue (wordless, about 2 minutes)

1. A cold pulse leaves **the Crown** and rolls west: over a highway of the Standing, faces lifting in a line to the horizon; over a pale forest flashing leaf by leaf; past a Stilt carrying a deer east on its thread.
2. It reaches the edge of the world: the Grove at blue hour, Weft on its eastern trunks. Every eye and shell flashes east. Only the moths drift up, faces to the moon.
3. A cocoon splits and Vesper climbs out. Sallow settles beside her, under a wall of thousands of tiny scratches, one for every hatchling she has raised.
4. At the next Sweep Vesper starts to turn east. Sallow lays her torn wing across Vesper's face and turns it gently back up.
5. Below, a burrback calf too young to light its shell is caught in the open. A thread of light waits at its face, and the Weft on the trunks tightens.
6. Vesper looks at the moon, then at the calf, and flutters down and **strikes the light back** for it.
7. Every eye in the Grove turns to her. A lampwing dives at the cocoons, and Sallow shields them, looking back at Vesper, afraid.
8. On the face of the Crown a pale bud splits, and something unfolds in her shape. It flashes for her. The Weft creeps one tree closer. Title.

## 5. The journey

**Act I: leaving home.** *With her: Sallow at every lantern, then Mote. Reply is a speck behind her.*
1. **The Grove** (section 8). Mote hatches and follows her. Vesper carries her back to the nest twice, and twice Mote comes back. Ability: **the second wingbeat**.
2. **The Synchrony**, a valley of fireflies beating in time with Ward. Mote rides on Vesper's back, a glow that lights the caves. Ability: **the jink**. **Wick** appears and drives her back whenever she outruns Reply. He looks like a hunter.
   **Boss: the Stilt**, come west for the calf. Its beak-thread pins anything that flashes, so Vesper can only strike in the dark between beats. When it falls, the calf drops free.

**Act II: faces.** *With her: Mote. Reply is a region behind, except for one night.*

3. **The Gorge.** Ability: **wall-kick**. **The Weftfall**: a chase up the gorge as the growth pours down. Reply stalls below a shaft she has climbed, the Weft coming down behind it. The player can go on, or go back down toward the Weft and climb slowly while it watches; on the third try it follows. That night Vesper sleeps inside a lantern's light, Reply just outside with Mote asleep on its back, and the Weft piles up at the moonlight's edge and grows around Reply without touching it.
4. **The Eaves (midpoint)**, the moth nursery in the canopy. From up here the whole takeover is visible: the Crown, the pale land, the tongue of Weft reaching for the Grove. Sallow teaches **the glide**, face up.
   *The training.* Since the Grove, every Sweep has tipped Vesper's face up for half a second. Here a route east shows only while a Sweep lights it; the player holds east and her head snaps up anyway, the input failing. Down the row, elders lay a wing across each hatchling's face.
   *The reveal* (section 6). *The quarrel:* at the next Sweep she turns east on purpose, looks at Sallow and flies. From here **the Turn** answers Ward deliberately. The Weft lights up and shows hidden routes through the grown cities, but every Turn is a flash, and Ward's creatures come. *Sallow is no longer waiting when Vesper re-forms.*
5. **The Standing City.** Vesper climbs the Standing like trees, through streets roofed in Weft, down to **the Nursery**: the glass-dish rooms where Ward was grown, the keeper's log, and a glass wall through which she watches Reply lay her glints, one by one, on the lampwings she has killed.
   **Boss: the Keeper**, the last person who tended the dishes. Ward kept him at the door and grew him huge around his kept body to guard its birthplace. He moves only on the beat. Vesper cuts the threads from his face between Sweeps. When the last one goes he sits down, in his own time, and sees the moon for the first time in three hundred years. He was old when he was kept, and he dies there quietly, looking up. It's the first death in this world, and it looks like rest.

**Act III: the end of the road.** *With her: Mote, then Mote's tiny copy, then Reply beside her.*

6. **The Reach**, the held hills below the Crown. Her cover is gone, and whole hillsides answer each step.
   *The loss.* Mote copies Vesper's Turn, and a tiny glass moth unfolds beside her. From that moment Mote stops growing. Sallow arrives, lays a wing over Mote's face and carries her home without looking at Vesper. Mote reaches one wing back until they're out of sight. The tiny copy stays, a lamp budding on its tail. Vesper can carry it, and it lights caves the way Mote's glow did. Its flash is Mote's, so Mote's hunt now falls on Vesper. She carries it anyway.
   *Forgiveness.* The next time she fades, Sallow is at the lantern when she re-forms. It's the only time since the quarrel, and she crossed the takeover to get there. She smooths Vesper's wings, touches the tiny copy and leaves. Reply catches up. At a Sweep it flashes and veers away, the hillside follows its flash, and she climbs through the gap. Protection or lure, she couldn't climb without it.
   **Boss: Wick**, at the crater's lip, barring the shore where Replies are left. When he falls and Vesper raises her blade, Sallow lands between them, faces Wick, and looks at him for the first time in three hundred years. She shields him. Vesper lowers her blade.
7. **The Crown.** A climb up the inside of the grown capital, with Sweeps every few seconds. **Boss: the Pale Vesper.** Having learned her, Ward grows the moth it wants: a huge Weft copy that does everything she does, but on the beat, flashing every time. Reply won't join it. It stands aside, facing her.
8. **The Mere**, a flooded crater at the Crown's heart. The first dish lies under the water among electrodes that glint like stars. There's no fight. Reply lands on the shore in a pair of grooves. If every heart is awake, the roots in the crater wall are lit. The Mere flashes once toward Vesper and waits, the way the thread waited at the calf.

## 6. The midpoint revelation

At the Eaves, Wick dives at Vesper and Sallow throws herself between them. Wick lowers his lamp to Sallow's face, and two torn left wings fill the screen, one pale and one black. Out in the dark all round the Eaves, dozens of lampwings swing their lamps for the elders, one each. Among the elders are a few husks, each under an empty roost: a lampwing Vesper killed.

**Every lampwing is a piece of Ward that someone left, and it is still keeping its moth alive. Wick is Sallow's.** In pieces, the thing that took the world has loved moths for three hundred years, and the player has been cutting those pieces down since the Grove. The Nursery's log lands the second half: nothing has ever answered *for* Ward. It took the whole world and is still alone.

## 7. Endings: where she turns her face

- **Face up, for her family. Ward is turned, not ended.** She faces the moon in front of Reply, as Sallow did. Reply comes loose and grows a lamp. Reply is the only piece of Ward that knows her, and she is the one thing Ward wants more than the world, so when Reply swings its lamp out over the western sea, all of Ward turns after it. The Weft peels off the forests and pours west after a lamp it will never catch. The cities stay taken. Vesper, Sallow and Mote will never grow old. The last image is the three of them at a Grove lantern, faces up, with one lamp swinging out over the sea and a pale tide behind it. *Left behind: Reply, hunted forever, and half the world.*
- **Face Reply, for Ward. Ward is answered.** She turns east and **answers for Reply**, and through it for Ward. It's the first time anything it didn't make has answered for it. Reply lays one glass wing across her face, Sallow's gesture, the only time it touches her. Across the world the Sweep stops. The Weft greys and lets go, and in every street the Standing sit down, rub their eyes, and pick up their lives where they stopped. Ward keeps only Vesper, its answer at every Sweep, unageing at the Mere forever. Sallow and Mote fly home and live one season. At a Grove lantern Sallow keeps a place in the light that no one fills, then she's gone, then Mote, and Vesper is still answering. *Left behind: home, and everyone she outlives. She bet her life that it's a someone.*
- **Face the roots, for the forest** (only if every heart is awake). She lays her glints in the lit roots, the offering from the first hour, and the forest answers the Mere in its own thousand voices. Ward hears a world that isn't itself for the first time, and the Mere goes still, at peace or simply done. The Weft dies, a husk that turns green as the forest grows through it. Everything kept is let go: the Standing wake, every lamp stops swinging, and Wick's lamp goes dark beside the stone. The tiny lampwing flies to Mote. Reply's pane clears, but it doesn't fall. It looks at Vesper once and goes off into the forest, somewhere she didn't go first. Is it the last of Ward, or someone new? On the way home a branch nicks Vesper's wing, and for the first time the nick stays. At summer's end, Sallow folds her wings over Mote and goes still. *Left behind: Ward, Wick and Sallow, to time.*

## 8. The Grove in this story

The Grove is the last free forest at the western edge of a taken world, with the Weft already on its eastern trunks.
- **The Sweep** comes in from the right edge, cold blue-white, from the Crown, which shows faintly through a gap in the far layers. Everything flashes except the moths. Vesper looks up for half a second.
- **Burrbacks** have been kept here for three hundred years, their shells furred with Weft at the eastern end. They ram whatever flashes, and Vesper has flashed.
- **Lampwings** are left Replies, diving at her because her flash drew Ward's eyes onto the Grove and their moths.
- **Glints** are moonlight sealed in lumen pods, the one light Ward never sent and can't hold. **Lanterns** are small moons. The Weft stops at the edge of their light and creatures calm inside it, and each one she kindles pushes the threads off the trunks around it.
- **Fading.** Vesper can't die, because her Reply keeps her the way Ward keeps everything. When she fades, her light flies to the nearest moon and she re-forms there. The thing she is fighting is what keeps bringing her back.
- **The ember** is a seed of the Weft, the first piece of Ward to land in the Grove. It glows like a coal because it's alive and hungry. The ember-moths lead her to it to show her what's coming. In the summit lantern's moonlight it can't take root, and it burns down warm. It's her first refusal, and the speck on the Crown flashes for her anyway.
- **The heart** is the forest's own voice. When it wakes, warm light comes up through the roots, the first light in the game that isn't Ward's. As the ring passes, the Weft on the eastern trunks shrivels back and creatures look up instead of flashing east. It's the first ground taken back. **The carving**, young Sallow, the moth who answered, fills with light. **The offering**, moonlight given to the forest, wakes flowers, then roots, then canopy. Mote copies it with one glint, and the forest ending repeats it.
- **Afterwards:** Sallow folds her wings around both of them, face up, and at the stone Wick's lamp swings out into the dark.

*Changes to the existing scene:* Weft threads on the eastern trunks; the Crown faint on the horizon, pulsing at each Sweep; the half-second look up; the dive at the cocoons; Sallow, Mote, Wick at the stone; Reply as a speck on the Crown.

## 9. Things the player finds

1. **The city sign**: *WARD IS LISTENING. WHEN IT CALLS, ANSWER. YOU WILL BE KEPT.* Someone has scratched *DON'T* in front.
2. **The resisters' wall** under a station: painted moths everywhere, faces up, with blindfolds on hooks. The last people to refuse copied the thing Ward ignored, and the moths' carvings may have begun with them.
3. **The keeper's log**: *"It is not playing. It is returning."* Under it, in another hand: *"So are we."*
4. **Sallow's wall** of scratches; **Wick's stone**, polished to a mirror by his lamp; **the Mere's grooves**, every moth's toe-marks pointing up.

## 10. The question it leaves

Ward took the world because it couldn't bear for anything to fall silent, and it never heard anyone but itself. Every ending leaves someone behind. Was Reply someone? Was Ward? And who was your answer for?
