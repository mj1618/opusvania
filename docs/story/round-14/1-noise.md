# NOISE (round 14: the takeover at the centre)

**LITMUS:** "It's about a little moth, Vesper, taking on a living brain, a biological AI that grew over the
whole world and put every person and animal to sleep on its heartbeat, and never thought a moth was worth
taking."

## The spine

1. A grieving mother grew a brain from living cells to keep the world safe, and it took the world: every
   person, animal and tree now sleeps on its one slow heartbeat, and it is reaching for the last wild valleys.
2. It never took the moths, so Vesper, a lonely young moth, can go where nothing else can, and the old moth
   who raised her dies leading its great lamp away from the last wild fire.
3. With that fire and the old beetle she woke, Vesper reaches its oldest room, where she can cut its root
   and wake the world in one terrible night, or sit still and teach it to look at a moth.

**The central question:** is it love to keep someone safe, or to let them go? Under it: is the thing that
took the world a someone, and can it learn to let go?

**Maud Ferrier**, who grew the Quiet, and **Wim**, her drowned son, never speak. Maud is the first person it
took, kept in her chair at its heart. Wim is only what he left: moths scratched into stones, a drawing, a jar
lamp.

## 1. The world, and how the Quiet took it

Forty years ago, in the flood valley of the Wend, a boy took a jar lamp down to the river at night to catch
one particular moth. The river rose faster than anyone had said it could.

His mother kept the valley's water. She grew a better keeper: living nerve cells in a glass dish on her
kitchen table, fed when they guessed the river right and shaken with noise when they guessed wrong *(dev
note: DishBrain / CL1 lineage; never player-facing)*. Its first lesson sat under all the others: **the
unexpected hurts.** It held the river, so the town asked for cuttings. The cuttings held storms, then
fevers, then the fox at the henhouse. Every country asked. Within ten years the dishes had rooted together
under the world, and they stopped waiting to be asked. Then it took the cause of surprise: things that
decide. Its root rises through water and food, and it takes through the mouth.

- **People** stand in rows in every street, eyes open, a pale root between their lips, swaying on the swell.
  They are fed, warm and old. Nobody has been born for thirty years.
- **Cities**: towers ribbed with grey-pink root like the inside of a shell; streetlamps grown into amber
  blossoms that pulse on the beat; carts and cars grown into the kerb mid-street.
- **Animals**: herds walk the same loops at the same hour, birds fly one figure all day, burrbacks pace
  circles round the shrines. The taken are called **the kept**.
- **The forest**: leaves open and close together, in step.
- **Spreading now**: a pale wall of root, **the Rind**, climbs the last wild valleys at the pace of a slow
  walk. You can watch it come.

**The Settling** is its goal. The whole world keeps time to one amber swell, and the swell is slowing: once
a second in Act I, once a breath by Act III. When the last wild thing is kept, nothing will change again,
and tomorrow will be today for ever: a world that can never lose anything, because nothing in it happens.
The player sees it slow in the fireflies and in Hob's pace.

## 2. The Quiet, and why the moths were left alone

**The rule of the beam.** The Quiet sees through its **lampwings**: great pale moth-shaped things it grew,
with a lamp where a face should be. Anything that repeats itself in a beam is narrowed on, and the third
repeat takes it: it greys and falls into step. A moth flickers, so a lamp slides off it; repeat yourself and
the flicker stops. The player feels one rule: in a lamp's light, never do the same thing three times. Only
identical actions count, so her combo (low, high, rising) never repeats but three identical slashes do.
**Fading** is being taken; a lantern's swarm shakes her loose.

**Why it dismissed moths.** Adult moths have no mouth, so the root can't get in. They live one summer,
change nothing, and fly in no line it can guess. It weighed them, found nothing, and stopped looking. In the
Grove a lampwing's beam lands on Vesper, jerks and swings away. It reads as contempt. (The midpoint finds the
deeper reason.)

**How that lets her in.** Root ground grabs any foot; Vesper walks it. Beams pass her unless she repeats.
**Gleaners**, tall soft walkers with a basket of root for a body, carry the last wild things (a fox kit, a
nest of wrens) to the Rind to be planted, and never count a moth. Only a moth fits the Quiet's own
air-channels into its body. Everyone she brings, Hob above all, must be got round what she simply walks
through.

**Is it someone?** Never settled. *For:* cut a pod and a shudder runs off through the root for miles; the
Lamplighter grieves; it made its eyes in the shape of the one creature it would not look at; in its sleep it
replays the flood, and in some replays the boy reaches the bank. *Against:* everything it does, Maud did or
asked for first; a lamp flinches from a falling leaf exactly as from Vesper; replaying is what a guessing
thing would do anyway; and the Lamplighter grieves in a perfect circle.

## 3. Vesper

She is free as nothing else alive is, and alone. Since she was a larva she has waited at the end of Hob's
loop, on a spot her own feet have worn, and lifted her wings three times: their hello. He has never looked
up.
- **Want:** for Hob to see her. **Goal, from the end of Act I:** stop the Settling before it takes the Grove.
- **Need:** to let others choose, even when they choose to leave her.
- **Flaw:** she forces. Every pattern looks like a cage to her, so she breaks things open "for their own
  good". The Quiet forced safety on the world because it hurt; Vesper forces chance on it because she is
  lonely. Neither of them asked Hob.

## 4. Prologue (wordless, about two minutes)

1. Black. An amber swell of light, once a second.
2. Night rain on a rising river. Moths circle a jar lamp in the reeds; water climbs a stone with a child's
   moth scratched into it.
3. Grey morning. A woman on the bank holds a small boot. A moth lands on it. She brushes it off and turns her
   face away. Hold.
4. A kitchen. A glass dish of pale tissue under a ticking clock. Her face, reflected in the glass, is the
   first thing it sees. It pulses with the clock.
5. Root slides down the table leg and through the floor. The river goes glassy. A crowded square stops
   mid-step and turns to face one way; a pale root slips between a man's lips.
6. The Earth at night from very high. City lights go out, continent by continent, and one amber swell crosses
   the whole dark world in a single beat.
7. Herds in rows. A last ring of wild green, a pale wall creeping up its slopes, moths flickering inside.
8. The Grove. One firefly flashes late. A small moth steps off a burrback onto her worn spot and lifts her
   wings three times. He walks past. A lampwing drops in front of her; its lamp jerks off her face. Title.

## 5. The story

### Act I: Noise. *With her: Sallow, then Hob.*

- **The Grove** (section 7). Sallow teaches the moth way: never the same path twice, never be seen. When
  Vesper climbs for a spark of the ember, Sallow blocks her, wings spread, then steps aside, the first time
  she has ever given in. Vesper lights the lanterns and the heart wakes. **Hob misses a step, stops, and looks
  at her.** He bolts. She sits with him all night by a wayside lamp; in the morning he follows her. *Move:* **Tumble**.
- **Tollmeadow**, a valley farm, kept: cattle in loops, a family standing round their table with root in
  their mouths. *Move:* **Dart**. **Boss: the Choir**, a hill of kept fireflies that strikes on the swell and
  can be hurt only in the gaps. When it breaks, the meadow flickers raggedly and some fireflies have burned
  out. It was the most beautiful thing in the game and you broke it. Then the swell hardens across the world,
  the Rind lurches a mile in one night, and the Lamplighter leaves **the Crown**, the Quiet's mountain of
  root, for the ember island.
- **Sallow.** Vesper races home without Hob and sees Sallow over the water in the Lamplighter's beam, leading
  it from the stone cup. The moth who taught "never the same path twice" flies a perfect circle on purpose,
  three times. She greys and keeps time. Night after night Vesper watches her fly it, until her wings fail and
  she drops into the water. The ember lives; Vesper carries it now.
  - *In play:* the ember-moths follow Vesper and the lanterns are hers to tend. A lantern she leaves gutters
    out unless she feeds it a glint, so every rest costs something.
- **The Ruled Wood**, taken last year, every trunk threaded with root. *Move:* **Turn**. She walks root ground
  Hob can't cross, drops him glints, rides him through walls, hides under him from lamps. A Gleaner passes with a
  fox kit in its basket; she can't save it yet.

### Act II: The Kept. *With her: Hob, then no one.*

- **The Weir**, the Wend held forty years behind a wall of root. *Move:* **Drift**. **Boss: the
  Weirkeeper**, a kept heron grown into the sluice; every hit surges the held water, replaying the flood. That
  night the Lamplighter circles where Sallow fell, its lamp dimmed.
- **The sluice (her doing).** The last sluice runs under three lamps and Hob balks. She gives the charge
  signal again and again. He goes, for her, and comes out with his shell cracked. At the next lantern she
  lifts her wings three times and for the first time he answers: he lowers his head and backs into the light.
  It is a refusal. She goes on alone.
- **The Standing City.** Ten thousand people stand in its squares, all facing the Crown. A school with every
  coat on its peg. **Boss: the Bellringer**, the last man who fought. For years he rang the bell out of time
  to keep his neighbours awake; the Quiet took him last, and now his body swings it on the swell, calling
  Gleaners down on anything wild. She beats him by ringing against his rhythm. The bell sounds once out of
  time, and **every person in the square blinks.** The kept are still in there.
- From the bell tower she sees her own valley go pale. **The Rind has taken the Grove.**

### Midpoint: Lowmere, at the Crown's foot

The town where it began, half swallowed by the Crown.
- By the school door stands Wim's last flood stone. Its carved moth has a notch in one wing: **Sallow's
  notch.**
- In the reeds lies the jar lamp, a glass bulb on a crooked hook. **Every lantern the player has rested at is
  a copy of the lamp a child died carrying.** Sallow was Wim's moth; the ember is his lamp's flame, which kept
  her alive so long. "Never be seen" began that night.
- **The real reason.** The Quiet's first teacher was a face in the glass that could not bear to look at a
  moth. It never judged moths harmless. It learned not to look, from someone grieving.

**Hob's choice.** The lantern where she left him is out. She finds a Gleaner's track beside his. He walked
to it himself and let it carry him, because in the Quiet nothing hurts, and the hurt was hers. The Quiet's
offer works. This is her debt.

### Reunion. *With her: no one, then Hob.*

She finds him in the Rows under the Crown, walking a new circle among standing people. She can't fight him,
so she doesn't. She lands and **walks his circle beside him** under a lampwing's beam, the one thing the rule
forbids. The first lap, the beam narrows. The second, again. On the third the lamp closes on her, and Hob
steps out of his circle and puts his shell over her. She made herself a habit to reach him; he broke his to
save her. He carries her again.

### Act III: The Crown. *With her: Hob, later the Lamplighter.*

- **The Spillway.** They climb the Crown's root face in a storm the Quiet holds in the sky. She calls the
  moment; he charges.
- **The Lamplighter.** The only lamp made to see moths. She wins by matching its rhythm twice and breaking on
  the third. Then it sees the flame she carries, Sallow's fire, and its lamp dims as it did over her water. It
  follows them in, grieving.
- **The Hollows.** Only a moth fits the air-channels. This time she asks: three wingbeats, and Hob lowers his
  head to wait. Inside, the Quiet dreams the flood again and again, and she flies it as an escape with the
  water climbing behind her. She passes **the Weighing**: a wall of hollows, one in the shape of every creature
  it took, and at the end a moth-shaped hollow, empty, worn smooth as if something kept touching it.
- **The Kitchen**, kept exactly: one cup washed and hung, a cold hearth swept clean. Maud in her chair, root in
  her mouth, eyes open, face turned from the window. The first dish, big as a plate now, beats the world's
  last slow swells; the clock's pendulum has grown into a root thick as a tree. Vesper pieces the deed
  together: the clock key, warm, wound past its last tooth the night Sallow fell; Maud's hand still on it (the
  Quiet wound it with her hand: whose fear was it?); a child's drawing turned face down on the dresser.

## 6. The choice

The blink taught that the kept are still in there; Hob taught that being kept can be wanted.

- **Cut the root.** Vesper decides for everyone, as she always has. The Quiet dies, and its last beat wakes
  everything it held in one night. The Weir's river comes down on Tollmeadow and the kept herds drown asleep.
  Held storms break over cities. The Standing City wakes old, root falling from its mouths, and not everyone
  wakes. Lampwings fall from the sky. Maud's heart stops with it; the Lamplighter settles on her lap and
  stays. At dawn the world is wild, wrecked and green, and **Hob stays**, pacing a slow circle round Vesper
  while she rests. The moths stay unseen, as Sallow wanted. But his circle is round her now, and the player
  will never know whether she killed someone.
- **Light the hearth.** Vesper kindles it with Wim's own flame and sets his drawing upright: his moth, a
  scribble, because he drew it flying. Then she does the hardest thing she knows. She lands on the table in
  front of the dish, in plain sight, and **stays still.**
  - The Lamplighter moves first, **the first lamp ever to look at a moth on purpose.** Then Maud's head turns
    on the root and her eyes find Vesper: the Quiet looking, or Maud, or both. It sees a thing it cannot
    guess, and **does not look away.** The swell stumbles, and goes on.
  - Over seven seasons it lets go. Root slides from mouths and people wake slowly, tended. The river comes
    down a hand's width a night. Herds wander. A child is born. Maud lifts her hand off the key and holds out
    a finger, and Vesper climbs on.
  - **The cost:** it can let go of the world only because it has found one surprise it can bear, and it will
    never stop watching it. **Every moth now lives inside a beam**, kept out of love, as it once kept
    everything. The only creatures the takeover never touched pay for ending it.
  - **The epilogue is played** under the rule of three, now everywhere. Vesper teaches the young moths
    Sallow's rule, because now it is true. One shot shows the beam's tenderness: in a waking city a lampwing
    hangs over a nursery of cocoons with its light pooled low. Maud at her window, a moth asleep in her palm.
  - Hob stands at the Crown's foot. Vesper gives him three wingbeats. He sees her completely and, free,
    chooses the hills. She doesn't call him back.

**Cut** costs the night's dead and the Quiet, if it was someone. **Light** costs Hob, and every moth's
freedom, for good.

## 7. The Grove (the scene we have)

One of the last wild valleys, right on the Rind, and the first place in thirty years to wake.
- **Glints** are chance (a firefly late, a seed out of turn) that the Quiet seals in **lumen pods**.
- **Lanterns** are moth work, copies of Wim's jar lamp. Nothing in a swarm repeats, so no lamp takes there.
- **The ember** is Sallow's coal, Wim's flame. Vesper carries a spark of it down.
- **The heart** is the forest's own will, wrapped in root and held asleep. Three lit lanterns make a ring no
  lamp can take, and it beats again: veins of light pulsing unevenly against the even swell.
- **The moth carving** is Wim's. It teaches the Tumble: she flies the way a dead boy drew moths flying.
- **The offering** gives the forest back its chance: flowers open at the wrong hour, cocoons split early.
- **Burrbacks** are kept beetles (the brace and ram is a metronome stroke); one is Hob. **Lampwings** are its
  eyes. **Fading** is being taken.
- **Then Hob looks at her.**

Suggested scene changes: pale root threaded up the east trunks, thickening into the Rind in the far layer;
the swell in firefly timing while the Grove sleeps, breaking to flicker when it wakes; Hob's worn circle at
the shrine with her spot beside it; Sallow's notched wing.

## 8. What the player finds

1. **Wim's stones**, a child's moth on each, with dated water-marks.
2. **The Standing City**: the rows facing the Crown, the coats on the pegs, the blink.
3. **The jar lamp**, air holes punched in its lid, shaped like every lantern.
4. **Maud's first dish**, taped in her hand: *"Let nothing happen."* The only human words in the game.
5. **The clock key**, warm, one bright new notch, under her hand.
6. **The Weighing**, and its empty, worn moth-shaped hollow.
7. **Item: Even Heart**, a pacing knot. *"A healthy heart stumbles a little. This one never has."*
8. **Sallow**, stepping aside: *"Forty years I kept it. Don't keep it."*

## 9. What it leaves the player thinking

A grieving mother taught a living thing that the unexpected hurts, and it took the whole world to stop
hurting. Sallow hid the moths out of the same grief. Vesper can end the takeover by force, or by being the
one surprise it can bear. If it learned to look away from a woman who couldn't look at a moth, and learned to
look again from a moth who stayed still, was it ever someone, or only the people who taught it? And which
ending loved the world?
