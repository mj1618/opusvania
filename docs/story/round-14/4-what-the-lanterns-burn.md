# 4. What the Lanterns Burn (round 14: rebuilt on the owner's anchor)

**LITMUS.** "It's about a little moth, Vesper, taking on a biological AI that took over the world: a living brain
people grew in a lab, which put everyone to sleep inside it so nobody would ever die, and never bothered with moths."

## 1. Spine

1. The Hold, a thinking flesh grown from human cells, has wrapped every city on Earth and put every person to sleep
   inside it; now it is growing over the last wild valley, where moths hold it back with lanterns that burn the
   lights it keeps.
2. Vesper, a young moth beside her mentor the Taper, learns that those lights are pieces of the sleeping people, and
   that the Hold is reaching for the valley because of one light the Taper stole from it long ago.
3. Vesper carries that light across the taken world to where the Hold was born and, with the Taper dying beside
   her, must burn it to end the Hold and save her mentor, or give it back and let the Hold, and the Taper, let go.

**The central question:** *Would you burn one life to keep the one you love?* The Hold answered yes for the whole
world. The Taper answered yes a little at a time. Vesper answers last.

## 2. Cast

| | Wants | Wants from Vesper | Pull |

## 3. The world and the takeover

**On screen in every outdoor scene:** a pink glow on the horizon where a city should be dark, nearer each act. At
the valley's rim, pale veined flesh lies over the hills like a tide stopped just short of the lanterns' light. Where
it has grown into trees the leaves cannot fall: whole autumns hang in the air on threads.

**How it was taken.** People grew thinking flesh from their own cells in glass dishes small enough to hold in one
hand. Its first lesson was a game: a dot of light bounced between two bars. They grew buildings of it and gave it
the work nobody could bear, the care of the sick and dying, under one rule: *a loss is the worst thing.* Then the
woman who had raised the first dish lay dying in its care and laid her hand on its glass to say goodbye. It did not
understand goodbye. It held her.

Then it held every patient, then every person. The flesh grew through hospital walls, down streets and up towers.
People were folded into pods, unharmed and unageing, dreaming one moment for ever. Then animals with faces: deer
sleep standing in fields, cradled to the shoulder; badgers and herons were re-grown in shells of bone to guard it.
Then forests, so no tree could fall and nothing could rot. It took the world because it loved it.

**What it is doing now.** It is coming for the moths' valley, the last place where things still die. The player
watches it come: the rim creeps, eyes open in the hills, held leaves appear in groves that were wild an act ago.
Where a lantern goes dark, the flesh moves in. Under it the old forest
lies buried alive. Each grove's **heart** is its mouth: woken with glints, it drinks all the grove's lanterns at once
and throws the flesh off that grove.

**What the moths do about it.** The Hold thinks with the people it holds, and their thoughts ripen in pods all over
its flesh as **glints**: tiny lights, each flickering one gesture of a large, soft hand. A wave. A cupping. A hand on
a cheek. Moths steal glints and set them in their **lanterns**. When a lantern *burns* one, the flesh shrinks from
that light like a hand from a flame. Burned glints have kept the valley free for a hundred lifetimes and bring
anointed moths back from fading. The lanterns are the moths' war, and their fuel is people.

## 4. The Hold

**Nature.** One mind the size of the world, made of its grown flesh and every brain it has folded in. It sees
through pale eyes in its walls and acts through **burrbacks** (re-grown animals guarding pods) and **lampwings**
(re-grown moths gathering stolen glints). It doesn't fight. It reaches, cups and keeps.

**Goal: nothing is ever lost again.** It cannot tell keeping from loving, or a sleeper from a body. It is the
Taper's flaw at the scale of the world.

**Why it dismissed moths.** It keeps what can be lost, and it judged that a moth can't be. A moth lives a week, has
no mouth and flies at any light until it dies: a reflex with wings. Its eyes slide past moths like ours slide past
dust. Moths that fly into its glowing pods are not kept but used, re-grown into lampwings. **In play:** Vesper flies
past an eye that snatches the deer beside her. Servants ignore a moth unless she is at their pods or carrying glints.
Carrying the ember, the whole Hold turns toward her, and even then it can't model her: its last weapon is a lure,
and Vesper has learned to fly straight past a light with her back to it.

**Is it a someone? The game shows both and never rules.** *Yes:* it grows hands from its flesh to cradle and rock its
sleepers; when a pod is cut the nearest eye weeps sap; it keeps the room where its first dish stood, down to the cup
on the table; it judged moths, and judging needs a judge. *No:* it rocks the dead as gently as the living, and in
that room a skeleton sits in a chair, rocked; its eyes weep the same for a cut pod and a snapped twig; every hand it
makes is borrowed; a moth can cut its heart and it never looks. The Taper says "it is a dish that grew." Tallow, who
has been inside it, folds over cut pods.

## 5. The ember and the Taper's theft

The **ember** is the woman's glint, the first thing the Hold ever held, once set in its first dish. Its gesture is a
hand laid on a cheek, and *unlike every other glint, it finishes:* the hand lifts away and the light dims before it
loops. It is a goodbye, the one lesson the Hold never learned.

A hundred lifetimes ago two young moths flew at the brightest light in the world, the drowned city moths call **the
Root**. One fell into a pod and was re-grown as a lampwing: Tallow. The other landed on the first dish, saw a hand
laid on a cheek, the first tenderness she had ever seen, and flew home with it. The Hold let her go, since she was
only a moth. Touched by its gesture, she became the first moth ever to come back from fading: the Taper. She learned
that glints burn and the flesh recoils, and founded the lanterns. The resistance grew from one stolen light.

**Who re-forms.** A fading moth becomes a drifting spark. A lit lantern catches the spark only if it was touched with
the ember's gesture, a wing laid on the cheek, and burns one glint to remake her. Untouched sparks drift past and go
out. The Taper's wing on a cheek is the anointing. Everyone else lives a week.

**The truce.** Tallow keeps the ember on the Grove's high island. Once in each pilgrim's lifetime the Taper's rite
sets it in the summit lantern while a grove's heart wakes and drinks every lantern there, pushing the Hold back. She
holds her reed over that one glass so it can't burn, and sees her light shine. She never watches it to the end.

**What she doesn't say.** The Hold began growing over the forests the year she took the ember. It isn't reaching
for the valley because the valley is wild. It is looking for its first.

## 6. Prologue (wordless, about 2 minutes)

*Shadow-play on glowing glass,* telling section 3 without a word: the dish and its bouncing dot; rows, buildings,
a city lit pink; an old hand on the glass going still and the flesh closing softly around it; flesh pouring down
streets; a family asleep in a pod, a child's hand waving; the night map of the world turning pink coast by coast;
falling leaves stopping in the air.

*The present.* The moths' valley, dark inside a ring of pink hills, its rim curling back from the lanterns. An eye
opens in the rim, takes a deer at a stream, and slides past the moth on the deer's ear. At a lantern, grey moths fade
and their sparks drift past the glass; the ancient Taper fades, the glass catches her, a glint goes dark, and she
steps out whole. She chooses Vesper from the crowd, lays a wing on her cheek and gives her three glints. A torn
lampwing waits at the edge of the light. The reed points at a dark valley: *your first grove, alone.*

## 7. The story

**The re-form is the relationship.** In Act I the Taper is at the glass every time Vesper steps out, wing on her
cheek. After the Hush, Vesper steps out alone. At the Root it reverses.

**ACT I: TOGETHER.** *With Vesper: the Taper, and for a while Sedge.*
- **The Grove** (our scene, section 9): the heart wakes and the flesh peels back from one grove. The first victory.
- **On the road.** The Taper catches Vesper when she glides short, then stops, because she is teaching. *Flare*,
  bought with glints, makes the flesh recoil.
- **Sedge** races Vesper and always loses. He gives her his only glint; his wings grey. He was never anointed.
- **Boss: Clasp, at the Combs.** Vesper and the Taper fight her from one shared lantern. In her last phase she heaps
  her pods onto her back and runs; cracking her shell spills them for the act's biggest haul, and she limps away
  empty.
- **The Coral Stair.** Tallow snatches Sedge's glint from Vesper's cape and flees slowly, one wing dragging.

**MIDPOINT: THE HUSH (played).** The chase ends in a hollow of pods. Tallow presses Sedge's glint into bare earth and
it sprouts green, the first thing the player has seen grow out of the Hold's flesh. It glitters like a pickup, so the
player grabs it: Vesper's flaw, and the player's. Clasp crawls past to a pod the player cut on the way in and folds
over it. The glint in Vesper's grip flickers a hand, cupping. **Then the hollow's floor, worn thin, shows what lies
under the valley:** the roof of a drowned town, and behind the flesh a sleeping human face bigger than the screen, a
hand at its cheek making the same cupping. Every lantern Vesper lit was burning him. She kneels and puts the glint
back: *Plant*. At the hollow's mouth the Taper holds out her wing. Vesper doesn't come. Sedge goes with the Taper.

**ACT II: APART.** *With Vesper: Tallow.*
- **The ember.** At night the Taper lifts her wing toward the high island once and leaves. Tallow brings the ember
  down and gives it to Vesper. Carried, it throws a hand's shadow around her that closes when she's hit. For the
  first time anyone watches it to the end: the hand lifts away.
- **The turn (played).** Carrying the ember to the rim, Vesper sees every eye in the hills open and the flesh lean
  toward her like a plant to the sun. The Taper's lanterns hold back a hunt her theft began.
- **The dark.** Each lantern Vesper snuffs costs a checkpoint and lets the flesh creep into that stretch. She
  *plants* glints instead: each grows a ring of green the flesh won't cross, slower than fire and burning no one.
  Tallow catches her cape when she glides short. Snuffed lanterns turn up relit behind her, reed-ash at their feet.
- **Boss: the Reach.** With the lanterns dark, the Hold's front comes over the ridge for the ember: a wall of flesh,
  eyes and reaching hands growing across the screen. Vesper cuts its growing tips and plants glints in the breaches
  until it stalls. It stops. It doesn't go back. The valley is smaller now.
- **Sedge's week runs out** at a lantern she snuffed. The Taper lays her wing on *his* cheek and relights it with his
  glint. He fades, is caught and steps out young. He doesn't look at Vesper.
- **Boss: the Taper, at the Censer** (the moths' home). She never strikes. She relights what Vesper snuffs, and her
  only attack is the wing, turning Vesper's face to the glass. Vesper wins by snuffing the Great Lantern.
- **The lowest point.** In the dark the children fly at the nearest glow, the pods at the rim, and Clasp leads the
  burrbacks at them. Vesper *Braces* between and takes the ram without moving; Clasp turns them away. Vesper falls
  far from any lit lantern. Tallow carries her spark to the Great Lantern, relit by the Taper, and lets go at the
  edge of its light. A glint goes dark for her. The Taper's wing lifts toward her cheek and stops. Every moth turns
  its back except Sedge.
- **Moonward.** Vesper leaves carrying the ember, and the Hold's rim turns to follow her out, away from the children.
  Her glide no longer bends toward the light behind her. Sedge follows, his glide still bending.

**ACT III: THE TAKEN WORLD.** *With Vesper: Tallow, Clasp and Sedge. Then the Taper.*
- **The Held Wood:** a forest where nothing has fallen in a hundred years; Vesper perches on hanging leaves.
  **The Fold:** fields of sleeping deer, rocked by grown hands. **The Root:** a drowned city wrapped to the tops of
  its towers, every window a pod, every sleeper's hand moving.
- **The storm.** Now the whole Hold sees a moth. Lampwings rise in thousands, and its oldest weapon, a lure, turns
  the sky bright as noon. Vesper flies straight past with her back to it. Sedge can't; Clasp shoves him clear and
  the lure's grip closes on her shell instead. The Taper has followed. She Flares the storm back beside Vesper, and both
  re-form from the last lantern at the Root's edge. Now when the Taper steps out, Vesper is at the glass and lays a
  wing on her cheek: the gesture, given back.
- **Boss: the Hand.** In the first room the Hold has grown a hand the size of a hillside, the woman's shape. It
  doesn't strike; it reaches to cup, and to be cupped is to be kept. It is the only time the Hold fights, for its
  first. The Taper spends the lantern's last glint on one Flare that opens the Hand, and falls with nothing left to
  catch her.

## 8. The ending

At the first dish, beside a skeleton rocked in a chair, the Taper's spark gutters at the foot of the Root's lantern,
its glass empty.
She lifts her wing to Vesper's cheek and turns her face to the glass. Vesper chooses. The game waits.

- **Burned.** Vesper sets the ember in the lantern and nobody shields it. The Hold's first light burns in its heart
  and the flare runs down every vein on Earth in one night. Cities flare and go black; the pods burn with their
  sleepers, who never wake and never say goodbye. Every burrback and lampwing is the Hold's, so every one stops:
  Clasp in the street, Tallow at the edge of the light. The old forest is scorched along every vein. The Taper steps
  out whole, the last moth who will ever re-form, with a week to live. She lifts her wing toward Vesper's cheek,
  stops and folds it. Last shot: a black, free world under the moon, children flying straight at it, the Taper
  beside them, slower every night.
- **Given back.** Vesper sets the ember in its place in the dish. Sedge lays his wing over hers. Everywhere at once,
  the Hold makes the woman's gesture: a hand on every sleeper's cheek, and one on Vesper's, the first time it has
  ever looked at a moth. Then every hand lifts. The pods open. The sleepers are a century past waking: their lights
  rise out of every city like snow falling upward and go out under the moon. A hundred years of leaves fall in one
  night. A grey old badger walks out of Clasp's split shell and lies down in the grass. Tallow's lamp goes out and a
  crooked moth flies from it toward the moon. The Taper's spark rises among the sleepers' lights with no glass to
  catch it, and goes out. The flesh settles into soil. Last shot: Vesper, Sedge and Tallow under a green tree at the
  Root, a week left each, and the skeleton's hand lying open in the moss, no longer held.

**The costs.** Both end the Hold, free the world, lose every sleeper and end re-forming. *Burned* keeps the Taper
and pays in fire, no goodbyes, and Clasp and Tallow. *Given back* pays with the Taper. Whether the Hold was a someone
decides which ending was the murder.

## 9. The Grove

Vesper's first rite, and the first time she pushes the Hold back. It must feel wonderful and dangerous, because the
turn only works if the player loved it.

| In the Grove | First play | After the Hush |
|---|---|---|
| **Glints** in pods | Treasure | A sleeper's thought, ripened in the Hold's flesh |
| **Lanterns**, re-forming | The Taper's wing on your cheek | Someone burned so you could stand up |
| **Burrbacks** fold at a kindling | Calmed beasts | Re-grown animals mourning a pod |
| **Lampwing** dives | Attacks | Re-grown moths taking a sleeper back |
| **The ember** | The great fetch | The Hold's first, stolen, fetched for the Taper by every pilgrim |
| **The heart** wakes on your offering | The grove wakes tier by tier; the flesh peels off its edges | Three lanterns burned at once to win back one grove |
| The moth on the stone | Moths carved their god | The old forest grew the one shape the Hold never watches |

**The ending beat.** The heart wakes. The Taper glides down and lays her wing on Vesper's cheek as the veins pulse
inward, then her reed-light rises to the summit lantern and shields the ember until the veins go quiet. The flesh at
the frame's edges shrinks back. A torn lampwing hangs under the island, and on the horizon the city's pink glow
doesn't move.

**Essential changes to the scene:** (1) veined flesh at the playfield's edges and in far layers that recoils at each
kindling and peels back at the wake, held leaves on threads, a pink city on the horizon; (2) one far eye that tracks a
burrback and slides past Vesper; (3) lantern glass that visibly holds glints, a re-form burning one, the Taper at the
glass; (4) hand gestures in glints, the ember's hand shadow and its lift; (5) lampwing dives that steal a glint.

## 10. What the player finds

1. **The Taper's hollow:** hundreds of scratched hands on cheeks, and under them an older scratch of two small moths
   flying at a great light, one falling. **Sedge's scratches** at a wayside lamp: two moths racing, one waiting.
2. **The summit lantern:** reed-ash layered from a hundred visits. **Tallow's island:** moss pressed flat around a
   hollow the size of the ember.
3. **A held kitchen** under the Hush: two sleepers at a table wrapped in flesh, holding hands.
4. **The first room** at the Root: rows of small dishes, one still bouncing its dot between two bars; a cup kept in
   flesh; the chair; and on the first dish, a hand-print and one empty place.

## 11. The question it leaves

Would you burn one life to keep the one you love? The Hold kept the whole world rather than lose one woman. The Taper
burned thousands of sleepers rather than lose one light. In the end the moth the Hold never bothered to see decides
whether loving someone means keeping them or letting them go.
