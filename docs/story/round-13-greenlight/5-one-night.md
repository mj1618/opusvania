# Greenlight review: ONE NIGHT

## 1. Decision
**GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES.**

## 2. The pitch, in three sentences
A slow god grown from living brain cells freezes whatever it looks at, and it never lets go, but it can't see moths. Over generations, moths have lit lamps across the land into a picture of a moth so that it will finally see one. Vesper has one night to finish the picture and stand in the god's gaze at sunrise, so that the sun ends her while she is held and the god learns to let go. Her grandmother Compline begs her to hide, because once the god can see moths, no moth will ever have a free night again.

(That runs to four sentences, but every one is plain. It retells.)

## 3. At the bar
- **The rules and the feelings are the same thing.** "Stillness is the one held input," and it escalates: Lune, then Compline's wings, then Compline's arms, then the god. Both endings are a way of being held: stand still in its look, or go into her arms. Few games tie their controls to their theme this closely.
- **Compline is a proper antagonist-as-person.** "She hides her family by setting the beasts on everyone else." Her hollow is lined with her daughters' coals, "there is one gap in the row," and the fight there is "a seal held shut by love." She is wrong in exactly the way the god is wrong, and the hollow lets her learn its lesson "smaller and sooner."
- **Sedge's arc does its job in a few gestures.** She teaches by doing a move first, they share glints, "There is no touch of antennae," then "Sedge touches antennae first," and her daughter does the same. Friendship, rupture and repair, all without a word of dialogue.
- **The world is told through specific objects.** "*Please let the children stay.* Answer received after 211 years. A root has grown through the words." The dish has 916 scratches and Vesper adds the 917th. There's the "LAMP 12" plate, and the Lamplighter blinks once when her lamp is lit.
- **The Grove fits nearly one-to-one.** Lune's coal is the ember on the high island, the cocoons on the hooks are eggs, re-forming at a lantern is a daughter waking, and the carved moth is the plan. The freeze that spares only the moths teaches the premise inside the scene we already built.

## 4. Foundation problems
None that block. One is close, so I'm making it a condition of the greenlight:

- **The climax runs on a rule the player is never shown working:** the god lets go only of what ends while it holds it. Everything else in the god's law gets taught in play, through the Lamplighter's blink, the hoods and the Grove freeze. This rule is only explained in the outline, and Vesper's death depends on it. If players don't feel why her ending frees the world, the sacrifice reads as arbitrary.
  **Fix:** seed it twice in play, without words. First, a Querystone that reads "*How do we make it let go?*" with no answer date. Second, a place in the Stillwood where a frost-held dewdrop melts in the first sun at the edge of a hold, and only that hand's breadth comes loose for a breath before the look settles back. That second one only demonstrates the rule on a small scale. The god still has never *learned* from it, because the drop was never looked at as a thing, so the law stays intact.

## 5. Polish notes
1. Show the moths' dance early, with Vesper and Sedge spiralling a lantern once. Otherwise "No moth ever dances again" is a price the player has never seen.
2. The matriarch fight contradicts the law. A look that catches her should freeze her, not let her be wounded. Freeze her mid-charge, then release her when the lantern lights, and *then* she lies down.
3. Two "hollows in the far hills" (the Eye and Compline's hollow) will be confused, and the outline never says when the dark path to the Eye gets lit. Rename one and stage the other.
4. Decide whether Sedge's daughter *is* Sedge. As written, "knows what she knew… same name" blunts the sacrifice. Let her keep the skills but not the friendship, so the antennae-touch is offered to a stranger.
5. Nobody ever faces the fact that Compline's hoods killed Sedge's sisters. Give the two of them one wordless moment, at the field or in the Seen epilogue.
6. Lune's coal "burns all night" by the rule, but it glows under snow for a year. Make the dawn-coal its own thing.
