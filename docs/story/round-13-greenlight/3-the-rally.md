# Greenlight review: 3. REPLY (round 12)

## 1. Decision: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES

The people, the world and the mechanics form one shape here. One load-bearing rule (what draws the hunt) needs a sentence locked before production starts. That is a condition of the greenlight, not a rewrite.

## 2. The pitch, in three sentences

In a forest where every creature must flash back at a pale light from the east, and whatever is answered for never grows old, a young moth named Vesper answers for a doomed calf. From then on she is followed by Reply, a glass moth in her shape that answers for her and brings the hunt down on everyone she loves. She defies Sallow, the ancient moth who raised her, and with little Mote copying her every move she travels toward the light's source. On the way she learns that every lampwing she has killed is a Reply some moth abandoned, and that the oldest, Wick, is the one Sallow left, who has kept her alive for three hundred years. At the crater lake where moths leave their Replies, Vesper has to choose who her answer is for: her family, the glass stranger who followed her, or a forest that can finally live and die.

(It pitches cleanly, though it is dense. The doc's own spine is more crowded than this.)

## 3. At the bar

- **A control that carries the theme.** At the Eaves "the input fails": the Sweep has been tipping her face up all game, and the player only notices when they try to look east. Then they watch the elders lay a wing over each hatchling's face, "all the way down the row". Sallow's love is built into the controls as conditioning. This is Ori/Nine Sols-grade.
- **A midpoint reveal told in one frame.** "Two torn left wings fill the screen, one pale and one black." There are husks under empty roosts, one for each lampwing the player killed. The player has been doing harm all along, as with the Apeman, and the reveal costs no dialogue.
- **Relationships shown through play.** In the Gorge, Reply waits at the foot of the shaft until, "on the third try it follows", and Mote sleeps on its glass back that night. Sallow is missing when Vesper re-forms after the quarrel, and is there exactly once for the forgiveness. At the boss, Sallow "shields him, and in doing so she saves her own life". This answers the owner's note directly.
- **The owner's wetware idea, without technobabble.** *"It is not playing. It is returning."* / *"So are we."* The Pong lineage becomes a haunting line. *PLEASE DO NOT ANSWER THE LAMPS* is a plaque a player will screenshot.
- **Endings that answer the question, each at a cost.** Each one is played by where she turns her face, and each leaves someone behind. In For Reply, the glass wing laid across her face is the only touch in the game.

## 4. Foundation problems

**One, borderline, and fixable in a sentence: what draws the hunt.** The prologue shows the hunt falling on silence (the burrbacks turn on the calf). After that, the doc says the hunt follows Reply's flash. Yet left Replies (lampwings) keep flashing for the elders, and the elders are not hunted, and the family ending depends on "the hunt lifts" once Reply is left. Players will feel the gap at the ending they are most likely to pick first.
*Fix:* make it one rule. **The hunt goes where the flash is.** A Reply flashing beside its moth draws the forest onto her. A left Reply swings its flash out in the dark, so the hunt falls on the lampwing, not the moth. That is why lampwings live outside the light. It also explains the Reach decoy, and makes Wick's three hundred years of drawing the hunt away from Sallow a love story. For the prologue, have the calf's silence set the Grove shivering and Vesper's strike pull every eye to her: same rule, the light's attention follows the flash. Lock this before any writing begins.

Nothing else blocks. The relationships are felt, the ending answers its question, and the core is not derivative.

## 5. Polish notes

1. Cut the central question to one line ("Who is your answer for?"), and let "is a world where nothing dies alive?" sit under For the forest.
2. A Radiance echo: a light every creature answers, plus a moth hero. Keep the Sweep's answer pale and cold (never orange eye-glow) and keep the "moths forgot the light" framing out.
3. After the reveal, lampwings need to be avoidable or sparable, or the moral reversal has no follow-through in play.
4. Glints are "bits of the Eastlight", yet lanterns "answer nothing". Re-mean glints as stray moonlight, or as unanswered calls, so feeding the lanterns isn't feeding the Eastlight.
5. Mote's exit is a separation rather than a loss. Give her one gesture (reaching back from Sallow's arms) so the goodbye lands.
6. Vesper being ageless is invisible. Give it one visible tell, such as her torn wing-edge healing at each re-form while Sallow's never does.
