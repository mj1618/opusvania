# MOUTHLESS: greenlight review (round 13)

## 1. Decision: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES

No foundation problems. One rule seam at the climax must be closed before production (polish note 1). It is a one-line fix, not a rewrite.

## 2. The pitch, in three sentences

A moth born without a mouth is the one creature that the starving lab-grown hunger under the forest cannot see, and she goes down with her brother to find what is crying. It turns out to be an abandoned infant that has drunk the world and is still empty, because nothing taken fills it, only what is given. When she finally fills it, it offers to keep her and her brother alive forever, the way it kept the woman who left it, and she has to choose between being kept together and spending their last warmth so that it can be born.

I could say that to the team cold, and they could retell it. That is the clarity test, and it passes.

## 3. What is at the bar

- **The hook is biology, not lore.** Adult moths of some kinds really have no mouth, so "it sees by appetite" explains why moths were spared and why Vesper underestimated matters, with no techno-babble. The line "Something that cares only for mouths never wondered what a mouthless thing could carry" is the owner's brief turned into one image.
- **Mechanics carry the theme.** Carrying glints makes her seen. She heals only where she gave. She "grows stronger only by being given to". Shiver spends her own warmth. In Spin, each circuit costs her glide, then jump, then dash. That ending is felt through the controller, which is exactly the Ori/Nine Sols standard.
- **The reversal at the midpoint is earned and quiet.** "It has not been drinking her. It has been feeding her." With the worn root at the woman's lips and the 113 scratched lines, the room tells its story without a word, the way Hollow Knight's places do.
- **The relationships push and pull clearly.** Sorrel wants them both to live. Dun wants to be the one who feeds it. The Nursling wants to be warm and to keep what it loves. Sorrel's arc (saves her, is hurt by her hiding, leaves her, gives up his dance to come back, follows her onto the rim) is the best brother arc in this set. Dun's turn ("She has just lost a baby too") is a Kuro-grade beat.
- **The ending costs something and answers the question.** Both branches are played rather than picked, and both resolve "kept together, or spent so something can be born". The last image ("a pale shape, neither root nor moth, presses toward the warmth of her hand") keeps the sentience question open without saying it.

## 4. Foundation problems

None. The core rule holds across the plot. The central relationships are felt in play. The ending answers the question. The familiar beats (Dun as a grieving parent like Kuro, a self-gift ending like Will of the Wisps, a choice of endings like Hollow Knight) are reframed enough that a player would not name a source on first play.

## 5. Polish notes

1. **(Must-fix) Say what is in the first nurse's jars.** If they hold hoarded *taken* food, pouring them down breaks "what is taken never fills it" at the climax. Fix: make them the woman's unopened jars of feed, the meal she meant to bring back, so they arrive as a gift at last.
2. **The central question is knotted, and "a child you love" leans on love we haven't seen yet.** Simplify it (e.g. "Kept together forever, or spent so something new can be born?"). Before Night 4, add one wordless beat of Vesper choosing the root-tip, such as shielding it from a burrback.
3. **Timescale:** "a century of jars" doesn't fit a woman who is only "older". Use decades.
4. **Lumen pods are cut open, but glints are "made to be given away".** Make ripe pods split at her touch, or have her cut a milkroot snare off the pod rather than the pod itself.
5. **The Stay epilogue has Dun hauling the fox again,** which undoes her Night 5 turn with no beat. Show her hesitate first, or let the cradle-roots do the taking.
6. **Forest and animal sentience is thin beyond the oak's line.** Give it one more voice (a dreaming sleeper, a gesture from Dun). Also, "drowned town" cellars contradicts "hill town".
