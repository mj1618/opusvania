# Greenlight review: NOISE (round 12)

## 1. Decision: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES

## 2. The pitch, in three sentences
Sixty years ago a mother lost her son to a flood while he was chasing a moth, and the living machine she grew has kept the whole world safe and still ever since, but it cannot bring itself to look at moths. Vesper, a lonely young moth, wakes her one friend, an old beetle who has walked the same circle all his life. Every crack they make in the stillness brings the machine's great lamp down harder, and the old moth who raised Vesper dies leading it away. Vesper carries that moth's last fire into the mother's kitchen, where she can free the world by force or sit still in plain sight and let the woman who never could look at a moth choose to look.

(That came out as three sentences plus one clause. The spine retells cleanly.)

## 3. What is at the bar
- **The image the prologue hangs on is earned and reused.** "She brushes it off and turns her face away." That shot becomes the reason moths were "underestimated" ("Something couldn't bear to look at them"), the Levee beat, and the hinge of the Light ending. The owner's idea is re-meant rather than dropped, and that is the Ori/Kuro grade of move.
- **The relationship with Hob is one the player plays, not one they're told about.** Three wingbeats he never answers, the night in the hollow, the glints she drops to him from ledges, the way he waits at the foot of every climb. The reunion is the best scene in the outline: "She made herself a habit to reach him, and he broke his to save her." The rule, the relationship and the theme all land in a single action.
- **The midpoint reframes what the player has already done.** "Every lantern the player has rested at all game is a copy of the lamp a child died carrying." Sallow's notched wing matches Wim's carving. It sits at the Hollow Knight and Nine Sols level: what you rested at was a memorial all along.
- **The mechanics carry the theme.** The rule of three (repeat yourself and you are kept), flicker against swell, the lanterns Vesper must tend after Sallow dies ("every rest now costs something"), moves learned from a dead boy's drawings, and a boss whose defeat breaks something beautiful.
- **It is restrained and specific.** "Let nothing happen." is the only human writing in the game. Sallow says "Sixty years I kept it. Don't keep it." The Even Heart reads "A healthy heart stumbles a little. This one never has."

## 4. Foundation problems
None block the greenlight. One note comes close, and it should be fixed before scripting:

- **Hob's fall is the relationship's lowest point, but nothing Vesper does causes it.** "The quarrel" has no quarrel in it: a branch falls, he won't go on, and she leaves him. The outline names her flaw as "she forces", so the low point has to come from that flaw. **Fix:** after Sallow's death, Vesper drives Hob through a lamp-lit sluice he balks at, using the same charge she will later call on the Spillway. He is cracked doing it for her. He stops at a lantern and gives her the wingbeat refusal, his first gesture toward her in the game, and she goes on anyway. Then "he walked back into the Quiet because it doesn't hurt there" becomes her debt, the reunion becomes her apology, and the Light goodbye becomes her growth.

## 5. Polish notes
1. Reconcile "lamps flinch from moths" with "lamps take Vesper on a third repeat" in one bible line. A moth flickers, so the lamp slides off it. Repeat yourself and you stop flickering, which turns you into a pattern the lamp can hold. The "two signals" rule already implies this.
2. The Cut ending says "the valley pays in lives", but Maud is the last person alive. Name who drowns (the held creatures downstream, the meadow herds) or the cost is empty.
3. The Lamplighter's turn when it sees the flame needs its cause staged: it dims the way it did over Sallow's water, because this is *her* fire.
4. The cast table gives Hob "all her life" and then calls him "he". Fix the pronoun slip.
5. A rule of three in combat collides with a three-hit combo. Count only identical actions, or make the combo's hits read as different.
6. The Light epilogue ("rule of three holds everywhere") needs one shot that shows Maud's look as tenderness, not punishment, so players read it as a soft cage rather than a bad ending.
