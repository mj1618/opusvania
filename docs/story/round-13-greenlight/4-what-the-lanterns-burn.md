# Greenlight review: 4. What the Lanterns Burn

## 1. Decision: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES

Nothing here needs to go back to the writers. Two rules are only implied, and the story leans on them. Each takes about a paragraph to state, and both must be settled before scripting (section 4).

## 2. The pitch, in three sentences

Vesper is a young moth lighting lanterns beside the Taper, the mentor who meets her at the glass every time she dies, until she learns that every light the lanterns burn, including the one that brings her back, is the echo of a dead human. She breaks with the Taper and goes off with the "monsters" who were guarding those echoes, while the Taper keeps relighting what Vesper snuffs. At the Root, the dying Taper asks Vesper to burn the one glint she has kept from the fire for a hundred lifetimes, so that someone can go on keeping the children.

## 3. What is at the bar

- **The respawn is the relationship.** "Every time Vesper fades, the Taper is at the glass when she steps out, and lays her wing on Vesper's cheek. After the Hush, Vesper steps out alone. At the Root it reverses." This is Ori's Naru in a mechanic, and the player feels it before anyone explains it.
- **The midpoint makes the player act out the flaw.** The sprouted glint "glitters like a pickup, so the player grabs it." That is the Apeman reversal from Nine Sols done through the hands rather than through a cutscene.
- **One image carries the theme.** The hand on a cheek is the ember's gesture, the Taper's wing, the hundreds of scratched hands on her wall, and the last frame of Kept. One motif does the work of a lore dump.
- **The antagonist is gentle.** In the Taper's boss fight "her only attack is the wing: she catches Vesper's cheek and turns her to the glass." The Taper is wrong, she is loving, and she makes Vesper's own devotion visible.
- **Both endings cost something.** In Kept the Taper "looks at the wing and folds it. She never makes the gesture again." In Planted the last shot is children with no lantern near and a burrback's shadow falling over them. Each ending answers the central question, and the player pays for the answer.

## 4. Foundation problems

None of these block the greenlight. Two conditions:

1. **Who gets to re-form is only implied.** The outline says "Only pilgrims are allowed this," but in the prologue grey moths vanish at the same glass the Taper re-forms from. Sedge, Act II and the finale all depend on this rule. **Fix:** make the Taper's wing on the cheek the anointing. A lantern catches only a spark that has been touched with the ember's gesture. Then the gesture literally is the gift of life. Sedge's re-form, Vesper laying her wing on the Taper at the Root, and the folded wing in Kept all start to mean more.
2. **The Taper's case needs a real threat.** The keepers hunt only moths that carry glints, and the moths carry glints because of the lanterns, so "keeping the children" reads as circular. **Fix:** make the moths' pull toward light real, not just the keepers' belief about them. Without lanterns, moth children fly into the pods and the keepers kill them. The lanterns are the light they are allowed to fly to. That makes the Taper's bargain genuinely hard: people burn so that children don't. It also gives "she flies straight with her back to it" a cost.

## 5. Polish notes

- Section 5 says Tallow carries the ember "home," meaning the island, but Act II says home is the Root. Pick one word for each.
- State that the Taper never knew the ember's human. She fell for the first tenderness she ever saw, and her wing gesture is borrowed. The spine's "the one person the Taper loved" overclaims this.
- Say whether lanterns burn glints continuously or only for a re-form and a wake. The summit-shield beat needs to know which.
- Kept doesn't say where Vesper ends up. Give her one shot.
- Keep the old forest to the single sighting plus the heart. It is a third force and should not get a fourth name.
- Snuffed lanterns in Act II remove respawn points, so pace the checkpoints with level design early.

## 6. Scores

Originality is held back because players will see the souls-as-fuel reversal (Nine Sols) and the two-ending choice over the fire (Dark Souls) as familiar. The hands, the moths and the borrowed gesture make it this story's own, so it doesn't block.
