# Review: Mouthless (round 24)

## 1. Anchor gate: PASS

All three cold readers open with the litmus sentence almost word for word: a moth with a week to live against "a pale, breathing growth" that "sealed every person and animal on earth". All three found the lab brain on their own: "a brain grown from human cells to run the food supply" (casual), "a brain grown in a dish... kept calm only by a woman's hand on the glass" (lore). The Hush acts in every act. Act I has root under the pool, the tower-root boss and Dun hunting the vixen. Act II has the mountain and the world breathing through its roots. Night 5 is the overrun wood. Act III is the city, the hollows and the final offer. It has scale (every case on earth) and it has momentum (root creeping, then hunting moths after Night 4).

Owner's ideas:
- Biological AI took over the world: **central.**
- Umber the moth saves the world: **central.**
- The AI overlooked moths and that underestimation matters: **central.** It is rule 1, the hook ("never noticed moths, because moths never eat") and the midpoint reversal.
- Sentience ambiguity: **central** for the Hush ("was that love, or had it only learned how to make the static stop?"). It is **minor** for the forest and the animals, which Dun carries almost alone.

## 2. Verdict: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES

## 3. At the bar
- **The premise grows out of the moth's body.** Being mouthless is why she is invisible, why the forest's light is "to give away", and why the true ending is "wings closed, the one thing that never needs feeding". Image, rule and theme are one fact.
- **The shiver motif is a relationship told through play.** Sorrel shivers for her in the prologue. She shivers for Dun in the pond, then for Sorrel, then for the caterpillar, and the whole moth population shivers for the Hush. The heart reader: "starts in the prologue and pays off three times."
- **The reveal reframes the opening without a word.** The prologue's "warm blur" turns out to be the sister's hand on the dish during the ball game. "Grown from us / First it learned to catch / Now it feeds us" is mythic on the surface and Cortical Labs underneath, with no technical words.
- **Tallow's turn.** "He burns one close enough to see the face inside breathe... looks back at his ring." The lore reader would keep this moment.
- **Sorrel round the seed-sized case** while the root closes over the lantern. Two of three readers named it as the image they would carry away.

## 4. Problems, ranked
1. **The Hush's diet and motive blur (POLISH, top priority).** The casual reader asks "feeds them with what? Why does a food-brain... seal everyone?" The heart reader asks "does it eat light or warmth?" Fix it without adding a rule. Keep **light = being seen** and **warmth = being soothed**. At the midpoint the glints don't feed it, they make it *see* the giver ("the first thing anyone has given it"). In the lab flashback, add one shot: after the sister is led out, its first root goes through the greenhouse pipe into the nearest town, and the first case closes round a sleeper's hand. Now the motive reads as "so no one lifts their hand away again", which the prologue already set up.
2. **Spin gives back its cost (POLISH).** The moths would die within the week anyway, and the epilogue restores everything else: the carving fills again, the partner hatches, the cubs are born and Tallow is reunited. The lore reader says Stay and Burn "feel like punishments." Fix: leave the stone empty for good, so the forest's mind is spent and the wood is ordinary now. Let Stay be honestly tempting by showing the sleepers warm and fed, without the hunger Spin wakes them into.
3. **Lines readers misread (POLISH).** Two of three read "people hang from the beams" as a mass hanging, so write "cases hang". "every lamp after him" should be "every lampwing". "never fights her... until it tramples over her" should be "it walks through her".
4. **Dun at the open heart on Night 1 (POLISH).** Two readers asked why. Fix: Dun sniffs at the vole's seed by the pool, and that is why it is in the wood. Seeding it once in the prologue is enough.
5. **The coal contradicts itself (POLISH).** It burns the wall yet stays in her hand for Burn. Fix: burning the wall *spends* it, so Burn is only open to a player who gave it to the lantern and climbs back for it on Night 7, or else cut the wall choice.
6. **Too many words to hold (POLISH).** The casual reader lists eleven terms. Fix: HEARTH appears only on the sign and is never used as a name in the text, and use "case" consistently (the pod/case slips already show).

No foundation problems.

## 5. Scores
anchor 9 · relationships 8 · clarity 7 · idea 9 · world 8 · mechanics-as-theme 9 · antagonist 8 · ending 7 · originality 9 · Grove fit 8 · balance 8

Grove fit: the three lanterns, the heart waking, the carving, lampwings diving at light and burrbacks calmed by glints all land as they are. The summit "biggest glint" replaces the high-island ember; either keep the ember as the forest's coal or say that it changed.

## 6. At the bar? **Yes.**
It is an idea as specific as the Apeman reversal. The spine is a fable a child could retell and the casual player did. The world is found in kitchens, posters and tally marks, and the relationships live in a single verb the player performs. The biggest gap before it is a true equal is the ending's cost. Spin must leave something permanently lost, not only the two moths who were dying anyway. After that, the light/warmth logic must be clear enough that a casual player can say in one breath why the Hush closed the world.
