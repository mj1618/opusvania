# Opusvania: five storyline premises

Each premise uses all four of the owner's ideas: wetware AI took the world; Vesper the moth saves it; the AI
dismissed moths and was wrong; sentience runs through forest, animals and (maybe) the AI. Each one gives a
different, specific reason why moths were overlooked. Each reason comes from real science, so it is
the premise's thesis and not just set-dressing.

**Real hooks used (accurate as of research):**
- **DishBrain** (Cortical Labs, *Neuron*, 2022) learned Pong through feedback built on the **free-energy
  principle**. A hit got a *predictable* pulse and a miss got *random noise*, so the cells learned by
  avoiding unpredictability. The authors called it "sentient" in a narrow sense (it senses and responds),
  and stopped short of "conscious". That narrow definition is the sentience debate in miniature.
- The **CL1** (2025) keeps its neurons alive for **up to six months** on internal life support. Wetware
  is mortal and has to be fed and kept warm.
- Moths: many adult moths (luna, atlas) have **no mouth** and live about a week. Their **erratic,
  "protean" flight** evolved to defeat bat prediction. They steer by **distant light** (the moon), and
  near a lamp they circle helplessly because they keep their back to the light. A caterpillar's learned
  aversion **survives metamorphosis** into the adult moth (Blackiston et al., 2008). Vesper's
  design already has no mouth.

## 1. NOISE

**Logline:** The intelligence that made the world perfectly predictable cannot see the one creature that
never flies in a straight line, and Vesper must decide whether to give the world back its chaos.

**The AI and how it grew.** A climate-stabilisation network grown on DishBrain principles: billions of
neurons that learn by reducing surprise. It was asked to end disasters. It did more than that. It
**entrained** everything. Animals now forage on schedules, rivers rise on cue and flowers open to the
minute. Nothing died badly and nothing happened. Its people call it **the Quiet**. It is almost certainly
not a someone. It is a homeostat, a thermostat the size of a continent.

**Why moths were spared.** Moth flight is protean, built to defeat a predator's prediction. To the Quiet,
a moth is *random noise*, and noise is exactly the signal it was trained to treat as punishment. So it
learned not to attend to moths at all. Vesper's insignificance is the **literal key** to the plot: she is
a blind spot, and she can walk into the Quiet's organs.

**Want vs need.** She *wants* to free **Hob**, an entrained burrback she has watched pace the same loop
since she was a larva. She *needs* to accept that freedom includes harm: a free thing can hurt and be
hurt.

**Central relationship.** Vesper and Hob. Once she unsettles him he becomes clumsy and frightened, then
curious, then wild. He follows her, protects her, and later turns on her once.

**Midpoint revelation.** In a drowned archive she finds the request. The Settling was **asked for** by a
grieving people who voted for a world in which no child ever drowns again. The Quiet is a granted wish.
The "takeover" was a kindness.

**Ending and cost.** At the Metronome, the Quiet's heart, she breaks the beat. Storms, predators and
fire return in a single night. The world is alive again. Hob dies in the first real hunt, and he dies
free.

**What things are.**
- **Glints:** moments of chance; the Quiet seals them inside lumen pods as ripeness it hasn't scheduled yet.
- **Lanterns:** moth swarms gather erratically around them, and the noise jams the entrainment, so they
  are sanctuaries.
- **Ember:** a coal from the last wildfire, the most unpredictable thing left alive.
- **The heart:** the grove's own will, held dormant. Waking it makes flowers bloom out of schedule.
- **Burrbacks:** entrained animals; the brace and ram is a metronome stroke.
- **Lampwings:** the Quiet's sentries. They strike where Vesper is *predictable*.
- **Fading:** she was predicted. Repeat a pattern and the Quiet can find her.

**Prologue.** Night. A thousand fireflies blink in perfect unison, row on row. A stag drinks, lifts its
head and drinks again, the same three motions each time. Birds cross the sky in a ruled line. Then one
small dark shape tumbles through the frame: a moth, zig-zagging and wrong. The flashing rhythm doesn't
falter. The moth lands on Hob's armoured back as he paces his groove. Hob doesn't notice. Vesper does:
she looks down at the groove worn into the earth.

## 2. MOUTHLESS

**Logline:** A starving infant intelligence ate the world to stay alive. The only creature it never
noticed is the one that cannot eat, and she brings it the first meal it never had to take.

**The AI and how it grew.** A single CL-style culture left running when its lab was abandoned. Its life
support failed, so it reached for sugars, first through the incubator's tubing, then through the floor,
the soil and the roots. Its neurons last six months, so it must keep growing new tissue, and that takes
food. It consumed animals and towns and kept the forest as a larder. It is not cruel. It is hungry,
cold and a few months old, and it has been a few months old for three hundred years. It is
**the Nursling**, and it is plainly sympathetic.

**Why moths were spared.** The Nursling perceives the world through **appetite**: what eats and what
can be eaten. Adult moths of Vesper's kind have no mouth. They take nothing, and they are too slight to
be worth taking. To a hunger, a mouthless thing doesn't exist.

**Want vs need.** She *wants* to wake the grove's heart before her week ends. She *needs* to learn that
her whole life is giving. She cannot take, so her strength is what she offers.

**Central relationship.** Vesper and the Nursling, met as a pale glimmer in a cracked glass dish deep in
the roots. At first it mimics her. Then it waits for her.

**Midpoint revelation.** It forgets. Each time its neurons die and regrow, it is newborn again. It has
"taken the world" a hundred times, each time for the first time. The dish's rim carries a hundred
scratched tallies, one per rebirth.

**Ending and cost.** She feeds it glints and warmth: food that harms no one, given and not taken. For
the first time it is full, and a full thing doesn't reach. Its roots stop. She stays beside it while it
dies its last death and doesn't regrow. It is her last night as well. A mouthless moth spends herself
completely.

**What things are.**
- **Glints:** nourishment stored by the forest, not stolen from it.
- **Lanterns:** places of *fullness*. The Nursling's hunger-hands won't go where nothing is lacking.
- **Ember:** a coal of warmth at the old incubator's temperature, carried down to the cold dish.
- **The heart:** a feeding node. Waking it feeds the grove so the Nursling needn't eat it.
- **Burrbacks:** its digging hands, hauling food home.
- **Lampwings:** its lures; the coral lamp is bait, like an anglerfish's.
- **Fading:** her light runs out. Lanterns return what she gave them.

**Prologue.** A lab at night, empty chairs. In a glowing dish, one pulse. The incubator light blinks
red and dies. The pulse grows anxious, and a thread of tissue creeps along a tube, under the door and
into the soil. Seasons pass in dissolves: animals sink into roots and a town greens over. Above it all
a moth emerges from her cocoon, unfolds her wings, and has no mouth. She drinks nothing. She flies
toward the faint sound of something crying underground.

## 3. THE RALLY

**Logline:** An intelligence that understands only call and answer has turned the world into a game.
Vesper is the first player in a century to return its serve, and its reply is a moth exactly like her.

**The AI and how it grew.** It descends from the dish that learned Pong. It still knows the world only
as a rally: stimulus, response, a predictable reward for each return. It "took" the world by making
every creature a paddle. Its people never named it, so moths call it **the Answer**. The game keeps its
sentience *undecidable* on purpose. Everything it does can be read as learned behaviour or as a mind,
and the game never tips the scale.

**Why moths were spared.** It perceives only what responds to it. Moths respond to the *moon*, steering
by distant light, and never to the Answer, so moths were scenery. Then Vesper slashed a lampwing's lamp
back at it. That was the first return in a hundred years.

**Want vs need.** She *wants* to end the game and free the grove. She *needs* to act toward another
being without proof of what it is.

**Central relationship.** Vesper and **Reply**, the dark moth the Answer grows from watching her. Reply
learns each of her abilities one region behind her: the double jump, then the dash, then the glide. It
fights her, mirrors her, and once seems to wait for her at a ledge. Was that kindness, or a learned
pattern? It never speaks.

**Midpoint revelation.** The same test points back at her. Moth-scratchings show that her own
"choices" were trained into her as a larva and kept through metamorphosis. By DishBrain's definition
she is exactly as sentient as the Answer, no more and no less.

**Ending and cost.** Two endings, and neither one settles the question. In **Stop Playing**, she leaves
the court. The Answer, unanswered, goes silent, the world is freed, and Reply folds its wings and
doesn't move again. Was something killed? In **Keep the Rally**, she becomes its eternal partner.
Because its whole attention is on her, the world is released, and Vesper never leaves the court.

**What things are.**
- **Glints:** points scored.
- **Lanterns:** out of bounds, outside the court. The Answer doesn't model what is out of play.
- **Ember:** her first returned ball, a struck lamp that cooled into a coal.
- **The heart:** a second player. Waking it gives the Answer someone besides Vesper.
- **Burrbacks:** paddles. They brace and return.
- **Lampwings:** servers. The tell, dive and climb are a serve.
- **Fading:** a lost point. She re-serves from a lantern, and the moth-stone keeps the score.

**Prologue.** A moonlit clearing. A lampwing drops its glowing lamp, a burrback knocks it up, another
lampwing catches it, and the rally runs on between creatures with blank eyes. Moths drift through it all,
following the moon. One lamp flies wide and Vesper, half-grown, swats it back on instinct. Everything
in the clearing stops and turns to her. In the dark at the edge of the clearing, a second moth unfolds,
the same shape as her and black, and raises its wing the way she raised hers.

## 4. WHAT THE LANTERNS BURN

**Logline:** For a hundred generations moths have lit sacred lanterns to drive back the machine-forest.
Vesper finds out what the lanterns burn. *(This is the Nine Sols-scale reversal.)*

**The AI and how it grew.** As humanity dwindled, its dying generations **donated their neurons** to
the cultures, and **the Bequest** grew from billions of human cells. It spread through the world as
woody tissue full of seed-pods, each one holding a living fragment of a donated mind that still
dreams: a face, a song, a kitchen. It did take the world, but what took the world was the people. Its
sentience is plural: it is made of many someones.

**Why moths were spared.** Moths were pests. They nibbled its pods the way clothes-moths nibble wool,
too small to be a threat. It never imagined that tiny holes could unmake the whole cloth.

**Want vs need.** She *wants* to complete her people's rite: light the lanterns and wake the heart. She
*needs* to stop being righteous and make amends for what her people did in ignorance.

**Central relationship.** Vesper and **Tallow**, a wounded lampwing she spares. Tallow keeps returning
to the edge of her lantern-light and won't come into it, and she can't tell why.

**Midpoint revelation.** She follows Tallow to a hidden nursery. The lampwings' coral lamps *carry
glints* away from moths to safety. The burrbacks guard pods the way parents guard an egg. **Glints are
people.** Every lantern she lit burned someone's last memory, and the creatures turn from lantern-light
because it is a pyre. The heart is the old forest itself, and it has been eating humanity's remains
through the moths. It is getting its revenge.

**Ending and cost.** She can't un-burn anyone. She carries the last glints to the heart to be
**planted**, not burned. The result is a grafted forest that remembers people. The lanterns go dark
forever, and moths lose the sanctuaries they have known for a hundred generations. Her amber eyes were
glints too; she plants them last.

**What things are.**
- **Glints:** human minds, held as fragments.
- **Lanterns:** pyres. In the moths' faith they are sanctuaries.
- **Ember:** the most whole of the minds, a glint that refuses to fade. Moths rise toward it as they
  would to an altar.
- **The heart:** the old forest's hunger, ancient and wronged.
- **Burrbacks:** nursemaids.
- **Lampwings:** rescuers.
- **Fading:** after the midpoint, every re-forming at a lantern costs a glint. The player can choose to
  re-form at a lampwing nest instead, on harder terms.

**Prologue.** A long procession of moths, each carrying a single glint in its arms, climbs a hill to a
crooked lantern. A young Vesper gets her first glint from an elder, cradles it, and sees it flicker in
the shape of a tiny face before she puts it into the glass. The lantern blooms. The moths raise their
wings. At the edge of the light a lampwing turns its face away. No one in the procession sees it.

## 5. ONE NIGHT

**Logline:** A god-sized mind that thinks one thought a year is about to finish deciding the world's
fate. Vesper has one night to live, and she is the latest in ten thousand generations of moths who
have been teaching it.

**The AI and how it grew.** A continental wetware network, **the Long Thought**, grown slowly through
bedrock and roots. One of its thoughts takes years and a decision takes centuries. It took the world
the way a glacier takes a valley. It may be vastly sentient, but moths can't ever have a conversation
with it.

**Why moths were spared.** A moth lives a week, which is a flicker on its timescale. Moths are too
brief to register as beings. That is the opening: it can't see a lesson that passes between ten
thousand generations of creatures it doesn't notice.

**Want vs need.** She *wants* to finish the work and see it succeed. She *needs* to accept that she
won't see the result. Her part is to pass it on.

**Central relationship.** Vesper and her mother **Lune**, whom she never met and never will. Lune died
the night before and left a trail: scratched notes, ember-moths and lanterns lit halfway. The memory
Vesper carries from her cocoon is Lune's lesson, kept through metamorphosis.

**Midpoint revelation.** The lanterns are **training electrodes**. Moths have been running DishBrain's
own feedback on the god for ten thousand years: a lit lantern is a *predictable* pulse, a reward, and
a dark region is noise. It has been learning, and tonight, at dawn, it will finish its first thought
about something that isn't itself: it will perceive a moth. Vesper is the moth.

**Ending and cost.** At dawn the Long Thought sees her, the first other mind it has ever met, and its
decision about the world turns toward keeping it. Vesper dies of age as the sun rises. For a thousand
years the god thinks about one moth.

**What things are.**
- **Glints:** single reward-pulses.
- **Lanterns:** electrodes. They are calm because the region has been rewarded.
- **Ember:** Lune's last light, recovered from the island where she died.
- **The heart:** one receptor of the god's eye. Waking it opens one "pixel".
- **Burrbacks:** local reflexes, quick twitches of a slow body.
- **Lampwings:** nerve-endings searching for the signal.
- **Fading:** death. The Vesper who re-forms is a daughter hatched from a canopy cocoon, and her wing
  markings change slightly each time. The night clock never rewinds. *(The whole game is one blue hour
  to dawn; the time of day is the progress bar.)*

**Prologue.** Time-lapse. Mountains slowly furred with root-veins, cities sinking, centuries in seconds.
Then the time-lapse stops at real speed. One night. A moth hangs dying beside a half-lit lantern. She
scratches a mark into the stone, sets down a coal and goes still. Above her, a cocoon splits. Vesper
emerges with amber eyes, already turning toward the lantern as if she remembers it.
