# Mouthless: choose the three big images

Full shot lists in `mouthless-key-scenes.md`. Pick one letter per beat (or mix).

## 1. The prologue's opening image

- **A. The breath** *(pick)*. Black; a small pale light breathing. A warm, out-of-focus blur (a palm, unreadable)
  rests on it, then lifts away. The light stutters into grey grain. Night 4 reveals the blur was the maker's hand on
  the dish.
- **B. The rally.** A pale bead bouncing between two dim strokes like a firefly between reeds; a miss floods the
  frame with static; it rallies until the static stays away. The same bead returns on the lab's screen.
- **C. Two lights.** A pale light and an amber cocoon breathing in step; the warm blur lifts off the pale one and it
  loses the rhythm; the camera pulls back: one is the mountain's core, the other a cocoon in the Grove.

*Why A:* most mysterious, cheapest, and it pays twice (the lab widens it; the final image answers it).

## 2. Night 4: the turning moment (she gives it every glint)

- **A. Her shape on every wall.** The roots turn to her like sunflowers; her moth shape rises in relief from every
  wall, the carving's pose. A cathedral of her. Costly.
- **B. The cloud draws a moth** *(pick)*. Close in on the dish: amber enters it for the first time, the static stops
  dead, and its threads draw a moth, the first picture it has ever made. Then every root turns to her.
- **C. Every lamp turns.** Every lampwing lights its coral lamp on her; across the world thousands of coral points
  open; in the Grove one lamp settles on Sorrel.

*Why B (ending on C's 4 s Grove cut):* love and appetite in one picture, at the dish where the gift goes; a shader
state, not a new set; C's last shot carries "through her, every moth" into Night 5.

## 3. The final image (after the Spin ending)

- **A. It presses outward.** The silk-wrapped mountain at dusk; something pushes out against the silk three times,
  amber showing through. Black before it breaks.
- **B. The breath turns warm.** Fox cubs play in the foreground; far behind, the mountain breathes once in the
  prologue's rhythm, amber now, not pale.
- **C. The hand, returned** *(pick)*. The maker, old, lays her palm on the mountain's silk, framed like the
  prologue's blur; from inside, something presses back against it. She doesn't lift her hand.

*Why C:* closes the loop (a touch that left, a touch that stays), gives the maker her gift, and keeps A's press;
the question stays open. B is the quieter fallback.
