## 2. Previous critique's problems

1. Lopsided dilemma; the true ending avoids it: **partly.** Vesper pays, and Reply has a routine. But Carried still keeps everyone except Vesper and breaks the hold, so the question "would you leave someone behind" is never actually answered there.
2. Rule and motive not staged: **fixed.** The cocoon dive, the burning ember and the husk trail all show the rule.
3. Midpoint truth doesn't reach the ending: **fixed.** Each ending shows what happens to the trained gaze.
4. Wick's body, meaningless combat: **fixed.** It is the round's best change.
5. Sallow vanishes: **fixed.** The re-form lantern absence and return is exactly right.
- The Turn's cost: **fixed.**
- Badeline and Kuro: **not fixed, and worse.** The changelog says "The final Reply duel becomes a fight beside Reply". That is Celeste's climax.

## 3. Remaining problems, ranked

1. **Carried wins on every count, so it isn't a choice.** It is "rehearsed in the first hour", it breaks the hold, it ends with "every moth in the Grove lowers its face", and Reply honours her. Home and Stay leave the world held. Players will treat those two as bad endings, as with HK's tiers. Then the central question becomes "would you die for everyone?", and the answer is the genre default. *Fix:* make Carried cost the ones she loves too (see 4).

2. **Celeste.** One of these could be coincidence. Taken together, the delayed-copy footholds, Reply duels I and II, and the final team-up are Badeline. *Fix:* cut both duels. Reply never strikes Vesper. Its only hostile act is the glint theft, which is original and better. At the Mere it doesn't fight. It stands on the shore groove, where Sallow stood, and waits. Its one-beat echo becomes a cost, not a power-up.

3. **The world rules contradict each other.** "Nothing grows old and nothing rots", yet there is an old guardian, a dying beetle, a three-hundred-year-old lampwing and husks. Lanterns are "light that asks nothing", but at the heart "the lanterns answering" are the first true call and answer. *Fix:* pick one. The hold keeps the answered from dying; that is why Sallow and Wick have lasted. The burrback dies because it can no longer answer.

4. **Home has a hole.** If facing the moon makes her "nothing again", why not do it in the Grove at minute three? The shore groove hints that a moth can only turn back at the Mere, in front of its Reply. That is the whole ending, and it is buried in prop 7. *Fix:* stage it. Sallow's groove is where the player first sees that turning back means doing it to Reply's face.

5. **Name soup and a faceless finale.** Asking, Sweep, answer, Reply, the Return, the Turn, the Long Echo: two abilities a syllable apart, both echo-words. The last boss is tissue. *Fix:* rename the Return to a moth word (a "flick", a "beat"). Give the Mere one ambiguous gesture when it falls: it flashes once toward Vesper and waits, exactly as the burrback couldn't. That puts "is the Asking someone?" at the climax.

## 4. The one change

**Make the hold the reason the old still live, so breaking it in Carried ends Sallow and Wick as well.** Sallow answered "long ago" and has lasted three hundred years on the world that answering kept still. In Carried, the forest's thousand voices come in, time starts again, and Sallow folds her wings over Mote and goes still beside Wick's lamp. That repairs the world rule (3) and makes all three endings leave someone behind (1). It also turns the owner's question onto the forest itself: is a world where nothing dies alive? That is a Nine Sols-grade reversal of the ending the game rehearsed.

## 5. Verdict

**Close.** The relationships, mechanics and environmental storytelling are at the bar. The Sallow/Wick reveal at the Eaves would survive any greenlight meeting. These keep it from yes:
- a lead at Moon or Red Candle would flag the Celeste overlap in the first read;
- the true ending is a free win wearing a sacrifice;
- the world's core rule ("nothing grows old") is broken by its own cast.

Fix those three and this is yes.
