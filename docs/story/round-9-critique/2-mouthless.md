# Critique: MOUTHLESS (round 8)

## 1. Scores

- **Relationships the player feels: 7.** Sorrel is now a played arc: he saves her, she saves him, he leaves, he comes back, he pays. Dun has a want and a face, but her turn happens off screen. The woman is a body with a twist.
- **Clarity: 7.** "A mouthless moth feeds the starving lab-baby under the forest, then chooses between being kept and cocooning it" is easy to retell. The spine is not: sentence two still has four clauses.
- **The idea: 8.** Love that lets go of being needed, a giver with no mouth, a hunger that learns her shape. Strong, and only this story has it.
- **World mystery and environmental storytelling: 8.** The handprint, the counted rim, the mossed torch ("She came back."), the root *at* her lips rather than in them, the upside-down moth-stone. This is the best set in any of these drafts.
- **Theme carried by mechanics: 9.** Carrying makes her brighter. She heals only where she has given, grows stronger only through gifts, and loses her scales as time passes. Stay refills her health, and Spin takes her abilities loop by loop. Every system says the theme.
- **Antagonist as a person: 6.** Dun is a person until Night 4, then a companion. Act III's opposition is a hunger and a boss that stands in for Dun ("Dun fights her own future" is a caption, not a fight).
- **Structure and the ending's cost: 8.** The Warm Room into Night 5 is excellent. Spin's cost is played and felt.
- **Originality: 7.** It still echoes these: Shriek (*Will of the Wisps*: an abandoned child becomes the land's hunger); the White Lady, inverted (*Hollow Knight*: a parent held in roots); the old nurse "wax over her eyes, sitting on a hoard" is close to Hive Queen Vespa (*Hollow Knight*: a hive matriarch at the end of the wax halls, and Vesper/Vespa is an awkward rhyme); Spin's ability-stripping climb is *Journey*'s summit ascent.
- **Fit with the owner's four ideas: 8.** It is literal wetware. "Underestimated" is a blind spot rather than a decision, which is defensible. The sentience ambiguity holds right to the last line.
- **Fit with the Grove: 8.** The gold veins re-meant as the hungry thing is a great cheap reversal. The eyed-wing bud, the root-tip and Dun are new render work.
- **Balance: 8.** Ori's fable shape, HK-grade traces, and a Nine Sols reversal ("It has been feeding her") carried by a sibling.

## 2. The previous critique's problems

1. Sorrel as a function: **fixed**. But see problem 3.
2. Spin reads as burial: **fixed**, if anything over-taught (four caterpillar demonstrations).
3. The woman wasted: **partly.** The Stay and Spin uses are excellent, but the fact now contradicts the reset rule (problem 2).
4. No antagonist with a face after Night 2: **partly.** Dun stays on screen, but she converts between scenes, and the old nurse is a proxy.
5. The rules leak: **partly.** The memory law was cut from the text, but the plot still needs it, and it resurfaces as "It doesn't know her. It remembers what she did."

## 3. Remaining problems, ranked

1. **The thesis is invisible until the climax.** Why does the baby fill on Night 7 when the whole world never filled it? The answer appears once, in the Grove notes: "its first meal that wasn't taken." Taken food never fills; given food does. A player has to *see* that rule, or the ending is just "brought enough". *Fix:* show it at a heart early. Dun's hauled fox is drunk and the heart stays clenched. Vesper's glints make it unclench.
2. **The reset contradicts the best fact.** Every newborn "knows no one", yet across 113 lives it "gave only to her". Who kept feeding the woman? Does newborn 114 drink her? The ending's offer to "keep her as it has kept the woman" depends on a memory the doc claims to have cut. *Fix:* make it one image, not a rule. On Night 5, when the newborn floods the world, every cradle-root tightens except the one at the woman's lips, which goes on dripping. Then cut the "remembers" line; the image says it.
3. **Sorrel is forgiven before his leaving hurts.** The cut to the canopy comes straight after "and **goes.**", so the player knows he's coming back before Vesper has spent a minute of play alone. *Fix:* play the lantern scene and Thread alone, and reveal his drop only as Night 6 wakes. Let her forgive him in play: the first time she uses Offer on a person, it is on him.
4. **Dun's turn is not a choice.** She goes from watching her baby turn away (Night 4) to lying at the edge of the light, to leading them (Night 6), and never gives up her want on screen. *Fix:* use the fade mechanic. When Vesper fades on Night 5, Dun picks up her spilled glints, the haul "toward a mouth" that she has always made, and carries them to Vesper's lantern instead. One played beat makes her turn, and "the same walk, the other direction" lands two nights early. Then give the old nurse a fight Dun takes part in, not one she stands for.
5. **Vesper's flaw is asserted, not played.** "Her flaw is that she hides" never costs anyone anything. The Lure is still a faceless allegory. *Fix:* on Night 3, let her hide at first (dim, drop glints) while Sorrel is drawn to the lamp, and lose ground for it, before Moonward. While there, cut the spine's second sentence to a single push and pull.

## 4. The one change that would most raise it

**Make "given fills, taken never does" something the player sees on Night 2, with Dun and Vesper feeding the same heart.** It turns the rivalry into a visible argument, sets up why the ending works, and gives Dun's later turn a reason. The idea also stops living in a design note. It raises clarity, the antagonist score and the idea at once.

## 5. Verdict

**Close: the nearest this storyline has come.** A narrative lead would greenlight the world, the Warm Room, Night 5 and the played ending. They would send it back for three things before production:
- the central logic hole (a mind that resets but has kept one person for 113 lives);
- the climax's rule (given food fills) never being shown before it pays off;
- the two key changes of heart (Dun's, and Vesper's forgiveness of Sorrel) happening between scenes instead of in play.

None of them needs a new idea. Each needs one scene.
