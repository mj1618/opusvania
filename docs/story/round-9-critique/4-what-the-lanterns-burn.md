# Round 9 critique: WHAT THE LANTERNS BURN (round 8)

## 1. Scores

- **Relationships the player feels: 8.** The re-form wing is now played "a hundred times", and Sedge stepping out young "with the Taper's wing on *his* cheek" is the best beat in the set. Tallow's Act II companionship is only described ("a wing's length off, then less"). It has no form in play.
- **Clarity: 7.** The spine retells. The ending does not: why the ember "never burned" in lantern 2 but "finally burns" at the Root, and what "its cupped hands of light open over the Taper and close" does, are both unexplained.
- **The idea: 8.** "The people who loved the dead most planted it" still lands. The mentor living on burned grief is a real moral weight.
- **World mystery and environmental storytelling: 7.** The same-face scratches, the cocoon silk and the pressed-moss wing are good. But there are only five finds, and nothing tells the player that people, or neurons, ever existed (problem 3).
- **Theme carried by mechanics: 9.** The shared glass, the Flares that cost the Root a glint, "every time the *player* falls, the same glass drops", and Moonward as the underestimate paid off.
- **Antagonist as a person: 8.** "Her reed stops a breath short of the glass", "doesn't look surprised", a boss whose "only attack is the wing", and the wing that "lifts... and stops". Her own reason ("believes they are echoes") is stated, never seen.
- **Structure and the ending's cost: 7.** Planted costs her, Sedge's life and every sanctuary. Kindled is rigged (problem 1).
- **Originality: 7.** Moths serving a light is still Hollow Knight's Moth Tribe and the Radiance. A mentor fed on burned people is Nine Sols' Solarians and the Apeman. "Monsters" that turn out to be the forest's nursemaids is Nausicaä's Ohmu. The Kuro image and the mirror fight are gone.
- **Fit with the owner's four ideas: 8.** "An empty moth is nothing" and flying straight past "where the pull should take her" make the underestimate playable. Sentience stays open.
- **Fit with the existing Grove: 7.** The ember is back and the day-one sprout sets up the Hush. But the changes defang the scene (problem 5).
- **Balance: 8.** Close to Nine Sols' mix. The old forest is still a line of text, not a presence.

## 2. Round 7's problems

- **The bond the climax spends was the least played: fixed.** The Taper is the Act I companion and the re-form is the relationship.
- **Changes of mind off screen: mostly fixed.** Clasp's Brace scene *is* her forgiveness. Sedge "turns round", but nothing shows why: his last beat was "He doesn't look at Vesper."
- **Ember and old forest dropped: partly fixed.** The ember is Tallow's charge. The old forest acts only offscreen ("the heart closes and sleeps").
- **Rules break at key moments: fixed,** with one new break: the ember gutters in lantern 2 and "never runs down".
- **The Taper's gesture makes the choice: fixed.** "Vesper takes the wing... and turns herself toward the earth" is hers. But see problem 1.

## 3. Remaining problems, ranked

**1. The choice is rigged, and its stakes are misstated.** The question is "burn a stranger", but Kindled withers "the valley's Bequest", which means every glint, and leaves the valley "safe and empty of anyone". Then the last shot editorialises: "the Taper's wing turns her face back". No player will pick it except to see it, so the moral weight goes nowhere. *Fix:* Kindled costs exactly one stranger and keeps the world as it was: the Taper lives, the lanterns burn on, the Bequest is drunk slowly as before. Show Planted's cost in a shot the player hates, such as grubs in the dark with no lantern. End both endings on an image with no verdict in it.

**2. The stranger has no face.** "The one nearly-whole glint" gets "two cupped hands" and nothing else. You can't weigh someone against a mentor if they're nobody in particular. *Fix:* see section 4.

**3. The owner's core idea is invisible in play.** The grief-bulbs, the vials of blood and the people exist only in section 2. The Root gives the player "rows of half-buried glass bulbs" and nothing to say what was in them. *Fix:* use hands. No creature in this world has them. Every glint's looping gesture is a human hand gesture: waving, cupping, smoothing hair. At the Root, put one cast of a hand pressed into the glass. Also cut the careless "glints in both hands" and "in her own hands" from the moths.

**4. Rule load.** Weeklong lives, re-forming young, pilgrims only, glide pull, empty-moth invisibility, Flare, Updraft, Plant, Brace, Moonward, sprouting, and "grubs... feed on pods and burrbacks crush them". *Fix:* cut Updraft and the grub-predation clause. Show the week in the Grove's prologue crowd (greying moths) instead of stating it.

**5. The Grove loses its threat and gives its ending away.** If creatures ignore an empty Vesper and lampwing dives "don't wound", the opening minutes have no danger. "The camera shows what Vesper can't see" is an omniscient tell, the same flaw as Noise's cutaways. *Fix:* she starts the scene carrying the prologue glint, so keepers react from the first minute. A snatch still knocks her down and costs her. Keep Tallow in frame at the wake, small and unremarked, with no cut to it.

## 4. The one change that would most raise it

**Make the ember the face on the Taper's wall.** It is the one person she loved and could never burn, and that is why it "never burned" in lantern 2: her reed stopped short every time. This one change solves several problems. It explains the ember rule. It gives the stranger a face the player has already found scratched a hundred times. It gives the Taper one seen moment of doubt, her own devotion and her own refusal to look past it. And it turns the final choice into this: when she turns Vesper to the glass, she is asking her to burn the one person the Taper herself never could.

## 5. Verdict

**Close.** It is still the best mechanics-as-meaning outline in the set, and every round-7 fix landed. Four things separate it from yes. The ending choice is rigged and misstates its stakes. The stranger at its centre is faceless. The human and wetware origin can't be found in play. And the Grove changes remove the scene's danger. Fix 1 and 2 and a narrative lead would greenlight it.
