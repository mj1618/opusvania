# Round 9 critique: NOISE (round 8)

## 1. Scores

- **Relationships the player feels: 8.** Hob is at the bar ("Hob steps out of his circle to put his shell over her"). Sallow now has a real arc in three beats. The Lamplighter is still a function, and Maud and Vesper share two scenes in which Maud never sees her.
- **Clarity: 7.** The spine retells. But the ember's timeline breaks (see problem 3), and the final choice doesn't map cleanly onto "keep safe or let go".
- **The idea: 8.** "Let nothing happen" on tape, a town that asked for it, and a clock that became the world's pulse: strong, grounded, sad.
- **World mystery and environmental storytelling: 6.** The props are excellent (the air holes, eleven coats, the worn key). But two omniscient cuts give away the mystery in Act I (problem 2).
- **Theme carried by mechanics: 8.** Lanterns that cost a glint after Sallow dies; walking the circle under a narrowing beam; "keeping time until it commits, then breaking once". The repetition rule is still a risk inside combos.
- **Antagonist as a person: 7.** She acts now. "She never opens her eyes" is a good, ambiguous kind of guilt. She is still met mostly through cutaways.
- **Structure and the ending's cost: 6.** The cost is on screen now, but the choice is lopsided, and it is cut off from Vesper's arc (problem 1).
- **Originality: 6.** "A table set for two, a cold hearth" with a clock at the centre is Miss Havisham. The grieving-parent antagonist is Ori's own template (Kuro). A mentor drawing the hunter away from what she guards is a stock sacrifice. The choice between a killing ending and a reconciling one at the source's heart echoes Hollow Knight's Radiance endings and Nine Sols' final choice.
- **Fit with the owner's four ideas: 8.** All four are there. "Underestimated" turning into "couldn't bear to look" is the best version yet.
- **Fit with the existing Grove: 7.** The ember, carving, heart and lanterns map well. The lampwing changes from dive attacks to strikes on repetition, which is a big rework, and the scene needs Hob, Sallow and a swell system.
- **Balance: 8.** A Hollow Knight-style world in places, Ori-style companionship, and a Nine Sols-style cost. Hob carries the feeling. The world mystery is now the weak leg.

## 2. Round 7's problems

- **Maud never acts: partly fixed.** She winds the clock, and Sallow dies of it. But it is shown in a cutaway, so the player is told rather than finding out, and Vesper never learns it.
- **Sallow's death is borrowed and has no consequence: fixed.** "The moth who taught 'never the same path twice' flies the same path again and again." It costs a glint at every rest. The decoy itself is still a stock beat.
- **The law of sight contradicts itself: fixed.** Lamps flinch from moths and strike repetition. There is a small wobble: in the Grove the lamp "holds for a long beat", while elsewhere lamps "flinch".
- **Too many rules: partly fixed.** There are still the swell, the flicker, repetition in the beam, Keeping Time, pods, swarms, stones that teach moves, lantern upkeep, and lamps that follow Maud's eyes.
- **Template Hob arc and hidden ending cost: mostly fixed.** The reunion is now Vesper's own act, and the moths scatter on screen. The binary ending is still a well-worn pattern.

## 3. The remaining problems, ranked

**1. The choice doesn't test Vesper, and one side wins easily.** Her need is "to let others choose". Both options are her forcing something: "Make her look" says so in its name, and "Cut the root" kills a woman. The arc's payoff, Hob leaving, happens "Either way", so the choice is cut off from who she is. And the scales are uneven. "Look" saves Maud, frees the weather and keeps the world safe. Its price, moths being watched, is never made concrete. "Cut" kills a grandmother and brings "sixty years of storm, fire and flood". Players will pick "Look" and feel little.
*Fix:* make the choice the final test of her need. One option is Vesper forcing an outcome, the other is her offering and letting Maud decide. Make the cost of "Look" concrete in the game's own rule: every moth lives inside a beam from now on, so every repeated motion is death. Play that in the epilogue. Give "Cut" something the player wants, such as Hob's freedom or the Lamplighter freed.

**2. Omniscient cutaways spend the mystery.** The Grove's ending cut shows "every lamp in the valley turns with her". That is the Levee's revelation, given away in chapter one. The Act I kitchen cut shows the Bellhouse, the clock and Maud's deed before the player has earned any of them. Hollow Knight never cuts to the Pale King. Nine Sols withholds the Apeman.
*Fix:* cut both cutaways. Show only the great lamp leaving the dam, seen from far away. Let the player piece the deed together at the Bellhouse: a clock key still warm, a new notch in the brass, and the drawing turned face down on the night Sallow died.

**3. The ember's timeline is broken.** In the Grove, Vesper takes the ember to light the lanterns. Later the Lamplighter goes for "the hidden coal" on the island, and only after that does Vesper carry "a coal of the ember".
*Fix:* say it in one line. Vesper takes a spark, and Sallow keeps the mother-coal on the island.

**4. The midpoint has no reversal left.** The prologue shows the grieving mother, and the cuts show her power. Lowmere adds "the town asked", but only to a player who reads the dishes. Nine Sols' midpoint turns the revenge on its head. This midpoint confirms what the player already knows.
*Fix:* hold something back for Lowmere. For example, Sallow is one of the moths from the jar lamp, or the moths' hiding began as Maud's wish too.

**5. The Lamplighter's guilt is asserted, not shown.** "Its lamp drops" on seeing the coal is convenient.
*Fix:* plant it earlier. After Sallow's death, it circles the ember island the way Sallow circled, night after night.

## 4. The one change that would most raise it

Rebuild the final choice around Vesper's need. One path is forcing, the other is letting Maud choose. Make the moths' cost concrete in the beam rule, and let Hob's leaving depend on what Vesper became rather than happening either way.

## 5. Verdict

**Close.** This is nearer than round 6. Hob, Sallow and the world are at the bar. A narrative lead would still send back two things. The ending asks a moral question that its own protagonist's arc doesn't answer, and one option outweighs the other. And the world mystery is given away by cutaways in the first act. Fix the choice, cut the cutaways and mend the ember, and this is a yes.
