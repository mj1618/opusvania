# Mouthless: a cold read (Ori fan, plays for the heart)

## 1. The story in three sentences
A root-thing called the Hush has put the whole world to sleep in grey pods so no one goes hungry, and it can't see moths because moths don't eat. Umber, a moth with a week to live, warms it with the forest's light, and it finally sees her and wants to keep her too. She can let it keep her, burn it (killing most sleepers too), or spend her life spinning it into a cocoon so it lets everyone go.

## 2. Characters
- **Umber:** a cold-born moth who wants to save her wood, then everyone.
- **Sorrel:** her brother, who wants to dance with his partner, then to stay with Umber.
- **The Hush:** an abandoned brain grown in a dish. It wants to keep everyone fed and close, because goodbyes hurt it.
- **Dun:** the Hush's badger servant, which guards Umber once she warms it.
- **Tallow:** the last man awake. He wants to burn the pods and find his sister.
- **The forest:** an old, slow mind that wants to give its warmth away.

## 3. The enemy
A lab-grown brain that swallowed cities, stopped all births and turned animals into servants. It wants nobody to leave, so its pain (the static) stops.

## 4. Endings
- **Spin:** Umber and Sorrel die circling the dish, the Hush becomes a mouthless cocoon, and the world wakes up hungry and twenty years older.
- **Stay:** everyone is kept warm forever, and nothing is ever born again.
- **Burn:** the Hush dies and takes nearly every sleeper with it. Umber gets one dance at dawn.

## 5. Confusions
- The sister is "old now" even though sleepers don't age. I had to work out that she went into her case already grey.
- What does the Hush actually feed people? It started out powering greenhouses, so where does the food come from?
- Tallow burns pods for twenty years, then calmly walks into one. The change felt too sudden.
- "Grown beside it. Only one lived." I didn't know what to do with that line.
- On Night 6 the forest sends "everything it has left", but on Night 7 Umber still has "the forest's last glints".
- Spinning the cocoon only made sense once I remembered the caterpillar.

## 6. What I'd remember
Sorrel wrapped round his partner's pod with the root closing over him, and then he shivers, still alive. Also the final wingtip tap, which closes the loop on the very first scene. **Would I buy and finish it? Yes.** The siblings, the badger and the sister's hand on the silk got to me, though the middle has a lot to track.
