# Mouthless: casual player retell

## 1. The story in three sentences
A lab-grown brain called the Hush has rooted over the world and put everyone to sleep in fed pods, but it ignores moths because moths don't eat. Umber, a moth with a week to live, warms it, and now it sees moths and wants to keep them too. Inside its mountain she chooses: be kept forever, burn it (killing most sleepers), or spend her life spinning it a cocoon so everyone wakes.

## 2. Characters
- **Umber**: little moth, a week to live; wants to save her wood, then everyone.
- **Sorrel**: her brother; wants to dance with his partner, then get more time with Umber.
- **The Hush**: lonely lab-grown brain; wants to keep everyone close because people leaving hurts it.
- **Dun**: root-armoured badger servant; at first fetches things for the Hush, then switches sides and guards Umber.
- **Tallow**: last man awake; wants to burn the pods and find his sister.
- **The forest**: an old slow mind that gives its warmth away.

## 3. The enemy
A brain grown in a dish to run the food greenhouses. When the lab closed and everyone left, every leaving hurt it. So it rooted over the world and put everyone in pods: fed and not ageing, but getting weaker, with nobody born. It wants everyone kept close so the hurt (the static) stops.

## 4. Endings
- **Spin (true):** Umber and Sorrel spend their lives circling it into a cocoon; people wake into a world twenty years older, and the moths die.
- **Stay:** Umber lies down in a pod; everyone is warm and safe forever, but nothing is ever born again.
- **Burn:** she drops the coal in; the Hush dies and takes almost every sleeper with it, and Umber gets one last dance.

## 5. Confusing bits
- "It does not age" vs "Tallow's sister, old now": it only clicked at the memory (she went in grey).
- Pods are "blind grey stone" to Tallow, yet later he sees a face; I re-read to find "worn thin as a leaf".
- "The carving empties": meaning unclear until the epilogue (forest used up?).
- When the Hush "comes after her", is it attacking or loving? Lampwings lift moths "gently"; I couldn't tell whether to fear it.
- In Spin, what's in the cocoon? A new Hush? A moth? Too vague for me.

## 6. What I'd remember
Sorrel curled in a dark knot round his partner's tiny pod as the lantern dies, then shivering: alive. Gutting.

Would I buy and finish it? **Yes.** A doomed moth against a lonely brain that just wants the hurt to stop is a hook I'd pass on, and "give warmth, don't fight" sounds fresh. The middle is crowded, though.
