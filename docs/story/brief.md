# Opusvania storyline brief (shared by every story agent)

## The anchor (owner, non-negotiable; overrides anything below that conflicts)
The owner's own words, after five drafts drifted: "it completely deviated from my original idea of biological AI that has
taken over the world."
- A biological / organic AI (grown from living neurons: the Cortical Labs lineage, scaled up and out of control) HAS TAKEN
  OVER THE WORLD. This is the central fact of the setting and the main antagonist force of the story.
- It is ON SCREEN and ACTIVE throughout: its living tissue grown over cities and forests, its works and its servants
  (creatures it has taken, re-grown or made), its reach still spreading during the game. A threat with momentum, not a
  backdrop, not a metaphor, not one small sad creature, not a local problem.
- The takeover is concrete: what it did to humans, cities, animals, the forest; how it spreads; what it is doing now; what
  it wants (an understandable goal, in the Ori/HK/Nine Sols tradition of antagonists who are wrong in an illuminating way).
- It deemed moths insignificant and left them alone; that underestimation is how Umber gets in, and it matters in play.
- The sentience question is about IT (and the forest and animals): is the thing that took the world a someone?
- Umber's goal is to save the world from it; the ending must resolve the takeover in some form, with a real cost.
LITMUS (relaxed by the owner, 2026-10-14): a player's first sentence about the game names a little moth against a
living thing that swallowed the world (they need not say "AI"). The truth that it is a mind grown from living brain
cells must be central to the story and discoverable in play (ruins, the lab, objects), ideally as a revelation, in the
Hollow Knight way: mythic on the surface, technical underneath. Moths and creatures may call it by a mythic name; no
player-facing text uses technical language.

## The owner's original ideas (2026-10-14)
- Organic / biological AI has gotten out of control and taken over the world. Reference: Cortical Labs (a real company
  growing human neurons on silicon chips; "DishBrain" learned to play Pong in 2022; the CL1 "biological computer" went on
  sale in 2025). "This kind of tech has grown out of control."
- Umber is a moth, the main character, trying to save the world.
- Maybe the AIs decided not to bother subjugating moths, deeming them insignificant and no threat, but they underestimated
  Umber.
- Sentience is a core concept: the forest has sentience, so do all the animals; maybe the AI has sentience or maybe it
  doesn't. That ambiguity "could be a cool concept".

## Owner direction on relationships and balance
- "there needs to be more inter-personal relationships in the stories, they're all very robotic, we want a story that really
  makes the players think because its interesting, but also feel because they're connected to the characters and their
  goals. it can't be too complicated either because people want a story they can understand, they understand the pushes
  and pulls of it."
- Then: "don't just rebuild around people, try and go based on what Ori/HK/NineSols have done, there needs to be a
  balance, I was just calling out the lack of relationships in the current stories."
So: a small cast (Umber + 3–5) whose simple wants pull on her in different directions, relationships shown through
actions and companionship in play (the game has little or no dialogue), a spine retellable in three plain sentences, one
clear central question, few names, simple rules; AND keep the strong idea, world mystery, environmental storytelling and
mechanics-as-theme. Model the balance the references strike:
- Ori: a mythic world and a simple fable carried by a few relationships (Ori and Naru; Kuro and her egg; Ku).
- Hollow Knight: mostly world, mystery and lore found in places, with a handful of characters who matter (Hornet, Quirrel,
  the Hollow Knight, the Pale King and White Lady) and quiet relationships at the core.
- Nine Sols: a strong idea and moral reversal (the Apeman) carried through very clear relationships (Yi and Shuanshuan,
  Yi and Kuafu, Lady Ethereal), with understandable motives on every side.

## What already exists in the game (the story must fit it, or say what would change)
One scene so far, "the Grove": an old-growth forest at blue hour, three zones (moonlit blue left, amber light-well
centre, violet right). Drawn in code, painterly, dark and subtle with small precious lights.
- Umber: a small dark plum moth with a pale lilac face, amber glowing eyes, plume antennae, swallow-tail cape/wings, no
  mouth; serious, quiet, "an aura like Ori", not a jester, cute simple face, effortless movement. Abilities: run,
  double-jump somersault, dash, wall-kick, glide, thorn-blade slash (combo, up, pogo).
- Goal of the scene: three moth-lanterns she kindles with "glints" (small lights gathered from the air and from "lumen
  pods"). The summit lantern needs an EMBER, a living coal fetched from a high island. Lit lanterns are rest points and
  sanctuaries; she re-forms at lights when she fades. Four smaller wayside lamps.
- The heart: a sleeping bud at a shrine with a moth carved into a standing stone. When all three lanterns burn the heart
  wakes (veins of light run through the ground to the lanterns, the carving fills, a ring of light crosses the grove); she
  offers leftover glints and more of the grove wakes (flowers, lit veins, canopy lights).
- Creatures: burrbacks (armoured beetle-like ground creatures that brace, ram, curl, and are calmed by lanterns) and
  lampwings (dark moth-like fliers with a coral lamp on a long tail that tell, dive, hang, climb). Ambient fireflies,
  moths, glow plants; guide ember-moths that lead to the next dark lantern.
Names can be kept or re-meant. The game is a metroidvania; this scene is one early area of a larger world.

## The bar
A mythic, simple surface over a deep, specific world; an antagonist who is understandable and wrong in a way that
illuminates the hero; theme carried by mechanics and places, not exposition; restraint (little dialogue, much implied);
a central relationship, a sacrifice, a reversal that recontextualises the journey; endings that cost something;
specific, original images and names; no generic "evil AI destroys nature" cliché, no techno-babble or lore dump, nothing
derivative beat-for-beat of Ori, Hollow Knight, Nine Sols, Celeste or Dark Souls.
