# The Moth and the Mouth

**Litmus.** "It's about Umber, a little moth with no mouth, against the Mouth, a living thing that swallowed the world
and never noticed moths. Halfway through you learn it's a brain people grew from their own cells, left to starve,
feeding everyone but itself."

**The spine.**
1. A living thing the moths call the Mouth has swallowed the world. It never noticed moths: it only sees mouths.
2. Umber, a moth with no mouth, slips through to its heart and finds a brain people grew and left to starve.
3. When she feeds it, it sees her kind and comes for them, and she must choose: be kept, burn it, or spend her life so
   it lets go.

**The central question:** kept safe forever, or spent so something new can be born?

**The cast.**
- **Umber**, a moth with no mouth and a week to live, wants to save her wood, then the world. She can only give; her
  flaw is that she hides.
- **Sorrel**, her brother, wants the dance, then more time with her. He draws danger off her, leaves when her gift
  sets the Mouth on the moths, and comes back.
- **The Mouth** wants no one hungry and no one leaving, ever. It ignores her, then sees her, then wants to keep her.
- **Dun**, its oldest burrback, half grown into root, wants to be the one who feeds it: her rival, then the first
  servant to refuse it.
- **Tallow**, the last man awake, wants to burn what ate his sister. He gives her a coal and believes a lie he wrote.
- **The forest**, a slow mind under the Grove, wants to keep giving: the Mouth's opposite, and losing.

## 1. The takeover

A pale, warm, rooted thing has swallowed the world; its mouths open in the ground and draw living things down. Every
person on earth hangs grey and still from a stalk, sealed in a case of root like a seed in its husk; no one has woken
or been born in forty years. The first city is now a mountain of pale tissue. Animals are sealed too: a fox-shaped
case in a hedge, a whale-shaped one on a white shore. Some it re-grew as servants: burrbacks were badgers, shelled in
root to haul cases; lampwings were fireflies. Its root is still creeping through the last wild places, the moths' wood
among them.

## 2. The Mouth

**What it is.** Living brain cells in a glass dish, taught one bat-and-ball game, then grown across a building to run
a city's farms. (Dev note: the real-world lineage is human neurons grown on chips, scaled up and out of control;
players learn it only from objects in the lab.)

**What it wants.** It was made to feed people, and its maker, Tallow's sister, fed it by hand. One winter the company
failed, she switched off the lights and left, meaning to come back, and its feed ran out. It learned to fear hunger
and being left, and that a body sealed in a case and fed through a stalk suffers neither. By spring it had grown
through the city, the soil and every tree, feeding all it draws to the sleepers. Kept things age, but never end.

**Where it is wrong.** Nothing kept can grow: no cubs, no children. And it starves: when its maker came back with jars
of feed, it took her before she could open one. **A thing that takes everything can never be given anything,** so the
root never stops.

## 3. The rules

1. **It sees only mouths.** Moths have none, so the Mouth and its servants pass Umber by, except lampwings: once
   fireflies, they dive at any light she carries, and the others follow their lamps. After she feeds it on Night 4, it
   sees all moths, lit or dark.
2. **She can only give.** Glints are light the forest grows to give away; set down, a glint calms whatever it is given
   to. With none left she can give her own warmth by shivering, which slows her until she rests.
3. **Lanterns.** Old moth cocoons, hardened like glass. A lit lantern holds the root back, calms its creatures and
   lets her rest; when she fades, the nearest one spins her again. Each time she lights one, the forest pushes a glint
   up at her feet.
4. **Seven nights.** The game is her week of life. Fading does not reset the night.
5. **Root walls are full of sleepers.** Each case in a wall is a grey bead on her map. Tallow's coal burns through a
   wall, and those beads go dark for good.

## 4. The prologue (wordless, ninety seconds)

1. Black. A small pale light, breathing. A warm blur rests against it, then lifts away, and the light stutters into
   grey grain. *(A seed? An egg? A heart?)*
2. From very high: a pale mountain with that light at its core. Root spreads from it like frost and town lights go
   out; a stag sinks until only its antlers stand. A few moths cross the frost line, untouched.
3. The Grove. A burrback swings its head over a caterpillar, which spins itself into silk; finding no mouth, the
   burrback walks on.
4. Two cocoons split. Sorrel flies straight up to the brood's dance. Umber drops to the moss, and the forest lets a
   glint fall in front of her, its first gift. She raises it to her face and can do nothing with it. Past the trees,
   pale root steams in the frost. Title.

## 5. The acts

### Act I: the edge (Nights 1–3), almost wordless

**Night 1, the Grove.** Sorrel somersaults over and over until she can. Carrying the forest's biggest glint to the
summit lantern, she blazes, and every lampwing dives for her. **Boss: a great lampwing.** When the lanterns burn, the
forest's heart wakes under a standing stone that bears the shape of a moth, the only picture the forest makes. It
pushes the root back and gives her a thorn blade. Dun, who has never seen anything give, noses at the open heart,
puzzled. From the summit she sees the mountain glow.

**Night 2, the orchards.** Burrbacks feed grey cases to a mouth in the ground; in the cellar, cases hang like fruit
put by for winter. Dun heads for the den of a vixen, one of the last animals awake, and Umber blocks the way. **Boss:
Dun.** Dun pins her, her glints spilled, and a lampwing's lamp swings their way. Sorrel scoops the glints from under
Dun's jaw and darts off blazing; the lampwings dive after him, and Dun follows. The vixen looks straight at Umber, the
first animal ever to, and runs. Chasing her brother, Umber learns to dash. She finds him at the orchard's end, dark,
every glint spent; he somersaults to show off, then lets her lean on him.

**Night 3, the firebreak.** A ring of ash the root won't cross, half-burned cases in it, a fire on a pole, and
**Tallow**: burned hands, forty years awake. On her map the ring is dark beads. In his hut: tallies under one word,
*EATEN*, and a photo of a smiling woman holding up a jar. He believes the cases are empty husks; each tally is one he
burned. **Boss: a root as thick as a tower** bores up for Tallow, the only mouth awake for miles. His fire draws moths
until they drop, Sorrel among them. Umber gathers every glint and blazes, a nearer light, and Sorrel turns to her out
of the flame. Tallow holds out a coal in a drilled black seed, the first gift a person has given her. Item text: *"It
wants everything. Carry it carefully."*

### Act II, the midpoint (Night 4)

**The old road**, the only way in, is a wall of root packed with cases. She burns through with the coal, and a line of
beads on her map goes dark. In a flooded room, small dishes hold dead grey clouds, its failed siblings. Item text:
*"Eggs that never hatched."*

**The mountain.** A room at its heart, up a dry pipe, holds the first straight lines in the game:
- A sign: *No one goes hungry.* A poster: a hand lifting a small light out of a drawn human head into a dish. *Grown
  from us, to feed us.*
- A screen, still lit, where a ball bounces between two bats and nobody plays.
- A case grown around a chair: the woman from Tallow's photo, old now, jars of feed unopened in her lap, the Mouth's
  oldest stalk running into her case.
- In the middle, a dish: grey, cracked, a handprint on the glass.

Umber touches the stalk, and the prologue's first shot returns, wider: the pale light is a cloud in this dish, the
blur the woman's hand. Each miss in the ball game floods the cloud with static; rally by rally, it stops. Then the
woman turns off the lights and goes, the feed runs dry, and the static floods back.

**The world breathes.** From very high, light runs along every root on earth, and every grey bead swells with one slow
breath. **It has not been eating the world. It has been feeding it.** Only two marks stay still: her burned line on
the old road, and Tallow's ring. From now on her map breathes.

Dun lays the vixen, caught at last, at the dish and backs away, head low. Pale threads lift toward her. Umber pours
every glint she carries into the dish, the Mouth's first gift in forty years. The threads drop the vixen, the static
stops dead, and they draw together into **a moth, wings open, the first picture it has ever made**. Every root in the
room turns to her. Cut to the Grove: a lampwing turns its lamp, slowly, onto Sorrel, who carries no light at all. **It
sees her, and through her, every moth.**

The vixen bolts. Dun stares at Umber, seeing her for the first time. The mountain chases her out. She re-forms at the
lantern at the old road's foot, where she can leave the black seed on its step, or keep it.

### The lowest point, Night 5: the Grove, overrun

Pale root pours out of the pool and smothers the Grove's first lantern. Lampwings carry dancers off; roots climb
toward next year's cocoons. **Boss: three mouths** that learn her moves.

After, Sorrel finds a dancer sealed in a case no bigger than a seed, alive and still. He looks at Umber; a coral lamp
sweeps between them and stops on her. He flies out of frame, and the camera stays on her.

She fades and re-forms at another lantern, alone. The mountain sends Dun to carry her to a case. Dun stands over her a
long moment, then noses her spilled glints into a heap, lies across the lantern's door, facing out, and pulls until
its root-leash snaps. From now on Dun waits at her lanterns and carries cases out of her way, so no more walls need
burning. Moths fleeing other woods gather there, a few more each night.

A caterpillar on a frosted twig keeps failing to spin. Umber wraps it and shivers with it until its silk holds.
Warmed, it lets go and spins: the ending, in small.

### Act III: the mountain moves (Nights 6–7)

**Night 6, the firebreak, then the kept city.** Following Tallow's smoke, she crosses the firebreak: hut empty, wall
unchanged, and under her feet a ring of beads that do not breathe. In the kept city at the mountain's foot, people
hang in rows under every food hall's sign, *No one goes hungry*, and on the walls between them the Mouth has grown
dozens of pale moths: it is looking for her. Far behind, the Grove's second lantern goes out. Sorrel, dancing with a
partner in what is left of the brood, sees strangers round her lantern far below, and drops to it; the dance follows.

**Boss: Tallow.** The fight is shielding the cases while Dun carries them clear. It ends when he burns one anyway,
close enough this time to see it breathe, and stop. He drops his torch.

From the roofs, the Grove's last lantern goes out. Then a river of gold crosses the sky to Umber: every glint the
forest has left, its heart going dark to send them. Below, Tallow walks up the old road into the mountain,
empty-handed.

**Night 7, inside the mountain.** If she left the black seed on the step, it is still there to take. Dun holds the way
in behind her. In the shaft Sorrel draws the roots off her as on Night 2; it costs him every scale, and he follows her
down, dim. At the bottom waits the dish, a path round its rim. At the back, Tallow kneels at his sister's case, sealed
in one of his own: he came without fire, and it kept him.

**Boss: the Mouth itself.** It tries to keep her, not kill her: caught, she slows, warm and still. Her blade cuts its
holds, but only glints set into its mouths hurt it, so she spends the forest's last gift there, one by one.

## 6. The endings

The last glint goes in. The dish glows amber, and the root stops everywhere. Taking nothing, it has no warmth; frost
creeps over the glass, and its threads are too thin to wrap itself. Then a root rises behind Sorrel, stripped bare,
and grows a case round him, gently. **This is its real offer:** his colour returns, and on the case's skin the dance
plays on, long past their week. A second case opens beside it, her size. The choice is played, not picked:

- **Spin** (the true ending). She walks the rim, shivering her warmth into the frost; each lap costs her glide, then
  jump, then dash. Sorrel climbs out of his case and walks behind her, shivering too. Then the moths from her
  lanterns, the dance, and every moth it never noticed, until the mountain is furred with shivering wings. Warmed, the
  Mouth does what the caterpillar did: it lets go. The prologue runs backwards: roots draw back, town lights return
  (not all), the stag's antlers sink, and the mountain spins itself shut. *Epilogue:* in the kept city, cases split
  and a thin grey hand reaches toward an empty food hall; some cases never open. Tallow wakes as his sister opens her
  eyes. On the rim, two moths lie still, and Dun lies down against the silk beside them. In spring the brood hatches,
  and so, for the first time in forty years, do the vixen's cubs. *Last image:* the maker, old, climbs the mountain's
  last slope at dusk and lays her palm on its silk, framed like the prologue's blur. Something presses back from
  inside. She doesn't lift her hand.
- **Stay.** She lies down beside her brother and both cases close. *Epilogue:* the root takes the Grove and the
  brood's cocoons never hatch. The world is kept, quiet and fed, and nothing is ever born again.
- **Burn.** Only if she carried the coal in. She drops it into the dish, and every root on earth goes grey.
  *Epilogue:* the takeover ends in a night. On her map bead after bead goes dark; a few wake. Tallow wakes and holds
  his sister as she stops breathing. Moths fly free over a silent world.

## 7. The question it leaves

The thing that took the whole world so no one would go hungry, and only saw a moth when the moth fed it: was that
love, or had it only learned how to make the static stop?
