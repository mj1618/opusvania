# Mouthless: casual player retell

## 1. The story in three sentences
A grey living thing called the Hush has sealed the whole world into cases where people sleep and get fed forever, but it ignores moths because they have no mouths. Umber, a shy moth with a week to live, fights by giving warmth, and learns the Hush is an abandoned lab-grown brain that "feeds" everyone so nobody can leave it. Warming it makes it see moths and want them too, so she must choose: be kept, burn it, or spend her life putting it to sleep.

## 2. Characters
- **Umber**: shy moth; wants to dance once with her brother, then save everyone.
- **Sorrel**: her brother; wants to dance with his partner and spend time with Umber.
- **The Hush**: the grown brain; wants nobody to ever leave it again.
- **Dun**: a badger-servant of the Hush; ends up guarding Umber instead.
- **Tallow**: the last awake human; wants to burn the cases and find his sister.
- **The forest**: a quiet good mind; wants to give its warmth away.

## 3. The enemy
A brain grown in a dish that ran the food supply. Everyone walked out, so its roots sealed people in cases to feed them and never be left again. Nobody is born or wakes. It wants everyone kept close so the hurt (the static) stops.

## 4. Endings
- **Spin (true):** the moths cocoon the Hush; people wake hungry, Umber and Sorrel die, something new stirs inside.
- **Stay:** Umber gets sealed in with Sorrel; everyone is safe and fed, and nothing is ever born again.
- **Burn:** she burns it; almost every sleeper dies with it, and Umber gets her one dance.

## 5. What confused me
- The sister walks back in with a torch, then pinches it out. Did she come to kill it? I couldn't tell.
- Sorrel is "bare of every scale" on Night 7. When did that happen? I think it's the lampwing glow, but it's never said.
- Tallow just lies down in a case next to his sister. Sudden, after 20 years of burning them.
- In Burn, "only those taken days ago wake", but the last line says "nothing else wakes". That read as a contradiction to me.
- I re-read the lab memory to get that "static" means "it got hurt or left".

## 6. What I'd remember, and would I buy it
The moth lifting the glint to her face like the vole with its seed, realising she has no mouth, and holding it out instead. Yes, I'd buy and finish it: a sad Ori-style story with a clear hook (you can't eat, so you give), and I'd want to see Spin. The endings just need to be clearer about who wakes.
