# 4. What the Lanterns Burn

## 1. Spine

**Logline:** A young moth lights lanterns beside the mentor who catches her every time she falls, and
learns what is burning inside them.

**The spine, as a player would tell a friend:**
1. Vesper is a young moth on a pilgrimage with her mentor, the Taper, lighting the lanterns that keep
   moths safe in a forest full of monsters.
2. She finds out that the little lights the lanterns burn are what is left of people, that the monsters
   were trying to save them, and that the Taper has always known.
3. In the end there is one light left. It could bring back the Taper, or spare the one person the Taper
   loved and never could burn.

**The central question:** *Would you burn one life to keep the one you love?*

## 2. The world before, and how it was taken

Late in humanity's dwindling, the dying gave a vial of blood. Their cells were grown into neurons in amber
glass bulbs, and grieving families taught each bulb to be the one it came from. When nobody was
left to tend the bulbs, the last people grafted them into the old forests so that the trees would feed
them. Each bulb had been taught one wish, *don't let me go*, so it grew: pale pods on every branch, each
holding a **glint**, a small light that dreams one moment over and over. The forest became the
**Bequest**, and the old forest lay buried alive beneath it. Nobody decided to take the world. The people
who loved the dead most planted it.

**How a player finds this: hands.** Every glint's flicker is a gesture made by a large, soft,
five-fingered hand, bigger than any creature in the game: a wave, a cupping, a hand smoothing hair, a
hand laid on a cheek. At the **Root**, rows of glass bulbs lie half-buried under the oldest trees, each
the exact shape of a lantern's glass, and each with the print of that same large hand pressed into it,
where someone held it last.

## 3. The forest-mind, and why moths don't count

The Bequest is a crowd, not a mind. Its keepers are the **burrbacks**, who guard the pods, and the
**lampwings**, who gather up loose glints. They care about one thing: *who is carrying our glints?* A moth
carrying none is nothing to them. A moth carrying glints is a thief, and the keepers believe what
everyone believes about moths, that she'll fly into the light and can't help it. Lantern light really
does bend her glide. Their mistake is played twice: a moth can carry a glint *away* from the light, and
Vesper learns to fly straight with her back to it.

Under the Bequest, the **old forest** is hungry for its body back. Lanterns are its lures. What burns in
them runs *down* the roots, and where it arrives the Bequest withers and the old wood breathes. It is seen once, and it isn't evil or kind.

Are glints people, or echoes of people? Each one repeats its gesture like a loop. The Taper says they are
echoes. The keepers treat them as children. The game never says.

## 4. The cast

| | Wants | Wants from Vesper | Pull |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vesper** | To be worthy of the Taper | | Her flaw is devotion: she does the holy thing beautifully and never looks past it. The player shares it |
| **The Taper**, old, with silk-mended wings and a lit reed | The moth children safe, her reed in Vesper's grip | Keep your face to the glass | Love. She catches Vesper every time |
| **Sedge**, a moth Vesper's age, clumsy and funny | To live, and to keep Vesper near | Come home. Later: let me live | Friendship |
| **Tallow**, a lampwing with a torn wing | The **ember** safe in the earth | Give it back | It never leaves her. It stops at the edge of her light |
| **Clasp**, a burrback mother | Her pods safe | Stop cutting them | First a threat, then the same as Vesper |

**One rule.** Any moth can re-form at a lit lantern, and the glass burns one glint to remake her. Only
pilgrims are allowed to. Everyone else lives a week.

## 5. What everything is

| Thing | First play | The truth |
|---|---|---|
| **Glints / pods** | Treasure / fruit to cut | Someone, or the echo of someone / a cradle |
| **Lanterns** | Sanctuary and rest | A lure with someone burning in it |
| **The ember** | The Grove's great fetch | The face scratched on the Taper's wall: the one glint she loved and never burned. She hid it high, and Tallow kept it |
| **Burrbacks** | Brutes that light calms | Nursemaids. The "calm" is the pose they take over a dead pod |
| **Lampwings** | Thieves | Rescuers. A dive is a snatch |

**Abilities carry the turn.** In Act I they are bought with glints: *Flare* makes creatures recoil, and
you are throwing someone at them. In Act II they come from the creatures you fought: *Plant*, *Brace*,
*Moonward*. The moths' faith carries weight: glints should be carried with care, and the lanterns really
do keep moth children safe. It was right about *how* to carry and wrong about *where to*.

## 6. Prologue (wordless, about 90 seconds)

Moths gather at a lantern at night. Most are young. A few are grey, wings fraying, and in the next shot
they're gone: a week is a lifetime. The Taper, ancient, stumbles and fades. The glass flares, one glint in
it goes dark, and she steps out whole. In the crowd, Vesper and Sedge nudge each other. The Taper picks
Vesper and gives her three glints. One flickers as Vesper carries it: a large hand, waving. At the edge of
the light a torn lampwing catches a rising spark. Vesper glances toward it, and the Taper lays a wing on
her cheek, steadying her and keeping her face to the glass. The lantern blooms. The Taper points her reed
at a dark valley, *your first grove, alone*, and takes a perch on the bluff above it, her reed-light
watching.

## 7. The story

**The re-form is the relationship.** In Act I, every time Vesper fades, the Taper is at the glass when she
steps out, and lays her wing on Vesper's cheek. A player sees it a hundred times. After the Hush, Vesper
steps out alone. At the Root it reverses.

**ACT I: TOGETHER.** *With Vesper: the Taper, and for a while Sedge.*
- **The Grove** (our scene): see 8.
- **On the road.** The Taper is at every lantern before you, reed raised. The first few times you fall
  short of a ledge, her reed swoops in and she catches you by the arm. Later she lets you fall, because
  she's teaching. At rests she mends your wing with silk. She sets glints in without looking at them, except once, when her reed stops a breath short.
- **Sedge** catches up. He races you and loses every time, and you can wait for him. When you're empty at
  a dark lantern he gives you his only glint. His wings begin to grey.
- **The Combs. Boss: Clasp.** You fight beside the Taper, both re-forming from the same glass, so every
  fall by either of you lowers its count. In her last phase Clasp heaps her pods onto her back and runs.
  You crack her shell to spill them, for the act's biggest haul. She limps off into the dark,
  empty-backed. You and the Taper kindle the next lantern side by side.
- **The Coral Stair.** Tallow snatches Sedge's glint from Vesper's cape and flees slowly, one wing
  dragging. Vesper chases it, furious.

**MIDPOINT: THE HUSH (played, not shown).** The chase ends in a hollow full of pods, guarded by burrbacks
that don't get up. Tallow presses Sedge's glint into bare earth, and it sprouts, as loose glints did in
the Grove on day one. It glitters like a pickup, so the player grabs it. That is Vesper's flaw and the
player's. Tallow only watches. Then Clasp crawls past to the pod the player cut on the way in and folds
down over it, in the pose every burrback took at every kindling. Vesper looks at the glint she holds. It
flickers a big hand, cupping. She kneels and puts it back: *Plant*. At the hollow's mouth stand the Taper
and Sedge. The Taper holds out her wing. Vesper doesn't come. The Taper doesn't look surprised. Sedge goes
with her.

**ACT II: APART.** *With Vesper: Tallow.*
- **The ember.** Vesper goes back to the Grove's summit lantern. Reed-ash is heaped at its foot, layer on
  layer, and the ember is still in the glass, unburned. She lifts it out. Carried, it throws the shadow of
  a big hand around her, palm open, and it closes when she's hit. It is her light now. Tallow wants it in the earth, and Vesper needs it to see.
- **Tallow, in play.** When Vesper glides short now, Tallow snatches her by the cape and lifts her, as
  the Taper used to. Its lamp lights what the ember doesn't. It hangs a wing's length off, then less.
- **The dark.** A snuffed lantern is a place she can't re-form any more, and a planted glint is one she can't spend on her
  own life. Boss arenas have only lanterns, so burning is a real trade. Behind her, snuffed lanterns turn up relit, with reed-ash at their feet and one glint
  fewer in the glass.
- **The old forest.** Following the veins down from the Grove, Vesper finds where all the light goes: a
  buried trunk wider than the screen, its grey bark greening where the light pours in, the Bequest's pale
  pods withering around it. The old forest breathes. It doesn't look at her.
- **Sedge's week runs out.** He's grey and falling at a lantern she snuffed. He holds a glint toward the
  dark glass. She can't. The Taper arrives and relights it, and Sedge steps out young, with the Taper's
  wing on *his* cheek. He doesn't look at Vesper.
- **The Censer, the moths' home. Boss: the Taper.** She never strikes Vesper. Vesper snuffs the ring of
  lanterns and the Taper relights them, Flaring off the creatures that close in. Her one attack is the
  wing: she catches Vesper's cheek and turns her to the glass, and its pull takes the glide. Vesper wins by
  snuffing the Great Lantern. Beaten, the Taper doesn't look at Vesper. She looks at the moth children
  under the dark glass.
- **The lowest point.** In the dark, burrbacks come for the children, who carry the glints they were
  given, and Clasp leads them. Vesper **Braces** over the children and takes Clasp's ram without moving,
  as Clasp held at the Combs. Clasp stops, looks, turns the burrbacks away and lies down beside the
  children. Vesper falls, and no lantern near her is lit. Tallow snatches her loose light out of the dark
  and carries it across the valley to the one lantern still burning, the Great Lantern, which the Taper
  has relit. It lets go at the edge, and her light rolls into the glass. The glass flares and one glint
  goes dark. It flickers a large hand waving, and Sedge is the one watching it. It is the same gesture as
  the glint burned for him. Vesper steps out. The Taper is there, and her wing lifts toward Vesper's cheek
  and stops. Every moth in the Censer turns its back on Vesper. Sedge turns round.
- **Moonward.** Vesper leaves with the Censer burning behind her, and for the first time her glide doesn't
  bend. Sedge follows.

**ACT III: THE ROOT.** *With Vesper: Tallow, Clasp and Sedge. Then the Taper.*

Among the hand-printed bulbs stands the valley's oldest lantern. A storm of lampwings rises against a moth
carrying the ember toward a lantern. They dive where the pull should take her, and she flies straight
past. The Taper has followed. She doesn't stop Vesper. She stands with her, Flaring the storm back, and
every Flare costs the Root's glass a glint. When she falls, Vesper is at the glass when she steps out, and
lays a wing on her cheek. Again. Every time the *player* falls, the same glass drops. It empties. The
Taper falls once more and doesn't come out.

**The choice.** Vesper holds the ember, and only now does it flicker its whole gesture: a large hand laid
on a cheek. It is the Taper's gesture, and she has been copying it for a hundred lifetimes. Fading at the
foot of the dark glass, the Taper looks toward the far light of the Censer, then lifts her wing to
Vesper's cheek and turns her face to the glass. She is asking Vesper to burn the one person she herself
never could, so that she can go on keeping the children. Vesper chooses. The game waits.

- **Kindled.** Vesper sets the ember in the glass, and nobody shields it. It burns. The Taper steps out
  whole, and her wing is on Vesper's cheek. Nothing else changes: the lanterns burn on, the moths are safe,
  and the old forest drinks slowly, as it always has. The Taper gives Vesper the reed. Last shot: moth
  children asleep in a lantern's light, and in the glass above them, once, a large hand laid on a cheek,
  then gone.
- **Planted.** Vesper turns herself to the earth and sets the ember in it. Sedge lays his wing over hers.
  The sprout's light makes its gesture over the Taper, a hand on her cheek, and she doesn't re-form. Vesper
  carries the unlit reed out of the Root. Sedge, Tallow, Clasp and a few moths follow her and set their
  glints in the earth. Most stay at the Censer, where the Great Lantern still burns. The ember grows into a
  tree with **one hollow for every glint you burned to re-form after the Hush.** Vesper and Sedge sit under
  the moon with a week left, and Tallow lands beside them, since there is no edge of light left here. Last
  shot: moth children in the dark by the tree, no lantern near, and a burrback's shadow falls over them.
  Cut.

## 8. The Grove, and what its ending means

The pilgrim's first grove is lit alone, with the Taper's reed-light on the bluff. Vesper enters carrying
the three glints she was given, so the keepers hunt her from the first minute. It must feel wonderful and dangerous: the turn only works if the player loved it.

| In the Grove | First play | Replayed after the Hush |
|---|---|---|
| Re-forming | The Taper's wing on your cheek | A glint went dark so she could do that |
| Burrbacks fold down at a kindling | Calmed | The pose over a dead pod |
| A lampwing dive | An attack | It hurt you, yes, and it took a glint back |
| The ember | The great fetch, a hand's shadow around you | The Taper's beloved, and Tallow's charge |
| A loose glint sprouting | Loot you missed | The answer, on day one |
| Glint flickers | Pretty | Hands |

**The ending beat, all in the normal camera.** The heart wakes. The Taper glides down from the bluff and
lays her wing on Vesper's cheek as the veins light and pulse inward to the heart. Then her reed-light
leaves Vesper's side, rises to the summit lantern, and stays there until the veins go quiet. On a first
play she is honouring the great kindling. Later we know she was holding her wing over its glass. At the
edge of the frame, small and unremarked, a torn lampwing hangs under the ember's island.

**Changes to the existing scene:**
1. **Sim:** a lampwing dive wounds *and* steals: a hit takes a glint into its lamp, and striking it drops
   the glint.
2. **Sim:** a creature ignores Vesper only while she carries no glints. She enters with three and is rarely
   empty. The first time she is, the nearest burrback stops mid-brace.
3. **One fade cost:** each lantern's glass visibly holds glints, and a re-form burns one. This replaces the
   lost third of the wallet. The Taper glides down to meet Vesper at the glass.
4. **Sprouting:** a loose glint left unpicked sinks and sprouts a small pod.
5. **Pull:** a glide near a lit lantern drifts toward it.
6. **The calm pose:** a calmed burrback folds flat. At the shrine, one folds over a pod.
7. **Hands:** glint flickers show hand gestures, and the ember throws a hand's shadow while carried.
8. **The wake:**
   - The Taper comes down from the bluff.
   - Her reed-light then rises to the summit lantern.
   - Vein pulses run inward.
   - Tallow hangs under the island.

## 9. What the player finds

1. **The Taper's hollow** in the Censer: hundreds of faces scratched into the wall, all of the same face.
   Her wing-patches are cut from the old cocoon hanging there, which is Vesper's.
2. **The Root's bulbs:** each with the print of a large hand pressed into the glass.
3. **The summit lantern** in Act II: reed-ash at its foot, layer on layer, from a hundred lifetimes of
   visits.
4. **Tallow's island:** moss pressed flat in the shape of a wing, beside an empty cup.
5. **Relit lanterns:** reed-ash at the foot, and one glint fewer in the glass.
## 10. The question it leaves

Would you burn one life to keep the one you love? The Taper did, every night of a hundred lifetimes,
except once.

## Changes from round 8

- **The ember is the Taper's beloved.** It is the face on her wall and the source of her own
  wing-on-cheek gesture. It never burned because she shielded it at the wake, and her reed-light rising to
  the summit lantern happens in frame. The final choice is the Taper asking Vesper to burn the one she
  never could.
- **Kindled is unrigged.** It costs exactly one life and leaves the world as it was. Planted shows its
  cost: the Taper dies, most moths stay at the Censer, and children are left in the dark. Both last shots
  carry no verdict. The question is now "one life", not "a stranger".
- **The human origin is findable** through hands: every glint's gesture, the ember's shadow, and the
  hand-prints on the Root's bulbs. The moths no longer have "hands".
- **The Grove keeps its danger.** Dives wound and steal, and Vesper enters carrying glints, so the empty-moth
  rule is a rare discovery. There is no cutaway at the wake.
- **Sedge turns round because** he watches the same waving hand burn for Vesper that burned for him. The
  old forest appears on screen once. Tallow catches Vesper's glides in Act II.
- **Cut:** Updraft, and the clause about grubs eating pods. The week is shown through greying moths, not
  stated.
