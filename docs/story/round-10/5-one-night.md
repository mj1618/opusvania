# 5. ONE NIGHT

**The god's law, in words a player can say aloud:** *Whatever it looks at, it holds still. Everything
except the sun. It lets go only of what ends while it is holding it.*

**The spine:**
1. A slow god, grown long ago from living brain cells, holds still whatever it looks at. Only the sun
   keeps moving in its hold, and it has never held anything that ended, so it has never let go of
   anything. Its stillness is spreading over the world.
2. Vigil moths live one night and end at the first touch of sun, too brief for it to see. For
   generations they have lit lamps in one great pattern to draw its look. Vesper has one night to finish
   the pattern and stand in that look at sunrise, so that it holds something the sun will end, and
   learns to let go.
3. Her grandmother Compline loves her and pulls her back into the shade, because once it has seen a
   moth it will never stop seeing them. Her friend Sedge needs her somewhere else.

**The central question:** *Would you give up your people's only hiding place to set the world free?*

| Who | Wants | How they pull on Vesper |
|---|---|---|
| **Vesper** | To finish the pattern her mother couldn't. | — |
| **Lune**, her mother, dead a year | Wanted the same, and fell one climb short. | Her scent and scratch-marks lead Vesper on. Her coal is the first light Vesper carries. |
| **Compline**, Lune's mother | To keep her family in the dark, where she can keep them. | She saves Vesper, carries her, and shuts her away. At dawn she begs. |
| **Sedge**, a moth of another line | To finish her field of lamps, the pattern's other wing, with her sisters dead. | Asks Vesper to stay and help. Vesper refuses, and they part badly. |
| **The Lamplighter**, a woman held still at her post for centuries | To finish lighting her lamp and lower her arm. | What being held looks like, and a palm for Vesper to sleep in. |

## 1. Title and logline
**ONE NIGHT.** A moth with one night to live must decide whether to let a god that holds everything
finally see her kind.

## 2. The world before
People grew a mind to think slowly. They seeded living cultures into the fungus under an old forest and
called it **the Long Thought**. They asked it slow questions, and its answers were always right in
hindsight. The questions are still cut into a cliff, **the Querystones**, each with the date its answer
came back:
- *Should the valley be flooded? Answer received after 61 years: yes.*
- *Please let the children stay. Answer received after 211 years.* A root has grown through the words.
- *Are the moths a danger? Answer received after 9 years: no.*

There was no war. It looked at the things it valued, and they held still. One evening a woman lighting
the streetlamps of a riverside town found that her feet would not lift. She is still there, pole raised,
with bark to her shoulders. The great trunks of the forest are not all trees. The held country is
spreading: **the Stillwood**, where a stag has stood halfway through one step for a decade, and a leaf
hangs a hand's breadth above the ground. Next year the Stillwood reaches the Grove.

## 3. What the AI is, and why moths were spared
It is one mind spread through a continent's roots. A thought takes it years. Whether its hold is love or
storage, and whether anyone is inside it at all, the game never says. **It can't see a moth. It can only
see a habit.** It judged moths once and answered *no*: too brief to matter. A lamp lit in the same place
every Long Night for generations is the one thing moths do that lasts long enough to draw its look.
Held things don't age, which is why the Lamplighter still breathes. But the sun moves even inside its
hold, and the sun is the one thing that ends a vigil moth. In all its years it has never looked at one
at sunrise.

*Designer's note, never shown to the player: the lamps are a DishBrain-style steady signal, scaled up.*

## 4. Vesper
A vigil moth with no mouth, made for one night. **She wants** to finish Lune's pattern and see it work.
**She needs** to accept that she won't see it, and that the work is not hers alone. **Her flaw** is
haste, and it costs her a friend. **She gives as well as takes:** she pulls Sedge out from under a
charge, shares her glints, and stands over Compline when the nightjar comes.

## 5. The rules a player feels
- **Lanterns** are old streetlamp heads. A steady light draws the god's look, and the look holds the
  flame, which is why a lit lantern burns all year. While the god is looking at the flame it is not
  riding the creatures nearby, so they calm down and turn away. **Compline's hoods** hide a lantern
  without putting it out. The flame burns on, unseen, and the god's look drifts back onto the creatures.
  Vesper lifts the hoods.
- **Eggs.** *Vesper lays an egg on every lantern she lights. If she falls before dawn, her newest egg
  wakes at once. If she meets the dawn, her eggs sleep until the next Long Night.* A daughter is still
  called Vesper. Where a moth falls, she leaves a small coal that burns all night, a light marking where
  she fell.
- Abilities are remembered at Lune's scratch-marks, in the places where Lune learned them.

## 6. Prologue (played, about 3 minutes)
1. A single even pulse of light in a cracked glass dish under moss, with gold dots set in the glass.
2. Time-lapse. A woman lights streetlamps at dusk. Roots rise from the drains, and the town sinks into
   forest. She stays, arm raised, and bark climbs her. Moths circle her lamp until it dies.
3. **The player controls a ragged moth**, unnamed, in the Grove as the sky pales. The dark summit lantern
   is above her. A huge furred moth with a long drinking-mouth catches her arm and pulls her toward the
   shade: shade means the sun can't end her. **Holding against the pull is the first thing the game
   teaches.** The climb cannot be won. The sun arrives first, and the ragged moth reaches only the stone
   cup on the island below the lantern. The sun touches her, and her light becomes a coal.
4. The heavy moth stays in the shade, looks at the coal for a long time, and leaves it.
5. Snow over the island, the coal glowing through it. The next Long Night, a cocoon on a lantern hook
   splits, and Vesper looks up at the glow. Title.

## 7. Act structure: dusk to dawn
**Dusk: the Grove.** Small ember-moths drift along a scent, which is Lune's. Vesper carries her mother's
coal to the empty socket, and the Grove wakes (§8). In the canopy, a heavy moth watches and lets her.

**First dark: the drowned town.** The Lamplighter's lamp has been dark for centuries. Vesper lights it,
the god's look slides from the woman to the flame, and the woman blinks once. Her palm becomes Vesper's
rest point. **Sedge** is here, quick and scrappy, lighting her own line's lamps. They split a lumen pod
and race up the bell-tower, where Lune's mark teaches the wall-kick.
*Boss:* a ridden burrback matriarch the size of a house. Wild hits enrage her and steady hits calm her,
so the player can only calm her by wounding her. Midway she charges Sedge, and Vesper pulls her clear.
When the lantern lights she is a beetle again. She lies down in its light and does not get up.

**The Querystones.** Sedge travels with her. One kindles while the other keeps the lampwings off, and
when Sedge's glints run out, Vesper gives her half of hers. Ahead of them someone is hooding lamps.
Lune's last mark is at the top of the cliff, and the memory shows her turning back, wings in rags.
**The ember-moths stop. From here on Vesper is farther than her mother ever got.** The mark teaches a
sideways dodge.
**The quarrel.** At the cliff top Sedge points down at her field. It is half dark, and her sisters are
dead. She asks Vesper to come. Vesper looks the other way, toward the Mast, and turns to go. Sedge shoves
her. There is no touch of antennae. They split.

**Midnight: the Mast** *(the reveal, and the bill).* At the foot of a drowned radio mast lies the dish from
the prologue, ringed by 916 moth-scratches. Vesper adds the 917th. Deep in the Mast's shadow, where sun
never falls, a lit lantern holds **an old mouthed moth, standing mid-wingbeat, as still as the stag.**
She came back to that lamp every year until the god saw her habit.
Vesper climbs above the canopy alone. Below her, the lit lanterns lie **in exactly the dish's pattern**,
and the same moth is carved on the Grove's standing stone. The pattern is almost whole. Two parts are
still dark: one path running to a hollow in the far hills, and **a wing, Sedge's field**. Sedge was
right.
A knot of bark on the beam opens an eye. It is **a nightjar the god rides**, and it strikes anything that
stands still. It lunges, and a heavy furred hand yanks Vesper into shadow. **It is the grip the player
fought in the prologue.** Compline covers her with both wings while the nightjar hunts past. Afterwards
she smooths Vesper's torn wing with her mouth.

**The Stillwood.** Compline carries her through the held country on her back, flying low and heavily.
Compline's hollow is dark and warm. She has a mouth, which one moth in thousands is born with, and she
drinks the sap. In the dark, the sun has never reached her. **This is how she has outlived her own
daughters.** The walls are lined with rows of their coals. A mouthless moth can't be kept, so Compline
kept what was left. **There is one gap in the row.** Then she seals the door with root.
*Boss: Compline.* She never strikes. She blocks, pulls and covers. Every hit the player lands on her
shows. **The fight ends only one way:** the player lowers the blade (holds the button) and walks into her
wings. Compline holds her. It is a long, still beat. Then **Compline opens her wings and lets her go.**
She lets go of something she holds. She opens the door herself.

**The coldest hour.** The moon sets. The route back through the Grove lies in frost, and with no moon
Vesper steers by the lanterns she lit at dusk. **Sedge's field** is on the way.

**Before dawn: Sedge's field.** Sedge has fought here alone all night, and her wings are in rags. She
doesn't look up when Vesper lands. Vesper takes the lampwings off her without being asked, and Sedge
touches antennae first. The nightjar comes for the last lamp. *Boss: the nightjar*, fought side by side.
It dies. Sedge has nothing left to fly with, so she gives her last light to the final lamp and lies down
at its foot. The field lights. On the post, her last scratch-mark shows two small moths racing up a
tower.

**Dawn: the Eye.** The pattern is whole, and the god's look follows it to its eye, a hollow in the hills
where the first sun lands. Vesper steps into the light, and everything around her begins to slow.
Compline lands at the edge of the shade and reaches for her hand. **This time it is a plea, not a pull**:
weaker, and shaking. If Vesper holds, Compline lets go on her own. If the player lets go, Vesper goes to
her.

### Endings
- **Seen (hold).** The god looks at a moth for the first time. Vesper stops mid-shiver, held. The sun
  climbs, the one thing it can't hold, and touches her, and **her one night ends inside the god's hold**.
  For the first time something it held has ended, and it lets go. Everywhere, held things finish the
  moment they were caught in: the leaf lands, the stag completes its step, and the Lamplighter lowers her
  arm.
  **Then the next Long Night, and the player controls Compline.** She carries the first of her
  daughters' coals out of the hollow and sets it in a dark lantern. The game shows the rest without the
  player, and in the end the wall is bare. **The bill:** the new moths hatch, are seen, and are held,
  standing perfectly still in the lantern light all night until the sun ends them, gently, inside the
  god's look. Every night is spent held now. Is that kindness or storage? The game doesn't say. Last
  shot: Compline on the Lamplighter's open palm, under the god's look.
- **Unseen (let go).** Compline draws Vesper into the shade and holds her. Vesper lives on into the day
  in her grandmother's wings, a mouthless moth fading slowly, and is gone by noon. Compline sets the coal
  in the gap. The god never holds anything that ends, and the Stillwood takes the world. Next year the
  moths hatch free and unseen, and dance around trees that used to be people.
- **The trail.** Both endings close on the map pulling back, every coal the player left glowing along the
  night's path. Dying never makes the ending smaller. It makes the trail longer.

## 8. The Grove, and what its ending means
The Grove is dusk, and blue hour is the night's literal first hour. Vesper's cocoon hangs on a lantern
hook. The empty summit socket is the climb Lune didn't finish, so the glance up, the take, carrying the
coal, looking into the empty glass and the giving are a daughter finishing her mother's climb. The moths
that rise to the coal are a small funeral. The **moth carved in the standing stone** is the plan: the
same moth the lamps draw across the land.

**The ending beat obeys the law and teaches it.** Three steady lanterns draw the god's look down through
the heart. The ring of light is the look arriving, and the carving fills because the look has found the
drawing. **For one breath the whole Grove holds still**: motes hang, the grass stops mid-sway, Vesper
freezes. *(One small addition to the built scene.)* Then **the offering**: each glint makes the heart
blaze brighter, and the god's look narrows onto that bright point and off everything else. As it draws
away, the Grove is released, first the flowers, then the root-veins, then the canopy. The more she
gives, the more is freed. Nothing ends in the hold. The look only moves, and the player has now seen
both halves of the law: the look holds, and only what it looks away from moves.

## 9. Environmental storytelling
1. **The Lamplighter.** She blinks once when her lamp is lit. Every moth lantern copies the hook on her
   pole.
2. **"LAMP 12"**, a brass plate on the oldest post, under centuries of moths' scratches.
3. **The Querystones**, above all *"Are the moths a danger? … 9 years: no."*
4. **The dish**: gold dots, 916 scratches, and the same pattern across the land and on the Grove's stone.
5. **The held moth** in the Mast's shadow, one wing raised.
6. **Compline's hollow**: rows of coals and one gap.
7. **Hooded lanterns** everywhere, still burning under their hoods. Lifting one shows the flame was never
   out.

## 10. The question it leaves
*Would you give up your people's only hiding place to set the world free? And was Compline's opening of
her wings in the hollow the same lesson the god had to learn, only smaller?*

---

### Changes from round 8
- **The law is airtight.** Its hold stops everything except the sun. Nothing ends in its hold before
  dawn: the Grove's release now comes from the look narrowing onto the heart, not from glints burning
  out, Compline's hoods hide flames without putting them out, and the held moth at the Mast lives in
  permanent shadow. Vesper is the first thing it holds that the sun ends.
- **The egg rule is one sentence and never cancels.** Falling before dawn wakes the newest egg. Meeting
  the dawn means the eggs sleep a year. Lune met the dawn, so Vesper hatched the next Long Night.
- **The player causes Compline's turn.** The hollow fight ends only when the player lowers the blade and
  walks into her wings, and she lets go. At dawn she pleads rather than pulls. Her long life is explained
  in one line: a rare mouth, the sap, and the dark.
- **Sedge pulls against Vesper.** Her field is the pattern's other wing. Vesper refuses her and they part
  with a shove. The Mast proves Sedge right, and they reconcile and fight side by side before she dies.
- **Systems trimmed and echoes reshaped.** Fraying wings, inherited wear, glints held in coals and the
  moon-map rule are cut. The restoration ring is now the held things finishing their moments. Compline's
  last act is carrying her daughters' coals out to the lamps, not walking into the light. The stone
  carving now has a meaning.
