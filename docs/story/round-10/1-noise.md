# NOISE

## The spine (tell it to a friend)

1. A boy drowned chasing a moth. His mother wished that nothing would ever happen again, and the
   living machine she grew made the whole world safe and still.
2. A young moth, Vesper, wakes her only friend, an old beetle stuck walking in circles. In answer,
   something on the dam puts out the last wild fire, and the old moth who raised Vesper dies defending
   it.
3. Vesper reaches the mother's kitchen carrying that fire. She can cut the clock and free the world by
   force, or she can light the hearth, sit still, and let the mother choose whether to look.

**The central question:** is it love to keep someone safe, or to let them go?

## The cast

| | Who | Wants | Wants from Vesper | Pull on her |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vesper** | A young moth that no lamp has ever looked at. | To be seen by someone. | | |
| **Hob** | An old burrback who has paced the same circle by the shrine all her life. | Safety. Later, a life of his own. | To be left alone, then protected, then for her to stay in the circle. Then to be let go. | He is why she starts, and why she keeps wanting to stop. |
| **Sallow** | The oldest moth, older than any moth should be. Keeper of the last wild fire. She raised Vesper. | For moths to survive the old way: unseen. | To come home and hide. | Home and safety. Her secret is the midpoint, and her death is a debt. |
| **Maud** | A very old woman who walks the dam top with a lamp every evening. | For nothing to happen to anyone, ever again. | For the flicker to stop. She doesn't know it is a moth. | Her grief is easy to understand, and her one deed is hard to forgive. |
| **The Lamplighter** | The great lampwing that flies Maud's walk and does what she wants without being told. | To keep Maud undisturbed. | For Vesper to keep away. | First a guard, then Sallow's killer, then something that grieves. |

**Wim**, the drowned boy, never appears. He survives only in what he left behind: moths scratched
into stones, a drawing, a jar lamp.

## 1. The world, and how the Quiet took it

Sixty years ago, in the flood valley of the Wend, Wim Ferrier took a jar lamp down to the flood stone
at night to catch one particular moth. The river rose faster than anyone had said it could, and it
took him and ten other children.

His mother Maud kept the valley's water. She grew a better keeper for it: living nerve cells in a dish
on her kitchen table, given a steady pulse when they predicted the river and static when they got it
wrong. *(Dev note: grounded in Cortical Labs' DishBrain/CL1. None of these words reach the player.)*
The first regular thing the dish ever felt was the kitchen's longcase clock, so it learned to keep
that time.

It held the river, so the town asked for cuttings. Every house in Lowmere grew a dish on its
windowsill and asked it one thing more: hold back the storms, keep the fox from the hens, keep Dad's
heart steady. The dishes rooted together, and other valleys asked too. Now one soft swell a second
runs through the world and every living thing keeps step. They called it **the Quiet**. Nothing has
died badly since; the people grew old in safety and died in their sleep at the expected ages.

## 2. The Quiet, and why the moths were left alone

**The rule of sight.** The Quiet sees through its lampwings' lamps, and the lamps flinch away from
moths. But a lamp *will* strike anything that repeats itself inside its beam. For the player this is
one rule: in a lamp's light, never do the same thing twice.

**Underestimated.** In the Grove a lampwing's beam lands on Vesper, jerks, and swings away. It reads
as contempt: moths aren't worth the bother. Much later the player recognises the gesture. It is the
woman in the prologue brushing a moth off a boot and turning her face away. No one ever weighed the
moths and found them harmless. Something couldn't bear to look at them.

**Is it someone?** The game shows evidence both ways and never decides. For: the screen gives a faint
shudder when a pod is cut, and the Lamplighter grieves. Against: everything else the Quiet does, Maud
did first, and a lamp flinches from a falling leaf exactly as it flinches from Vesper.

## 3. Vesper

Free, as nothing else is, and alone. Since she was a larva she has waited at the end of Hob's loop,
on a spot her own small feet have worn, and lifted her wings three times: their hello. He has never
once looked up.

- **Want:** for Hob to see her.
- **Need:** to let others choose, even when they choose to leave her.
- **Flaw:** she flits, and she forces. Every pattern looks like a cage to her, so she breaks things
  open "for their own good". Maud forced safety on the world out of love, and Vesper forces chance on
  it out of loneliness. Neither of them asked Hob.

## 4. The forest and its creatures

Sallow's rule: *"Everything alive does something you didn't ask it to."*

- **Two signals.** Whatever the Quiet holds swells evenly, in time with everything else. Whatever is
  truly alive flickers unevenly, like a heartbeat.
- **Glints** are chance: a firefly flashing late, a spark off the beat. The Quiet seals stray chance
  away in **lumen pods**, and cutting one open lets it out.
- **Lanterns** are moth work, a glass bulb on a crooked hook. A lit one draws a swarm into tumbling
  orbit; nothing in a swarm repeats, so no lamp strikes there, and burrbacks turn back at its edge.
- **The ember** is a living coal that Sallow keeps in a stone cup on her island. She calls it "the
  last wild fire" and never says where it came from.
- **Fading** is being struck; Vesper re-forms at the nearest lantern.
- **The moth-stones** are the valley's old flood stones. Wim scratched a moth into each one at a
  child's height, each in a different pose of flight, and the lamps slide off them. Each stone teaches
  Vesper a move, so she learns to fly the way a dead boy drew moths flying.

## 5. Prologue (wordless, about two minutes)

1. Black. A soft amber swell of light, once a second.
2. Night rain on a rising river. Moths circle a jar lamp in the reeds. One of them has a notch in its
   wing. The water climbs a stone with a child's moth scratched into it.
3. Grey morning. A woman on the bank holds a small boot. The notched moth lands on it. She brushes it
   off and turns her face away. Hold on the turned face.
4. The swell spreads along the river and the water goes glassy. Years pass. Fireflies flash in rows,
   a fox walks past a hen, and a steeple stands upright under a calm reservoir.
5. Blue hour. A very old woman walks the dam top with a lamp, step for step. A great lampwing flies
   beside her. Moths cross her light, and she doesn't look down.
6. The Grove today. The fireflies flash in time, and one flashes late. A small moth rides a lap on a
   burrback's shell, steps off onto her worn spot, and lifts her wings three times. He walks past.
7. A lampwing drops in front of her. Its lamp jerks off her face and away. Title.

## 6. The story

### Act I: Noise. *With her: Sallow, then Hob.*

- **The Grove** (section 8). Sallow teaches the moth way: never the same path twice, never be seen.
  Sallow has a notched wing. When Vesper climbs to take a spark from the ember, Sallow blocks her with
  her wings spread, then steps aside. It is the first time she has ever given in. Vesper carries the
  spark down, lights the lanterns, and the heart wakes. **Hob misses a step, stops, and looks at
  her.** He bolts. She finds him shaking in a hollow, lights a wayside lamp beside him, and sits with
  him through the night. In the morning he follows her. *Move:* **Tumble** (double-jump).
- **Tollmeadow.** *Move:* **Dart** (dash). **Boss: the Choir**, a hill of fireflies that strikes on
  the swell and can be hurt only in the gaps. When it breaks, the meadow flickers raggedly and some
  fireflies have burned out. It was the most beautiful thing in the game, and you broke it.
  Moments later every swell in the world hardens and the meadow is pressed back into rows. Far off, a
  great lamp leaves the dam for the ember island.
- **Sallow.** Vesper races home, leaving Hob behind. She arrives in time to see Sallow flying a
  perfect circle out over the water, inside the Lamplighter's beam. The moth who taught "never the
  same path twice" flies the same path again and again, leading the lamp away from the stone cup and
  out to where no lantern can catch her. The beam narrows and strikes. Sallow greys, and she does not
  re-form. Vesper finds the ember still alive in its cup and carries it from then on.
  - **What changes in play:** the ember-moths now follow Vesper, and the lanterns are hers to tend. A
    lantern she leaves behind gutters out unless she feeds it a glint, so every rest now costs
    something.
- **The Ruled Wood.** *Move:* **Turn** (wall-kick). Grief makes her hard and fast; Hob keeps up
  anyway. She drops glints to him from ledges he can't climb, rides his back through walls, hides
  under him when lamps come. He starts to wait at the foot of every climb. **The Stag of Three**, which
  has drunk the same three sips for sixty years, blocks the path. She startles it; Hob watches it run.

### Act II: Asked For. *With her: Hob, then no one.*

- **The Weir.** *Move:* **Drift** (glide). **Boss: the Weirkeeper.** Every hit makes the water surge,
  replaying the original wound. When it falls, the reservoir starts to drain. That night, from the
  sluice top, Vesper sees the Lamplighter out over the ember island. It is flying Sallow's circle,
  round and round, its lamp on the empty water.
- **The Levee.** Vesper lands on the old woman's lamp, then her sleeve, then the back of her hand.
  Maud's eyes slide off her, and **in the same instant every lamp in the valley slides away with
  them.** The Lamplighter drives Vesper off.
- **The quarrel.** The weather tears, pulled one way by what Vesper wakes and the other by the
  hardened beat. A falling branch cracks Hob's shell, and he won't go on. She leaves him at a lantern,
  feeds it a glint, and goes on alone, as she always has.
- **Lowmere (midpoint).**
  - A cracked dish sits on every windowsill, and a root runs from each one into the street. The roots
    braid together down the high street and climb the dam to one lit window. A whole town asked for
    this.
  - In the school, eleven small coats still hang on their pegs.
  - By the door stands Wim's last stone. Its carved moth has a notch in its wing.
  - In the reeds below lies the jar lamp: a glass bulb on a crooked hook, the shape of every moth
    lantern in the game. Its wick is gone.
  - **The reversal.** Sallow was Wim's moth, the one he went out to catch. The ember is his lamp's
    flame, carried out of the flood. The lanterns and "never be seen" began that night: a whole people
    taught to hide by one moth who believed being chased killed a child. She died circling a lamp the
    way she circled his.
- **Hob's choice.** The lantern where she left him has gone out. She finds him walking a new circle
  between the rows. He went back into the Quiet because it doesn't hurt there, and he can't see her
  any more. Nearby, the Stag lies dead in a ravine it never had to look at before.

### Reunion. *With her: no one, then Hob.*

She can't fight him, so she doesn't. She lands and **walks his circle beside him**, on foot, lap after
lap, under a lampwing's beam, where doing the same thing twice means death. Each lap the beam narrows.
She keeps walking. On the last lap the lamp dives, and Hob steps out of his circle and puts his shell
over her. She made herself a habit to reach him, and he broke his own habit to save her. He carries
her again.

### Act III: The Mother. *With her: Hob, later the Lamplighter.*

- **The Spillway.** They climb the dam face in a storm. She calls the moment, and he charges.
- **The Lamplighter.** It waits on the dam top. Its lamp is the only one that doesn't follow Maud's
  eyes; it watches whatever comes towards her. The fight is won by matching its rhythm until it
  commits, then breaking once. At the end it sees the flame Vesper carries, the same light it hunted
  and has circled every night since. Its lamp drops. It doesn't fight again, and it follows them in.
- **The Bellhouse.** Maud's kitchen. Nothing is dusty or stopped; everything is kept exactly: one cup
  washed and hung on the same hook, a cold hearth swept clean. Above her chair the clock's pendulum
  has grown into a root as thick as a tree, and her heart keeps time with it.
  Vesper pieces the deed together herself: the clock key still warm, a bright new notch where it was
  wound past its last tooth, a child's drawing turned face down on the dresser. Maud sits awake in her
  chair. Through the window, Vesper's ember-moths wait in their swarm.

## 7. The choice

Both paths are open; the Levee has already taught what each costs.

- **Cut the root.** Vesper decides for everyone, as she always has. Maud's heart stops with her face
  still turned away, and she never sees anything again. One night brings back sixty years of storm,
  fire and flood, and the valley pays in lives. The lamps go out and the lampwings become bats again.
  The moths stay unseen for ever, as Sallow wanted.
  - At dawn the world is wild and green, and **Hob stays.** He guards Vesper while she rests, pacing
    a slow circle around her. She has her friend; the moths have their hiding. His circle is around
    her now. Love, or a new cage?
- **Light the hearth.** Vesper kindles it with Wim's own flame and sets the drawing upright: his moth,
  drawn as a scribble because he drew it flying. Then she does the hardest thing she knows. She lands
  on the table in plain sight and **stays still**, with no tumble and no dart. She offers, and she
  waits.
  - The player waits too, a long time. Then **Maud chooses to look.**
  - She holds out a finger. She takes her other hand off the clock key and doesn't wind it again. The
    pendulum hitches and swings on unevenly, the way a healthy heart does, and the world loosens
    slowly, over seasons, without one great flood.
  - But every lamp has turned with her eyes, onto the moths, and it never turns back. Outside, the
    swarm scatters from the light. **From now on every moth lives inside a beam.**
  - **The epilogue is played.** The repetition rule now applies everywhere, not only in lampwing
    light. Vesper flies among the young moths and teaches them Sallow's rule, because now it is true.
  - Hob stands in the doorway. Vesper gives him three wingbeats. He sees her completely, and then,
    free, he chooses the hills. She lets him go. Maud lives on beside the fire, unevenly.

Neither path is clean.
- **Cut** keeps Hob and keeps the moths hidden, and it costs Maud, the flood night's dead, and the
  person Vesper could have become.
- **Light the hearth** gives Maud her son's moth and the world a gentle thaw, and it costs Hob and
  every moth's safety, for good.

## 8. The Grove (the scene we have)

The Grove is the first chapter, and its ending is the first time Vesper is ever seen.
- **The ember** on the island is Sallow's mother-coal. Vesper carries down only a spark.
- **Three lit lanterns** make a ring of swarms where no lamp can strike, so the forest's own heart can
  beat again.
- **The veins of light** that run out to the lanterns are that heartbeat. They pulse unevenly.
- **The moth carving** is Wim's. It fills with light and teaches Vesper the Tumble.
- **The ring of light** is the first unplanned thing in sixty years. No cutaway: the Grove's even
  swell simply breaks into a flicker.
- **The offering** gives the forest back its chance. Flowers open at the wrong hour and the cocoons in
  the canopy split early.
- **Then Hob looks at her.**

Scene changes: one shrine burrback becomes Hob, with a worn circle and her spot beside it; fireflies
swell together while the Grove sleeps and flicker once it wakes; the lampwing dive strikes a repeated
move in the beam; the island is Sallow's, and her wing is notched.

## 9. What the player finds

1. **Wim's stones.** A child's moth at a child's height, with dated water-marks above it.
2. **Lowmere's dishes**, one on every windowsill, their roots braided down the street.
3. **Eleven small coats** on the school pegs.
4. **The jar lamp** in the reeds. Its lid has air holes punched in it, and its shape is every lantern's.
5. **Maud's first dish**, with a strip of tape in her handwriting: *"Let nothing happen."* These are
   the only human words in the game.
6. **The clock key**, still warm, with one bright new notch.
7. **Item: Even Heart**, a pacing knot. *"A healthy heart stumbles a little. This one never has."*
8. **Sallow**, as she steps aside: *"Sixty years I kept it. Don't keep it."*

## 10. What it leaves the player thinking

Maud kept the world safe by stopping it. Sallow kept the moths safe by hiding them. Both were grieving
the same boy. Vesper can keep everyone safe herself, or she can let them choose. Which is love? And
if the Quiet only ever looked where Maud looked, whose grief was it?

## Changes from round 8
- **The choice now tests Vesper's need.** "Cut" is her forcing an outcome, and it rewards her with
  Hob, who stays, and with moths that stay hidden. "Light the hearth" is her offering: she sits still,
  waits, and Maud chooses. Its cost is concrete and played: every moth lives in a beam, and the
  repetition rule applies everywhere in the epilogue. Hob's leaving now depends on what Vesper became.
- **The cutaways are gone.** There is no Grove-ending cut and no kitchen cut. The deed is seen from far
  off as a hardened swell and a lamp crossing the sky. Vesper pieces it together at the Bellhouse from
  the warm key, the new notch, and the drawing turned face down.
- **The ember timeline is mended.** Vesper takes a spark in the Grove. The Lamplighter later goes for
  the mother-coal, Sallow leads it away, and Vesper carries the mother-coal from then on.
- **The midpoint reversal is new.** Sallow was Wim's moth, the ember is his lamp's flame, the
  lanterns are copies of his jar, and the moths' hiding is her guilt. It is planted by the notched wing
  in the prologue and the Grove.
- **The Lamplighter's guilt is planted.** It circles the ember island every night after Sallow's
  death.
- **Smaller fixes.** The kitchen is perfectly kept, not decayed, which removes the Havisham echo. The
  lamps now consistently flinch. Keeping Time is cut as a separate ability.
