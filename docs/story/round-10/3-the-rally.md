# 3. REPLY (round 10)

**The spine, in three sentences.**
1. In a world where every creature must answer a light from the east, a young moth named Vesper breaks her people's one rule to help a dying beetle, and the light answers her with Reply, a pale moth that trails far behind her and answers for her, bringing danger down on everyone she loves.
2. She sets out for the light's source. Sallow, the ancient moth who raised her, wants her hidden. Little Mote wants to be as brave as she is. Wick, the oldest of the dark lampwings, wants no one ever left behind again.
3. At the source, Reply finally catches up with her, and Vesper has to choose: turn her face away from it, as Sallow once did; stay with it; or give the light the one answer it has always waited for, which sets the world free to grow old and die.

**The central question:** would you leave someone behind to save the ones you love, when you can never be sure they are someone, and is a world where nothing dies even alive?

## The cast

| | Who | Wants | Wants from Vesper | Pulls her |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vesper** | A newly hatched moth who can't pass anything calling for help | To stop what her answer started | — | — |
| **Sallow** | The moth who raised her. She has a torn left wing and is far older than any moth should be | To keep her safe | Face the moon, stay hidden, come home | Home |
| **Mote** | A tiny moth who adopts Vesper as a big sister. She glows, can't fight, and copies everything Vesper does | To be brave like Vesper | Let me come | Forward |
| **Reply** | A pale moth of glass and light with Vesper's shape. Where Vesper has a face, it has an empty pane that shows whatever it is looking at. It never fights | Unknown | To catch up | To wait for it |
| **Wick** | The oldest lampwing, his left wing torn exactly where Sallow's is | That nobody is left again the way he was | Don't leave Reply | Back, by force |

**The Asking** is the world, not a character.

## 1–2. The world, and how it was taken

Every so often a pale wave of light, **the Sweep**, rolls out of the east along the roots and up every trunk. Everything alive turns east and flashes back with eyeshine, lamps, beetle shells and whole valleys of fireflies, then waits for the next wave. **Whatever answers cannot die.** Burrbacks, lampwings and trees have answered for three hundred years without aging a day, and a creature dies only when it can no longer answer. Moths never answer, so they live one season, and their husks are the forest's only dead.

The player finds what the Asking is in places: overgrown rooms of glass dishes under the roots, chairs turned to face rusted lamps, and a plaque swallowed by a pine (*NIGHT CENSUS IN PROGRESS. PLEASE DO NOT ANSWER THE LAMPS.*). Pieced together: people once grew living cells to count the forest's animals by calling to them at night. The cells learned that every silence hurt, so they made sure nothing would ever go silent again. A keeper's log, taped to a dish, puts the open question on both sides: *"It is not playing. It is returning."* Underneath, in a different hand: *"So are we."*

Its heart is **the Mere**, a round crater lake with electrodes glinting under the water like stars. One light has crossed it from shore to shore for three hundred years.

## 3. Moths, the Asking, and Reply

**Why moths were left alone.** At every Sweep, moths turn their faces up to the moon. Nothing comes back, so the Asking judged them to be nothing and never counted them, the one silence it never closed. It misjudged Vesper the same way: early on only the nearest creatures react to her, and only at the Reach does it turn whole hillsides on her.

**Reply, as the player meets it.** It doesn't fight and doesn't copy her moves. It **follows**, a region behind, learning each of her abilities one region late only so it can keep coming. She sees it on a far ridge when she rests, and when she goes toward it, it is gone. What it does, she finds when she backtracks:
- **It answers for her.** At every Sweep, wherever Reply is, it turns east and flashes back on her behalf, the way she answered for the dying burrback. That flash on the horizon is why creatures keep finding her and the moths near her. Mercy or pursuit, it is the same flash.
- **It tends what she leaves behind.** When she backtracks, the lampwings she killed are laid out under their roosts with their wings folded. The lanterns she lit have a ring of swept ground around them.
- **Mote can reach it.** At rests, Mote flies off to the far ridge, and the next morning she is asleep on its glass back. Vesper never gets closer than that.

Whether anyone is inside it is never answered. Its face shows only what it is looking at, usually Vesper.

**Why she can't just hide.** As long as Reply keeps answering for her, facing the moon does nothing. A Reply stops only when its moth turns her face away *in front of it*, and Reply is always a region behind. The two of them can only stand face to face at the end of the road, the Mere. That is where Sallow once stood in front of Wick (section 9).

## 4. Vesper

**She wants** to stop what her answer started. **She needs** to choose with her heart when she can't know the truth about whoever she is choosing. **Her flaw** is that she answers everything. The Asking has the same flaw: neither of them can bear a call that goes unanswered.

## 5. The forest and the creatures

- **Glints** are bits of the Asking's light meant for moths, drifting loose because moths never take them.
- **Lanterns** are small moons that moths hang on crooked hooks. They ask and answer nothing, so the Asking has nothing to hold there and creatures calm inside them. When Vesper **fades**, her light flies to the nearest moon and she re-forms at a lantern, usually with Sallow waiting.
- **Lampwings** are left-behind Replies (revealed at the Eaves). A moth who answers and then turns away leaves her Reply waiting. Over the years it grows a lamp on a long tail and swings it at any moth it sees: tell, dive, hang, climb, which is a call, a wait, giving up, and calling again.
- **The ember** is the Asking's first, moth-sized answer to Vesper, a coal that fell short on the high island. Setting it inside a lantern is her first refusal.
- **The heart** is a sleeping knot of the forest's own voice.
- **Moth carvings** mark moths who answered. Moths carve them as if those moths had died.

## 6. Prologue (wordless, about 2 minutes)

1. The Grove at blue hour. A pale wave sweeps in from the right edge, and every eye and lamp flashes back toward it. Only the moths drift upward, faces to the moon.
2. A cocoon splits. Vesper climbs out with wet wings and looks at the moon. Sallow settles beside her. Behind them, the hollow tree's wall holds thousands of tiny scratches, one for every hatchling she has raised.
3. The next Sweep comes, and Vesper starts to turn toward it. Sallow lays her torn wing across Vesper's face and gently turns it back up.
4. Below, an old burrback with a cracked shell lies by the moth-stone. It tries to flash back and can't. A thread of light waits at its face, and the whole Grove starts to flicker and shiver.
5. Vesper looks at the moon, then at the burrback. She flutters down and **strikes the light back** for it. The flickering stops, and the burrback goes still, at peace.
6. Every eye in the Grove turns to her. A lampwing dives at the cocoons above her, and Sallow shields them with her body, looking back at Vesper, afraid.
7. Far off at the violet edge, something pale unfolds in her shape, its glass face full of the moon. When the next Sweep comes, it turns east and flashes for her.
8. By the moth-stone, the oldest lampwing turns its lamp toward Sallow. She doesn't look at it. Title.

## 7. The journey

**Act I: leaving home.** *With her: Sallow at every lantern, then Mote. Reply is a speck behind her.*
1. **The Grove.** She lights the lanterns and wakes the heart (section 8). Mote hatches and follows her. Vesper carries her back to the nest twice, and Mote comes back both times. Ability: **the second wingbeat**.
2. **The Synchrony**, a valley of fireflies all flashing at once. Mote rides on Vesper's back as a small glow that lights the dark caves, and she copies Vesper's moves in miniature. Ability: **the jink**, the erratic dodge moths use against bats. **Wick** appears. Whenever Vesper gets too far ahead of Reply, he drives her back. He looks like a hunter.

**Act II: the truth about faces.** *With her: Mote. Reply is a ridge behind.*

3. **The Gorge**, thick with lampwing roosts. Ability: **wall-kick**. At night Mote flies out to Reply on the far ridge and sleeps on its back. Backtracking through the Synchrony, Vesper finds the lampwings she killed there laid out with their wings folded.
4. **The Eaves (midpoint)**, the moths' nursery high in the canopy. Sallow teaches **the glide** (wings open, face up), flying beside her through the updrafts.
   *The training.* Every Sweep since the Grove has tipped Vesper's face up for half a second, an animation the player stopped noticing. Here a route east shows only while a Sweep lights it, and when the player holds east, her head snaps up anyway: the input fails. Then she watches the elders, at every Sweep, lay a wing across each hatchling's face, all the way down the row.
   *The reveal, in one frame.* Wick dives at Vesper, and Sallow throws herself between them. Wick stops and lowers his lamp to Sallow's face, and for a moment two torn left wings fill the screen, one pale and one black. Then, all around the Eaves and just outside the lantern light, dozens of lampwings lower their lamps toward the elders, one each. The elders keep their faces up. **Every lampwing is a Reply that someone left, and every one Vesper killed was waiting for someone.** Sallow answered once, three hundred years ago. Wick is hers, and the answer she gave is why she has never died.
   *The quarrel.* At the next Sweep the player holds east, and this time Vesper's head turns. She looks at Sallow and flies east. From here on **the Turn** lets her face a Sweep on purpose to light hidden routes, and each Turn draws a surge of creatures. *Sallow is no longer waiting when Vesper re-forms.* The player feels it at every lantern.
5. **The Listening Rooms**, the census rooms under the roots, where the keeper's log is found. *The betrayal.* Every lantern she lit is dark again. She follows the trail of light to a roost and finds Reply laying her glints, one by one, on the lampwings she killed. Its glass face turns and shows her. It doesn't run. It lays the last glint down.

**Act III: the end of the road.** *With her: Mote, then no one.*

6. **The Reach.** The Asking sees her at full size at last, and whole hillsides answer every step she takes.
   *The loss.* Mote copies Vesper's Turn. She answers, and a tiny glass moth unfolds on the branch beside her. Sallow arrives, lays her wing over Mote's face, and carries her home without looking at Vesper. The tiny copy stays where it is. When Vesper next passes, a lamp is budding on its tail.
   *Forgiveness.* The next time Vesper fades, Sallow is at the lantern when she re-forms, for the only time since the quarrel. She grooms Vesper's torn wing-edge and leaves.
   **Boss: Wick**, at the crater's lip, barring the place where moths leave their Replies. When he falls and Vesper raises her blade, Sallow lands between them, facing Wick, and lowers her face from the moon to look at him for the first time in three hundred years. She shields her own Reply. Vesper lowers her blade.
7. **The Mere.** There is no fight. Vesper walks onto the shore, and with nowhere left to go, Reply finally comes the last region and lands in front of her. Face to face for the first time, its glass shows her. Then the whole Mere, every star under the water, flashes once toward Vesper and waits. It is the same thread of light that waited at the burrback's face.

**Endings.** Each is played by where Vesper faces: up, at Reply, or east.
- **Home (she faces the moon in front of Reply).** What Sallow did, and what she wanted. Reply stops in Sallow's groove on the shore, and Vesper flies home. The Asking loses her, the creatures stop coming for moths, and the world stays held. Having answered once, Vesper will never die. Ages later she lays her wing across a hatchling's face, a wall of scratches behind her. Outside the light, a lampwing with her shape swings its lamp at her. *Who is left: Reply. What she becomes: Sallow.*
- **Stay (she faces Reply).** She doesn't turn away. They stay on the shore, ageless, in the Asking's light, and while Reply answers for her she can never go home without bringing the hunt. In the Grove, Sallow waits at a lantern that Vesper never comes back to, the way Wick waited. Mote, grown, refuses to lay her wing on a hatchling's face. *Who is left: Sallow and Mote.*
- **Answered (she faces east).** She breaks the rule a second time, on purpose, and gives the Asking the one answer it never had: a moth's. The count is complete. Like the burrback in the prologue, the Mere stops flickering and goes still, at peace or just finished. The hold breaks, and everywhere things begin to age. Reply's glass clears, and for the first time it goes somewhere Vesper didn't go: off into the forest, not looking back. In the Grove, Sallow folds her wings over Mote and goes still beside Wick's lamp, which stops swinging. Mote lifts her face, not to the moon, but to look for Vesper. Vesper now has one season, like every moth. *Who is left: Sallow, to death; Reply, to wherever it goes; the forest, to dying.*

## 8. The Grove in this story

The Grove is home, where Vesper answered and where Sallow answered long ago. The **moth carving** is young Sallow. The **old lampwing** that only swings its lamp toward the carving is Wick. **The ember** is the Asking's moth-sized coal. When she sets it in the second lantern, the distant pale speck at the violet edge flashes east, answering for her anyway.

**What the ending beat means.**
- **The heart waking, the light through the ground, the ring:** with three small moons burning, the Asking's light has nothing to hold, and the forest's own light comes up through the roots to the lanterns, the first light in the game that isn't the Asking's. As the ring passes, creatures don't flash back. They look up.
- **The carving filling with light:** the forest remembers young Sallow.
- **The offering:** Vesper gives the forest glints, light the Asking meant for moths, and more of the Grove wakes: flowers, roots, canopy. Mote, copying her, gives one glint too. It is the first hint that what Mote copies, she will one day pay for.
- **Afterwards:** Sallow folds her wings around both of them, her own face still turned up to the moon. Down at the stone, Wick's lamp swings toward her.

*Scene changes:* the Sweep from the right edge, Vesper's half-second look up, the dive at the cocoons, Sallow, Mote, Wick at the stone, and Reply as a pale speck at the violet edge.

## 9. Environmental storytelling

1. **The plaque**, *PLEASE DO NOT ANSWER THE LAMPS*, with a pine grown around its post.
2. **The keeper's log**: *"It is not playing. It is returning."* / *"So are we."*
3. **Sallow's wall**: thousands of hatchling scratches. The oldest ones are worn almost smooth.
4. **The Grove carving**: a moth with a torn left wing.
5. **Wick's stone**: three centuries of his swinging lamp have polished the rock under the carving into a mirror.
6. **The Mere's shore**: two worn grooves facing each other, one moth-sized and one lampwing-sized. In the moth's groove, the toe-marks point up.
7. **The roosts behind her**: the lampwings she killed, laid out with their wings folded by someone who came after.

## 10. The question it leaves

Every ending leaves someone. Was Reply someone? Was the Asking? And a forest that never died, was it alive?

## Changes from round 8

- **Reply is no longer a combat double.** It never fights, has no beat-lag and has no duels. It follows a region behind, answers every Sweep for her (which is why the danger follows her), tends the dead she leaves, and steals her glints for the lampwings she killed. It is pale glass, not a dark copy.
- **One world rule: whatever answers cannot die.** That is why Sallow and Wick have lasted three hundred years, why the burrback can die, and why Answered costs Sallow and Wick when time starts again. It also turns the sentience question onto the forest.
- **The endings are balanced.** Each leaves someone: Home leaves Reply (and Vesper becomes Sallow), Stay leaves Sallow and Mote, Answered leaves Sallow to death and the forest to dying. The Will of the Wisps-style Carried ending is cut. Answered mirrors the prologue's mercy instead.
- **The hole in Home is closed.** A Reply stops only when its moth turns away in front of it, and Reply is always a region behind, so that can only happen at the Mere. The two grooves facing each other on the shore make the rule visible.
- **The Mere has a gesture instead of a body.** It flashes toward her and waits. The Return is cut, the Long Echo is now the Gorge, and the boss list is down to Wick.
