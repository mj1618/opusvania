## 2. Previous critique's problems

1. Carried is a free win: **fixed.** The ending is cut, and Answered kills Sallow and Wick.
2. Celeste/Badeline: **fixed.** "Reply is no longer a combat double."
3. The world rules contradict each other: **partly.** There is now one rule, but Sallow breaks it (see 3.1).
4. Home's hole: **partly.** "Face to face for the first time" at the Mere contradicts the Listening Rooms, where "its glass face turns and shows her. It doesn't run." Mote also reaches Reply every night.
5. Name soup and a faceless finale: **partly.** The Return and the Long Echo are gone, and the Mere gestures. But the set Asking/answer/Reply/Answered plus Sweep/Turn remains.

## 3. Remaining problems, ranked

1. **The rule fails at the reveal.** "Whatever answers cannot die... a creature dies only when it can no longer answer." Sallow answered once, then turned away in front of Wick, and her Reply stopped. By the rule she should have died that season. Then "having answered once, Vesper will never die" quietly swaps in a different rule. Killing lampwings also clashes with "cannot die". The midpoint reveal ("the answer she gave is why she has never died") rests on this logic, so the logic has to hold. *Fix:* see section 4.

2. **The question's subject is the one character the player can't reach.** "Vesper never gets closer than that." Reply's only contact scene breaks its own distance rule, and it is framed as a "betrayal" when it is really grief. *Fix:* keep the distance and build a correspondence. Glints she leaves at a lantern are gone at the next visit, and something is left in their place: a folded wing-scale, a swept ring. Stage the Listening Rooms through a wall of glass dishes. She sees it lay the last glint, and she cannot get to it.

3. **Mote's thread is dropped.** Her "tiny glass moth" grows "a lamp... budding on its tail", which is the most felt loss in the story. Then Mote leaves Act III, and no ending mentions her Reply. *Fix:* pay it off in every ending. In Home, Mote's little lampwing swings at her forever. In Answered, its lamp goes out. In Stay, Vesper brings it to the shore.

4. **The forest's sentience disappears after the Grove.** The heart is "the first light in the game that isn't the Asking's", and then it never appears again. In Answered, "the forest, to dying" happens without it. *Fix:* the heart should take part in the climax. In Answered, the light from the Grove's roots reaches the Mere and answers alongside her. The forest chooses mortality with her rather than having it done to it.

5. **Stay reads as the bad ending.** It offers no good that the others lack, only "she can never go home without bringing the hunt." *Fix:* make Stay the only ending where Reply touches her and the ambiguity tips. It is a companion gained, at the price of home and Sallow.

## 4. The one change

**Make Wick's swinging lamp the answer that keeps Sallow alive.** A Reply that is left behind never stops answering for its moth. A lampwing's tell, dive, hang and climb is that answer, swung at her across the dark for three hundred years. This repairs the rule: whoever is answered for cannot die. It makes Wick's waiting literal love, and it makes Sallow shielding him at the crater an act of love that saves her own life. It explains Home, where Vesper's Reply will answer for her forever as her lampwing. It makes Answered retellable: the lamps stop swinging, so the old moths die. It also gives every lampwing kill a quiet cost that the folded bodies already imply, without needing a paragraph of rules.

## 5. Verdict

**Close.** The Eaves reveal (two torn left wings, then a row of lamps lowered to the elders), the empty re-form lantern and the endings chosen by facing are at the bar. A narrative lead at Moon, Team Cherry or Red Candle would not greenlight it yet, for these reasons:
- The rule the midpoint depends on contradicts itself at Sallow, the one character it explains.
- The central question is about a character the player never meets until the last screen.
- Mote's loss is left open.
- The ending frame is a recognisable Dark Souls / Sekiro choice.

Problems 1 and 3 are an afternoon's work. Problem 2 is the real gap between this and yes.
