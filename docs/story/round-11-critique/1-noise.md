# Round 11 critique: NOISE (round 10)

## 1. Scores

- **Relationships the player feels: 8.** Hob has a complete arc, from looking up to staying or going. Sallow's "Sixty years I kept it. Don't keep it." lands. The Lamplighter and Maud trail.
- **Clarity: 7.** The body reads cleanly. But the spine's second sentence misstates its own story (problem 2), and the core rule doesn't hold together (problem 1).
- **The idea: 8.** "Let nothing happen" grown into the world's pulse; keeping against letting go, run through three characters.
- **World mystery and environmental storytelling: 8.** Cutting the cutaways fixed this. "Every lamp in the valley slides away with them" at the Levee is the best reveal in the five storylines.
- **Theme carried by mechanics: 7.** There are strong pieces: lanterns that cost a glint, walking the circle, an epilogue played under the rule. But the rule contradicts the world.
- **Antagonist as a person: 7.** The ending finally gives Maud a real choice. Before that she gets two scenes, and her one deed happens off-screen.
- **Structure and the ending's cost: 7.** The choice now tests Vesper's need, and Cut tempts by giving her Hob. But Light's cost doesn't follow from anything (problem 1). Act III is three short beats.
- **Originality: 7.** Several beats are borrowed. The grieving parent who stills the world is Ori's Kuro template. A mentor drawing the hunter away is stock. Sitting still until the antagonist relents is Undertale's sparing. Killing the source versus healing it echoes Hollow Knight's Radiance endings and Nine Sols' final choice.
- **Fit with the owner's four ideas: 8.** "Underestimated" becoming "couldn't bear to look" is still the best version.
- **Fit with the existing Grove: 7.** It needs Hob, a Sallow NPC with a blocking beat, the swell-and-flicker firefly system and a lampwing rework. The Grove also gives Vesper every move, while the story hands them out one region at a time.
- **Balance: 8.** World, people and cost each carry weight. This is the right shape.

## 2. Round 9's problems

- **The choice didn't test Vesper: mostly fixed.** One path is forcing and the other is offering, and Hob's leaving depends on what she became. The moths' cost is concrete but has no logic behind it.
- **Cutaways spent the mystery: fixed.**
- **The ember timeline: partly fixed.** The body is mended. The spine breaks it again: "puts out the last wild fire". The fire survives.
- **No midpoint reversal: partly fixed.** "Sallow was Wim's moth" is real and well planted. But it reframes a side character, not the journey. The prologue's notched moth together with Sallow's notched wing gives it away by minute ten.
- **The Lamplighter's guilt was only asserted: fixed.** It now circles the empty water. Its arc then stops (problem 3).

## 3. Remaining problems, ranked

**1. The central rule contradicts the world it lives in.** "A lamp *will* strike anything that repeats itself inside its beam." But the Quiet is repetition. Hob paces "the same circle", the fireflies flash "in rows", and the Stag "has drunk the same three sips for sixty years". None of them is ever struck. At the reunion, "doing the same thing twice means death", yet Vesper walks "lap after lap". The rule also fights the thorn-blade combo. Worst of all, Light's cost is arbitrary. Maud looks at a moth tenderly, and "every lamp has turned with her eyes, onto the moths", so now they strike. Why would her love make the lamps hunt?
*Fix:* being struck means being *taken into the swell* (greyed, made to keep time), not killed. Creatures already in the Quiet are already taken, so beams pass over them. The beam narrows with each repeat and takes on the third, which also covers the laps and combos. Then let Light's cost come from the theme: Maud has finally seen the moths, and she loves them the only way she knows, by keeping them. Her gaze does to the moths what it did to the world.

**2. The spine misstates the plot.** "In answer, something on the dam puts out the last wild fire" gets the cause wrong (the Choir breaking, not waking Hob) and the outcome wrong (the ember survives). The spine is the one thing a player retells.
*Fix:* "Every crack she makes, the dam clamps down harder. Its great lamp hunts the last wild fire, and the old moth who raised her dies leading it away."

**3. Maud and the Lamplighter end thin.** The Lamplighter "follows them in" and then has no beat in either ending. "The lampwings become bats again" was never planted. Maud never acts toward Vesper before the kitchen.
*Fix:* give the Lamplighter the kitchen's decisive gesture: it is the first lamp to look at Vesper on purpose, the eye that killed Sallow now turned on her while she sits still. Give Maud one on-screen habit at the Levee that is Wim's, such as touching each moth-stone as she passes without looking at it.

**4. The midpoint is visible early and lands sideways.** "One of them has a notch in its wing" in the prologue, then "Sallow has a notched wing" in the Grove: an attentive player solves it straight away.
*Fix:* drop the notch from the prologue, so the notched carving at Lowmere is the first match. Then make the reveal land on the player's own play. Every lantern they have rested at is a copy of the lamp a child died carrying, so the rest points become memorials. Give "older than any moth should be" its one intended answer, for example that the ember has kept her alive.

**5. The world's people are unaccounted for.** The prologue shows "a steeple ... under a calm reservoir", yet Lowmere is walkable, with lit windows. Where did everyone go, and is Maud the last one left? If the dam drowned old Lowmere, that is a huge and unspoken thing.
*Fix:* decide, and seed it. One option: no child has been born since the Quiet, because nothing new happens, so Maud is the last. That would be a Hollow Knight-grade silence.

## 4. The one change that would most raise it

Make the beam rule coherent: struck means taken into the swell, and the third repeat takes. Then make Light's cost come from Maud's love ("now she keeps the moths") instead of being a penalty. That one fix repairs the reunion, Sallow's death, the combat and the ending at once.

## 5. Verdict

**Close, and one pass away.** People, world and cost are now in balance, and the Levee reveal and Hob's arc are at the bar. A narrative lead would still send it back for two reasons. The game's single rule contradicts the Quiet it belongs to, and that breaks the reunion and the good ending's price. And the spine misstates the plot. Fix the rule, the spine and the Lamplighter's last beat, and it is a yes.
