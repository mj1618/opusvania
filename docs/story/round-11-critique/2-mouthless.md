# Critique: MOUTHLESS (round 10)

## 1. Scores

- **Relationships the player feels: 8.** Sorrel now has a full arc in play: he saves her, she saves him, he leaves her, he comes back, he pays for it. Dun's turn at the fork is the best beat in the document. The root-tip "like a dog at a door" is a quiet third bond.
- **Clarity: 7.** The fable is easy to follow. The spine is not: sentence two still has three clauses and a "while", and "the cocoon it cannot spin for itself" means nothing until Night 7.
- **The idea: 8.** "Taken never fills, given does" is now on screen. Love that can't stop being needed (Dun, the Nursling with the woman) is a real thought.
- **World mystery and environmental storytelling: 8.** The rim with 113 lines, "She came back.", the root *at* her lips, the jars scored by a horn and never opened. HK-grade.
- **Theme carried by mechanics: 9.** Carrying makes her visible, she heals only where she has given, Shed spends scales, Spin strips her abilities. Nothing here is decoration.
- **Antagonist as a person: 7.** Dun is a person and turns on screen. Act III's opposition is a stranger (the first nurse) and a newborn with no history.
- **Structure and the ending's cost: 8.** The Warm Room into Night 5 is excellent. Act III is overloaded.
- **Originality: 7.** The last act tracks *Will of the Wisps*: an abandoned child's hunger blights the land (Shriek), and the hero spends her body so the world can heal (Ori becoming the new Spirit Tree). Thread, silk-binding and a world laced with feeding threads also come close to *Silksong*'s Pharloom.
- **Fit with the owner's four ideas: 8.** Literal wetware; "underestimated" works as a blind spot; the sentience question holds to the last line.
- **Fit with the Grove: 8.** Re-meaning the gold veins as the hungry thing is still a great, cheap reversal. But see the ember below.
- **Balance: 8.** Ori's fable, HK's traces, a Nine Sols reversal ("It has been feeding her"), all carried by a sibling.

## 2. The previous critique's problems

1. Thesis invisible until the climax: **fixed.** Night 2's hare and the three glints show the rule.
2. The reset contradicts the woman: **fixed.** "except the old one at the woman's lips, which goes on dripping" does it with one image.
3. Sorrel forgiven too early: **fixed.** Night 5 is played alone, and her first Offer to a person goes to him.
4. Dun's turn not a choice: **fixed.** "At the fork she stops" is a played turn.
5. Flaw asserted, not played: **fixed.** "Her hiding cost him. Shining saves him." The spine got shorter but is still not one push and pull.

## 3. Remaining problems, ranked

1. **No one in the cast has a stake in the final choice.** Stay vs Spin is argued only by the Nursling. Sorrel "waits at the door, then goes" in one ending and walks beside her in the other, but he never pulls either way. Dun's want was settled on Night 5. The central question ("let them stop needing you") belongs to Dun and the woman, not to Vesper. *Fix:* put someone she loves in the Nursling's offer. Sorrel has no scales left after Night 7, so the second cradle weaves around *him*. Stay keeps both siblings alive, kept together. Spin spends him too. Then the choice is between Sorrel's want ("live their week as moths do") and the world.
2. **The Nursling bond is cut and never paid off.** The root-tip that knew her dies on Night 5, and the newborn "doesn't know Vesper". So the last line ("was that love, or had it only learned her?") asks about a mind that is gone. *Fix:* in the climax, let the newborn learn her again on screen, in the same gestures the root-tip used: a root waiting at the edge of her light, and the dish opening in her shape before it gives. The question then lands on the newborn.
3. **Act III is crowded, and its boss arrives cold.** Night 6 holds Sorrel's return, a caterpillar feeding, the Thread wrap, and a boss introduced the night you fight her. Night 7 opens with yet another demonstration (*"They have watched a cocoon be a birth"* is an authorial caption). *Fix:* seed the first nurse in Act I. Dun's haul-path could pass her jars, or Dun could visit her. Then cut the Night 7 hatch and let the climax teach it.
4. **Continuity breaks the ending's best image.** Sleepers "never wake", yet on Night 2 "the fox runs", and in the Spin epilogue "the fox wakes" again (in Stay, Dun cradles a fox). The ember was spent on the Grove's second lantern, yet on Night 4 "She lays the ember by the dish". *Fix:* on Night 2 the fox is saved, not woken: Vesper stops the hauling and the fox stays asleep. The first waking belongs to the epilogue. Make the Night 4 warmth a different ember, or have Vesper carry the Grove's on.
5. **The Will of the Wisps outline.** Remove the names and you have Shriek plus Ori's self-gift. The difference is that the child is born, not killed; keep it, but the final shot still sells sacrifice for renewal. *Fix:* end on what is new. A first image of whatever hatches (or the cocoon's first movement under Dun's watch) instead of the hero lying down at dawn.

## 4. The one change that would most raise it

**Make Sorrel part of the Nursling's offer, so the final choice is a choice between people.** The second cradle weaves around her ruined brother. Stay keeps them both, together and forever, the way the woman was kept. Spin spends both of them so the baby can be born. Then the central question becomes Vesper's own, Sorrel's want finally collides with hers, and the ending costs someone the player loves.

## 5. Verdict

**Close.** All five of last round's notes are fixed, and fixed with scenes, not captions. A narrative lead would greenlight the world, Acts I and II, Dun's turn and the Spin sequence. They would send it back for:
- a final choice the cast doesn't pull on;
- the Nursling relationship dying on Night 5 with nothing after it;
- an Act III that adds a new character and over-teaches in its last 15% (it is the *Will of the Wisps* shape most clearly there);
- two continuity slips that undercut the epilogue.

Problems 1 and 2 are each one scene in the climax. That would make it a yes.
