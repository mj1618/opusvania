# Round 11 critique: WHAT THE LANTERNS BURN (round 10)

## 1. Scores

- **Relationships the player feels: 8.** The reversal at the Root ("Vesper is at the glass when she steps out, and lays a wing on her cheek. Again.") is earned by a hundred played re-forms. The best new beat is that the wing-on-cheek is "the Taper's gesture, and she has been copying it", learned from a dead human. But Kindled drops Sedge, Tallow and Clasp entirely.
- **Clarity: 6.** The spine retells. The ember's history does not (problem 1). Act III has no stated goal. The wall shows "hundreds of faces", but glints only ever show hands.
- **The idea: 8.** "The people who loved the dead most planted it" is still the strongest sentence in the set. Making the mentor's tenderness a copied human gesture deepens it.
- **World mystery and environmental storytelling: 8.** The hands fix works: "a hand smoothing hair", and the hand-prints "where someone held it last". The heart and the moth carving are unaccounted for (problem 3).
- **Theme carried by mechanics: 9.** The shared glass, Flares that drain the Root, and "every time the *player* falls, the same glass drops."
- **Antagonist as a person: 8.** Her motive is now seen: "She looks at the moth children under the dark glass." Her one weakness, the ember, is real. Her final ask ("burn the one person she herself never could") is the best line of characterisation she has had.
- **Structure and the ending's cost: 8.** Kindled is no longer rigged: "It costs exactly one life and leaves the world as it was." Planted's "burrback's shadow falls over them. Cut." is properly ambiguous.
- **Originality: 6.** There is a new derivative, and it's the biggest one: **Dark Souls**. Bonfires fuelled by souls and humanity, a Firekeeper, and a final choice between Link the Fire (keep the light burning on a sacrifice, take up the role: "The Taper gives Vesper the reed") and letting it go dark. Even the ending is named "Kindled". On top of that: moths serving a light (Hollow Knight's Moth Tribe and the Radiance), a civilisation running on harvested people (Nine Sols' Apeman), and monsters that turn out to be nursemaids (Nausicaä's Ohmu).
- **Fit with the owner's four ideas: 7.** The moth is underestimated ("a moth carrying none is nothing") and the sentience question stays open. But the out-of-control AI has shrunk into a grief garden with guards. "The Bequest is a crowd, not a mind," so nothing in the world feels like an intelligence that took over.
- **Fit with the existing Grove: 7.** The danger is restored. But it takes eight changes, the ember's fetch makes no sense in the fiction, and the heart is ignored.
- **Balance: 8.** This is Nine Sols' mix. The old forest finally appears once ("It doesn't look at her") and that is enough.

## 2. Round 9's problems

- **Kindled rigged, stakes misstated: fixed.** It costs one life, the world is unchanged, and neither ending passes a verdict.
- **The stranger has no face: fixed.** It is the Taper's beloved and the source of her gesture.
- **The human origin can't be found: fixed.** Hands on glints, on the ember's shadow and on the bulbs.
- **Rule load: partly fixed.** Updraft and the grubs are cut. But Section 8 still needs eight scene changes, and Act II adds snuffing and relighting.
- **The Grove loses its threat or gives itself away: fixed.** Dives steal, she enters carrying glints, and Tallow stays in frame.
- **Sedge's turn unmotivated (round 9 section 2): partly fixed.** "The same gesture as the glint burned for him" is a cause, but a cryptic one. Is it the same person, or just a gesture they share?

## 3. Remaining problems, ranked

**1. The ember's story doesn't hold together, and the climax hangs on it.** "She hid it high, and Tallow kept it." But the Grove, "the pilgrim's first grove", makes every pilgrim fetch it from that island and set it in glass, where it should burn. The Taper then shields it with her reed, leaving "reed-ash... from a hundred lifetimes of visits", and it somehow goes back to the island before the next pilgrim comes. So who carries it back? Why would she build a rite that puts her beloved in fire? *Fix:* make it her rite on purpose. Once a lifetime she sends a pilgrim to bring it to the glass so that she can watch its gesture lit, holding her reed over it. Tallow carries it home each time, and that is their old, wordless truce. Show one shot of that in Act II.

**2. Act III has no goal.** In Act II Vesper wants to stop the burning. At the Root she is just "a moth carrying the ember toward a lantern". Why toward a lantern? Why the Root? *Fix:* she brings the ember home to plant it among the hand-printed bulbs, as Tallow wants. The Taper's fall interrupts the planting, so the choice comes out of the action instead of being staged.

**3. The heart, the Grove's actual climax, means nothing here.** In the scene, Vesper feeds her leftover glints to the heart and the grove wakes. In this fiction that is feeding people to the old forest, the most loaded thing the player does in the scene, and the outline never mentions it. The carved moth on the standing stone is also unexplained. *Fix:* make the heart the old forest's mouth. The carving is a moth because moths have always fed it. Replayed after the Hush, waking the grove reads as a meal.

**4. Kindled abandons the cast.** Tallow wanted the ember "in the earth", and Clasp and Sedge stood with Vesper. In Kindled none of them gets a frame. *Fix:* one shot. Tallow at the edge of the new light, stopped again where it always stopped.

**5. Loose rules at the key moments.** "Her loose light": does a faded moth become a glint? That is a revelation the outline doesn't know it has made. Also, where does Vesper re-form at the Root once its glass is empty? And "faces" on the wall clashes with the hands everywhere else. *Fix:* the wall shows a hand laid on a cheek, scratched over and over. Decide, out loud, what Vesper's light is when she fades.

## 4. The one change that would most raise it

**Make the Grove the Taper's secret rite** (problem 1). Every pilgrim's first grove exists so that once a lifetime she can see her beloved lit, and her reed-light at the summit at the wake is her keeping it from burning. Tallow carries it home after every visit. This one change makes the ember coherent and explains the reed-ash. It retroactively turns the Grove we have built into the Taper's love story. It also gives the final ask its full weight: she spent a hundred lifetimes arranging *not* to burn it, and now she asks Vesper to.

## 5. Verdict

**Close.** Every round-9 problem except rule load is fixed, and the Root reversal is greenlight quality. Four things separate it from yes. The ember's backstory falls apart under one question. Act III has no objective. The existing scene's heart is left meaningless. And the ending structure is Dark Souls' Link the Fire, down to the name "Kindled". Fixes 1–3 are a day's work. The Dark Souls echo needs a decision: own it with a third, specific image, or rename and reshape Kindled so it isn't "take up the flame".
