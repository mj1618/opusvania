# Mouthless: casual player retell

## 1. The story in three sentences
A pale growth has put the whole world to sleep in pods, and only moths slip past because it only notices things that eat. Umber, a moth with a week to live, reaches its heart and finds an abandoned lab-grown brain that "feeds" everyone so nobody leaves it. When she warms it, it notices moths and comes for them, and she must join it, burn it, or spend her life wrapping it in a cocoon so the world wakes.

## 2. Characters
- **Umber**: moth with a week to live; wants to save her wood, then everyone.
- **Sorrel**: her brother; wants his partner, then more time with Umber.
- **The Hush**: the brain-thing; wants everyone kept close and fed.
- **Dun**: root-armoured badger servant; fetches for the Hush, then guards Umber.
- **Tallow**: last awake human; wants to burn the pods and find his sister.
- **The forest**: a slow old mind; wants to give its warmth away.

## 3. The enemy
A lab-grown brain abandoned when the lab closed. Its roots put the world in feeding pods; nobody wakes or is born. It wants nobody to leave it again, because leaving hurts it.

## 4. Endings
- **Spin** (true): moths cocoon the Hush, Umber dies, people wake, something new stirs inside.
- **Stay**: she joins the pods; everyone warm forever, nothing born again.
- **Burn**: Tallow's coal kills it and most sleepers; she gets one dawn dance.

## 5. What confused me
- The ball game in the memory: why is a brain playing ball? Only the dev note explains.
- Why can Umber see a face in a pod when Tallow sees "grey stone"? Then on Night 6 he sees one anyway.
- Why does Tallow lie down in a pod on Night 7? It just happens offstage.
- Do pods stop aging? The partner comes out "no older", yet Burn says only people kept days survive.
- "Fades" and "re-forms": is she dying and respawning in the story, or is that just the game?
- Spin is lovely but abstract ("stalks spun into threads"). What did the Hush become?

## 6. Memorable moment / would I buy it
Sorrel curled round his partner's tiny pod; you think he's dead, then he shivers. Also Dun snapping its own root to guard Umber's door.
Yes, probably. "A moth with a week to live, against a thing that can't see moths" hooks me instantly, and Sorrel, Dun and Tallow give me people to care about. Finishing depends on the lab flashback being clearer in play than on paper.
