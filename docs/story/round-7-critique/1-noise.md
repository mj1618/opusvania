# Round 7 critique: NOISE (round 6, balanced version)

## 1. Scores

- **Relationships the player feels: 7.** Hob is earned ("He starts to wait for her at the foot of every climb"). Sallow gets three beats. Maud gets one touch, and the Lamplighter is a boss.
- **Clarity: 7.** The spine retells well. But it contradicts itself on sight, and the step from Hob to Maud ("To finish what she started, she has to make the mother look") is never explained.
- **The idea: 8.** "A whole town asked for it" and a grief that becomes a blind spot are both strong. This is the Nine Sols kind of reversal, turned inside out.
- **World mystery and environmental storytelling: 7.** The coats, the dishes, the air holes and the tape are excellent. Prologue shot 3 ("A glow on every windowsill") half spends the Lowmere reveal before the game starts.
- **Theme carried by mechanics: 8.** Seeing habits, moves learned from a dead child's drawings, the staying boss, and a door only Hob can open all carry the theme. The question is whether the habit rule can actually be played.
- **Antagonist as a person: 5.** Maud's whole relationship to Vesper is "Nothing. She cannot see her." An antagonist who exerts no pull is a setting, not a person.
- **Structure and the ending's cost: 7.** The acts are sound. Sallow's death changes nothing in play, and in the Offbeat Hob walks off happy, so the stated cost is gentle.
- **Originality: 6.** Several beats are borrowed (see problem 2).
- **Fit with the owner's four ideas: 8.** All four are present, but "underestimated" fights with "can't see".
- **Fit with the existing Grove: 7.** The ember, the carving, the heart and the lanterns map beautifully. Lampwings that dive and burrbacks that ram would have to become blind sentries and rammers on the beat, and the scene needs a swell system plus two NPCs.
- **Balance: 8.** This is the right mix: lore found in places, as in Hollow Knight; a reversal, as in Nine Sols; companionship, as in Ori. It tips slightly toward systems, and the antagonist is missing.

## 2. Round 6 against round 4

**Gained.** Sallow is now a person rather than a quoted line. The ember take is a small drama ("blocks her with her wings spread, then steps aside"). Hob's companionship is played, not stated: dropped glints, riding his charge, hiding under his shell. The quarrel is real ("goes on alone, as she always has"), so his betrayal has a cause on Vesper's side. "Neither of them asked Hob" finally says the Vesper and Maud mirror in five words. The spine and the cast table make the pushes and pulls readable.

**Lost.** Very little of the world, which is the point of this version. What was lost is restraint. Round 4 let Lowmere speak for itself. Round 6 narrates it: "The old woman was never a prisoner, and nobody conquered this world." That is the writer explaining the twist. Round 4's prologue also opened on black and a single amber swell, then "One flashes late", which taught the visual language with no words. Restore that, and cut the narration at the midpoint.

## 3. The five problems that matter, ranked

**1. Maud never acts.** Kuro hunts. The Pale King lied, and that was a deed. The Sols farm people. Maud walks, misses a step, and looks away. The player can pity her, but cannot fear or resent her, so forgiving her in the ending costs nothing.
*Fix:* give her one deed, done without looking. After the Grove's ring she senses the flicker, cannot bear it, and asks the dish for more stillness. The Lamplighter is sent to put out the last wild fire, and Sallow dies defending the ember. Now the Offbeat means something: Vesper lights the hearth of the woman whose wish killed her mother figure, using that mother figure's coal. When the Lamplighter later lowers its lamp, it is guilt, not mercy after a boss fight.

**2. Sallow's death is borrowed and has no consequence.** "Curled around the coal, and it is still warm. She kept it alive until Vesper came back" is Kuro shielding her egg, and Naru collapsing, in Ori and the Blind Forest. Vesper shows no reaction and nothing in play changes. The cause is vague too ("the storm has reached the ember island").
*Fix:* let the game's own rule kill her. Sallow, who taught "never the same path twice", flies the same search path night after night looking for Vesper, and is caught as a habit. Her love made her predictable, which is exactly the lesson Vesper must learn for Hob, and here it is fatal. Then change the game: from then on, the lanterns are hers to tend.

**3. The law of sight contradicts itself.** "It can't see Vesper", and yet "It saw her, and it decided she was nothing." A player can't keep both. The second rule then makes "underestimated" a matter of blindness, not judgement.
*Fix:* make the weighing beat a replay of the prologue's boot. The lamp finds her, flinches, and turns its face away, exactly as Maud did. The moths weren't left alone because they were judged harmless. They were left alone because something couldn't bear to look at them. That keeps the owner's idea and makes it sadder.

**4. Too many rules, and one of them may not be playable.** There is the swell and the lub-dub, habit lock-on, amber rings and lilac scatter, Keeping Time, chance walled up in pods, swarms that hide things, and stones that teach moves. Metroidvania play is made of repeated arcs, so "do the same thing twice and they find you" will feel arbitrary in the middle of a fight.
*Fix:* limit habit sight to inside a lamp's beam, so lamps work as sentries. Cut the amber and lilac landing feedback. Keep two readable signals: an even swell for the Quiet, a flicker for life.

**5. The Hob arc and the endings run on stock templates.** The settled friend you win back by refusing to fight is Celeste's Badeline, or Undertale's spare. Three endings with a hidden condition for the true one is Hollow Knight's pattern. Storm and Stillness are punishments, not real choices. In the Offbeat, the real cost is buried in the post-credits: "Lanterns no longer hide her."
*Fix:* put that cost in front of the player. Making Maud look ends the moths' invisibility for every moth, forever, which is everything Sallow lived for. Vesper gives up her people's hiding place to let one old woman grieve properly. Show other moths, seen for the first time, scattering from a lamp. That cost belongs in Nine Sols.

## 4. The one change that would most raise it

Make Maud an actor. She orders the fire put out without looking, and Sallow dies of it. That one change gives the antagonist a deed, gives Sallow's death a cause and a meaning, gives the Lamplighter's change of heart a debt, and turns lighting the hearth into forgiveness the player has paid for.

## 5. Verdict

**Close.** The world, the idea and Hob are at the bar. What separates it from yes is a passive antagonist, a borrowed death with no consequence, a contradiction in the central rule, and an ending whose real price is hidden after the credits.
