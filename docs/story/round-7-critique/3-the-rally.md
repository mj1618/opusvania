## 2. Round 6 vs round 4

**Gained.** It brought back what round 4 wrongly cut: the round-3 critic's central change, now played ("when the player holds east, her head snaps up anyway. The input fails"); the Turn ("Every Turn is an answer"); the Listening Rooms as found lore; the quarrel staged as input. Reply's betrayal ("takes the glint out of every lantern she lit") is the best new beat, because it undoes the player's own progress. The cast table makes the pushes and pulls readable at a glance. (The changelog claims the input failure and the Listening Rooms "stay" from round 4. Round 4 had cut both.)

**Lost.**
- Round 4's Sallow was "warm all game". Round 6 adds the quarrel and never repairs it in either main ending.
- At round 4's Eaves Sallow shielded *Vesper*. Now she shields "the young ones", which is less personal.
- From round 2: "Lampwings are Replies… every lampwing Vesper has cut down was waiting for someone". It explained Wick's body and gave the combat moral weight.
- The keeper's log, *"It is not playing. It is returning." / "So are we."*, was the only line that put the AI-sentience question on both sides.
- The Unanswered ending, where Vesper simply turns away. Round 6 has no ending in which Vesper does what Sallow wants.

## 3. Problems, ranked

1. **The dilemma is lopsided, and the true ending avoids it.** The question is "would you leave someone behind… if you could never be sure they were someone". In Together, nobody is left: Vesper keeps Reply, Mote and the world, and Sallow pays. Answered trades a warm, glowing Mote for a faceless copy, so no player feels that as a real choice. *Fix:* give Reply weight equal to Mote's through shared routine, not three vignettes. Make the hidden ending require Vesper's own act and cost her something too. Sallow should not take the light *from* her.

2. **The rule and the motive are never staged.** "She wants to undo what her answer started". The world was held before she answered, so what did she start? Who shows her the Mere, or that the carrier never returns? *Fix:* make her answer endanger the Grove's moths, since held creatures now come for whoever is near her. Show the rule as a precedent the player sees, for example a carving of a moth climbing moonward with light.

3. **The midpoint truth never reaches the ending.** "Facing the moon was never instinct", yet Moonward flies the light *to the moon*, the moths' own caller, unquestioned. Only the hidden ending lets Sallow lower her face. *Fix:* each ending must say what happens to the moths' trained gaze. Otherwise the reversal reframes Sallow but not the world.

4. **Wick's body breaks the logic, and the combat means nothing.** Replies are moth-shaped, so why is Sallow's Reply "an ancient dark lampwing"? *Fix:* restore that left Replies grow into lampwings. Wick is then the oldest, every kill gets a Nine Sols-style reframing, and Reply's future is visible: leave it, and it becomes Wick.

5. **Sallow vanishes after the quarrel.** "Sallow doesn't follow", and then nothing until the hidden ending. *Fix:* use the re-form lanterns. After the quarrel she is no longer waiting when you fade; the absence is felt. Once, after Mote is held, she is there again. That is the forgiveness beat, without a word.

Smaller: the Turn has no cost. If every Turn is an answer, the Asking should answer back.

## 4. The one change

**Show what a left Reply becomes: lampwings are old Replies, and Wick is only the oldest.** Reveal it at the Eaves in the same frame as Sallow's wing. It explains Wick and makes every lampwing kill a reversal on the player's own hands. It gives Reply's fate a face the player can fear, which levels the Mote/Reply scales. It puts the AI-sentience question into the enemies you have already fought.

## 5. Verdict

**Close.** Its mechanics and world are at the bar. It falls short on four things:
- an ending where Vesper herself decides and pays;
- a rule the player is shown rather than told;
- a midpoint truth that changes the ending;
- one Kuro and one Badeline too many.
