# Round 7 critique: WHAT THE LANTERNS BURN (round 6)

## 1. Scores

- **Relationships the player feels: 7.** Sedge's race ("loses every time, and you can wait for him") and the glint kept "in her cape instead of burning it, because it came from him" are felt. The Taper, whose relationship the climax spends, gets the prologue, one fight and the wake. Sedge and Clasp change their minds off screen.
- **Clarity: 7.** The three-sentence spine is the best in the set. Underneath it, who can re-form ("Pilgrims are the exception", then "Sedge re-forms young"), where Sedge's glint is at any moment, and why "the valley's lanterns go dark" all blur.
- **The idea: 8.** "The people who loved the dead most planted it" is a real idea. Moths that live on burned grief-bots is a Nine Sols-grade reversal.
- **World mystery and environmental storytelling: 8.** Bulbs "shaped like lantern glass", the hundreds of scratched faces that are all "the same face", wing-patches cut from Vesper's own cocoon, and reed-ash beside a lowered count are all found, not told.
- **Theme carried by mechanics: 9.** The shared glass ("Your deaths spend her"), Flare as "throwing someone at them", Plant as a real trade, Brace and Moonward all mean something. Its rules break at the two moments that matter most (problem 4).
- **Antagonist as a person: 7.** "The Taper doesn't look surprised" and "won't look at Vesper" are strong. She never does anything tender, or anything doubtful, that the player sees before the Root.
- **Structure and the ending's cost: 8.** The Root is now on screen, and Planted costs her, Sedge ("he has a week") and every sanctuary. Kindled is three lines long and gives no reason to choose it.
- **Originality: 6.** Moths serving a devouring light is Hollow Knight's Moth Tribe and the Radiance. A mentor living on burned people is Nine Sols' Solarians and the Apeman. "The Taper covers her the way Clasp covered her pods" is Kuro's wings over her egg in Ori, and Clasp's last phase is the same image again. The mentor boss "with Vesper's own moves, older" is a stock mirror fight.
- **Fit with the owner's four ideas: 7.** The wetware and the DishBrain-like loop ("the same small gesture every time") are grounded, and sentience stays open. The underestimate shrinks to a dodge in one storm.
- **Fit with the existing Grove: 6.** The ember, the Grove's centrepiece fetch, has vanished from the story. All that's left of it is "the ember's empty cup". The scene also needs the Taper, Sedge and dancing moths, and "her first lanterns, alone" contradicts a prologue that sends them out together.
- **Balance: 7.** The mix is close to Nine Sols. But five full arcs with turns, in this word count, crowd out the world. The old forest, set up as "hungry to have its body back", is never seen again.

## 2. Round 6 against round 4

**Gained.** A cast with clear wants. The round-5 "one change" (shared glass, the Taper spent on screen at the Root). The facing gesture is now consistent ("turns her face back to the glass", later "toward the earth"). The prologue is no longer Ori's rescue montage. The same-face scratches and the cocoon silk keep the ambiguity alive. Pods are no longer free checkpoints, there is one fade cost, and the endings stay inside this valley. And the best line in the document: "Vesper and Sedge sit under the moon with a week left."

**Lost.** First, the **ember**: in round 4 it was "someone you've felt", with cupped hands around Vesper, her light for all of Act II, and the hands that close over the Taper. It was what the Grove is built around, and it should come back. Second, **planting shown on day one** ("A glint left uncollected sinks and sprouts"), which let the Grove argue for the ending. Third, **Vesper's flaw**, "devotion... The player shares the flaw". She now has a want and nothing else. Fourth, round 4's open last image, "A grub on a green leaf turns toward a pod, and the game cuts before it chooses."

## 3. The five problems that matter, ranked

**1. The bond the climax spends is the one the player has played least.** Sedge gets "On the road". The Taper gets the prologue, the Combs and the wake. The player has to grieve "She doesn't get up" for a mentor they barely walked beside. *Fix:* make her the Act I companion, like Naru or Ku: she waits at lanterns, catches a bad glide, and shares the glass from the first fall onward (see 4). Shrink Sedge's Act I to the race and the gift.

**2. The changes of mind happen between scenes.** Sedge goes from "He doesn't look at Vesper as he leaves" to laying "his wing over hers on the soil" with nothing in between. Clasp forgives, then leads the burrbacks against the grubs, then turns them away, which is three turns in one act. *Fix:* give Sedge one witnessed turn. When "every moth in the Censer turns its back on Vesper", Sedge alone turns round, and he is in the Root storm because he followed her. For Clasp, merge the forgiveness and the lowest point into one scene: the Brace over the children *is* the forgiveness.

**3. The ember and the old forest are dropped, and the Grove loses its meaning.** The scene's hardest fetch, and the heart's tiered waking, mean nothing in Acts II and III. *Fix:* the ember is Tallow's charge. That empty cup is already on Tallow's island. Tallow's want, "To get one glint back into the earth", becomes *that* glint, the one Vesper took in the Grove. Show what the unfed heart does in Planted (it sleeps, and the old wood keeps its moss), or cut the old forest as a party.

**4. The rules break where the story is most important.** At the lowest point she "re-forms far off under the bare moon", with no lantern, which breaks the game's only death rule. At the Hush, Tallow plants the glint, "a pod begins to grow", and then Vesper takes it, because "She can't plant it yet", which is an ability gate posing as a story beat. *Fix:* at the lowest point she doesn't re-form. Tallow snatches her fading light out of the dark, the way lampwings rescue loose glints, and carries it to the moon. That is Tallow's one deed. At the Hush, Vesper pulls the sprouting glint out of the earth herself, and Tallow watches. Now it's her flaw, not a gate.

**5. The Taper's gesture makes Vesper's choice for her.** At the Root the Taper "turns her face toward the earth", so Planted is obedience, and Kindled defies a dying wish for no reason the story gives. *Fix:* the Taper turns Vesper's face toward the glass one last time, as she always has. Planting has to be Vesper's refusal of her, made with Sedge's wing on hers. Kindled then becomes the loving, devoted choice, the flaw winning, and it deserves a real final shot rather than "Vesper walks behind her."

## 4. The one change that would most raise it

**Make the re-form animation the relationship.** In Act I, every time Vesper fades, the Taper is at the glass when she steps out, and her wing steadies Vesper's cheek. After the Hush, Vesper steps out alone. At the Root it reverses: Vesper waits at the glass, steadying the Taper each time she re-forms, until she doesn't come out. A metroidvania's most-repeated moment becomes hundreds of small kindnesses, and the climax spends them one by one.

## 5. Verdict

**Close.** Its mechanics-as-meaning is the strongest in the set, and the idea and its found lore are at the bar. Four things separate it from yes. The mentor's love is told, not played. Sedge's and Clasp's turns happen off screen. The Grove's ember has been orphaned. And the last choice belongs to the Taper, not to Vesper.
