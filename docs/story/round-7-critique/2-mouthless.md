# Critique: MOUTHLESS (round 6)

*Note: `round-6/2-mouthless.md` is byte-identical to `round-4/2-mouthless.md` and is still headed "round 4". This storyline was not revised for the Owner Correction, so this critique judges the round-4 text against it.*

## 1. Scores

- **Relationships the player feels: 6.** Dun is a real person, and her arc runs rival, then "two nurses of a child that knows neither of them", then ally. Sorrel is two bookends with four nights of absence between them. The woman is a body in a cradle.
- **Clarity: 6.** "A moth feeds the starving baby under the forest" can be retold. But a player could not say why wrapping the baby in silk saves the world, or why the baby "restarts".
- **The idea: 8.** "Can you love something enough to let it stop needing you?" A mouthless giver, and a hunger that learns her shape. It is strong and it belongs to this story alone.
- **World mystery and environmental storytelling: 7.** The rim, the carving drawn from beneath, and the mossed torch are all good. There is less here than in round 2, and some of it is spelled out where round 2 left it implied.
- **Theme carried by mechanics: 8.** Carrying makes her brighter. Offer, healing only at lanterns, Spin stripping abilities loop by loop, and Stay refilling her health all express the theme.
- **Antagonist as a person: 5.** There is no villain. Dun is spent as a boss on Night 2. The Lure and the Hive are allegories with thesis captions.
- **Structure and the ending's cost: 7.** The midpoint and the lowest point are excellent, and the price of Spin is felt in her wings. Act III is thin, and Spin's mechanism is fuzzy.
- **Originality: 7.** Several beats are derivative: Shriek (*Will of the Wisps*: an unloved creature becomes the land's devourer); the White Lady (*Hollow Knight*: a parent bound in roots beside what she abandoned); Spin as *Hollow Knight*'s Sealed Siblings (binding a god in your own body's material, then being lost); and Sorrel's return as the generic cavalry-of-friends finale.
- **Fit with the owner's four ideas: 8.** It is literal wetware. "Underestimated" is a blind spot rather than a decision, which is defensible. The sentience ambiguity is well kept.
- **Fit with the Grove: 8.** The ending beat is re-meant cheaply and well. The root-tip and Dun's cameo are new render work.
- **Balance: 7.** It has Ori's fable shape and a strong idea. From Night 3 on it goes lonely without Hollow Knight's density of lore to carry the loneliness.

## 2. Gained over round 2, and what was lost

**Gained.** Dun is the best addition in any of these drafts: "the same walk, the other direction" is now *her* walk. The root-tip makes the Nursling present. "It already knows her" turns the Warm Room into a reunion. Cutting the Firstling restores the systemic "never full". The ending now offers a real life ("her wings grow their scales back") instead of a death that was already guaranteed.

**Lost, and these should come back:**
- **The legible premise.** Round 2 has her catch a glint: "holds it, and can do nothing with it." Round 6 has "Close on her face: she has no mouth." On Vesper's already-simple face, no player will read that. Mouthlessness has to be shown by an action.
- **The cocoon rule, seeded early.** In round 2's prologue a caterpillar goes rigid and spins, and the burrback "loses interest, as if a light had gone out." That taught the ending's grammar in the first minute.
- **Why Spin works.** Round 2 says it outright: "This one can't spin, and it is cold." Round 6 drops the reason.
- **Kindnesses.** Health upgrades that creatures give her were mechanics-as-relationship. They are gone.
- **The counted rim.** "One more line" is weaker than the 114th.

## 3. Problems, ranked

1. **Sorrel is a function, not a relationship.** He "asks her to come home" every night, which is a want that never moves. He leaves on Night 3 straight after she saves him, then "comes back" on Night 7 with the dancers. His change of mind happens off screen, and his return is the most generic beat in the document. *Fix:* keep him in play through the Warm Room. Give him one rescue of her, and let him make his own wrong choice, with a cost the player sees. Then show the turn: on Night 5 she climbs up empty, he sees her clear wings, and he chooses. The cavalry is earned only if we watch him decide.

2. **Spin can read as sealing or smothering, not metamorphosis.** The baby is a cloud in a dish, and "larva" is a metaphor the screen can't show. "No new line on the rim" could just as easily mean it died. *Fix:* on Night 6, Vesper gives her Thread to a starving caterpillar like the Orchard one, and the player watches a moth come out. The final choice then has a meaning the player has already seen. Also show one sign of life inside the final cocoon.

3. **The woman is the doc's richest fact, and it is wasted.** "In all these years it never drank her" is the strongest evidence in the story for love over appetite, and it sits in a subordinate clause. It also breaks the rule that it sees anything that eats, without the doc noticing. *Fix:* make that rule-break deliberate and findable. Let the Stay ending show Vesper in a second cradle beside her. Stay then reads as "being kept, like the mother was kept", and the mirror between them (she left it; Vesper stays, then lets go) becomes the theme.

4. **No antagonist with a face after Night 2. Act III is two allegories and a cavalry.** "Hunger that forgot why it was saving" is a caption, not a character. *Fix:* give the late bosses bodies tied to the cast. The Hive could be a previous nurse, Dun's predecessor, worn into a hoard, so Dun fights her own future. Make the final descent's pressure the newborn grabbing *specifically at her*, not a crowd.

5. **The rules still leak.** The spine's second sentence has four clauses. The mind forgets and the roots keep habits; mindless reaches hoard; a root-tip dies with its life; the newborn learns faster. That is still a section that has to explain itself. *Fix:* keep only what the screen shows: the grey ring, the crumbling tip, the new line, and wings opening once. Cut "old reaches hoard" to a single image in the Hives.

## 4. The one change that would most raise it

**Make Sorrel a played relationship with an on-screen change of mind.** He stays at her side until the Warm Room. He fails her once, and leaves for a reason the player understands. We see him choose to come back, at the cost of his own week, before Night 7 arrives. This repairs the cast's weakest link and gives the climax an emotional cause. It also turns "who waits for her" into something the player can play.

## 5. Verdict

**Close.** The idea, Dun, the midpoint-to-lowest-point run and the played Spin are at the bar. Three things separate it from yes:
- a brother who is present only at the edges of the story;
- an ending whose action (wrapping a god in silk) the player hasn't been taught to read as birth rather than burial;
- a mother-figure whose single loaded fact goes unused.
