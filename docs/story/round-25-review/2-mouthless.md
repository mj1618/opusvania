# Review: Mouthless (round 25), narrative director, cold read

## 1. Anchor gate: PASS

All three readers' first sentences are the litmus almost word for word: a little moth with a week to live against "a living thing" that "sealed the world asleep" and "ignores moths because they never eat". All three also reached the technical truth through play: the lore reader says "dish-grown brain", the heart reader "a lab brain grown to feed the world", and the casual reader "a lab-grown brain". The poster, the dish and the second showing of the prologue let the player discover it as a revelation, and no player-facing text is technical. The Hush is on screen and active at world scale in every act: the prologue's spreading frost-root, root steaming under the pool, the orchard of cases, a root "as thick as a tower", the world-wide pulse, the wood overrun, the dead city, and the boss itself.

Owner's ideas:
- Biological AI that took the world: **central**.
- Umber the moth saves the world: **central**.
- Moths deemed insignificant and underestimated: **central**. It is the hook, rule 2, and the midpoint reversal ("It has learned one more thing that can get away").
- Sentience is ambiguous: **central** for the Hush ("was that love, or had it only learned how to make the static stop?"). It is **minor** for the forest, which is cast but never acts as a mind.

## 2. Verdict: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES

The foundation is sound. Every problem below is a scene-level fix.

## 3. At the bar

- **A hook that is also the rule:** "never noticed moths, because moths never eat." The moth's mouthlessness is anatomy, the hook, the stealth mechanic and the theme all at once. That is Hollow Knight-grade compression.
- **The prologue pays off at the lab:** "the warm blur the sister's hand on the glass while it plays the ball game." All three readers got the reveal, and the lore reader named it as the hook that would keep them hunting.
- **Mechanics as theme:** "Give, don't fight" and **Shiver**. The wingtip tap and the shared shiver run from the cocoon to the death scene, passed from Sorrel to Umber to the caterpillar. Every reader remembered the tap.
- **An antagonist who is wrong in an illuminating way:** *HEARTH: No one goes hungry*. It keeps everyone fed and safe, against a heroine who cannot eat and lives for a week. Stay is a real temptation, not a trap.
- **Images with weight:** Sorrel "a dark knot round a pale seed" as the root closes over him, Dun "lies across the lantern's door, facing out", and the last palm against the silk with "Something presses back".

## 4. Problems, ranked

1. **Tallow does not add up (POLISH, top priority).** All three readers stumbled here. He gives the coal "for the thing in the mountain" and then is fought "burning his way" there. He writes *EATEN* about a sister who is alive. He then walks in unarmed. *Fix, no new rule:* make *EATEN* his belief, shown in action. He burns cases as mercy because he thinks they are digesting people. The Night 6 boss is him burning the dead city's cases, not marching on the mountain, and the breathing face disproves *EATEN*. His dropped torch is the turn, and his walk in unarmed follows from it. Also show the sister set her torch down before she lays her hand on the glass, change "oldest stalk" to "thickest stalk" or make hers the first case, and cut "burned" from "hand in burned hand".
2. **Burn plays sweet while it kills most of humanity (POLISH).** Put the cost on screen during the flight home. They fly over the kitchen-table family's cases, which go dark one by one, and Umber's summit dance happens in a wood where nothing else is moving. Keep the dance, but make the silence weigh on it.
3. **Sorrel's whereabouts and his apparent death (POLISH).** The heart reader lost him between Night 3 and Night 4. The lore reader thought he died on Night 5. Add one shot at the end of Night 3 of Sorrel flying home toward the summit, which is his want. On Night 5, have the camera hold until he moves one wingtip.
4. **Boss verbs are unclear (POLISH).** The casual reader asked "what am I doing" against Dun and the three hollows. Make Dun the tutorial for giving: the first glint it takes makes it falter on screen. The hollows should teach the same thing the final boss needs, closing a hollow by giving into it, so the player has already learned the finale.
5. **The forest is cast but passive (POLISH).** The heart, the amber and the glints read as three separate things. Use one word for it throughout ("the forest's amber"). Make the river of gold on Night 6 its choice: show its heart dimming on purpose as the gold leaves.
6. **Small logic gaps (POLISH).** "Shut down" contradicts a brain that is still running, so change it to "switched off its warmth". "She fades on the road" needs a cause: she has given every glint, and Shiver has drained her.

## 5. Scores

Anchor 9 · Relationships 8 · Clarity 7 · Idea 9 · World 9 · Mechanics-as-theme 9 · Antagonist 9 · Ending 8 · Originality 9 · Grove fit 8 · Balance 8

Clarity is held at 7 because every reader retold the spine and the enemy correctly, but all three tripped on Tallow and on "hand in burned hand". Grove fit is 8 because Night 1 maps the Grove's three lanterns, high island, carving and heart closely, burrbacks and lampwings are re-meant cleanly, and the ember survives as "the biggest glint".

## 6. At the bar? Yes

I would put this outline next to Hollow Knight, Ori and Nine Sols as an equal. The idea, the antagonist, the brother and the mechanics hold up at that tier. The single biggest gap is Tallow. He is the only human and the bridge to the sister reveal, and right now he is the one character a player cannot explain. Fix his *EATEN* belief and the middle stops feeling crowded.
