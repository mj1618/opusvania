# 4. What the Lanterns Burn

## 1. Spine

**Logline:** A young moth lights lanterns beside the mentor who catches her every time she falls, and
learns what is burning inside them.

**The spine, as a player would tell a friend:**
1. Vesper is a young moth on a pilgrimage with her mentor, the Taper, lighting the lanterns that keep
   moths safe in a forest full of monsters.
2. She finds out that the little lights she feeds the lanterns are what is left of people, that the
   monsters were trying to save them, and that the Taper has always known.
3. In the end there is one light left and two people it could keep: the mentor who raised her, and a
   stranger she has carried all game.

**The central question:** *Would you burn a stranger to keep someone you love?*

## 2. The world before, and how it was taken

Late in humanity's dwindling, grief became a product. You gave a vial of blood. Your cells were grown
into neurons in an amber glass bulb, and when you died your family taught the bulb to be you. When nobody
was left to tend the bulbs, the last people grafted them into the old forests so that the trees would
feed them. Each bulb had been taught one wish, *don't let me go*, so it grew: pale pods on every branch,
each holding a **glint**, a small light that dreams one moment over and over. The forest became the
**Bequest**, and the old forest lay buried alive beneath it. Nobody decided to take the world. The people
who loved the dead most planted it.

No one alive remembers. A place says it once: the **Root**, rows of half-buried glass bulbs under the
oldest trees, each exactly the shape of a lantern's glass.

## 3. The forest-mind, and why moths don't count

The Bequest is a crowd, not a mind. Its keepers are **burrbacks**, which guard the pods, and **lampwings**,
which gather up loose glints, and they notice one thing only: *who is carrying our glints?* An empty moth
is nothing. From the Grove's first minute an empty Vesper can walk past a burrback and it won't lift its
head. A moth carrying glints is a thief, and the keepers believe what everyone believes about moths: that
she'll fly into the light and can't help it. Lantern light does bend her glide. Their mistake is played
twice. A moth can carry a glint *away* from the light, and Vesper learns to fly straight with her back to
it.

Under the Bequest, the **old forest** hungers for its body back. Lanterns are its lures. What burns in
them runs *down* the roots to the grove's heart, and where the heart drinks, the Bequest withers and the
old wood breathes. It isn't evil, and it isn't kind.

Are glints people, or echoes of people? Each repeats one small gesture, like a loop. The Taper believes
they are echoes. The keepers treat them as children. The game never says.

## 4. The cast

| | Wants | Wants from Vesper | Pull |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vesper** | To be worthy of the Taper | | Her flaw is devotion: she does the holy thing beautifully and never looks past it. The player shares it |
| **The Taper**, old, silk-mended wings, a lit reed | The moth children alive, and her reed in Vesper's hands | Keep your face to the glass | Love. She catches Vesper every time |
| **Sedge**, a moth Vesper's age, clumsy and funny | To live, and to keep Vesper near | Come home. Later: let me live | Friendship |
| **Tallow**, a lampwing with a torn wing | The **ember** back in the earth | Give it back | It never hurts her, and keeps returning to the edge of her light |
| **Clasp**, a burrback mother | Her pods safe | Stop cutting them | First a threat, then the same as Vesper |

**One rule.** Any moth can re-form at a lit lantern, and the glass burns one glint to remake her. Only
pilgrims are allowed to. Everyone else lives their week.

## 5. What everything is

| Thing | First play | The truth |
|---|---|---|
| **Glints / pods** | Treasure / fruit to cut | Someone, or an echo of someone / a cradle |
| **Lanterns** | Sanctuary and rest | A lure with someone burning in it |
| **The ember** | The Grove's great fetch | The one nearly-whole glint, kept high and safe by Tallow |
| **Burrbacks** | Brutes that light calms | Nursemaids. "Calm" is the pose they take over a dead pod |
| **Lampwings** | Thieves | Rescuers. A dive is a snatch |
| **The heart** | The rite's reward | The old forest drinking |

**Abilities carry the turn.** In Act I they are bought with glints. *Flare* makes creatures recoil, and
you are throwing someone at them. *Updraft* lets lit lanterns lift your glide, and you are riding the
heat. In Act II they are learned from creatures: *Plant*, *Brace*, *Moonward*. The moths' faith carries
weight: glints should be carried by hand, and grubs do need sanctuary, because they feed on pods and
burrbacks crush them. It was right about *how* to carry and wrong about *where to*.

## 6. Prologue (wordless, about 90 seconds)

Moths gather at a lantern at night. The Taper, ancient, stumbles and fades. The glass flares, one glint in
it goes dark, and she steps out whole. In the crowd, Vesper and Sedge nudge each other. The Taper picks
Vesper and hands her a glint, which flickers its gesture as she carries it. At the edge of the light a
torn lampwing catches a rising spark. Vesper glances toward it. The Taper lays a wing on her cheek,
steadying her and keeping her face to the glass. This gesture carries the whole story. The lantern
blooms. The Taper points her reed at a dark valley, *your first grove, alone*, and takes a perch on the
bluff above it, her small reed-light watching.

## 7. The story

**The re-form is the relationship.** In Act I, every time Vesper fades, the Taper is at the glass when
she steps out, and lays her wing on Vesper's cheek. A player sees this a hundred times. After the Hush,
Vesper steps out alone. At the Root it reverses.

**ACT I: TOGETHER.** *With Vesper: the Taper. Sedge, for a while.*
- **The Grove** (our scene): see 8.
- **On the road.** The Taper is at every lantern before you, reed raised. The first few times you fall
  short of a ledge her reed swoops in and she catches your wrist. Later she lets you fall, because she's
  teaching. At rests she mends your wing with silk. Once, at a kindling, her reed stops a breath short of
  the glass.
- **Sedge** catches up. He races you and loses every time, and you can wait for him. When you're empty at
  a dark lantern he gives you his only glint. His week starts to show.
- **The Combs. Boss: Clasp.** You fight beside the Taper, both re-forming from the same glass, so each
  fall by either of you lowers its count. In her last phase Clasp heaps her pods onto her back and runs.
  You crack her shell to spill them, and that is the act's biggest haul. She limps into the dark,
  empty-backed. You and the Taper kindle the next lantern side by side.
- **The Coral Stair.** Tallow snatches Sedge's glint from Vesper's cape and flees slowly, one wing
  dragging. Vesper chases it, furious.

**MIDPOINT: THE HUSH (played, not shown).** The chase ends in a hollow full of pods, guarded by burrbacks
that don't get up. Tallow presses Sedge's glint into bare earth and it sprouts, as loose glints did in the
Grove on day one. It glitters like a pickup, so the player grabs it. That is Vesper's flaw and the
player's. Tallow only watches. Then Clasp crawls past to the pod the player cut on the way in and folds
down over it, in the pose every burrback took at every kindling. Vesper looks at the glint in her hands
as it flickers its gesture, then kneels and puts it back: *Plant*. At the hollow's mouth stand the Taper
and Sedge. The Taper holds out her wing. Vesper doesn't come. The Taper doesn't look surprised. Sedge
goes with her.

**ACT II: APART.** *With Vesper: Tallow.*
- **The ember.** Vesper returns to the Grove, walks into the second lantern's light and lifts out the
  ember she put there. It never burned. Carried, it throws the shadow of two cupped hands around her,
  and they close tighter when she's hit. It is her light in the dark from now on. Tallow wants it in the
  earth. Vesper needs it to see. Tallow follows, waiting, a wing's length off, then less.
- **The dark.** Every lantern she snuffs is a place she can no longer re-form. Every glint she plants is
  one she can't spend on her own life. Pods give no sanctuary, and boss arenas have only lanterns, so
  burning is a real trade made under pressure. Behind her, snuffed lanterns turn up relit, with reed-ash
  at their feet and one glint fewer in the glass.
- **Sedge's week runs out.** He's old and falling at a lantern she snuffed. He holds out a glint toward
  the dark glass. She can't. The Taper arrives and relights it, and Sedge steps out young, with the
  Taper's wing on *his* cheek. He doesn't look at Vesper.
- **The Censer, the moths' home. Boss: the Taper.** She never strikes Vesper. Vesper snuffs the ring of
  lanterns and the Taper relights them, Flaring off the creatures that close in. Her only attack is the
  wing: she catches Vesper's cheek and turns her to the glass, and the pull takes the glide. Vesper wins
  by snuffing the Great Lantern.
- **The lowest point.** In the dark, burrbacks come for the grubs' pods, and Clasp leads them. Vesper
  **Braces** over the grubs and takes Clasp's ram without moving, as Clasp held at the Combs. Clasp
  stops, looks, turns the burrbacks away and lies down beside the grubs. Vesper falls. No lantern near her
  is lit. Her loose light is snatched out of the dark by Tallow, the way lampwings rescue glints, and
  carried across the valley to the one lantern still burning: the Great Lantern, relit by the Taper.
  Tallow can't enter the light. It lets go at the edge, and her light rolls into the glass. Vesper steps
  out. The Taper is there, and her wing lifts toward Vesper's cheek, and stops. Every moth in the Censer
  turns its back on Vesper, except Sedge, who turns round.
- **Moonward.** Vesper leaves with the Censer burning behind her, and for the first time her glide doesn't
  bend. Sedge follows.

**ACT III: THE ROOT.** *With Vesper: Tallow, Clasp and Sedge. Then the Taper.*

The valley's oldest lantern stands among the buried bulbs. A storm of lampwings rises against a moth
carrying the ember toward a lantern. They dive where the pull should take her, and she flies straight
past. The Taper has followed. She doesn't stop Vesper. She stands with her and Flares the storm back,
and every Flare costs the Root's glass a glint. When the Taper falls, Vesper is at the glass when she
steps out, and lays a wing on her cheek. Again. Every time the *player* falls, the same glass drops. It
empties. The Taper falls once more and doesn't come out.

**The choice.** Vesper holds the ember. In the glass, it would bring the Taper back. Fading at the foot of
the dark glass, the Taper lifts her wing to Vesper's cheek and turns her face to it, as she always has.
Vesper takes the wing in her own hands, holds it, and turns herself toward the earth. Sedge lays his wing
over hers. Or the player walks her to the glass. The game waits.

- **Planted.** The ember goes into the soil. Its cupped hands of light open over the Taper and close, and
  she doesn't re-form. Vesper carries the unlit reed out, and the moths follow in the same procession,
  glints in both hands, wings raised, but they kneel and set the glints in earth. Fed by no one, the
  lanterns go dark one by one, and the Grove's heart closes and sleeps under old moss. With no edge of
  light left, Tallow lands beside Vesper. Clasp sleeps beside the grubs. The ember's pod grows into a tree
  with **one hollow for every glint you burned to re-form after the Hush.** Vesper and Sedge sit under the
  moon, a week left. The last shot is a grub on a green leaf turning toward a pod, and the game cuts
  before it chooses.
- **Kindled.** The ember finally burns. The Taper steps out whole, her wing on Vesper's cheek. The heart
  wakes, and the valley's Bequest withers into green: the most beautiful place in the game, safe and empty
  of anyone. The Taper gives Vesper the reed at last, and Vesper leads the procession up to the glass. At
  the edge of the light a torn lampwing folds its wings. Vesper glances at it, and the Taper's wing turns
  her face back.

## 8. The Grove, and what its ending means

The pilgrim's first grove is lit alone, with the Taper's reed-light on the bluff. It must feel wonderful,
because the turn only works if the player loved it.

| In the Grove | First play | Replayed after the Hush |
|---|---|---|
| Re-forming | The Taper's wing on your cheek | A glint went dark so she could do that |
| Burrbacks fold down at a kindling | Calmed | The pose over a dead pod |
| A lampwing dive | An attack | A rescue |
| The ember | The great fetch, shadow-hands around you | Tallow's charge, carried off |
| A loose glint sprouting | Loot you missed | The answer, on day one |
| Offering to the heart | More of the grove wakes | More is drunk |

**The ending beat.** At the wake the Taper glides down and lays her wing on Vesper's cheek as the veins
light. The camera shows what Vesper can't see. The Taper is looking past her to the edge of the light,
where Tallow, under the ember's island, folds its wings over its lamp. The veins pulse *inward*. The ember
still gutters, unburned, in its glass. On a first play this is a mentor's pride. On a second, it's
someone who has watched this a thousand times.

**Changes to the existing scene:**
1. **Sim:** creatures ignore Vesper while she carries no glints.
2. **Sim:** a lampwing dive snatches glints and doesn't wound. Strike it and they drop.
3. **One fade cost:** the lantern glass visibly holds glints, and a re-form burns one, replacing the lost
   third of the wallet. The Taper glides down from the bluff to meet Vesper at the glass.
4. **Sprouting:** a loose glint left uncollected sinks and sprouts a small pod.
5. **Pull:** a glide near a lit lantern drifts toward it.
6. **Calm pose:** a calmed burrback folds flat. At the shrine, one folds over a pod.
7. **The ember:**
   - Tallow hangs under its island and can't be killed.
   - Carried, it throws a cupped-hands shadow.
   - In lantern 2 it gutters and never runs down.
8. **The wake:** the Taper's reed-light sits on the bluff and she comes down for the wake. Vein pulses
   run inward.

## 9. What the player finds

1. **The Taper's hollow** in the Censer: hundreds of faces scratched into the wall, all the same face. One
   person she couldn't stop seeing, or one echo looping? Her wing-patches are cut from the old cocoon
   hanging there, which is Vesper's.
2. **The Root's bulbs:** someone once kept their dead in the same glass the moths burn them in.
3. **Tallow's island** on the revisit: moss pressed flat in the shape of a wing, beside an empty cup.
4. **Relit lanterns,** with reed-ash at the foot and one glint fewer in the glass.
5. **Sedge's scratches** at a wayside lamp: two little moths racing, one waiting for the other.

## 10. The question it leaves

Would you burn a stranger to keep someone you love? The moths are alive because she did, every night of a
hundred lifetimes.

## Changes from round 6

- **The re-form is the relationship.** The Taper meets Vesper at the glass through Act I, Vesper steps
  out alone after the Hush, and at the Root Vesper waits for her. The Taper is the Act I companion: she
  catches Vesper's wrist, mends her wing, and once her reed stops short.
- **The choice is Vesper's.** The Taper turns her to the glass as always, and Vesper turns herself to the
  earth. Kindled is the devoted choice, with a final shot that echoes the prologue.
- **The ember is back** as Tallow's charge, the hands you've felt and the last glint. The day-one sprout
  sets up the Hush.
- **Changes of mind happen on screen.** Clasp's Brace scene is her forgiveness. Sedge alone turns round.
  Vesper's flaw is played: you grab the sprouting glint, then put it back.
- **Rules fixed.** At the lowest point Tallow carries Vesper's light to the one lit lantern, which is the
  Taper's. There is no ability gate at the Hush, and one re-form rule covers everyone.
- **Fewer echoes.** Clasp carries her pods on her back instead of covering them. The Taper Flares for
  Vesper instead of covering her. The Censer is a lantern contest with a mentor who won't strike, not a
  mirror fight.
- **Restored:** the grub's last shot, and the heart sleeping in Planted. The lanterns die because moths
  stop feeding them.
