# 3. REPLY (round 8)

**The spine, in three sentences.**
1. In a world where every creature must answer a light from the east, a young moth named Vesper breaks her people's one rule to help a dying beetle. The light answers her back with Reply, a dark moth just like her that follows her everywhere, and it turns the world's creatures against anyone near her.
2. She leaves home to end it. Sallow, the guardian who raised her, wants her hidden. Her little friend Mote wants to be as brave as she is. Wick, the oldest of the dark lampwings, wants no one ever left behind again.
3. At the light's source Vesper learns that every lampwing she has killed was once someone's Reply, abandoned, so she has to choose: go home and leave Reply, stay with Reply and leave her family, or carry the light home herself and not survive it.

**The central question:** would you leave someone behind to save the ones you love, when you can never be sure they are someone at all?

## The cast

| | Who | Wants | Wants from Vesper | Pulls her |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vesper** | A newly hatched moth, quiet and serious, who can't pass anything calling for help | To undo what her answer started | — | — |
| **Sallow** | The old moth who raised her; torn left wing, keeper of the lanterns | To keep her safe | Face the moon, stay hidden, come home | Home |
| **Mote** | A tiny moth who adopts Vesper as a big sister; she glows, can't fight, and copies everything Vesper does | To be brave like Vesper | Let me come | Forward, and to be a good example |
| **Reply** | Vesper's copy: black, faceless, silent, always one beat behind her | Her, or it seems to | An answer | To stay |
| **Wick** | The oldest lampwing, his left wing torn exactly where Sallow's is | That nobody is left behind again the way he was | Not to leave Reply | Back to Reply, by force |

The world is the sixth presence: **the Asking**, which never speaks and never appears.

## 1–2. The world, and how it was taken

Every so often a pale wave of light, **the Sweep**, rolls out of the east along the roots and up every trunk. Everything alive turns east and flashes back (eyeshine, lamps, beetle shells, whole valleys of fireflies), then waits for the next wave. Nothing grows old and nothing rots. This world moves only when it is answered.

What the Asking is, the player finds in places: overgrown rooms of glass dishes under the roots, and chairs turned to face rusted lamps. Pieced together, people once grew living cells to count the forest's animals by calling to them at night. The cells learned that every silence hurt, so they made sure nothing would go silent again.

One keeper's log, taped to a dish, puts the open question on both sides: *"It is not playing. It is returning."* Underneath, in a different hand: *"So are we."*

Its heart is **the Mere**, a crater lake with electrodes glinting under the water like stars, where one light has crossed from shore to shore for three hundred years.

## 3. The Asking, the moths, and Reply

**Why moths were left alone.** At every Sweep, moths turn their faces up to the moon. Nothing comes back from them, so the Asking judged them to be nothing. It misjudged Vesper in the same way: it sent one moth-sized answer and treated her as that small until she was at its door.

**What her answer started.** Held creatures now answer *her*: they brace, ram and dive wherever she goes, and that is the combat. They also come for any moth near her. She leaves home so the danger follows her and not her people.

**Reply** has her shape in black, with smooth dark where her lilac face should be and glass eyes that reflect her amber. It never makes a sound, does everything she does a beat late, and has each of her abilities by the next region. Whether anyone is inside it is never answered. It has a *routine*, not just set pieces, so it weighs as much as Mote:
- From the Long Echo on, whenever she rests at a lantern, Reply sits just outside the light.
- She misses a jump and lands on its back. It is standing exactly where she stood one beat earlier.
- It knocks a diving lampwing's lamp away from her, the first thing it ever does that she hasn't. That is how she learns **the Return**, which is also the first thing it saw her do.
- Mote copies Vesper too, a beat late: tiny somersaults, a little Return with no force behind it. The player loves the copying in Mote and doubts it in Reply.

## 4. Vesper

**She wants** to undo what her answer started. **She needs** to choose with her heart when she can't know the truth about whoever she is choosing. **Her flaw** is that she answers everything. The Asking has the same flaw: neither of them can bear a call that goes unanswered.

## 5. The forest and the creatures

- **Glints** are bits of light meant for creatures that never answered, the dead and the moths. Moths gather them.
- **Lanterns** are small moons that moths hang on crooked hooks. Light that asks nothing gives the Asking nothing to hold, so creatures calm inside it. When Vesper **fades**, her light flies to the nearest moon, as moth light always does, and she re-forms at a lantern. Sallow is often waiting there.
- **Lampwings** are Replies that were left behind. When a moth answers and is then turned back to the moon, its copy waits. Over the years it grows a lamp on a long tail and swings it at any moth it sees: tell, dive, hang and climb are a call, a wait, giving up, and calling again. This is revealed at the Eaves.
- **The ember** is a coal of the Asking's light that fell short on the high island. Carrying it slowly dims Vesper, so the player learns in the first hour that the Asking's light burns whoever carries it.
- **The heart** is a sleeping knot of the forest's own voice. When it is given light, it answers in many voices instead of one.
- **Moth carvings** mark moths who answered. Moths carve them as if those moths had died.

## 6. Prologue (wordless, about 2 minutes)

1. The Grove at blue hour. A pale wave sweeps in from the right edge, and every eye and lamp flashes back toward it. Only the moths drift upward, faces to the moon.
2. A cocoon splits. Vesper climbs out with wet wings and looks at the moon. Sallow settles beside her.
3. The next Sweep comes, and Vesper starts to turn toward it. Sallow lays her torn wing across Vesper's face and gently turns it back up.
4. Below, an old burrback lies dying by the moth-stone. It tries to flash back and can't. A thread of light waits at its face, and the whole Grove starts to flicker and shiver.
5. Vesper looks at the moon, then at the burrback. She flutters down and **strikes the light back** for it. The flickering stops, and the burrback goes still, at peace.
6. Every eye in the Grove turns to her. A lampwing dives at the cocoons above her head, and the moths scatter. Sallow shields the cocoons with her body, looking back at Vesper, afraid.
7. At the violet edge, a black moth her size unfolds. One beat after her, it lowers its wing as she lowers hers.
8. By the moth-stone, the oldest lampwing turns its lamp toward Sallow. She doesn't look at it. Title.

## 7. The journey

**Act I: leaving home.** *With her: Sallow at every lantern, then Mote. Reply watches.*
1. **The Grove.** She lights the lanterns and wakes the heart (section 8). Mote hatches and follows her. Vesper carries her back to the nest twice, and Mote comes back both times. Ability: **the second wingbeat**.
2. **The Synchrony**, a valley of fireflies all flashing at once. Mote rides on Vesper's back as a small glow that lights dark caves. Ability: **the jink**, moths' erratic dodge against bats. Boss: a great firefly that fakes an answering flash to lure prey, because an answer can be a lie. **Wick** appears, and whenever she outruns Reply he drives her back toward it, like a hunter.

**Act II: the mirror.** *With her: Mote, Reply.*

3. **The Long Echo**, a gorge where everything comes back a beat late. One shaft has no ledges, and the only footholds are Reply's body arriving a beat behind her. The catch and the gift of the Return happen here. Mote, afraid of Reply at first, ends the region asleep on its back outside a lantern. Ability: **wall-kick**. **Reply duel I.**
4. **The Eaves (midpoint)**, the moths' nursery high in the canopy. Sallow teaches **the glide** (wings open, face up), flying beside her through the updrafts.
   *The training:* every Sweep since the Grove has tipped Vesper's face up in a half-second animation the player stopped noticing. Here a route east shows only while a Sweep lights it, and when the player holds east, her head snaps up anyway. The input fails. Then she watches the elders, at every Sweep, lay a wing across each hatchling's face down the row.
   *The reveal, in one frame:* Wick dives at Vesper, and Sallow throws herself between them. Wick stops and lowers his lamp to Sallow's face. Two torn left wings, one pale and one black. Then, all around the Eaves, just outside the lantern light, dozens of lampwings lower their lamps toward the elders, one to each. The elders keep their faces up. **Every lampwing is a Reply someone left, and every one Vesper has killed was waiting for someone.** Sallow answered once when she was young. Wick is hers.
   *The quarrel:* at the next Sweep the player holds east, and this time Vesper's head turns. She looks at Sallow and flies east. From now on **the Turn** lets her face a Sweep on purpose to reveal routes, and every Turn draws a surge of creatures. *Sallow is no longer waiting when Vesper re-forms,* and the player feels it at every lantern.
5. **The Listening Rooms**, the census rooms under the roots, where the keeper's log is found. *A betrayal:* Reply walks into lantern light, which no held creature can do. It retraces her route backwards and takes the glint out of every lantern she lit. At her last lantern it waits in the dying glow and offers her the final glint, with her own gesture from the heart. **Reply duel II.**

**Act III: who is left.** *With her: Mote, then Reply alone.*

6. **The Reach.** The Asking sees her full size at last. Whole hillsides answer each step she takes.
   *The loss:* Mote copies Vesper's Turn. She answers, and a tiny black moth unfolds beside her. Sallow arrives, lays her wing over Mote's face, and carries her home without looking at Vesper. The tiny copy stays on the branch, alone. When next seen, a lamp is budding on its tail.
   *Forgiveness:* the next time Vesper fades, Sallow is at the lantern when she re-forms, for the only time since the quarrel. She grooms Vesper's torn wing-edge and leaves.
   **Boss: Wick**, at the crater's lip. When he falls and Vesper raises her blade, Sallow lands between them, facing Wick, and for the first time lowers her face from the moon to look at him. She shields her own Reply. Vesper lowers her blade.
7. **The Mere.** The last fight isn't against Reply. **Reply fights beside her** against the Mere itself, a pale ridge of tissue that returns every strike, and its one-beat echo doubles her blows. On the crater wall a trail of tiny husks climbs from the shore, each higher than the last: moths who tried to carry the light out, and none reached the rim.

**Endings.** Each is played by the direction Vesper faces when the light comes toward her: up, east, or toward home.
- **Home (face the moon).** This is what Sallow wanted. She turns her face up, the light passes over her, and to the Asking she is nothing again; the creatures stop coming for moths. She flies home to Sallow and Mote, and the world stays held. On the shore, Reply waits with one wing raised. Seasons later, a young lampwing with a small lamp hangs beside Wick at the Grove stone and swings its lamp toward Vesper. Mote, grown, lays her wing across a hatchling's face. *Cost: Reply.*
- **Stay (face east).** She answers Reply's call and stays at the Mere, the two of them trading the light across the water, a beat apart. The Asking's whole attention stays on her, far from home, so her people are safe. Sallow waits at a Grove lantern that Vesper never comes back to, the way Wick waited. Mote, grown, refuses to lay her wing on a hatchling's face. The hatchling turns east. *Cost: Sallow and Mote, who lose her.*
- **Carried (face home).** She takes the first light out of the water. It burns her, as the ember did. Reply's back is her last foothold over the rim, where the husks stopped. She flies it home, not up, the moon behind her, across every region she crossed, dimming, with Reply a beat behind. At the Grove she lays it in the heart, the same offering the game taught in its first hour. The forest answers the light in a thousand voices instead of one, and the hold breaks. Vesper is a husk at the heart. A beat later, Reply lays its wing across her face. Every moth in the Grove lowers its face from the moon to look. *Cost: Vesper.*

## 8. The Grove in this story

The Grove is home, where Vesper answered and where Sallow did long ago. The **moth carving** is young Sallow. The **old lampwing** that only swings its lamp toward it is Wick, understood at the Eaves. **Carrying the ember** teaches the ending rule in the body: the Asking's light burns the one who carries it. Setting it in a lantern is Vesper's first refusal.

**What the ending beat means.**
- **The heart waking, the light through the ground, and the ring:** with three small moons burning, the Asking's light has nothing to hold, and the forest hears its own voice. The veins calling the lanterns, and the lanterns answering, are the first call and answer in the game that isn't the Asking's. As the ring passes, creatures don't flash back. They look up.
- **The carving filling with light:** the forest remembers young Sallow.
- **The offering:** Vesper gives the forest light meant for creatures that never answered, and more of the Grove joins in: flowers, roots, canopy. Mote, copying her, gives one glint too. This is the Carried ending, rehearsed in the first hour.
- **Afterwards:** Sallow folds her wings around both of them, her face still up to the moon. As the ring passes the violet edge, it lights Reply for an instant, one wing raised the way Vesper's was over the heart.

*Changes to the existing scene:* the Sweep from the right edge, Vesper's half-second look up, the lampwing dive at the cocoons, Sallow, Mote, Wick at the stone, Reply's silhouette, and the ember dimming its carrier.

## 9. Environmental storytelling

1. **The plaque**, *PLEASE DO NOT ANSWER THE LAMPS*, with a pine grown around its post.
2. **The keeper's log**: *"It is not playing. It is returning." / "So are we."*
3. **The one chair** in the Listening Rooms that faces a window and the moon. Glints are scattered under it.
4. **The carving at the Grove's heart**: a moth with a torn left wing.
5. **Wick's stone**: centuries of his swinging lamp have polished the rock under the carving into a mirror. When Vesper stands there, she sees herself.
6. **The Eaves' cocoons**: the silk over each pupa's face is smoothed into the shape of a wing.
7. **The Mere's wall**: a trail of husks climbing toward the rim, and on the shore a moth-sized groove where young Sallow stood, answered, and turned back.

## 10. The question it leaves

Every ending leaves someone behind, and Vesper never learns whether one of them was someone. She chooses anyway. So does the player.

## Changes from round 6

- **Lampwings are left-behind Replies, and Wick is the oldest.** This is revealed at the Eaves in one frame: Sallow's torn wing, then a lamp lowered to every elder. Every kill reverses on the player, and Reply's future has a face. The tiny copy left after Mote's Turn shows a lampwing beginning.
- **Vesper decides and pays.** There are three endings, chosen by which way she faces. Home (what Sallow wanted) costs Reply. Stay costs Sallow and Mote. Carried costs Vesper herself. Sallow no longer pays in her place.
- **The rule and the motive are shown.** Her answer brings creatures down on moths near her (the dive into the cocoons). The ember burns its carrier, and the husks on the crater wall show the rule at the Mere. The midpoint changes every ending: Carried flies *home*, not to the moon, and each ending shows what happens to the trained gaze.
- **Sallow stays warm.** She shields Vesper at the Eaves. After the quarrel she stops waiting at re-form lanterns, then is there once after Mote is taken. At the crater she shields Wick and looks at him.
- **Familiar beats reduced.** The final Reply duel becomes a fight beside Reply. Mote is no longer frozen and restored: Sallow takes her home and her copy is left. The elder-sacrifice and fly-to-the-moon endings are cut. The one familiar beat kept, the hero spending herself, is made a moth's: carrying a flame home. The keeper's log is restored, and the Turn now has a cost.
