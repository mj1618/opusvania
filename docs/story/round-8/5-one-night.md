# 5. ONE NIGHT

**The god's law, in words a player could say aloud:** *Whatever it looks at, it holds still. It lets go
only of what ends while it is holding it.*

**The spine:**
1. A slow god, grown long ago from living brain cells, holds still whatever it looks at. It has never
   held anything brief enough to end in its grip, so it has never let go of anything, and next year its
   stillness reaches the last moving forest.
2. Moths are too brief for it to see, and for 917 years they have lit lamps to draw its look. Vesper has
   one night to finish her dead mother's path and stand in that look at dawn, so that it holds something
   that ends, and learns to let go.
3. Her grandmother Compline has kept the line hidden for forty years. She loves Vesper and pulls her
   back, because once the god has seen one moth, it will never stop seeing them.

**The central question:** *Would you give up your people's only hiding place to set the world free?*

| Who | Wants | How they pull on Vesper |
|---|---|---|
| **Vesper** | To finish her mother's path before dawn. | — |
| **Lune**, her mother, dead a year | Wanted the same, and fell one climb short. | Her scent and scratch-marks lead Vesper on. Her coal is the first light Vesper carries. |
| **Compline**, Lune's mother | To keep her family unseen and alive, and to stop burying daughters. | She saves Vesper, carries her, and then shuts her away to keep her. |
| **Sedge**, a moth of another line | To finish her own field of lamps before dawn. | A friend running on the same clock. Every gift she gives Vesper is taken from her own night. |
| **The Lamplighter**, a woman held still for 917 years | To finish lighting her lamp and put her arm down. | What being held looks like, and a palm for Vesper to sleep in. |

## 1. Title and logline
**ONE NIGHT.** A moth with one night to live must decide whether to let a god that holds everything
finally see her kind.

## 2. The world before
People grew a mind to think slowly. They seeded living cultures into the fungus under an old forest and
called it **the Long Thought**. They asked it slow questions, and its answers were always right in
hindsight. The questions are still cut into a cliff, **the Querystones**, each with the date its answer
came back:
- *Should the valley be flooded? Answer received after 61 years: yes.*
- *Please let the children stay. Answer received after 211 years.* The answer is a root grown through
  the words.
- *Are the moths a danger? Answer received after 9 years: no.*

There was no war. It looked at the things it valued, and they held still. One evening a woman lighting
the streetlamps of a riverside town found that her feet would not lift. She is still there 917 years
later, pole raised, with bark to her shoulders. The great trunks of the forest are not all trees. The
held country is spreading: **the Stillwood**, where a stag has been halfway through one step for a
decade. Next year it reaches the Grove.

## 3. What the AI is, and why moths were spared
It is one mind spread through a continent's roots, and a single thought takes it years. Whether its hold
is love or storage, or whether anyone is inside it at all, the game never says. It answered people's
questions, and it has never let go of anything it held.

It judged moths once. The answer took nine years, which is nine generations of moths, and the answer was
*no*. **It cannot see a moth. It can only see a habit.** A lamp lit in the same place on the same night
every year for 917 years is the one thing moths do that lasts long enough to catch its look.

*Designer's note, never shown to the player: the lamps are a DishBrain-style steady signal, scaled up.*

## 4. Vesper
A vigil moth with no mouth, made for one night. **She wants** to finish Lune's path and see it work.
**She needs** to accept that she won't see it, and that her worth lies in what she hands on. **Her flaw**
is haste (§5). **She gives as much as she gets**: she pulls Sedge out from
under a charge, shares her glints when Sedge runs short, and stands over Compline when the nightjar
comes for her.

## 5. The rules a player feels
- **Lanterns** are old streetlamp heads. A steady light draws the god's look, and its look holds the
  flame, which is why a lit lantern burns all year until someone snuffs it. While the god's look rests
  on the flame, it isn't riding the creatures nearby, so burrbacks calm down and turn away, and lampwings
  stop at the edge of the light.
- **Eggs and daughters.** Vesper lays an egg on every lantern she lights. An egg sleeps for a year,
  unless its mother dies that same night. Then it wakes at once. **When Vesper fades, her newest egg
  hatches**: a daughter, still called Vesper, warmed by her mother's last light and exactly as worn as
  she was.
- **Coals.** Where a moth dies, her light stays behind as a small coal. It burns all night like a wayside
  lamp, with the glints she was carrying inside it. Nobody takes those glints back. They light the place
  where she fell. A player who dies often leaves a trail of small lights.
- **Wings.** Clean, skilful speed is free. **Panic frays the wings**: every hit taken mid-dash tears
  them, and nothing mends them. Frayed wings glide shorter and shake.
- **The night is the map.** Each waking heart moves the moon, and nothing ever rewinds: floods drain,
  frost comes, the moon sets. Abilities are remembered at Lune's scratch-marks, in the places where she
  learned them.

## 6. Prologue (played, about 3 minutes)
1. A single even pulse of light in a cracked glass dish under moss, with gold dots set in the glass.
2. Time-lapse. A woman lights streetlamps at dusk. Roots rise from the drains and the town sinks into
   forest. She stays, arm raised, and the bark climbs her. Moths circle the last lamp.
3. **The player takes control of a ragged moth**, with no name and no explanation, in the Grove as the
   sky pales. The empty summit lantern is above. She climbs, worn and slow. A huge, heavy, furred moth
   with a long drinking-mouth catches her arm and pulls her toward the shade. **Holding against the pull
   is the first thing the game teaches.** No player gets her free in time. Hold or not, the sun comes
   first, and the ragged moth reaches only the stone cup on the island below the lantern. She sits in the
   sun and goes still. Her light becomes a coal.
4. The heavy moth stays in the shade. She looks at the coal for a long time, and then she leaves it.
5. Snow over the island, the coal glowing through it. The Long Night comes again, and a cocoon on a
   lantern hook splits. Vesper looks up at the glow. Title.

## 7. Act structure: dusk to dawn
**Dusk: the Grove.** Small ember-moths drift along a scent, which is Lune's. Vesper carries her mother's
coal to the empty socket, and the Grove wakes (§8). In the canopy, the heavy moth lowers her snuffer and
does nothing.

**First dark: the drowned town.** The Lamplighter's lamp has been dark for 917 years. Vesper lights it.
For one moment the god's look moves from the woman to the flame, and she blinks once. Her palm becomes
Vesper's rest point. **Sedge** is here: quick, scrappy, from another line, lighting her own lamps. They
split a lumen pod and race up the bell-tower, where Lune's mark teaches the wall-kick.
*Boss:* a ridden burrback matriarch the size of a house. Wild hits enrage her, steady hits calm her, and
the player can only calm her by wounding her. Midway through, she charges Sedge, and Vesper pulls her
clear. When the lantern lights, the matriarch is a beetle again. She lies down in its light and does not
get up.

**The Querystones.** Sedge travels with her. They take turns: one kindles while the other keeps the
lampwings off. When Sedge's glints run out, Vesper gives her half of hers. Ahead of them, something heavy
is snuffing the lamps. Lune's last mark is at the top of the cliff, and the memory there shows her
turning back, wings in rags, toward home and the dawn. **The ember-moths stop. From here on, Vesper is
farther than her mother ever got.** The mark teaches her a sideways dodge. Then the two friends part:
Sedge to her field, Vesper to the Mast. They touch antennae.

**Midnight: the Mast** *(the reveal, and the bill)*. At the foot of a drowned radio mast lies the dish from
the prologue, ringed by 916 moth-scratches, and Vesper adds the 917th. Beside it a lit lantern holds an
old, fat sap-drinking moth, **standing in the light mid-wingbeat, as still as the stag**. She came back to
this lamp for so many years that the god saw her. Vesper climbs alone above the canopy. Below, the lit
lanterns lie **in exactly the dish's pattern** and draw a moth, complete except for one dark path that
runs to a hollow in the far hills.
Then a knot of bark on the beam opens an eye. It is **a nightjar the god rides**, and it strikes whatever
stands still. It lunges, and a heavy furred hand yanks Vesper into shadow. **It is the same grip the
player fought in the prologue.** Compline covers her with both wings while the nightjar hunts past, and
the player must hold still in the dark. Afterwards Compline smooths Vesper's torn wing with her mouth.

**The Stillwood.** Compline carries her across the held country on her back, flying low and heavily past
fireflies hanging in the air. The nightjar follows them. In the slow forest it is at home, and Compline,
heavy and nearly still herself, is its easiest prey. **Boss: the nightjar.** Vesper fights it standing
over Compline, and it dies.
Compline's hollow is warm and lit. Rows of coals line the walls: her daughters, forty Long Nights of them.
She brought every one home rather than leave it for a lantern, and that is why so many lamps have stayed
dark. **There is one gap in the row.** Vesper understands whose coal is missing, and that Compline left
it on the island for her. Then Compline seals the door with root. *Boss: Compline.* She never strikes.
She blocks, pulls and covers. Every hit the player lands on her shows.
**Sedge** comes. She has followed the snuffed lamps. She tears at the root seal from outside, and its
thorns shred her wings. Compline turns to face her, the huge moth over the small one. Sedge does not
move. Compline looks at Sedge's torn wings, then at Vesper, and steps aside.

**The coldest hour.** The moon sets. The way home runs back through the Grove in frost, and with no
moon, Vesper steers by the lanterns she lit at dusk. Sedge flies beside her as far as her field, more
slowly each minute.

**Before dawn: Sedge's field.** One lamp is left, high on a pole. Sedge tries three times, but her torn
wings won't carry her up. She dies at its foot. There is no fight here. Vesper lights the lamp for her.
On the post, Sedge's last scratch-mark shows two small moths racing up a tower.

**Dawn: the Eye.** A hollow where the god's roots break into the open, where the first sun lands.
Vesper steps into the light, and everything around her begins to slow. Compline lands beside her and
takes her hand, **the grip from the prologue and from the Mast**, and pulls her toward the shade.
Holding against her is harder on frayed wings. Letting go is always allowed.

### Endings
- **Seen (hold).** The god looks. Vesper stops mid-shiver, held, like the stag. Then the sun reaches her,
  and her one night ends **inside its hold**. For the first time, something it held has ended. The
  stillness breaks outward in a ring, the same ring the Grove made at dusk, now crossing the whole land.
  Far off, the Lamplighter's arm comes down. **Then the player controls Compline** for one last beat. The
  heavy moth walks out of the shade, into the sun, for the first time in forty years. She lays her
  snuffer in the cup beside Vesper's coal, and leaves the coal where it lies. **The bill:** on the next
  Long Night, the moths hatch to lanterns that nobody lit. They stand in them all night, perfectly still,
  and are let go at dawn. They are seen now, for good. Is that kindness or storage? The game doesn't
  say.
- **Unseen (let go).** Compline draws Vesper into the shade, and Vesper ends in her grandmother's hold.
  Compline carries the coal home and sets it in the gap in the row. The god never holds anything that
  ends, and the Stillwood takes the world. Next year the moths hatch free and unseen, and dance around
  trees that were people.
- **The trail.** At dawn the map pulls back, and every coal the player left glows along the path of the
  night. Dying never makes the ending smaller. It makes the trail longer.

## 8. The Grove, and what its ending means
The Grove is dusk, and blue hour is the night's literal first hour. Vesper's cocoon hangs on a lantern
hook, and the canopy cocoons are her sisters. The empty summit socket is the climb Lune didn't finish.
Glancing up, taking the coal, carrying it, looking into the empty glass and giving it are a daughter
finishing her mother's last climb, and the moths that rise to the coal are a small funeral.

**The ending beat obeys the law, and teaches it.** Three steady lanterns draw the god's look down
through the heart. The ring of light is that look arriving, and **for one breath the whole Grove holds
still**: motes hang in the air, the grass stops mid-sway, Vesper freezes. *(This needs one small
addition to the built scene.)* Then **the offering**: each glint Vesper gives burns out inside the
heart, a small thing ending in the god's hold, and with each one it lets go a little more. First the
flowers, then the root-veins, then the canopy. The more she gives, the more it lets go. This is the
whole game's idea in a handful of glints, and at dawn Vesper herself will be the last of them. Far off,
a stone woman's fingers stir.

## 9. Environmental storytelling
1. **The Lamplighter.** Her fingers open a little more with each heart that wakes. Every moth lantern
   copies the hook on her pole.
2. **"LAMP 12"**, a brass plate on the oldest post, under centuries of moths' scratches.
3. **The Querystones**, above all *"Are the moths a danger? … 9 years: no."*
4. **The dish**: gold dots, 916 scratches, and the same pattern laid out across the land.
5. **The held sap-drinker** in her lantern at the Mast, a wing raised for years.
6. **Compline's hollow**: rows of warm coals, and one gap.
7. **Old coals** from earlier years, burning deeper and deeper in. The farthest lies a few steps short of
   the Eye.

## 10. The question it leaves
*Would you give up your people's only hiding place to set the world free? And when Compline finally
let go of one coal, was that the same lesson the god needed, learned forty years sooner?*

### Changes from round 6
- **One law, said aloud and obeyed everywhere.** Its look holds; it lets go only of what ends in its
  hold. It is in the spine. Lanterns, the Lamplighter's blink and the Eye all follow it, and the Grove's
  heart now teaches it with a one-breath freeze and glints that end in its hold.
- **Compline's kinship and turn are on screen.** She is Lune's mother. She left Lune's coal (the gap in
  her row), steps aside for Sedge, and in *Seen* the player walks her into the sun.
- **Lune's climb is played.** It is the prologue, and the player is the one pulled to the shade, so the
  grip at the Mast and at the Eye is recognised.
- **The question is aimed at the true cost**, her people's hiding place, and the bill is shown at the
  Mast (the held sap-drinker) before the choice.
- **The lineage rules are simpler**: eggs at lanterns, sleeping a year unless the mother dies that night.
  Ember lanterns take any moth's own light. Compline's hoarded coals are why lamps are dark.
- **The last third is uncrowded.** The nightjar dies in the Stillwood. Sedge dies of the thorns she tore
  through for Vesper, quietly, beneath the lamp Vesper lights for her.
- **The echoes are reshaped.** Coals are not retrieved (no shade): they become lights. There is no
  kneel-to-see-memories. Memories live only in Lune's marks. There are no roots closing over the hero,
  the forty eggs are now a row with a gap, and two boss names are cut.
