# NOISE

## The spine (tell it to a friend)

1. A mother's little boy drowned chasing moths. She wished nothing would ever happen again, and the
   living machine she grew made the whole world safe and still.
2. A young moth, Vesper, wakes her only friend, an old beetle stuck walking in circles. The mother
   feels the world stir and sends her guardian to put out the last wild fire. The old moth who raised
   Vesper dies defending it.
3. In the end Vesper stands in the mother's kitchen holding that fire. She must choose: stop the
   mother's heart and set the world loose, or make her look at a moth for the first time since her son
   died, and end the moths' hiding for ever.

**The central question:** is it love to keep someone safe, or to let them go?

## The cast

| | Who | Wants | Wants from Vesper | Pull on her |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vesper** | A young moth no lamp has ever looked at. | To be seen by someone. | | |
| **Hob** | An old burrback pacing the same circle by the shrine all her life. | Safety; later, a life of his own. | To be left alone, then protected, then for her to stay in the circle, then to be let go. | Why she starts, and why she keeps wanting to stop. |

| **Maud** | A very old woman who walks the dam top with a lamp every evening. Wim's mother. | For nothing to happen to anyone, ever again. | For the flicker to stop. She doesn't know it's a moth. | Her grief is easy to understand; her one deed is unforgivable. |
| **The Lamplighter** | The great lampwing that flies Maud's walk and does what she asks. | To keep Maud undisturbed. | For Vesper to keep away from Maud. | A guard, then Sallow's killer. Its later turn is guilt. |

**Wim**, the drowned boy, never appears. He is there in what he left behind: moths scratched into
stones, a drawing, a jar lamp.

## 1. The world, and how the Quiet took it

Sixty years ago, in the flood valley of the Wend, Wim Ferrier went down to the flood stone at night
with a jar lamp to catch moths. The river rose faster than anyone had said it could. It took him and
ten other children.

His mother Maud kept the valley's water, and she grew a better keeper: living nerve cells in a dish on
her kitchen table. When the cells predicted the river they got a steady pulse; when they were wrong
they got static. *(Dev note: grounded in Cortical Labs' DishBrain/CL1. None of these words are
player-facing.)* The first regular thing the dish ever felt was the kitchen's longcase clock, and it
learned to keep that time.

It held the river, so the town asked for cuttings. Every house in Lowmere grew a dish on its
windowsill and asked it one thing more: hold back the storms, keep the fox from the hens, keep Dad's
heart steady. The dishes rooted together, and other valleys asked too. Now one soft swell a second
runs through the world's ground and every living thing keeps step: fireflies in rows, rain when due.
They called it **the Quiet**. Nothing has died badly since. The people grew old in safety and died in
their sleep at the expected ages.

## 2. The Quiet, and why the moths were left alone

**The one rule of sight.** The Quiet sees through its lampwings' lamps, and those lamps look away from
moths. A lamp that falls on a moth flinches and turns aside, the way Maud turned from the moth on the
boot. But a lamp *will* strike anything that repeats itself inside its beam. The player feels this as
one rule: in a lamp's light, never do the same thing twice.

**Underestimated.** In the Grove, a lampwing's beam lands full on Vesper's face, holds for a long beat,
then swings away. On the surface, the moths were judged harmless and not worth the bother. Later the
player recognises the turn as the woman on the riverbank turning away from the boot. Nobody weighed
the moths. Something couldn't bear to look at them.

**Is it someone?** The game shows both and never chooses. For: a faint shudder when a pod is cut;
every lamp dips when Maud nods; the Lamplighter's guilt. Against: everything else it does, Maud did
first, and a lamp gives a falling leaf the same long hang it gave Vesper.

## 3. Vesper

Free, as nothing else is, and alone. Since she was a larva she has waited at the end of Hob's loop, on
a spot her own small feet have worn, and lifted her wings three times: their hello. He has never once
looked up.

- **Want:** for Hob to see her.
- **Need:** to let others choose, even when what they choose is to leave her.
- **Flaw:** she flits, and she forces. Every pattern looks to her like a cage, and she breaks things
  open "for their own good" without asking. Maud forced safety on the world out of love, and Vesper
  forces chance on it out of loneliness. Neither of them asked Hob.

## 4. The forest and its creatures

Sallow's rule: *"Everything alive does something you didn't ask it to."*

- **Two signals.** Whatever the Quiet holds swells evenly, together. Whatever is truly alive flickers
  unevenly, like a heartbeat.
- **Glints** are chance: a firefly flashing late, a spark off the beat. The Quiet seals stray chance
  in **lumen pods**, and cutting one lets it out.
- **Lanterns** are old moth work: a lit one draws a swarm into tumbling orbit, and nothing in a swarm
  repeats, so no lamp strikes there. Burrbacks lose the beat at its edge and turn back.
- **The ember** is a coal from the last lightning fire before the Settling. Sallow has kept it alive
  for sixty years.
- **Burrbacks** ram on the beat; **lampwings** are bats remade into eyes. **Fading** is being
  struck; Vesper re-forms at the nearest lantern.
- **The moth-stones** are the valley's old flood stones. Wim scratched a moth into each one at a
  child's height, each in a different pose of flight, and the lamps slide off them. Each one teaches
  Vesper a move. She learns to fly the way a dead boy drew moths flying.

## 5. Prologue (wordless, about two minutes)

1. Black. A soft amber swell of light, once a second.
2. Night rain on a rising river. Moths circle a jar lamp in the reeds. The water climbs a stone with
   a child's moth scratched into it.
3. Grey morning. A woman on the bank holds one small boot. A moth lands on it. She brushes it off and
   turns her face away. Hold on the turned face.
4. The swell spreads along the river and the water goes glassy. Years pass: fireflies blink in rows,
   a fox walks past a hen, and a steeple stands upright under a calm reservoir.
5. Blue hour. A very old woman walks the dam top with a lamp, step for step, and a great lampwing
   flies beside her. Moths cross her light, and she doesn't look down.
6. The Grove today. The fireflies blink in time, and one flashes late. A small moth rides a lap on a
   burrback's shell, steps off onto her worn spot, and lifts her wings three times. He walks past.
7. A lampwing drops in front of her. Its lamp holds on her face, then turns away. Title.

## 6. The story

### Act I: Noise. *With her: Sallow, then Hob.*

- **The Grove** (section 8). Sallow teaches the moth way: never the same path twice, never be seen.
  When Vesper climbs to take the ember, Sallow blocks her with her wings spread wide, then steps
  aside. It is the first time she has given in. Vesper lights the lanterns, and the heart wakes.
  **Hob misses a step, stops, and looks at her.** He bolts. She finds him shaking in a hollow, lights
  a wayside lamp beside him, and sits with him through the night. In the morning he follows her.
  *Move:* **Tumble** (double-jump).
- **Tollmeadow.** *Move:* **Dart** (dash). **Boss: the Choir**, a hill of fireflies that strikes on
  the swell and is hurt only in the gaps. Broken, the meadow flickers raggedly, some fireflies burned
  out. It was the most beautiful thing in the game, and you broke it.
- **Maud's deed.** Cut to her kitchen. The meadow's flicker reaches her as a tremor. She shuts her
  eyes, puts her hand on the clock's key, and winds it. Every swell in the world grows harder, and
  the meadow is pressed back into rows. The great lamp leaves the dam for the ember island. She never
  opens her eyes.
- **Sallow.** Vesper sees the lamp cross the sky and races home, leaving Hob behind. She arrives in
  time to see Sallow flying a perfect circle out over the water inside the Lamplighter's beam. The
  moth who taught "never the same path twice" flies the same path again and again, to lead the lamp
  away from the hidden coal and out where no lantern can catch her. The beam narrows and strikes.
  Sallow greys, and she does not re-form.
  - **From then on**, the ember-moths follow Vesper, and the lanterns are hers to tend. A lantern she
    leaves behind gutters out unless she feeds it a glint, so every rest now costs something. She
    carries a coal of the ember to the end.
- **The Ruled Wood.** *Move:* **Turn** (wall-kick). Grief makes her hard and fast; Hob keeps up
  anyway. She drops glints to him from ledges he can't climb, rides his back through the walls he
  breaks, and hides under him when the lamps come. He starts to wait at the foot of every climb.
  **The Stag of Three**, which has drunk the same three sips for sixty years, blocks the path. She
  startles it and it bolts. Hob watches it run.

### Act II: Asked For. *With her: Hob, then no one.*

- **The Weir.** *Move:* **Drift** (glide). **Boss: the Weirkeeper.** Every hit makes the water
  surge: the original wound, replayed. When it falls, the reservoir starts to drain.
- **The Levee.** Vesper lands on Maud's lamp, then her sleeve, then the back of her hand. Maud's eyes
  slide off her, and **in the same instant every lamp in the valley slides away with them.** Now the
  player knows: the lamps look where Maud looks. The Lamplighter drives Vesper off.
- **The quarrel.** The valley is pulled two ways now, by what Vesper wakes and by what Maud winds
  tighter, and the weather tears. A falling branch cracks Hob's shell, and he won't go on. She leaves
  him at a lantern, feeds it a glint, and goes on alone, as she always has.
- **Lowmere (midpoint).** The drained town. A cracked dish sits on every windowsill, with a root from
  each one running into the street. The roots braid down the high street and climb the dam to one lit
  window. In the school, eleven small coats hang on their pegs. Wim's stone stands by the door, with a
  jar lamp in the reeds below it. *(No text.)*
- **Hob's choice.** The lantern where she left him has gone out. She finds him walking a new circle
  between the rows. He went back into the Quiet because it doesn't hurt there, and he can't see her
  any more. Nearby, the Stag lies dead in a ravine it never used to have to look at.

### Reunion. *With her: no one, then Hob.*

She can't fight him, so she doesn't. She lands and **walks his circle beside him**, on foot, lap after
lap, under a lampwing's beam, where doing the same thing twice is death. Each lap the beam narrows.
She keeps walking. On the last lap the lamp dives, and Hob steps out of his circle to put his shell
over her. She made herself a habit to reach him, and he broke his to save her. She learns **Keeping
Time**: landing exactly on the swell opens settled gates. Hob carries her again.

### Act III: The Mother. *With her: Hob, later the Lamplighter.*

- **The Spillway and the Lamplighter.** They climb the dam face in a storm. On top waits the one lamp
  that doesn't look where Maud looks: it watches whatever comes towards her. The fight is won by keeping time until it commits, then breaking once. At the end
  it sees the coal Vesper carries, Sallow's fire, and its lamp drops. It doesn't fight again. It
  follows them in.
- **The Bellhouse.** Maud's kitchen: a table set for two, a cold hearth, a child's drawing face down.
  Above her chair, the clock's pendulum has grown into a root as thick as a tree. Its swing is the
  world's beat, and her heart keeps time with it. Through the window, Vesper's ember-moths hang in a
  swarm.

## 7. The choice

Both options are open and neither is locked. The player already knows the price, because the Levee
taught them that every lamp looks where Maud looks.

Vesper lights the hearth with Sallow's coal. Firelight flickers across Maud's face for the first time
in sixty years. She turns from it, as always, and her hand knocks the drawing over: Wim's moth, drawn
as a scribble because he drew it flying. As her eyes begin to move towards it, the Lamplighter's lamp
turns with them, and so does every lamp beyond the window, towards the swarm. Vesper hangs between
the drawing and the pendulum's root.

- **Make her look.** Vesper flies the scribble: the tumble, the dart and the turn the stones taught
  her. **Maud looks.** She holds out a finger, and Vesper lands on it. Every lamp in the world turns
  onto the moths. Outside, the ember-moths, seen for the first time, scatter from the light, and so
  do moths across the valley. Sallow's whole life was the hiding, and it is over. The pendulum
  hitches one beat late and swings on, never quite even again, the way a healthy heart never is. Rain
  falls that nobody scheduled. Maud lives, unevenly, by the fire. Lampwings watch every moth from now
  on. *This is forgiveness, paid for with Sallow's legacy.*
- **Cut the root.** Maud's heart stops, her face still turned away. One night brings back sixty years
  of storm, fire and flood, and some things die badly. The lamps go out, the lampwings are bats
  again, and the moths stay unseen for ever, as Sallow wanted. At dawn a bat hunts a moth through a
  blazing green valley, and the moth flies the old way. *This is Sallow avenged and her way kept,
  paid for with Maud and the world's safety.*

Either way, **Hob goes**. He stands in the doorway, and Vesper gives him three wingbeats. He sees her
completely. Then, free, he walks into the hills. She doesn't follow. Grass grows in his old circle,
and her small worn spot is still beside it.

## 8. The Grove (the scene we have)

The Grove is the first chapter. Its ending is the first time Vesper is seen, and the first thing Maud
cannot bear.
- **Three lit lanterns** make a ring of swarms where no lamp can strike, so the forest's own heart
  beats again.
- **The veins of light** running to the lanterns are that heartbeat. They pulse unevenly.
- **The moth carving** is Wim's. It fills with light and teaches her the Tumble.
- **The ring of light** is the first unplanned thing in sixty years. A two-second cut follows: on the
  dam the old woman misses a step and turns her head away, and every lamp in the valley turns with
  her. This plants her deed.
- **The offering** gives the forest back its chance. Flowers open at the wrong hour, and the canopy
  cocoons split early.
- **Then Hob looks at her.**

Scene changes: one shrine burrback becomes Hob, with a worn circle and her spot beside it.
Fireflies swell together while the Grove sleeps and flicker once it wakes. The lampwings'
tell-dive-hang-climb becomes a strike at a repeated move in the beam. The ember island is Sallow's.

## 9. What the player finds

1. **Wim's stones.** A child's moth at a child's height, with dated water-marks above it.
2. **Lowmere's dishes**, one on every windowsill, their roots braided down the street.
3. **Eleven small coats** on the school pegs.
4. **The jar lamp** in the reeds, with air holes punched in its lid.
5. **Maud's first dish**, with tape in her hand reading *"Let nothing happen."* These are the only human
   words in the game.
6. **The clock key**, its brass worn thin by sixty years of winding.
7. **Item: Even Heart**, a pacing knot. *"A healthy heart stumbles a little. This one never has."*
8. **Sallow**, as she steps aside: *"Sixty years I kept it. Don't keep it."*
9. **The leaf.** A lamp gives a falling leaf the same long hang it gave Vesper.

## 10. What it leaves the player thinking

Sallow kept the moths safe by hiding them, and Maud kept the world safe by stopping it. Vesper has to
undo one of those loves. Which was right? And if the Quiet only ever looked where Maud looked, whose
grief was it?

## Changes from round 6
- **Maud acts.** She winds the clock with her eyes shut and sends the Lamplighter to put out the
  fire, and Sallow dies of it. The Lamplighter's later turn is guilt, and the hearth lit with
  Sallow's coal is forgiveness the player has paid for.
- **Sallow's death is new.** It no longer echoes Kuro or Naru: she makes herself a habit on purpose,
  circling in the beam to lead it from the coal. It changes play: the lanterns are now Vesper's to
  tend, and every rest costs a glint.
- **One rule of sight.** Lamps flinch from moths, as Maud did, and strike only repetition inside the
  beam. "Underestimated" reads as dismissal at first and as grief later. The amber/lilac landing
  feedback is cut, leaving two signals.
- **The ending is one open choice, not three endings with a hidden condition.** "Look" is
  forgiveness, and it costs the moths their invisibility on screen, at the moment of choice. "Cut" is
  Sallow avenged, and it costs Maud and the world's safety. Stillness and the locked true ending are
  gone. Hob leaves in both.
- **The reunion is not a spare-to-win boss.** She walks his circle under a narrowing beam until he
  breaks his habit to shield her.
- **Restraint restored.** The prologue opens on the single swell and the late firefly. The windowsill
  shot is cut, and Lowmere has no narration.
