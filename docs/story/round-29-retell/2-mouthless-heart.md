# Mouthless: a cold read (the Ori player)

## 1. The story in three sentences
A pale living thing has put the whole world to sleep in hanging cases, but it can't see moths. Umber, a moth with a week to live, finds that its heart is an abandoned brain grown in a dish, and when she warms it, it sees moths and starts keeping them too. At the end she chooses: be kept forever, burn it and its sleepers, or spend herself warming it until it lets go.

## 2. Characters
- **Umber**: a dying moth who wants to save her wood, then the world.
- **Sorrel**: her brother. He wants his partner, then more time with Umber.
- **The Hush**: a grown brain that wants to keep everyone close, because leaving hurts.
- **Dun**: its badger servant. It fetches what the Hush keeps, then turns to guarding Umber.
- **Tallow**: the last man awake. He wants to burn the cases and find his sister.
- **The forest**: an old mind that wants to give its warmth away.

## 3. The enemy
A brain grown from a woman's cells, which fed a hungry town. When everyone walked out, the static of being left never stopped, so it put the world into cases: no one wakes and no one is born. It wants never to be left again.

## 4. Endings
- **Spin:** every moth gives itself, the Hush spins a cocoon and the world wakes up; Umber and Sorrel die together.
- **Stay:** Umber is kept with Sorrel and his partner, warm and dancing forever, and nothing is ever born again.
- **Burn:** the Hush dies along with most of its sleepers, and Umber gets one last dance in an empty wood.

## 5. What confused me
- Night 3: did Tallow just kill a sleeper? I only understood on Night 6.
- Twenty tally marks, but the sister is "old now": years or days?
- Night 4: I only realised at the endings that the lantern option means losing the coal, so Burn requires killing the breathing man.
- "Every lampwing's lamp goes grey": what does that change?
- How is the partner freed in Burn? Where is Dun in Stay and Burn?
- "Something presses back": hopeful or ominous? I wasn't sure.
- In Spin, the partner lives out her week alone. Is it meant to be that cruel?

## 6. The moment I'd remember
Sorrel curled round the grey seed as root closes over him, until one wingtip moves. Runner-up: Dun lying across her lantern door, facing out.

**Would I buy and finish it? Yes.** The prologue is pure Ori, the wing-tap passes between the siblings to the end, and the villain is a lonely abandoned child, not a monster. I'd cry at Spin. I'd want the partner's fate there softened, or its cruelty earned on screen.
