# Mouthless: casual player retell (round 29)

## 1. The story in three sentences
A giant grown brain called the Hush has covered the world in pale root and put every person and animal to sleep in pod-like cases, but it can't see moths, because moths don't eat. Umber, a moth with one week to live, goes from her little wood to the mountain at its heart and finds out it was a lab brain that people abandoned, and it's basically been lonely and hurting ever since. When she warms it, it finally notices moths and comes for them too, and at the end she has to choose whether to be kept safe in a case forever, burn it (killing most sleepers), or give her life so it can turn into something new.

## 2. Characters
- **Umber**: the hero moth; wants to save her wood, then the world.
- **Sorrel**: her brother; wants to dance with his partner, then wants time with Umber.
- **The Hush**: the grown brain; wants to feed and keep everyone so nobody leaves it.
- **Dun**: a root-covered badger servant; brings the Hush things to keep, then switches sides and guards Umber.
- **Tallow**: last awake man; wants to burn the cases and find his sister.
- **The forest**: a slow old mind; wants to give its warmth away.

## 3. The enemy
A lab-grown brain fed with noise when it failed. Once everyone walked out, it spread root and cases over the world so no one would be hungry or leave. Nobody is born any more. It wants to keep everyone close.

## 4. Endings
- **Spin**: the moths warm it until it cocoons itself, people wake, Umber and Sorrel die, something inside presses back.
- **Stay**: Umber gets in the case; everyone's warm forever, nothing is ever born again.
- **Burn**: the Hush dies and takes most sleepers with it, Tallow's sister included.

## 5. Confusions
- Why does being "warmed" make it hunt moths? I got that it wants to keep them, but on first read it felt like a punishment for being kind.
- I didn't get Tallow's coal at first. He says it's "for the thing in the mountain," which sounds like a weapon, but putting it in the lantern is the nice path. Pressing it to the wall is the evil one.
- Night 5: I couldn't tell whether Sorrel was dead ("one wingtip moves") until Night 6.
- What the Hush's threads "curling and falling open" mean only clicked at the very end (the caterpillar).
- Glints, lanterns, coals, lampwings, cases and seeds: a lot of stuff to track.

## 6. Memorable moment / would I buy it
Sorrel curled round his partner's tiny grey seed while root closes over the lantern, then one wingtip moves. Yes, I'd buy it. A dying moth that fixes things by giving warmth away, not by fighting, is a hook I haven't seen. The endings actually hurt. I'd finish it if the "give, don't fight" play feels good.
