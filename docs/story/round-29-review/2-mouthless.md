# Review: Mouthless (round 29, narrative director, cold read)

## 1. Anchor gate: PASS
All three readers open the same way: a moth with a week to live against a pale living thing that put the world to sleep. Two of them also name the grown brain in their first sentence ("A grown brain that people abandoned in a lab", "A giant grown brain called the Hush"), and the third reaches it in her second. The brain is found in play (the poster, the coat, the dish) and pays off as a revelation in the memory scene. The Hush acts at world scale in every act: the root under the pool and the breathing mountain (I), the orchard of cases, the tower root, the walled lab door and the turn (II), the overrun wood, the dead city and the boss inside the mountain (III).
- Biological AI took over the world: **central**
- Umber saves the world: **central**
- Moths deemed insignificant, underestimated: **central** (rule 3 is both the stealth mechanic and the hinge of the midpoint)
- Sentience (forest, animals, maybe the AI): **central** (the forest acts three times, Dun turns, and the closing question asks it outright)

## 2. Verdict: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES
No foundation problems. The notes are about legibility.

## 3. At the bar
- **The mechanic is the theme.** "A moth has no mouth to keep it." She has no mouth, so she can't keep anything, and the Hush exists to keep. Give-don't-fight and shivering spell out her nature in play, and the casual reader picked it up as the hook ("fixes things by giving warmth away, not by fighting").
- **An antagonist who is wrong in an illuminating way, rooted in real science.** The static of a missed ball becomes the static of being left: "each leaving hisses like a missed ball." Every reader explained its motive correctly. The Ori reader called it "a lonely abandoned child, not a monster."
- **The relationship images carry the story without dialogue.** The wing-tap and folded wing pass between the siblings from the prologue to the end. Two of three readers named "Sorrel curled round his partner's tiny grey seed… one wingtip moves" as the moment they'd remember. Dun lying across the lantern door, facing out, is Ori-grade.
- **The sister's arc is the lore-hunter's jackpot.** She grows the first light from her own hand, comes back with a torch, pinches it out and is kept. The last image, "Something presses back from inside. She doesn't lift her hand", closes the prologue's first shot.
- **The endings cost something and differ in kind.**

## 4. Problems, ranked
1. **POLISH: the turn reads as punishment for kindness (casual).** The cause is there ("the first picture the Hush has ever made") but it doesn't land in play. *Fix:* when the Hush first comes for moths after the turn, its first act should be tender. A root lifts a fleeing moth gently into a tiny case that glows warm, with no attack. Wanting, not anger, should be the first thing the player sees.
2. **POLISH: the coal choice is unreadable when it's made (2 of 3 readers).** "It is for the thing in the mountain" makes the coal sound like a weapon, yet giving it up is the kind path. *Fix:* make the cost visible on the spot. Setting it in the lantern makes the coal visibly burn down to ash in her hands. Pressing it to the wall makes it flare red and stay in her HUD. Then the player can see they are choosing between keeping a weapon and giving it away. No new rule needed.
3. **POLISH: the partner's fate is loose in two endings.** In Burn the seed never opens on screen, and in Spin her lonely week feels cruel without being earned. *Fix:* in Burn, show the seed split as the root goes grey. In Spin, give her one beat: she lands where the two moths lie and taps Sorrel's wingtip before she circles. That makes the cruelty a goodbye.
4. **POLISH: Tallow's Night 3 burn is ambiguous (did he kill a sleeper?).** *Fix:* hold on a face inside the case going dark before the static. Then Night 6's "he sees the face" is a recognition, not the first time the player learns it.
5. **POLISH: too many objects and loose threads.** Glint, coal, case, seed, pit, thread. Readers asked whether the tallies count days or years, where Sorrel's glints come from, what grey lamps change, where Dun is in Stay and Burn, and whether the dead oak is the summit. *Fix:* call the bead "a case no bigger than a seed" so there's no separate seed object. Make the marks "twenty winters". Put the dance at the summit from Night 1. Let grey lamps visibly dive at moths. Put Dun in one shot of each ending.
6. **POLISH: Grove fit.** The brief's burrbacks are beetle-like, and Dun is a badger. *Fix:* either say that burrbacks are re-meant as badgers grown into root armour (brace, ram and curl still work), or make Dun a great burrback.

## 5. Scores
anchor 9 · relationships 9 · clarity 8 · idea 9 · world 9 · mechanics-as-theme 9 · antagonist 9 · ending 9 · originality 8 · Grove fit 8 · balance 8

(Originality: hanging pods echo familiar sci-fi. Balance: Nights 4–7 are dense for a casual player.)

## 6. AT THE BAR? Yes
It stands beside Ori and Nine Sols. It has a fable surface and a technical truth underneath, a sibling bond told through gesture, and a villain whose wrongness is the hero's virtue turned inside out. The gap still worth closing is the moment of the coal choice, the one place where the player can't yet read what they're deciding.
