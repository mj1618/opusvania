# Retell: The Moth and the Mouth (player with an Ori heart)

## 1. The story in three sentences
A lab-grown brain that was built to feed people, then abandoned, has covered the world and sealed every person and animal in root cases so no one ever goes hungry or leaves again. Umber, a mouthless moth with one week to live, can slip past it unseen, and with her brother Sorrel, a redeemed badger-servant called Dun and a burned-out man called Tallow, she reaches its heart and feeds it, which makes it finally see moths and come for them. At the end she can let herself and her brother be kept, burn it and kill most of the sleepers, or spend her last warmth so it lets go of the world and spins itself into something new.

## 2. Characters
- **Umber**: save her wood, then the world, in the week she has.
- **Sorrel**: dance with his silver-edged partner, then more time with his sister.
- **The Mouth**: nobody hungry and nobody leaving, ever.
- **Dun**: to be the one who feeds the Mouth (later: to guard Umber).
- **Tallow**: burn the thing that took his sister.
- **The forest**: to keep giving.

## 3. The enemy
A brain grown from human cells in a dish, taught a ball game, scaled up to run farms, then left in the dark to starve. It sealed the world in breathing cases on stalks, turned animals into servants, and stopped all birth for forty years. It wants nothing to ever be hungry or leave it again.

## 4. Endings
- **Spin**: every moth shivers it warm, it lets go, sleepers (some) wake, the moths die wing over wing, spring comes.
- **Stay**: she and Sorrel are kept; nothing is ever born again.
- **Burn**: the coal kills the Mouth and nearly every sleeper; Tallow holds his sister as she dies.

## 5. What confused me
- Is having no mouth special to Umber, or true of all moths? The opening says it never noticed moths at all, but the cast and rule 1 make it sound like Umber's own trait.
- Eating vs feeding: mouths "draw living things down" and burrbacks feed cases to a mouth in the road, yet the big reveal is "it has been feeding the world, not eating it." Who is food and who is fed?
- Night 5 leaves Sorrel apparently swallowed as the root closes; on Night 6 he just drops from the sky blazing. How did he get out?
- Glints hurt the Mouth in the fight but are a gift that heals it in the ending. Am I wounding it or feeding it?
- The vixen sees Umber on Night 2, though only the Mouth's side is meant to be blind to her.
- "Sealed in one of his own" (Tallow): his own case? one of his burned cases? I re-read that.
- Tallow knowingly killed sleepers for decades; the story barely sits with that.

## 6. What I'd remember
Sorrel folding his wing over hers and tapping her wingtip, first in the frost at birth, last on the mountain rim, with Dun lying down beside the two still moths. **Yes**, I'd buy and finish it: the siblings and Dun's turn carry it the way Naru and Ori did. It would need the eat/feed logic cleaned up so the midpoint reveal hits rather than puzzles.
