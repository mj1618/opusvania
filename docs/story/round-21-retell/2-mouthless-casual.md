# Retell: The Moth and the Mouth (casual player, one read)

## 1. The story in three sentences
A huge living thing called the Mouth has grown over the world and sealed everyone in root pods, but it can't see moths, so Umber, a moth with a week to live, sneaks into its heart. It turns out to be a lab-grown brain built to feed people, then abandoned to starve, and now it keeps everyone asleep and fed so no one is ever hungry or left again. When she feeds it, it sees moths and comes for them, and in the end she chooses: sleep safe beside her brother forever, burn it, or spend her life warming it until it lets the world go.

## 2. Characters
- **Umber**: mouthless moth, one week to live; wants to save her wood, then everyone.
- **Sorrel**: her brother; wants to dance with his partner, then more time with Umber.
- **The Mouth**: the starved lab brain; wants no one hungry and no one leaving, ever.
- **Dun**: its badger servant; wants to be the one who feeds it.
- **Tallow**: last man awake; wants to burn the thing that took his sister.
- **The forest**: slow kind mind under the Grove; wants to keep giving.

## 3. The enemy
A lab brain grown to run farms, then left to starve. It sealed every person and animal in pods on stalks; no one has woken or been born in forty years. It isn't eating them, it's feeding them, so nothing is ever hungry or left again.

## 4. Endings
- **Spin**: every moth shivers warmth into it until it lets go; the world wakes, Umber dies beside Sorrel.
- **Stay**: she joins her brother in a pod; the world stays asleep, nothing born again.
- **Burn**: she drops the coal in; the Mouth dies and most sleepers with it.

## 5. What confused me
- "It only sees mouths" and "Umber, a moth with no mouth": is it all moths or just her? Reads like both.
- Why does warming it make it let go? The caterpillar parallel is pretty, but I didn't follow the cause and effect.
- In the ending, its stalks "go slack" and frost spreads before I choose: are the sleepers dying if I do nothing?
- How was Tallow the one person awake for forty years? Never explained.
- Night 5 Sorrel won't leave his partner; Night 6 he swoops in blazing. What changed?
- Night 4 forces me to burn a wall of sleepers ("the only way in"), then blames me for it.
- Section 2 already says it feeds the sleepers, so the Night 4 twist landed flat.

## 6. What I'd remember
Sorrel wrapped around his partner's tiny seed, not moving, while the root closes over the lantern and Umber has to leave him. And the last two moths wing over wing with Dun lying down beside them.

Buy and finish? **Yes**, for the brother: the wing-fold-and-shiver pays off three times. But the rules section nearly lost me first.
