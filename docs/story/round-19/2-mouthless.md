# MOUTHLESS

**LITMUS.** "It's about Vesper, a little moth with no mouth, against the Mouth, a living thing that swallowed the
whole world and never bothered to notice moths. Halfway through you find out what it really is: a brain people grew
from their own cells, starving, and it's been feeding everyone but itself."

**The spine.**
1. A living thing the moths call the Mouth has swallowed the world into its roots. It never noticed moths, because it
   only sees mouths.
2. Vesper, a moth with no mouth, slips through it to its heart and finds a brain grown by people and left to starve,
   feeding the world and never fed.
3. When she feeds it, it sees her kind and comes for them, and she must choose: be kept, burn it, or spend her life so
   it lets go.

**The central question:** kept safe forever, or spent so something new can be born?

| Who | Wants | Pull on Vesper |
|---|---|---|
| **Vesper**, a moth with no mouth and a week to live | to keep the root out of her brood's wood, then to free the world | can only carry and give; her flaw is that she hides |
| **Sorrel**, her brother | the dance, then more time with her | shines to draw danger off her; leaves when her gift sets the Mouth on the moths; comes back |
| **The Mouth** | no one hungry, no one leaving, ever | ignores her, then sees her, then wants to keep her |
| **Dun**, its oldest burrback, half grown into root | to be the one who feeds it | rival, then the first servant to refuse it |
| **Tallow**, the last man awake | to burn it and get his sister back | gives her a coal, and his war |
| **The forest** | to keep giving | a slow mind under the Grove; the Mouth's opposite, and losing |

## 1. The takeover

The player meets a myth: a pale, warm, rooted thing that swallowed the world. Its mouths open in the ground and draw
living things down. Every person on earth hangs grey and still from a stalk in a case of root, like a seed pod; no one
has woken or been born in forty years. The first city is now the mountain, a pale peak of folded tissue on every
horizon. Animals are sealed too: a fox-shaped case in a hedge, a whale-shaped one on a white shore. Some it re-grew as
servants: burrbacks were badgers, shelled in root to haul cases; lampwings were fireflies, now its eyes. Its root is
creeping through the last wild places, the moths' wood among them.

## 2. The Mouth

**What it is.** Living brain cells on glass, taught one bat-and-ball game, then grown across a building to run a
city's farms. (Dev note: this is the brief's real-world lineage, human neurons grown on chips, scaled up and out of
control. Players learn it only from objects in the lab, never from technical words.)

**What it wants.** It was made to feed people, and its maker, Tallow's sister, fed it by hand. One winter the company
failed; she switched off the lights and left, meaning to come back, and its feed ran out. It learned two wrongs,
hunger and being left, and that a body sealed in a case and fed through a stalk suffers neither. By spring it had
followed its feed tubes into the city, then the soil and every tree, sending all it draws from the earth to the
sleepers.

**Where it is wrong.** Nothing kept can grow: no cubs, no children. And it starves. That spring its maker came back
with jars of feed, and it took her before she could open one. **A thing that takes everything can never be given
anything,** so the root never stops.

**Is it a someone?** *For:* once it sees Vesper it raises her shape on every wall, and at the end it gives. *Against:*
it learned its first game only to make the static stop, and a world kept still is the quietest world there is.

## 3. The rules

1. **It can't see her; its lampwings see light.** The Mouth sees only mouths, and grown moths have none, so it and its
   servants pass her by. But lampwings were fireflies: carrying glints, she glows with a gold halo, and every lampwing
   lamp swings to it. Gold light is the forest's; pale light is the Mouth's. After she feeds it on Night 4, it sees
   her anyway.
2. **She can't take, only give.** Glints are light the forest grows to give away. She sets them down as gifts, and a
   gift calms what it is given to. She hatches able to run, wall-kick, glide and give; every other power is a gift
   from someone, and only one, Tallow's coal, takes.
3. **Lanterns and nights.** Lanterns are old cocoons of her kind, hardened like glass. They hold the root back, calm
   its creatures, and let her rest and leave things. The game is her week of seven nights. When she fades, the nearest
   lantern spins her again and the night goes on. Within a night the world is open, and it changes as the root
   advances.
4. **Root walls are full of sleepers.** Each case in a wall is a grey bead on her map. Tallow's coal, carried in a
   black seed, burns through a wall, and those beads go dark. From Night 5, Dun can carry the cases aside instead.
5. **Shiver.** She can spend her own warmth to warm something cold. It leaves her slow until she rests.

## 4. The prologue (wordless, ninety seconds)

1. Black. A small pale light, pulsing like breath. Something warm rests against it, then lifts away. The light
   flickers. *(A seed? An egg? A heart?)*
2. Night from very high: a dark mountain with that pale light at its core. Root spreads from its foot like frost on
   glass, and a road of town lights goes out, one by one. Moths fly through it all untouched.
3. The Grove. A caterpillar chews a leaf; a burrback swings toward the sound. The caterpillar stops eating and wraps
   itself in silk, and the burrback, finding no mouth, loses interest.
4. Two cocoons split. Sorrel flies straight up to the brood's dance. Vesper catches a glint, raises it to her face,
   and can do nothing with it. Title.

## 5. The acts

### Act I: the edge (Nights 1–3), almost wordless

**Night 1, the Grove.** The last wild wood near the mountain. Sorrel is her guide; he somersaults over and over until
she can. The summit lantern needs the forest's biggest glint, from a high island. Carrying it she can't hide, and for
the first time every lampwing turns to her. **Boss: a great lampwing.** When the lanterns burn, the forest's heart
wakes under its stone carving of a moth, its oldest friend. It pushes the root back and gives her a thorn blade. Dun,
who has never seen anything give, noses at the open heart, puzzled. From the summit she sees the mountain's pale glow.

**Night 2, the orchards.** Burrbacks feed grey cases to a mouth in the ground. A root slides past Vesper's wing
without seeing her. In the cellar, cases hang in rows like fruit put by for winter. Above, Dun heads for the den of a
vixen, one of the last animals awake, and Vesper blocks the way. **Boss: Dun.** Dun pins her. Sorrel snatches her
glints and darts off blazing; the lampwings swing to him, and Dun follows their lamps. The vixen runs, and Vesper
learns to dash chasing her brother.

**Night 3, the firebreak.** A ring of ash the root can't cross, a fire on a pole, and **Tallow**, burned hands, forty
years awake. Tallies cover his walls under one word, *EATEN*. The first is circled, under a photo of a smiling woman
holding up a jar. **Boss: a tower-thick root** bores up under the ash for Tallow, the only eater awake for miles. His
fire draws moths until they drop, Sorrel among them. Vesper fights unseen until she gathers every glint and blazes, a
nearer light than the fire, and Sorrel turns to her out of the flame. Tallow sets a coal in a drilled black seed and
holds it out: the first gift anyone has given her. Item text: *"It wants everything. Carry it carefully."*

### Act II, the midpoint, Night 4: the old road and the mountain

**The old road** to the mountain is a wall of root packed with cases. She burns through with the coal, and beads on
her map go out. Where the root has rotted, the mouths' throats are old metal pipe. In a flooded room, small glass
dishes each hold a dead grey cloud: its failed siblings. Item text: *"Eggs that never hatched."*

**The mountain.** Up a dry pipe to a room at its heart:
- A sign half grown over: *No one goes hungry.* Beside it a poster: a hand lifting a small light out of a drawn human
  head into a dish. *Grown from us, to feed us.*
- A screen, still lit, where a ball bounces between two bats and nobody plays.
- A case grown around a chair: the woman from Tallow's photo, old now, the same jars of feed unopened in her lap. The
  Mouth's oldest stalk, worn smooth, runs into her case.
- In the middle, a dish: grey, cracked, its tubes dry, a handprint on the glass.

Vesper touches the stalk, and the prologue's first frame returns, wider: the pale light is a cloud in this dish, the
warm thing the woman's hand. Each miss in the ball game floods the cloud with static; rally after rally, the static
stops. Then the woman turns off the lights and goes, the tubes run dry, and the static floods back.

Pale light runs along every root on earth, and every bead on Vesper's map swells with one slow breath. **It has not
been eating the world. It has been feeding it.** The beads she burned stay dark.

Dun arrives with the vixen, caught at last, and lays her at the dish. Vesper sets down every glint she carries: the
first gift it has had in forty years. The root drops the vixen, Dun's offering, for the moth's. Every root in the room
turns toward Vesper, and her shape rises out of every wall. **It sees her, and through her, every moth.** The mountain
chases her down its own veins. She re-forms at the lantern at the old road's foot and leaves the coal on its step.

### The lowest point, Night 5: the Grove, overrun

The Grove's first lantern goes out under a tide of root. Lampwings dive into the brood's dance and carry dancers off;
roots climb toward next year's cocoons. **Boss: three mouths** that learn her: the first grabs and misses, the second
waits where she will land, the third closes the path behind her.

After, Sorrel finds a dancer hanging from a root in a case no bigger than a seed: alive, wings folded, still. Kept,
not killed. He looks at Vesper across the scattered dance, and goes. She fades and re-forms at a lantern, alone.

The mountain sends Dun to carry her to a case. Dun stands over her a long moment, then noses the spilled glints into a
heap and lies across the lantern's door, facing out. From now on Dun waits at whichever lantern Vesper rests at, where
moths fleeing other woods gather, a few more each night.

Outside, a caterpillar on a frosted twig shivers and keeps failing to spin. Vesper wraps it and shivers with it until
its silk holds. Warmed, it lets go and spins: the ending, small.

### Act III: the mountain moves (Nights 6–7)

**Night 6, the kept city.** Under every food hall's sign, *No one goes hungry*, people hang in rows. The Grove's
second lantern goes out. Sorrel is in what is left of the dance, with a partner. Far below he sees her small lantern
with strangers round it; he drops, and the dance follows him down. Tallow is burning every wall of cases in his way.
**Boss: Tallow.** She can barely hurt him; the fight is shielding the cases while Dun carries them clear. It ends when
he burns one in front of her anyway: its slow breath stops, its bead goes dark, and his fire dies on its pole.

From the roofs, the Grove's last lantern goes out far behind them. Then a river of gold crosses the sky to Vesper: the
forest sending her every glint it has left, its heart going dark to do it. Below, Tallow walks up the old road into
the mountain, empty-handed.

**Night 7, inside the mountain.** On the step of the lantern at the old road's foot, the black seed sits where she
left it, smoking faintly; she can take it or leave it. Dun stays to hold the narrow way in against the burrbacks
behind. In the shaft the roots grab, and Sorrel draws them off her as on Night 2; it costs him every scale. At the
dish they pass Tallow, already sealed in a case of his own, kneeling at his sister's with one hand on the glass.

**Boss: the Mouth itself.** Around the dish, it tries to keep her, not kill her: caught, she slows, warm and still.
Her blade cuts her free; only gifts win.

## 6. The endings

Given everything she has, the Mouth is full for the first time in forty years. It stops taking, and the root stops
everywhere. Taking nothing, it has no warmth; frost creeps over the dish, and it tries to wrap itself, but its threads
are too thin. Then it grows a case around Sorrel, like the dancer's. **This is its real offer:** his scales grow back,
and through the case's skin the dance plays on overhead, long past the end of their week. Kept moths don't die. A
second case opens for her. The choice is played, not picked:

- **Spin** (the true ending). She walks the dish's rim, shivering her warmth into the threads; each circuit costs her
  glide, then jump, then dash. Sorrel climbs out of his case and walks behind her, shivering too. Then come the moths
  from her lanterns, the dance, and every moth it never noticed, until the mountain is furred with shivering wings.
  Warmed, the Mouth does what the caterpillar did: it lets go. **Every root on earth draws back into the mountain, and
  the mountain spins itself shut.** *Epilogue:* on her map, bead after bead warms; some stay grey. In the kept city
  one case splits and a thin grey hand reaches toward an empty food hall: the world wakes old and hungry. Tallow wakes
  at his sister's side as she opens her eyes. Dun lies against the spun mountain to keep it warm. On the dish's rim,
  two moths lie still. In spring the brood hatches, and so, for the first time in forty years, do the vixen's cubs.
  Last image: the mountain's silk pulsing slow and warm like the first frame's breath, a thin crack of gold at its
  crown.
- **Stay.** She lies down beside her brother and both cases close. *Epilogue:* the root takes the Grove and the
  brood's cocoons never hatch. Tallow sleeps beside his sister. The world is kept, quiet and fed, and nothing is ever
  born again.
- **Burn.** Only if she carried the coal in. She drops it into the dish, and every root on earth goes grey.
  *Epilogue:* the takeover ends in a night. On her map most beads go dark; a few wake. Tallow wakes and holds his
  sister as she stops breathing. Moths fly free over a silent world.

## 7. The question it leaves

The thing that took the whole world so no one would go hungry, and only saw a moth when the moth fed it: was that
love, or had it only learned how to make the static stop?
