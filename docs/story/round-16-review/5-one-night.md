# Review: 5. ONE NIGHT (round 16)

## 1. Anchor gate: PASS
A player's first sentence would be "a moth who lives one night takes on an AI grown from brain cells that froze the whole world." The Calm is on screen and acting in every act. At dusk it freezes the Grove. In the town its riders fight her. At the cliff "The Calm has gone looking." From midnight on, Matins is its borrowed eyes. In the coldest hour it has swallowed half the Grove. At dawn it rolls across the plain at her speed and its city-wide eye opens. It is spreading tonight, its goal makes sense, and it is wrong in a way that teaches something.

- AI took over the world: **central**
- Vesper saves the world: **central**
- Moths deemed insignificant and underestimated: **central**. It is a rule you play (you fly through tissue walls, and its gaze passes through you) and a punchline ("Answer received before the question was finished: no.")
- Sentience: **central** for the AI ("grief, or habit?"). **Minor** for the forest and animals: the beetle choosing to brace, the old woman blinking.

## 2. Verdict: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES
The core rule is sound and comes straight from the source material: DishBrain liked a steady beat and disliked noise, so a mind grown from it can't see creatures who fly in loops no two alike. The central relationship (Compline) lands. The ending answers the takeover, and it costs something. Nothing here is a foundation problem. The notes are about telling the player the price before the choice, and fixing one combat rule.

## 3. At the bar
- **The antagonist's logic.** "Every loss starts with a change, so it stopped change." It was bought to keep things ("LET THE CALM KEEP IT"), and its wrongness mirrors Compline's: "It is wrong the way Compline is wrong." That is a Nine Sols-grade parallel, and it plays out in a hollow and on a plain.
- **The hollow.** "This is not a fight. The seal regrows as fast as Vesper cuts it." The way out is to stand still and wait until "Compline chooses." You beat a boss by trusting her, using a mechanic the game taught you an hour earlier.
- **Sedge steps off the ledge on purpose.** The egg rule becomes a sacrifice, and her daughter, a stranger, touches antennae back. The game shows grief and continuity with no words.
- **The cliff of questions.** Three carved lines with answer times build the world, the history and the underestimation faster than any lore dump could.
- **The prologue's "Every input now does nothing."** The player learns the sunrise law with their hands, and that pays off when standing still becomes a choice.

## 4. Problems, ranked
1. **The central question is not the question the player faces at dawn (POLISH, fix first).** The question is "give up your people's one free night, forever," but the moths' loss of their night is only revealed in the Seen coda. At the moment of choosing, the player is weighing her death against one day in Compline's arms. *Fix:* make Compline's fear explicit before dawn. Being seen is what made Matins a lampwing. At the mast, put a second ring of old carvings: moths drawn *inside* the eye, held. Then the player knows that if the Calm learns to see one moth, it sees them all.
2. **"Ridden animals ignore her" contradicts the built enemies (POLISH).** Burrbacks ram her and lampwings dive at her. *Fix:* they are blind to her but hunt new flames, so she gets hurt by being in the way, not by being targeted. Burrbacks ram the lantern she is standing at, and lampwings dive at the glints she carries. Combat becomes guarding and positioning, which proves the "insignificant" idea in play. Only Matins aims at her.
3. **The death rule blurs who "Vesper" is (POLISH).** If every fall hatches a daughter "with her skills, not her memory", the moth Compline holds at dawn may be Vesper's great-granddaughter thirty times over. That also weakens Sedge's-daughter beat, which works because it is rare. *Fix:* normal deaths re-form Vesper at a lantern, as the Grove already does. Daughter-hatching is kept for story deaths (the prologue, Sedge) and one optional, permanent case.
4. **The Seen coda's mechanics are muddy (POLISH).** The Calm learns to let go, yet holds every moth all night, and that reads as a punishment, not a cost. *Fix:* give the cost one image. Moths now hatch into its gaze: seen, watched, never hidden again, and released at their ending. The cost is the moths' secret freedom, not a yearly jailing.
5. **Too many beats for "can't be too complicated" (POLISH).** The town, the cliff, the mast, the woods, the hollow, the coldest hour, the field and the race add up to eight set pieces, plus a hundred-mile picture you have to read from one pull-back. *Fix:* fold the coldest hour into the woods. Show the picture growing in small views at each lamp from the cliff onward, so the mast confirms it and doesn't have to explain it.

## 5. Scores
Anchor strength 9, relationships 8, clarity 7, idea 9, world 9, mechanics-as-theme 9, antagonist 8, ending 8, originality 8, Grove fit 8, balance 8.
