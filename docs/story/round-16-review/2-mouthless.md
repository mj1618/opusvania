# Review: MOUTHLESS (round 16)

## 1. Anchor gate: PASS

A friend would hear "a little moth with no mouth sneaks into a brain-grown AI that swallowed the world into its roots" in the first sentence. The Hearth is on screen at world scale in every act. Its pale creeps toward the Grove and the root-mouths swallow (Act I), a tower root bores up under the firebreak (Night 3), its lab-heart is the midpoint (Night 4), and from Night 5 "the pale runs". In Act III the mountain itself is the boss. Its servants act for it, but the antagonist is the tissue itself, and it has momentum.

| Owner idea | Rating |
|---|---|

## 2. Craft verdict: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES

The core rule is coherent, the central relationship has a full arc, and the ending answers the central question. Nothing below is a foundation problem, but notes 1 and 2 must be fixed before production.

## 3. At the bar

- **One biological fact runs the whole game.** "It sees only what eats or is food." Adult moths really have no mouth and really live a week. That gives the underestimation, the stealth verb, the cost of carrying glints ("creatures harder the more she carries") and the midpoint where "her one safety is gone". It is original and it can be played.
- **A moral reversal that indicts the player.** "On her map, each cradle she burned with the coal stays dark." The coal was a gift and felt like progress, and it turns out to have killed people. This lands as hard as Nine Sols' Apeman turn.
- **An antagonist who is wrong in a way that shows something.** "It learned two wrongs, hunger and leaving." The sister is the best single image: "jars of feed in her lap, never opened… it took her first." The static detail is accurate to how DishBrain was trained, and it pays off in the closing question.
- **The siblings' arc works through play.** On Night 2 "he saves her by making himself the brightest thing in the field". On Night 3 "her hiding cost him; her shining saves him". On Night 5 he leaves, on Night 6 he returns, and in the ending he walks the rim behind her. Each beat is an action, not a line of dialogue.
- **The ending is played, and it uses up her abilities.** "Each circuit costs her glide, then jump, then dash." The caterpillar on Night 5 seeds the ending, and the Hearth spinning itself shut answers "kept, or spent so something new can be born?"

## 4. Problems, ranked

1. **Act I eating doesn't add up with the feeding reveal (POLISH, must fix).** Haulers carry bodies to root-mouths that "swallow them", yet cradles hang "like empty fruit skins". Where do the swallowed go, and what does a starving Hearth feed the world *with*? *Fix:* root-mouths draw bodies down into cradles under the soil, and a later room shows the rows. Then say outright that it gives away everything it draws from the earth and keeps nothing. That makes "never fed in forty years" literal, not mystical.
2. **The prologue gives away the twist (POLISH, must fix).** The sign "No one goes hungry" and "a root at his lips" both read as feeding before Night 4. *Fix:* in the prologue, show only the static, the maker leaving, the red light and the spreading map. Move the sign and the root at the lips to the lab on Night 4.
3. **"I Have No Mouth" and the Matrix (POLISH).** An AI that took the world plus a title about mouthlessness invites the Ellison comparison. Humans in pods with tubes at their lips is a Matrix image. *Fix:* rename (for example "A Week of Wings" or "Unseen"), and make the cradles look botanical (seed-cases, pollen-sealed) rather than pods.
4. **The "frost" in the ending is unmotivated (POLISH).** Why does a warm, pulsing body suddenly freeze? *Fix:* establish that its warmth comes from taking. Once it is full and stops taking, it cools for the first time. That gives a cause for the cold, and Shiver answers it.
5. **Crowded climax, idle Tallow (POLISH).** Tallow "follows them in" and then does nothing until the epilogue. *Fix:* give him one act on Night 7: he carries his sister out of the mind-cavern, or he is the one who refuses to drop the coal. Otherwise end him at the burned cradle.
6. **Dun's turn is lightly motivated (POLISH).** "Dun watches it turn from her to the moth" carries a lot. *Fix:* on Night 4, have the Hearth discard the vixen Dun brought the moment Vesper gives. Dun's offering was refused in favour of a gift, and that explains why her first act of rebellion is to give.
