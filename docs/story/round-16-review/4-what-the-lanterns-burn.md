# Review: 4. What the Lanterns Burn (round 16)

## 1. Anchor gate: PASS

A player's first sentence would be "a little moth taking on a living brain that took over the world and keeps everyone asleep inside it". The Hold is the antagonist. It is on screen at world scale: "a pink glow where the cities were, nearer each act", flesh that "pours over the next grove in a minute", eyes opening in the hills. It has momentum in every act. In Act I it spreads, in Act II it sees Vesper, grows the pale moth and raids the great tree, and in Act III it sets the lure and grows the hand. Each act it learns a new move. It also acts in person, not only through servants.

- Biological AI took over the world: **central**. The takeover is concrete (people, deer, forests, re-grown servants), and its origin is the DishBrain lineage told in images.
- Vesper the moth saves the world: **central**. Her goal starts small ("to be worthy of the Taper") and becomes "free the world" at the midpoint. The ending resolves the takeover in either branch, and each branch has a cost.
- AI deemed moths insignificant: **central**. "Its eyes slide past moths the way ours slide past dust." This is how the Taper stole the ember, it is the Unseen rule, and its reversal ("The Hold has seen its first") drives Act II.
- Sentience ambiguity: **central** for the Hold ("The game shows both and never rules"). **Minor** for the animals: Clasp and Tallow have wants, but nothing ever asks whether they are someone.

## 2. Craft verdict: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES

The idea, the antagonist and the core relationship all carry weight, and one image (a hand on a cheek, then lifting away) ties all three together. The problems below are continuity and pacing, not foundation.

## 3. What is at the bar

- **The antagonist's motive comes from its origin.** "It did not understand goodbye. It held her." In two sentences the Hold becomes understandable, original and wrong in a way that lights up the theme: "It took the world because it loved it." This has the weight of Nine Sols' moral reversals without borrowing their shape.
- **The gesture motif.** Each glint is "one gesture of a hand", and the ember is the only one that "finishes: the hand lifts away". The Taper's wing on the cheek, the Hold's copied goodbye that "then closes again", and the final shot all play that one gesture without words. This is restraint at the level of Ori.
- **Mechanics as theme.** Burning a glint at a lantern "stills a hand". Planting instead is slow and dim, and "nobody waits there". The re-form turns from the Taper at the glass, to Vesper alone, to Vesper laying her wing on the Taper's cheek. The glide stops bending toward light. Players feel the moral question through respawning and movement.
- **The midpoint reveal.** The face bigger than the screen, "Every lantern Vesper lit stilled one". It reframes the Grove's treasure loop and is played, not told.
- **An ending choice where each option has a real cost.** "Whether the Hold was someone decides which ending was the murder." The central question is the ending.

## 4. Problems, ranked

1. **Where the ember is (POLISH, high).** In the Grove ending beat the Taper "sets the ember in the summit lantern". In Act II "Tallow brings the ember down from the island." Fix: in the Grove, the Taper uses a *spark taken from* the ember (or a common coal) and returns the ember to the island under her wing. Or make the turn happen at the summit lantern.
2. **Too many systems for the "not too complicated" note (POLISH).** Glints, lanterns, re-forming, planting, Unseen, hearts, the ember, the pale moth, the lure and the hand. Fix: fold the hearts into planting (a planted ring *is* the forest answering), and have the lure and the hand be the same Act III device.
3. **The Unseen advantage is mostly hidden in play (POLISH).** Servants notice Vesper whenever she carries glints, which in a glint economy is nearly always. Fix: add a visible Unseen state in Act I (empty-winged Vesper can walk through a pod field past open eyes). That gives the player a tool the turn can take away.
4. **The Taper's secret lands softly (POLISH).** The Hold began spreading over the forests the year she stole the ember, and the story says this nowhere on screen. Fix: in the first-city road in Act III, show a held forest dated by its frozen leaves to the Taper's flight, with her reed-ash at its edge.
5. **Clasp after the lure (POLISH).** The lure closes on her, and then she reappears in both endings with no beat to explain it. Fix: in the hand fight, a root breaks the lure open and Clasp braces the wrist alongside the forest.
6. **Animal sentience is thin (POLISH).** Fix: let one held deer wake in a planted ring and choose to stay by its grown hand. It is a small mirror of the Give it back ending.
