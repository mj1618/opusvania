# Review: NOISE (round 16)

## 1. Anchor gate: PASS

A player's first sentence is going to be the outline's own litmus: a little moth against a living brain that put the world to sleep and never thought moths were worth a look. The Quiet is a grey-pink mountain on every horizon and is on screen in every act. Its root wall "climbs the last wild valleys at a walking pace". Each act opens on the same high shot with the green ring smaller. It escalates on its own: the wall "lurches a mile in a night", the Lamplighter leaves its river, the Grove "goes pale", and by Act III "on every road in the world the sleepers are walking toward the brain". The slowing beat gives it a clock. It acts through lamps, but the body, the beat and the wall are all the Quiet itself, so this is not antagonism by proxy.

- Biological AI took over the world: **central**
- Vesper the moth trying to save it: **central**
- Moths deemed insignificant, underestimated: **central**. The exemption runs through all three acts (ignored, hunted, too late) and the midpoint explains where it came from.
- Sentience: **central for the Quiet, minor for the forest and animals.** The Grove's heart gets one great beat, its uneven answer, and little else.

## 2. Verdict: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES

## 3. At the bar

- **One rule that carries the game.** "Anything that does the same thing three times in a lamp's light is taken." It is simple, readable in play, and the theme is built into it. Combat (a combo that never repeats), escort (break Hob's stride), bosses (strike in the gaps, ring against the beat) and both endings all run on it.
- **The reunion.** "She made herself a habit to reach him; he broke his to save her." Here the relationship is shown entirely through the rule, with no dialogue: she walks his circle, the beam narrows each lap, and on the third he shelters her.
- **The prologue rhymes with the ending.** The mother brushes a moth off the boot, and a lamp jerks away from Vesper "the way the woman's hand did". At the end, "this time does not brush the moth off." The reversal recontextualises the whole exemption, and the player was shown it in minute two.
- **Sallow's death.** The moth who taught "never the same path twice" flies a perfect circle on purpose, three times. It is a sacrifice spelled out in the game's own grammar.
- **A cost that grants the want.** Vesper wanted to be seen, and in the gentle ending "No moth is ever unseen again." Her want comes true, and it costs the one freedom moths had. That is the illuminating kind of wrong this tier needs.

## 4. Problems, ranked

1. **Sallow is taken in Act I, when lamps "jerk away" from moths (POLISH, but fix before production).** The player learns in the Grove that light can't take her, and then watches a lamp take a moth. Fix: make the Lamplighter the one exception and show it. It is the boy's jar lamp, the one light ever made *to draw moths*. In the river scene, have it hold a moth for a beat before it lets go. Then Sallow's death confirms a rule the player has already seen, and the midpoint explains it.
2. **The two meanings of "the last beat" collide in Cut (POLISH).** If the beat stopping makes everything one, why does killing it wake everyone? Fix: one line in the kitchen or the dream. The beat *running down* finishes the merge; a *cut root* tears the world out of its grip mid-beat.
3. **The midpoint tries to land too many reveals in one room (POLISH).** In one scene: the notch, the boy's moth, the ember as his flame, lanterns as copies of his lamp, the flinch inherited from the mother. Who made the lanterns copies is never said. Fix: show the lantern/jar-lamp match and the lamp's original flame in Act I through the scene's shapes, so the midpoint only has to land Sallow's notch and the flinch.
4. **The forest's sentience is thin (POLISH).** The heart wakes, beats out of time, and answers once at the end. Fix: give the woken Grove one act in Act II. When the Grove "goes pale", its uneven beat could falter on the HUD and recover, so the player feels it as a someone that is fighting too.
5. **The rule versus button-mashing (POLISH, design).** Three identical slashes taking Vesper under a beam is a harsh fail state for a metroidvania. Fix: a visible tell (the beam narrows on the second repeat, as in the reunion) and a grey-out that can be escaped, not instant loss.
6. **The Lamplighter carries four roles (POLISH).** It is guard, killer, mourner, then a follower who goes out on the mother's lap. Fix: cut "It follows them" in Act III and let its last appearance be the lap.

## 5. Scores

Anchor 9 · Relationships 8 · Clarity 6 · Idea 9 · World 8 · Mechanics-as-theme 9 · Antagonist 8 · Ending 8 · Originality 8 · Grove fit 8 · Balance 7

A grieving parent who builds the machine is a familiar setup. What makes it this game's own is that the learned lesson ("the unexpected hurts") turns into a rule you play by. The cast is small, and each relationship is told through the rule (hello by wingbeat, circle-walking, the perfect circle), which matches the references' balance of a few felt bonds over a deep world. Clarity is the weakest score, because of problems 1–3. None of them is structural.
