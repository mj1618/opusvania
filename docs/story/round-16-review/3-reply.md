# Review: round 16, "Reply"

## 1. Anchor gate: PASS

The first sentence names the anchor: "a little moth named Vesper taking on Ward, a brain grown from living cells in a dish that took over the whole world." Ward is the world-scale antagonist on screen. The capital pulses on the grove's horizon, the call rolls in "every few minutes", and the growth "creeps a tree further west" every night. It moves in every act: in Act I it swallows Sallow's wall, in Act II it "pours down the gorge after her", and in Act III whole hillsides turn and the tower itself fights her. The call and the growth are Ward's own body, so it is not acting through proxies. Its goal, "that nothing ever goes silent again", is understandable and wrong in a way that teaches something. It also grows out of the real DishBrain science (cells that learn an answer soothes), which is the right kind of grounding.

## 2. Craft verdict: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES

There is no foundation break. The spine fits in three sentences, and the central question ("is it a someone, and what will you give up?") is answered by all three endings, each of which bets differently and costs something real. The relationships are carried by gestures, not dialogue. The problems are about how much the glass system has to carry, and one continuity slip at the climax.

## 3. At the bar

- **Wrong in an illuminating way.** "A world that can't fall silent can't answer either, so every flash Ward receives is its own call coming back. It took the whole world and is still alone." That is the villain and the theme in one line.
- **One gesture carries the cast.** Sallow lays her wing across Vesper's face to turn her back to the moon. Vesper "lifts it off, gently" and leaves. Reply gives the gesture back in ending 2: "the only time it touches her." This is Ori-level storytelling without words.
- **The keeper.** "Sees the moon for the first time in three hundred years, and dies quietly, looking up. The first death in this world looks like rest." This boss quietly sets up the third ending's cost.
- **Mechanics as theme.** Vesper re-forms because Reply keeps her: "The thing she fights is what keeps bringing her back." Answering at will opens paths but brings Ward's creatures, so the player learns the temptation by using it.
- **The reveal** that every lampwing is a moth's glass, still answering for her and keeping her alive. The husks among the elders and Reply laying glints on the dead make the player's own kills the reversal, in the Nine Sols tradition.

## 4. Problems, ranked

1. **The glass system outgrows "three rules" (POLISH, high priority).** By the midpoint the player also needs these: facing the moon in front of your glass leaves it behind; a left glass becomes a lampwing; it keeps the moth alive; Ward grows west after every one. That is seven rules. *Fix:* fold them into Rule 2: "Whatever answers, Ward grows a glass of it, and the glass keeps it." Show the image before explaining it: in Act I, an elder moth asleep under a lampwing's swinging lamp, left unexplained. Move the "grows west after glasses" rule into ending 1's own cutscene rather than the log.
2. **Mote's glass breaks the tower beat (POLISH).** Act III starts "with her: Mote's tiny glass", and Ward "sees through it". So in the tower, Reply staying dark as "the one glass that could show Ward where she is" proves nothing. *Fix:* at the forgiveness lantern, Sallow takes the tiny glass home to Mote. Forgiveness becomes an action, and Vesper's cover comes back only through Reply's choice.
3. **The lampwings' behaviour contradicts itself (POLISH).** Lampwings "dive at her whenever Reply is close" to guard against Ward's eye, yet Wick "drives her back toward it". Players will read these as one enemy type with no single logic. *Fix:* one rule for all lampwings: they dive at whatever a glass is looking at, to pull the eye away (the same thing Wick did for Sallow). Wick alone herds Vesper toward Reply, and he is marked by his torn wing.
4. **Guilt without agency (POLISH).** The reveal blames the player for kills the game forced on them. *Fix:* after the nursery, let a lantern's light or a slash to the lamp drive a lampwing off without killing it. Show the player's count as husks or living elders at the nursery in every ending.
5. **Echo of Hollow Knight's Radiance (POLISH).** Light that calls, creatures that answer with lit eyes, and a moth-centred mythology could all read as "the Infection". *Fix:* keep the call in tissue, not eyes. Use veins flushing, shells pulsing and skin-sealed windows, with no glowing eyes and nothing like a dream.
6. **Reply "a region behind" is hard to read in play (POLISH).** The stealth layer needs a tell the player can see. *Fix:* a cold sheen creeps along Vesper's wings when Reply's line of sight reaches her, and on-screen creatures twitch toward her one beat before they turn.
