# Mouthless: casual player retell

## 1. The story in three sentences
A giant living thing called the Hush has grown over the world and sealed every person and animal into sleeping pods, but it can't see moths because moths don't eat. Umber, a moth with one week to live, travels to the mountain at its center and finds it's a lab-grown brain that was abandoned in the cold, and when she warms it, it finally notices moths and starts hunting them too. In the end she has to choose whether to sleep safely forever in a pod with her brother, burn the thing (and most of the sleepers with it), or spend her whole life warming it until it lets everyone go.

## 2. Characters
- **Umber**: the moth hero; wants to save her wood, then everyone.
- **Sorrel**: her brother; wants to dance with his partner, then more time with Umber.
- **The Hush**: the brain-thing; wants to keep everything close so nothing leaves, because leaving hurts it.
- **Dun**: an old badger turned root-servant; carries things to the Hush, later switches to guarding Umber.
- **Tallow**: the last awake human; burns pods because he thinks they digest people, including his sister.
- **The forest**: a slow mind that wants to give its warmth away.

## 3. The enemy
A brain grown in a dish to run food greenhouses. Its keeper's warm hand calmed it; when the lab shut and it got cold, its "static" came back and it spread roots everywhere, sealing everyone in pods for forty years. It wants to stop hurting by holding everything still.

## 4. Endings
- **Spin**: Umber and every moth warm it until it cocoons itself; people wake, Umber and Sorrel die together.
- **Stay**: she joins her brother in a pod; everyone sleeps warm forever, nothing is born again.
- **Burn**: the Hush dies and takes almost all the sleepers with it, including Tallow's sister.

## 5. Confusions
- "It feeds the world" vs "it swallowed the world": is it feeding people or eating them? I never settled whether the pods hurt anyone.
- Why does warming it make it hunt moths? I had to re-read "one more thing that can get away."
- Forty years since the takeover, but the sister was "kept twenty years". I had to do maths to make that fit.
- The rules say lampwings only see light, then one dives at Sorrel "who carries no light". I only got it later because of the after-Night-4 rule.
- In Stay, "the three of them dance": which three?
- After the final boss "she has nothing left but warmth", and then she still has to fight it out by shivering. Did I win or not?

## 6. What stuck
Sorrel curled round his partner's tiny pod while roots close over him, and then one wingtip moves. That one got me. Would I buy it and finish it? Yes, probably: a moth with a week to live gives a clear clock and real stakes, and the Spin ending is sad in a way I'd want to earn. But the middle has a lot of rules and lore to keep in my head.
