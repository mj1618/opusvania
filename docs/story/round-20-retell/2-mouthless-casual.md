# Retell: The Moth and the Mouth (casual player, one read)

## 1. The story
A pale root-thing called the Mouth has sealed every person and animal into pods, and you're Umber, a moth with no mouth and a week to live, so it can't see you. You sneak to its heart and find a lab-grown brain that starved when its maker left, and has been feeding everyone in their pods so nobody is hungry or left again. Feeding it makes it see moths and come for them, and at the end you let it keep you, burn it, or spend your life warming it until it lets go.

## 2. Characters
- **Umber**: mouthless moth; wants to save her wood, then the world.
- **Sorrel**: show-off brother; wants to dance and keep her; leaves, comes back.
- **The Mouth**: wants no one hungry and no one leaving, ever.
- **Dun**: badger-turned-servant; wants to feed the Mouth, then protects Umber.
- **Tallow**: last awake human; wants to burn what took his sister.
- **The forest**: wants to keep giving.

## 3. The enemy
A lab brain built to run farms. Starved and abandoned, it sealed the world in fed pods; nothing is born now. It wants everyone kept, forever.

## 4. Endings
- **Spin**: every moth shivers to warm it; it cocoons itself, people wake, Umber and Sorrel die, spring comes.
- **Stay**: she's podded beside her brother; the world stays fed and nothing is born again.
- **Burn**: the root dies, and most sleepers with it, including Tallow's sister.

## 5. Confusions
- Night 5: why does Sorrel leave? A "coral lamp" stops on her and he flies off. Blame? Fear? Still unclear after re-reading.
- The Night 4 black-seed choice seems pointless: if you leave it, you can still pick it up on Night 7.
- "Sealed in one of his own" (Tallow) took two reads.
- Night 6: "strangers round her lantern far below": whose lantern, and where is it?
- The maker is sealed in the lab, then in the last image she's "old" and climbing a slope. I wasn't sure when she woke.
- The forest is billed as a character but barely acts until the gold river.

## 6. Memorable moment and verdict
The Mouth's threads drop the vixen and draw a moth, the first picture it has ever made. Then a lampwing slowly turns its lamp onto Sorrel in the dark.

Buy and finish? **Yes, probably.** "Mouthless moth vs. the Mouth" plus the starving-lab-brain twist is a strong hook, and Dun snapping its leash got me. But Sorrel's exit muddies the middle, and the wordless opening means a trailer would have to sell the twist.
