# Litmus: 2-mouthless

**LITMUS: PASS, with one weakness.** A player would plausibly open with "a little moth with no mouth taking on a biological AI that put the whole world to sleep in its roots." The takeover is concrete and has scale: "Every person on earth sleeps in a **cradle** of milkroot", cities "wrapped like cocoons", the first city now "a mountain of folded tissue". It has servants (burrbacks, lampwings) and momentum: "the Hush, a front of pale root creeping through the last wild places", and "on Night 5 it surges". On screen, a deer is "dragged into a cradle that closes over it while Vesper watches, unseen."

**Weakness.** From the midpoint on, the Hearth shrinks toward the small sad creature the owner rejected. It "leans into her like a sleeping child", it is a "newborn", it was abandoned by Mara, and the climax has Vesper keep it "warm while it spins itself". The Hearth is never itself the thing she fights. The Act III bosses are a dog and Tallow, a human, and the reveal "It never ate anyone" removes much of its menace.

## The four ideas
1. **Biological AI took over the world:** present and central. Section 1 shows what it did to humans, cities, animals and farms, and how it spreads.
2. **Vesper saves the world:** present and central. In the Spin ending "every root on earth lets go", and the cost is real: the world wakes "old, weak and hungry", and not everyone wakes.
3. **AI dismissed moths, underestimated her:** present and central. "It sees by appetite": moths have no mouth, so "roots part around her". Carrying glints makes her visible, which turns the idea into a mechanic. Best in the round.
4. **Sentience of forest/animals/AI:** present. It is central for the AI, with an explicit "Is it a someone?" and arguments both ways. It is minor for the forest and animals: the fox dreaming and Dun's grief carry it, but the forest's own mind is thin.

## Changes needed
- Make the final confrontation the Hearth at world scale: the whole Heartwood moving against her on Night 7, with Tallow cut back to a mid-act fight.
- Keep the Hush visibly advancing every night after Night 5, for example the Grove's lanterns going out one by one, so the threat stays on screen through Act III.
- Cut the "sleeping child / newborn / dog at a door" imagery by half. Its tenderness should read as a world-sized will, not a baby.
- Make it notice her and adapt on screen before the end: a moment of "it can see moths now" pressure in Act III, not only in the Stay epilogue.
- Give the forest a voice in the sentience question, for example the Grove's heart as a counter-mind.

## Scores
- Relationships: 8. Sorrel's sacrifice and return, Dun's arc from rival to mourner, Tallow and Mara.
- Clarity: 6. Coal, ember, glints, lines, newborn: too many moving parts.
- Originality: 8. Seeing by appetite, and "mouthless" as stealth, are fresh.
- Balance (idea/world/people): 7. World and idea are strong; the late acts tip into intimate grief.
