# Litmus: 4. What the Lanterns Burn

Checked against OWNER CORRECTION 2 in the story brief. The brief file in the scratchpad holds only the correction; its "The ask" section was missing, so the four owner ideas are taken from the task text.

## 1. LITMUS: PASS

The AI drives the whole setting and is the main force against Vesper. It works at world scale, is on screen, and is still advancing. The Hold "has wrapped every city on Earth and put every person to sleep inside it; now it is growing over the last wild valley." It shows up "in every outdoor scene": "a pink glow on the horizon where a city should be dark, nearer each act." It also gains ground during play: "the rim creeps, eyes open in the hills ... Where a lantern goes dark, the flesh moves in." It has servants (burrbacks, lampwings) and two bosses that are the Hold itself (the Reach, the Hand), and Act III crosses "the taken world." Its goal is clear and wrong in a way that teaches something: "nothing is ever lost again ... It took the world because it loved it." A player would plausibly say the draft's own litmus line first.

Weak spot: Acts I and II centre on the moths' own conflict (lanterns burn people, and the Act II boss is the Taper). The Hold's line "It doesn't fight. It reaches" risks reading as a slow tide rather than an opponent, and a player could sum up the midgame as "moths burn human souls."

## 2. The four owner ideas

- **Biological AI took over the world:** present and central. It is grown from human cells, starts with the dish-and-dot game, and has taken every city, animal and forest.
- **Vesper saves the world:** present and central, but softer in Act II. Her goal there turns inward (stop the burning, defy the Taper), and both endings lose every sleeper, so "saved" needs to read clearly.
- **The AI dismissed moths and underestimated her:** present and central. "Its eyes slide past moths like ours slide past dust." In play, an eye snatches the deer and skips her. The Taper's theft worked because "she was only a moth," and in *Given back* the Hold looks at a moth for the first time.
- **Sentience of forest, animals and AI:** present and central for the AI (the yes/no evidence is even-handed) and for the animals (Clasp, Tallow). **Minor for the forest**: the buried old forest has one line ("grew the one shape the Hold never watches") and makes no choice.

## 3. Changes needed

- Give the Hold one active move per act that the player sees (a lampwing raid on the Censer, a grove taken while Vesper watches), so Act II isn't moths against moths.
- Have the Hold escalate visibly after "The turn": something new hunting the ember, beyond the Reach.
- Give the old forest a voice and a choice. For example, grove hearts are the buried forest choosing to wake, and in the ending it grows back by its own decision.
- Show what is saved in each ending (death and seasons return, animals freed, the forest wakes) so "save the world" isn't only a loss.
- Keep the Hush reveal (the lanterns burn people) as a twist inside the AI story, not its headline.

## Scores

- **Relationships 9**: the re-form gesture passes from the Taper to Vesper and back; Sedge, Tallow and Clasp each have their own arc.
- **Clarity 6**: there are many terms (glints, lanterns, ember, anointing, hearts, truce, re-forming) and the pitch has to carry all of them.
- **Originality 8**: the AI holds the world out of love, the moths' resistance runs on burning people, and the Hold's first weapon, a lure, fails against a moth that flies with her back to the light.
- **Balance (idea/world/people) 8**: all three are strong. The idea slightly crowds the AI out of the midgame.
