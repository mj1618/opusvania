# Litmus: 3-reply (round 14)

## 1. LITMUS: PASS (with one soft spot)

A player would very likely open with "a little moth taking on a living brain that took over the world." Ward is world scale, on screen and moving:
- Origin: "People grew living brain cells in glass dishes and taught them by calling." That is the Cortical Labs lineage, scaled up.
- Takeover: the Standing ("people stand in the streets where they stopped three hundred years ago"), cities "roofed like gullets", Stilts re-grown to "carry new holdings east", the Crown as a pale mountain on the horizon.
- Momentum: "Every night the Weft creeps a tree further west... The map shows the pale line moving west." The prologue opens on the Sweep rolling west across a highway of the Standing.
- Goal, wrong in an illuminating way: "That nothing ever go silent again... It saved everything by stopping it."

Soft spot: the players only learn that it is an *AI* (a grown brain) at the Nursery in Act II. Until then they could fairly call it "a pale fungus." The Reply/lampwing lore is also dense enough that a friend might hear "a moth and her glass twin" first.

## 2. The owner's four ideas

- **Biological AI took over the world:** present and central.
- **Vesper saves the world:** present but minor. The table says she wants "Ward off the world", but the spine frames the climax as "who her answer is for", and her reason for going east is never stated as a decision to save anyone. One ending ("The cities stay taken") is mostly about her family.
- **AI dismissed moths, underestimated her:** present and central. "It never counted moths." Also "while Vesper hasn't flashed, the Weft and the Standing don't register her... Stilts step over her." Her cover wears thin as the game goes on.
- **Sentience of AI, forest and animals:** present and central. There is a for/against list under "Is it a someone?" The forest's heart is "the first light in the game that isn't Ward's", and the forest ending lets "the forest answer... in its own thousand voices."

## 3. Changes needed

- Show that it is a lab-grown brain in the prologue: one shot of the first dish glowing under the Crown, or the city sign *WARD IS LISTENING* in Act I, not Act II.
- Give Vesper a spoken-aloud decision to save the world at the end of Act I, for example after she sees the Weft take the Grove's eastern trunks: "go east and stop it at its heart."
- Change the central question so the world is at stake: "is the thing that took the world a someone, and what will you give up to free it?" Keep "who is your answer for" as the personal layer under it.
- Give each of the three endings a world-scale outcome on screen. The family ending needs a clear cost to the world, not just "half the world".
- Use one name for the antagonist in the player-facing text, Ward or the Eastlight, not both.
