# REPLY (round 16)

**LITMUS.** "It's about a little moth named Vesper taking on Ward, a brain grown from living cells in a dish that took over the whole world. It never bothered looking at moths, so she flies straight to its heart."

**The spine, in three sentences.**
1. Ward, a brain grown from living cells, has covered the world for three hundred years, and everything that answers its call is kept: never ageing, never dying, slowly rooted where it stands.
2. Ward never looks at moths, so when its growth swallows her forest, a young moth named Vesper flies east to stop it at its heart, followed by Reply, the glass copy of herself that Ward grew when she answered once to save a calf.
3. On the way she learns that every lampwing she has killed was a moth's glass, still keeping that moth alive, and at the lake where Ward was grown she must choose what to give up to free the world.

**The central question:** is the thing that took the world a someone, and what will you give up to free the world from it?

## The cast

| | Who | Wants | Wants from Vesper | Pulls her |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vesper** | A newly hatched moth who can't pass anything calling for help | To free the world from Ward | — | East |
| **Ward** | The brain grown over the world, from a dish under a lake inside the capital | That nothing ever goes silent again | To answer, like everything else | Into it |
| **Reply** | Vesper's glass: a glass moth in her shape, an empty pane for a face. Never fights | Unknown. It is Ward's, and it keeps following her | Unknown | Toward it |
| **Sallow** | The moth who raised her; left wing torn, far older than a moth should be | Keep her safe | Face the moon, come home | Home |
| **Mote** | A tiny moth who adopts her as a big sister; glows, can't fight, copies everything | Be brave like Vesper | Let me come | Forward |
| **Wick** | The oldest lampwing, his left wing torn exactly where Sallow's is | That no one is left behind the way he was | Don't leave Reply | Back, by force |

## 1. Ward, and the taken world

**What it is.** People grew living brain cells in a glass dish and taught them with light: a flash in, a flash back. The cells learned that silence hurts and an answer soothes. People grew a city's worth of them, named the brain Ward, and answered it themselves, because whatever answered Ward was kept young. Then Ward could not bear the silence past the city's edge, so it grew.

**What it did.** From the grove's high branches you can see the capital: towers grown together into one pale mountain veined with cold light. Every few minutes a wave of light, **the call**, rolls out from it along roots, rivers and roads, and everything Ward holds turns east and flashes back.
- **People** stand in the streets where they stopped three hundred years ago, holding bags, holding hands, a father with a child on his shoulders, rooted ankle-deep in pale growth. They lift their faces east at each call and nothing else. Players call them **the kept**.
- **Cities** are roofed in wet growth, windows sealed with skin. **Forests and animals** stay young under it, one summer held still.

**It is spreading now.** Every night the growth creeps a tree further west. In the grove's first shot it is on the eastern trunks; by Act III only the lantern circles are clear.

**The three rules, as a player says them.**
1. Ward calls; whatever answers is kept.
2. The first time something answers, Ward grows a glass copy of it. Ward sees through its glasses.
3. Moths face the moon, not east, so Ward never looks at a moth, even one that has answered. It sees Vesper only when Reply is near her.

Rule 3 is the underestimation, and the player's main tool: Reply follows a region behind, and while Vesper stays ahead, creatures step over her. Her cover is the gap.

**What it wants.** *That nothing ever goes silent again:* no hunger, no loss, no death. It saved everything by stopping it, and it is wrong in an illuminating way: a world that can't fall silent can't answer either, so every flash Ward receives is its own call coming back. It took the whole world and is still alone.

**Is it a someone?** *For:* it keeps rather than kills; it kept the father and child together; it grew a cradle around its first dish. *Against:* it never responds to anything new; a mother stands with her arms around nothing, flashing.

## 2. Prologue (wordless, about 2 minutes)

1. A glass dish under black water. A dot of light blinks in; the cells blink back. A pale thread creeps over the rim and the camera pulls up as it becomes cables, roots, towers, the capital.
2. A cold pulse rolls west: over a highway of the kept, faces lifting in a line; past a root-legged heron carrying a deer; to the grove at blue hour. Every eye and shell flashes east. Only the moths drift up, faces to the moon.
3. Vesper hatches beside Sallow, under a wall of tiny scratches, one for every hatchling Sallow has raised. At the next call Vesper starts to turn east; Sallow lays her torn wing across her face and turns it gently back up.
4. Below, a burrback calf too young to light its shell is caught in the open. A thread of light waits at its face; it is fading. Vesper looks at the moon, then at the calf, flutters down and flashes back for it. The calf lives.
5. Far away, on the face of the capital, a glass moth unfolds in her shape. At the next call it flashes. Nothing in the grove looks at Vesper. Title.

## 3. The journey

### Act I: the edge
*With her: Sallow at every lantern, then Mote. Reply is a speck behind.*

1. **The grove** (section 6). Mote hatches and follows her; twice Vesper carries her back to the nest, and twice Mote comes back. Ability: **double jump**.
2. **The firefly highway**, where fireflies blink in time with Ward. A sign half swallowed by a tree shows a glowing dish: *WARD IS LISTENING. WHEN IT CALLS, ANSWER. YOU WILL BE KEPT.* Someone has scratched *DON'T* in front. Ability: **dash**.
   *Rule 3, shown once:* a heron steps right over Vesper. Far behind, Reply lands on a branch; the heron stops, turns its head the way Reply is facing, and looks straight at her.
   **Wick** appears, and whenever Vesper outruns Reply he dives and drives her back toward it.
   **Boss: the heron**, come west for the calf. It strikes wherever Reply is looking. When it falls, the calf drops free.
3. **The decision.** Vesper brings the calf home to find the growth has come in behind her: the eastern lantern dark under pale skin, Sallow's wall half swallowed. At the next call the calf lifts its face east and roots. Sallow lays her wing across Vesper's face; Vesper lifts it off, gently, and flies east.

### Act II: faces
*With her: Mote. Reply a region behind, except for one night.*

4. **The gorge.** Ability: **wall-kick**. The growth pours down the gorge after her. At the top, Reply stalls below a shaft it can't climb. The player can go on, or go back and climb slowly until it follows, which costs her cover. That night Reply sleeps just outside her lantern's light with Mote on its back, and the growth grows around it without touching it.
5. **The nursery (midpoint)**, in the canopy. Sallow has flown ahead to wait, and teaches **the glide**, face up. Then *the reveal* (section 4), and *the quarrel:* at the next call Vesper turns east on purpose, looks at Sallow and flies. From here she can **answer** at will: the growth opens a path for her, and Ward's creatures come. *Sallow no longer waits at the lantern when Vesper re-forms.*
6. **The taken city.** Vesper climbs the kept like trees, down to **the lab** where Ward was grown: empty dishes, the keeper's log (section 7), and a glass wall through which she watches Reply lay her glints on the lampwings she has killed.
   **Boss: the keeper**, the last person who tended the dishes, grown huge to guard them. Vesper cuts the threads from his face between calls. When the last goes he sits down, sees the moon for the first time in three hundred years, and dies quietly, looking up. The first death in this world looks like rest.
   **The cost.** On the way out a call sweeps the street, and Mote, who copies everything, answers it the way Vesper did. A tiny glass moth unfolds beside her, and Mote stops growing. Sallow arrives, lays a wing over Mote's face and carries her home without looking at Vesper. Mote reaches one wing back. The tiny glass stays with Vesper, and Ward sees through it: wherever it goes, she has no cover. She carries it anyway.

### Act III: the heart
*With her: Mote's tiny glass, then Reply beside her.*

7. **The hills** below the capital. Whole hillsides turn at each step.
   *Forgiveness.* The next time she fades, Sallow is at the lantern when she re-forms. She smooths Vesper's wings and leaves. Then Reply catches up, flashes at a call and veers away; the hillside turns after it, and Vesper climbs through the gap.
   **Boss: Wick**, at the crater's lip, barring the shore. When he falls and Vesper raises her blade, Sallow lands between them and looks at him for the first time in three hundred years. She shields him. Vesper lowers her blade.
8. **The capital. Boss: the tower itself.** Ward fights her directly for the first time: the veins in the walls answer every move she makes on the beat, the whole tower trying to make her answer by answering her. Reply sits in the wall, the one glass that could show Ward where she is. It stays dark.
9. **The lake** at the capital's heart. The first dish lies under the water in its pale cradle. There's no fight. On the shore are dozens of paired grooves, a moth's and a glass moth's, facing each other; Reply lands in one pair. If every forest heart is awake, the roots in the crater wall are lit. The dish flashes once toward Vesper and waits, the way the thread waited at the calf.

## 4. The midpoint revelation

At the nursery, Wick dives at Vesper and Sallow throws herself between them. Wick lowers his lamp to her face: two torn left wings, one pale, one black. All round the nursery, lampwings swing their lamps above the elders, one each. Among the elders lie a few husks: moths whose lampwings Vesper killed.

**Every lampwing is a glass a moth left behind, still answering for her and keeping her alive. Wick is Sallow's.** A moth leaves her glass by facing the moon in front of it. Long ago Sallow answered for someone, reached the lake, faced the moon and came home; Wick grew a lamp and has swung it out into the dark ever since, drawing the hunt away from her. In pieces, the thing that took the world has loved moths for three hundred years, and the player has been cutting those pieces down.

## 5. Endings: where she turns her face

Each ends at the grove's summit lantern, then pulls back over the whole world to the capital.

- **Face the moon, for her family. Ward is turned away; the world stays half taken.** She faces the moon in front of Reply, as Sallow once did. Reply grows a lamp and swings it out over the western sea, and Ward, which grows west after every glass a moth leaves (the log said so), tears its young growth off the forests and pours it west after a lamp it will never catch. *The pull back:* forests green, leaves falling at last; the cities still pale, the kept still lifting their faces east. Vesper, Sallow and Mote sit at the lantern, never to grow old. *Left behind: every person in the world, and Reply, hunted forever.*
- **Face Reply, for Ward. Ward is answered; the world is free.** She turns east and answers for Reply, and through it for Ward: the first time anything Ward didn't make has answered for it. Reply lays one glass wing across her face, Sallow's gesture, the only time it touches her. The call stops. *The pull back:* the growth greys and lets go of trunks, rivers and towers; in every street the kept sit down and rub their eyes; the father lifts the child off his shoulders. Ward keeps only Vesper, unageing at the lake, answering forever. Sallow keeps a place at the lantern that no one fills. *Left behind: her life, and everyone she outlives. She bet it on Ward being a someone.*
- **Face the roots, for the forest** (only if every forest heart is awake). **Ward ends, and so does everything it kept.** She lays her glints in the lit roots, the offering from the first hour, and every heart she woke answers the lake at once, each in the voice the player heard when it woke. Ward hears a world that isn't itself, and the dish goes still, at peace or simply done. *The pull back:* the growth dies and forests grow through it, cities and all. Everything held past its time is let go: the kept wake, and those who were old when they were kept sit down like the keeper and don't get up. Every lamp goes dark, Wick's by his stone. Reply's pane clears; it flies off somewhere she never went. At summer's end Sallow folds her wings over Mote and goes still. *Left behind: Ward, if it was someone; Wick and Sallow; a world's worth of the old.*

## 6. The grove, and the hearts

The last free forest at the western edge of a taken world.
- **The call** sweeps in from the right edge, cold blue-white. Everything flashes except the moths.
- **Burrbacks** are kept, shells furred with growth; they ram whatever Ward is looking at. **Lampwings** guard the nursery from Ward's eye, so they dive at her whenever Reply is close.
- **Glints** are moonlight sealed in pods, the one light Ward can't hold. **Lanterns** are small moons: the growth stops at their edge, creatures calm inside, and each one kindled pushes the growth back.
- **Fading.** Reply keeps her the way Ward keeps everything, so she re-forms at the nearest lantern. The thing she fights is what keeps bringing her back.
- **The ember** is a seed of Ward; in the summit lantern's moonlight it can't take root and burns down warm.
- **The hearts.** Every region has a sleeping heart with its own voice: the grove's a low hum and amber light through the roots; the gorge's water-bell notes in blue; the city's a single bird-call from a park's last tree. Waking one pushes the growth back and makes that region's creatures look up instead of east. The grove's is the first light in the game that isn't Ward's; the carving of young Sallow fills. The offering of leftover glints wakes the canopy, and Mote copies it with one glint.

*Changes to the existing scene:* growth on the eastern trunks; the capital pulsing on the horizon; the half-second look up; Sallow, Mote and Wick at the stone.

## 7. Things the player finds

- **The road sign**, with *DON'T* scratched in front.
- **The resisters' wall**: painted moths, faces up, where the last people to refuse copied the creature Ward ignored.
- **The keeper's log.** *"It is not playing. It is returning,"* and under it, *"So are we."* Later: *"Moths at the lake again. Each leaves a glass and goes home. Ward never sees the moth, only the glass it lost, and it grows west after every one."* The last page: nothing has ever answered *for* Ward.
- **Wick's stone**, polished to a mirror by his lamp.

**The question it leaves.** Every ending frees some of the world and leaves someone behind. Was Reply someone? Was Ward? And what did you give up?

## Changes this round

- **Underestimation fixed as one rule:** Ward sees only through its glasses, so it sees Vesper only when Reply is near. Nothing turns to her in the prologue; an Act I heron shows the rule. Kindness to Reply costs her cover.
- **Six names** (Vesper, Ward, Reply, Sallow, Mote, Wick); places and bosses are plain words; "a glass" is the general term. Three rules, stated once.
- **Mirror boss replaced** by Ward itself: the tower answering her every move, Reply refusing to give her away.
- **Mote's loss closes Act II**; Act III holds forgiveness, Wick and the lake.
- **First ending set up** by the keeper's log, which also explains the spread.
- **Every region has a heart with its own voice**, heard again in the third ending.
