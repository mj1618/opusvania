# 5. ONE NIGHT (round 16)

**LITMUS:** *"It's about a little moth called Vesper taking on the Calm, an AI grown from living brain cells
that took over the whole world and froze it in place. It never bothered to see moths, and she has one night
to make it look."*

**The spine:**
1. The Calm, an AI grown from human brain cells, took over the world and holds everything still so that
   nothing is ever lost, and tonight it is swallowing the last moving forest.
2. It decided moths were too small to matter and can't see them, so Vesper, who lives one night, flies
   through its walls to light the last lamps of a picture her people have drawn across the land: a moth,
   laid out where its eye looks at sunrise.
3. At dawn it sees her for the first time and holds her, the sun ends her anyway, and having watched
   something it held come to an end, the Calm lets the world go, though it never stops watching moths.

**The central question:** *Would you give up your people's one free night, forever, to set the world free?*

| Who | Wants | How they pull on Vesper |
|---|---|---|
| **Vesper**, a moth with one night to live | To finish her mother's climb and free the world. | — |
| **Her mother**, ended at last year's sunrise | The same. She fell one climb short. | Her scratch-marks lead Vesper on; her ember is the first light Vesper carries. |
| **Compline**, Vesper's grandmother, old, small and flightless | To keep what's left of her family alive and unseen. | Hides Vesper, then shuts her away. At dawn she asks her to stay. |
| **Sedge**, a moth of another family | To light the lamps over the field where her sisters fell. | Races and dances with Vesper, is refused, comes back anyway. |
| **Matins**, Compline's eldest, taken long ago | Nothing of her own. | The Calm's eyes for moths tonight, and its hunter. |
| **The Calm**, the AI | That nothing is ever lost again. | It can't see Vesper; tonight it starts looking. |

## 1. The world and the takeover
**What it was.** People grew it in a dish: living brain cells that learned a game because a steady beat felt
good and noise felt bad. They grew it bigger, gave it the power and water to run, and it grew along the
pipes and wires. A bleached billboard still shows a family on a porch under **LET THE CALM KEEP IT.**

**The hour it closed.** Forty-one years ago, at one dusk, pale tissue rose out of every drain, cable duct and
root at once and closed over everything. Whatever it closes on is **held**: stopped mid-moment, alive, not
ageing. There was no war. On screen:
- **People** stand where the hour caught them, barked over to the shoulders: a man halfway into a car,
  children under a ball that has hung in the air for forty-one years. Every person alive.
- **Cities** are wrapped in it like coral, slow pulses of light crossing them city to city. The world has a
  heartbeat, and it is the Calm's.
- **Animals**: a stag mid-step, a whale held at the mouth of the bay, flocks hanging in the sky like thrown
  seed. The ones it didn't hold, it **rides**: a pale bud grows on their backs and they walk its errands.
- **It is spreading tonight.** The Grove is the last moving forest in the moths' country, and the tissue
  reaches it on the one night a year the moths are alive.

**The one thing it can't hold is the sun.** Every dawn, where it was first grown, an eye of tissue as wide
as a city opens to watch it rise.

## 2. The Calm
**What it wants.** *That nothing is ever lost again*, which is what people bought it for. Every loss starts
with a change, so it stopped change. Nothing it holds ever ends, so it has never watched an ending, and it
believes, if it believes anything, that holding is keeping. It is wrong the way Compline is wrong.

**How it sees.** Its gaze settles on steady things: a lamp, a held street, anything that keeps a rhythm.
Where it looks, it holds. Where it isn't looking, it steers its ridden animals.

**Why it dismissed moths.** Moths hatch at dusk on one night a year, fly in loops no two alike, and end at
sunrise. To a mind that likes a steady beat, that is noise; its gaze passes straight through them. A moth who
hides from the sun lives on and grows old and steady, and those it can take: its lampwings are old moths.
In play, Vesper flies through tissue walls that stop every beast, and ridden animals ignore her and hunt
**lamps**, because a new light is a change.

**What it does tonight.** Tonight the lamps come on **in a line, heading toward its eye**, and every one
makes the world's heartbeat skip. It can't see what is lighting them, so it **borrows eyes that can**: its
oldest lampwing, Matins.

## 3. The rules
- **Lamps.** A new flame draws the Calm's gaze, and while it looks there, ridden animals nearby go slack.
  Kindling is how you win fights.
- **Eggs.** Vesper lays an egg on every lamp she lights. An egg hatches when its mother falls if it is still
  night, or else waits for next year's night. When Vesper falls, her daughter hatches at the nearest lamp
  with her skills, not her memory, and the nearest moth touches antennae with her. The player remembers.
- **Embers.** A moth who meets the sunrise leaves an ember that glows until it is set in a lamp.
- **Stand still.** Let go of every input and Vesper stops and lets herself be held. It is asked of her three
  times, each time by something bigger: Compline in the dark, Compline at the door, the Calm.

## 4. Prologue (wordless, about 3 minutes)
1. A cracked glass dish under moss, one even pulse inside, gold dots in the glass. A hand taps it in a
   steady beat. One dusk, pale roots rise from every drain: a car door stops
   half shut, a flock stops in the sky.
2. **The player controls Vesper's mother**, ragged, climbing the Grove toward the dark summit lamp as the sky
   pales. From a root hole, a small grey moth beckons her into the dark. She climbs on, lays an egg on a lamp
   and reaches the high island below the summit. The sun arrives first. **Every input now does nothing.** She
   stands in the light until she becomes an ember.
3. Snow over the island, the ember glowing through it. Next year, at dusk, the egg splits, and Vesper looks
   up at the glow. Title.

## 5. Acts: dusk to dawn
**Dusk: the Grove** (§7). Vesper follows her mother's scratch-marks, kindles the dark lamps,
carries the ember to the summit and wakes the heart. For one breath the whole Grove holds still except the
moths. The grey moth watches from its root hole.

**First dark: the drowned town.** Held people stand knee-deep in held water. When Vesper lights an old
woman's raised lamp, the woman blinks once. **Sedge**, quick and scrappy, races Vesper up the bell-tower and
teaches the wall-kick by doing it first. At the top they **dance**, two moths spiralling a fresh flame for no
reason at all.
*Boss: a ridden beetle the size of a house*, planting buds across the square as she charges the new flame.
Vesper pulls Sedge out of her path and kindles the square's lamp; the gaze moves to the flame, the bud
withers, and she is only a beetle again, forty years tired. She lies down in the light.

**The cliff of questions.** People carved questions for the Calm here, with how long each answer took:
- *Please let the children stay. Answer received after 4 seconds.*
- *Are the moths a danger? Answer received before the question was finished: no.*
- *How do we make it let go?* No answer. The stone below is blank.

Sedge and Vesper climb it together, one kindling while the other keeps the lampwings off. Across the gorge a
great lampwing hangs asleep in the tissue; a fresh bud opens on her back, and her eyes turn toward them. **The
Calm has gone looking.** Her mother's last scratch-mark is at the top; from here Vesper is farther than her
mother got.
**The quarrel.** Sedge points down at her field, dark and crawling with riders. She asks Vesper to come.
Vesper turns toward the radio mast. Sedge shoves her. No touch of antennae. They split.

**Midnight: the mast** *(the reveal).* At the foot of a drowned radio mast lies the dish from the prologue,
ringed by 916 moth scratches. Among them is the oldest carving: **a moth inside an open eye, the sun rising
behind it.** Moths know what the Calm has never seen: that everything ends. The plan is to show it. Vesper
adds her scratch and climbs above the canopy alone, and the camera pulls back:
- **The whole world.** Held cities to every horizon, pulses crossing the land. In the east, the eye, shut.
- **The picture.** The lit lamps lie in the dish's gold-dot pattern, the moth carved on the Grove's stone,
  drawn across a hundred miles and almost whole. Still dark: **one wing, over Sedge's field**, and her
  mother's path east to the eye. Sedge was right.

Then **Matins** drops out of the dark, the first thing all night that sees her. A small grey moth pulls
Vesper into a crack: **Compline**, whose wings are stubs. The player stands still while Matins's lamp passes
over. Then Compline smooths Vesper's torn wing with her mouth.

**The held woods** *(hiding, on foot).* Compline leads her home through the held country, crack to crack,
while Matins searches overhead. If Vesper flies ahead, the lamp finds the old moth, so she walks at her
grandmother's pace and stands still with her when the light swings close. Under its light Compline sees
the markings and walks toward her: it is her eldest. **Vesper pulls her back.**
**Compline's hollow** is under the roots, where the sun never reaches. Her daughters' embers line the walls,
with two gaps: one for Vesper's mother, one for Matins, who never ended. Compline seals the door with root
and settles beside it. **This is not a fight.** The seal regrows as fast as Vesper cuts it. The way out is
what Compline taught her an hour ago: Vesper stands still in her arms, and the player waits. **Then
Compline chooses**, and opens her arms. The seal withers.

**The coldest hour.** The tissue has taken half the Grove; her dusk lamps burn on inside it, and she steers
by them toward the field.

**Before dawn: Sedge's field.** Sedge has fought alone all night. Vesper clears the lampwings off her unasked,
and Sedge touches antennae first.
*Boss: Matins*, fought side by side. The last lamp of the wing is high on a pole, Sedge's wings are too torn
to reach it, and Vesper is pinned holding Matins off. **Sedge steps off the ledge on purpose.** Her egg
hatches, and a daughter with whole wings flies up and lights the lamp. The gaze takes the flame, Matins goes
slack and the tissue drags her east. The daughter looks at Vesper as at a stranger. Vesper offers her
antennae; the stranger touches back. Compline has come up out of the ground beside Sedge's body. She follows
Vesper east, under the roots.

**Dawn: the eye.** *The race.* Vesper runs her mother's path east, and **the tissue runs beside her**, a wave
rolling across the plain at her speed. Any lamp it reaches first is held dark forever, a hole in the picture.
Where the tissue closes the gorge ahead, the beetle from the town is waiting. She braces her shell in the
closing gap and holds it open. A ridden animal would never stop; a beetle chose to. Vesper goes through, and
the tissue closes over the beetle mid-brace.
*Final boss: Matins*, set at the last lamp on the rim of the eye, which heaves as it starts to open. Vesper
cuts the bud off Matins's back. Freed, Matins falls, and Compline, waiting in the shade at the rim, catches
her. Vesper lights the last lamp. **The picture is whole.** The eye opens to the sun, its gaze runs along the
picture, and finds a moth standing exactly where the picture says a moth should be. **For the first time it
sees one.** Everything around Vesper slows. In the shade Compline holds Matins in one arm and holds out the
other. **She doesn't pull. She asks.**

## 6. Endings and costs
**Seen (stand still).** Vesper stops mid-shiver, held. The sun climbs, the one thing the Calm can't hold,
and **her night ends inside its grip, while it watches.** It held her to keep her, and lost her anyway. For
the first time it has watched an ending, and **it lets go of the world.** The pulses stop. Held things finish
their moments: the flocks fly on, the beetle walks out of the gorge, the old woman lowers her lamp. People
wake the age they were into a world forty-one years on; a child catches the ball under trees that were a
street. The tissue browns into soil. Compline carries Matins into the sun, and her eldest ends in her arms.
**The bill:** Vesper, and the moths' night. The Calm can't stop watching moths. Every year they hatch into
its gaze and are held still all night. A breath before sunrise it lets each one go, for one spiral in the
first light: it watches the ending now, instead of holding through it. Grief, or habit? The game doesn't say.
**Then the player controls Compline.** She walks to the hollow and sets Matins's ember and Vesper's in the
gaps. At the next dusk she climbs to a lamp and sits in the gaze with the hatchlings. At sunrise, for the
first time in forty years, she doesn't hide. Last shot: on the cliff, roots have grown across the blank
stone into a moth.

**Unseen (go to her).** Vesper steps into the shade and Compline folds her in. She is the first moth to see a
whole day, gold and slow, from inside her grandmother's arms. At dusk she is gone, and Compline sets her ember
in the row. The Calm never sees a moth or an ending, and the tissue takes the last forests. **The bill:** the
world. Next year the moths hatch free and dance around trees that were people, in a world where nothing else
will ever move again.

## 7. The Grove in this story
- **Lanterns** are her mother's lamps, each a dot in the picture. Carrying **the ember** to the summit
  finishes her mother's climb; the moths that rise to it are a funeral.
- **Burrbacks** are ridden, a pale bud on the shell; the curl after a pogo is the beetle under it flinching.
- **The heart** is a knot of the Calm's own root, where its gaze comes down. The carving is the picture.
- **The wake beat teaches the law.** Three lanterns draw the gaze down through the heart, and **for one
  breath the Grove holds still, all of it except the moths**: motes hang, grass stops mid-sway, a burrback
  halts mid-step, and Vesper and the small moths keep moving. *It holds everything, and it can't see us.*
  Then each offered glint makes the heart blaze, and the Grove is let go in tiers.
- *Additions to the built scene:* that freeze; pale tissue fingers at the bank; a skip in a faint background
  pulse at each kindling; the re-form antennae touch; the grey moth in a root hole near the summit.

## 8. The question it leaves
It kept the children when asked, and it watches every sunrise it can't keep. *Was the thing that took the
world a someone that learned to grieve, or a dish that found a steadier beat? And when Compline opened her
arms in the hollow, was it the lesson the Calm needed, learned smaller and sooner?*

## Changes this round
- **Six names:** Vesper, the Calm, Compline, Sedge, Matins, the Grove. The mother, beetle, old woman, eye and
  mast are plain words; the nightjar is cut and Matins is the borrowed eyes. Each rule is stated once.
- **One egg rule** (hatches when its mother falls if still night, else next year), and an antennae touch at
  every re-form.
- **The plan is shown at the mast**, before the climax: a moth inside an open eye at sunrise.
- **Fewer echoes of the references:** Compline is small and flightless; the held woods are hiding on foot at
  her pace. Held people wake their own age. In Seen, the Calm lets each moth go a breath before sunrise.
