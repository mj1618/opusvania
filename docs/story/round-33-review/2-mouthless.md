# Review: Mouthless (round 33)

## 1. Anchor gate: PASS

All three readers name a world-swallowing living thing in their first sentence and get to the moth within a clause ("it can't see moths because they have no mouths"). The heart reader names Umber in her second sentence; the other two name her in their first. Two of three put "brain grown in a dish" or "lab-grown brain" up front, and all three get it right in "The enemy". The reveal is earned in play rather than told: the poster (*Grown from us.* / *Now it feeds us.*), the greenhouse coat, the sister's palm on the dish, then the prologue's breathing light recast as a cloud of cells playing a ball game. No player-facing text is technical. The Hush is on screen and acting at world scale in every act: the mountain on the horizon and root under the pool (I), "every root on earth turns toward moths" (II), the wood overrun (low point), and the dead city and the mountain itself (III).

- Biological AI took over the world: **central**
- Umber the moth saves the world: **central**
- Moths deemed insignificant, underestimated: **central** (it notices only things that eat; she gets into the lab through a moth-sized crack; warming it is what makes it see her)
- Sentience ambiguity: **central** (the closing question; the forest acts; the Hush's first picture is a moth; Dun's turn)

## 2. Verdict: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES

## 3. At the bar

- **The title is the theme.** *"Warmth the forest grows to give away. A moth has no mouth to keep it."* A mouthless hero against a mind that keeps everything, ending in "a cocoon with nothing left to feed through, mouthless, like her." That is wrong in an illuminating way, and it is Ori-grade.
- **The antagonist's logic is one image.** Each missed ball hisses static, so "each leaving hisses like a missed ball." All three readers explained its motive in one line. That is Nine Sols clarity under HK mystery.
- **Seeing versus not seeing.** "To him it is blind grey stone. Umber, pressed to its skin, sees a sleeping face inside, and the camera stays on the face as the flame takes it." The lore reader's strongest memory, and it pays off when Tallow sees a face himself.
- **Sorrel.** "A dark knot round a pale case... Then he shivers: alive." Two readers named it as what they'd remember. The wingtip tap runs from the hatching to the last scene.
- **The cost is played.** "Each lap costs her glide, then somersault, then dash." Mechanics carry the sacrifice.

## 4. Problems, ranked

1. **The climax boss contradicts itself (POLISH, near-foundation).** "Its root stops creeping across the world" and then "still it reaches". The lore reader asked: "Did I win?" *Fix:* cut "its root stops creeping". The hollows close, but every root turns inward toward the dish and curls and fails like the caterpillar's threads. The fight ends on the Hush trying and failing to cocoon itself, so the endings follow from what the player just saw.
2. **The ageing rule breaks on the sister (POLISH).** "Does not age" sits beside "Tallow's sister, old now", and two readers stumbled on it. *Fix:* at the lab, write "grey-haired, as she was when she walked back in". The rule stays as it is.
3. **Tallow's turn is two beats short (POLISH).** "Burns pods for twenty years, then calmly walks into one" (heart reader), and "Did Tallow know his coal would kill his sister?" (lore reader). *Fix:* no new rule. On Night 7, at his sister's case, he holds his hand out for the coal. Umber can keep it. That makes Burn a betrayal of him that the player can see, and it lets him close his own arc: he sees her palm on the dish, lays his own down, and the case closes.
4. **The middle is crowded (POLISH).** Heart and casual both say so. Nights 5–6 carry the overrun, the learning-hollows boss, Sorrel's partner, Dun's turn, the caterpillar, the streetlamp moths, the Tallow boss and the carving emptying. *Fix:* cut the streetlamp-moths beat, because the overrun already shows moths being taken. Make the three learning hollows the Hush's own act rather than a boss, since it "learns her" anyway.
5. **Is the pursuit threat or love? (POLISH).** "Lampwings lift moths gently... I couldn't tell whether to fear it" (casual reader). The ambiguity is the point, but the player still needs stakes. *Fix:* on Night 5, show one cased moth again at dawn, still and pale, its week used up asleep. Being kept is a kind of death for something that only lives a week.
6. **Small continuity slips (POLISH).** The forest sends "everything it has left" on Night 6, yet Night 7 still has "the forest's last glints". And what is inside the Spin cocoon? *Fix:* on Night 6 the forest sends one last river of glints, and she carries it into Night 7. The sleeping-moth shape and the press-back are enough for the cocoon. Keep them.

No foundation problems.

## 5. Scores

Anchor 9 · Relationships 9 · Clarity 7 · Idea 9 · World 8 · Mechanics-as-theme 9 · Antagonist 9 · Ending 9 · Originality 8 · Grove fit 8 · Balance 8

Notes: Clarity is held down by the climax contradiction and the crowded middle, but all three readers retold the spine correctly and said they would finish. Grove fit is 8 because badgers stand in for burrbacks, and the summit's EMBER becomes a big glint while the coal moves to Tallow. Both changes are fine, but the art team should be told. Originality is 8 because the moths-from-every-wood finale leans toward Ori's swell. The spinning and the cost in abilities make it its own.

## 6. At the bar? **Yes.**

This sits next to Ori, Hollow Knight and Nine Sols as an equal. It has a mythic fable on the surface (a mouthless moth with a week to live) and a technical horror underneath (a dish of human cells taught that every leaving is pain). Its antagonist is understandable, and its ending is paid for in abilities. The remaining work is clarity polish. The idea, cast and spine should not be reopened.
