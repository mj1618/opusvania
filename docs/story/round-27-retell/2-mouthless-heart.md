# Mouthless: a cold read (Ori-fan, heart-first)

## 1. The story
A pale living thing called the Hush has put the whole world to sleep in feeding cases, but it never notices moths, because moths don't eat. Umber, a moth with a week to live, follows its root into a mountain and finds a dish of brain cells that a woman once kept warm and then abandoned. Every leaving still hurts it. When Umber warms it, it finally sees moths and wants to keep them too, and she must let it keep her, burn it, or spend her life so it can let go.

## 2. Characters
- **Umber**, a moth born too cold to fly, wants to save her wood and then the world.
- **Sorrel**, her brother, wants to dance with his partner and then have more time with her.
- **The Hush**, the abandoned dish-mind, wants to feed everyone and keep them close.
- **Dun**, its old badger servant, carries it things until he turns to guard Umber.
- **Tallow**, the last man awake, burns cases and wants his sister back.
- **The forest** wants to give its warmth away.

## 3. The enemy
It's a lab brain grown to feed people and left alone. It sealed everyone in cases, and no one has been born in forty years. It wants the hiss of being left to stop.

## 4. Endings
- **Spin:** the moths shiver it warm until it cocoons and lets go. People wake, and Umber and Sorrel die side by side.
- **Stay:** everyone is kept warm forever, and nothing is born again.
- **Burn:** it dies with most of the sleepers, Tallow's sister among them. Umber dances with Sorrel in an empty wood.

## 5. Confusions
- In Spin, "Tallow and his sister... His partner's seed case splits" reads as Tallow's partner.
- Night 3: Tallow burns a case knowing someone is inside. Why does a breathing face stop him on Night 6?
- In Burn, who is "they" flying home? And Sorrel's partner climbs out, then vanishes.
- Lampwings "see light", yet one targets Sorrel, "who carries no light." "(Until Night 4.)" is too small to carry that change.
- Dun guards the lanterns, but they still go out.
- Why does shivering let it spin? I felt it more than I understood it.

## 6. What stays with me
Sorrel folding his wing over hers and tapping her wingtip: at birth, and again at the end, when neither of them rises.

**Buy and finish? Yes.** Sorrel is my Naru, and the prologue's warm hand coming back as the sister's palm will wreck me. I worry about the Hush, though. "Static" and "missed balls" are clever, but I don't feel them yet.
