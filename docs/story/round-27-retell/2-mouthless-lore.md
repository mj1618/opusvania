# Mouthless: cold retell (Hollow Knight lore-hunter)

## 1. The story in three sentences
A living pale growth called the Hush has put the whole human world to sleep in fruit-like cases it feeds forever, but it never notices moths, because moths don't eat. Umber, a moth with a week to live, follows it to a mountain lab and finds its heart is an abandoned dish-grown brain that has been cold and hurting since everyone walked out on it. When she warms it, it finally sees moths and wants to keep them too, so she has to choose: be kept, burn it, or give her life (with every other moth) so it can spin itself a cocoon and let the world go.

## 2. Characters
- **Umber:** dying moth; wants to save her wood, then everyone.
- **Sorrel:** her brother; wants to dance with his partner, then wants more time with Umber.
- **The Hush:** the dish-brain; wants to feed and keep everyone, because every leaving hurts.
- **Dun:** old badger-servant half turned to root; brings the Hush what it wants, later guards Umber.
- **Tallow:** last awake human; burns cases thinking they eat people; wants his sister back.
- **The forest:** an old unmade mind; wants to give its warmth away.

## 3. The enemy
A brain grown from cells (I think the sister's) that learned a ball game, was fed static when it failed, and was abandoned when the lab closed. Its root fed the hungry town, then everything: forty years of no waking and no births, animals regrown as servants. It wants the static of being left to stop.

## 4. Endings
- **Spin:** every moth shivers on it, it cocoons itself, humans wake, Umber and Sorrel die, the forest is spent.
- **Stay:** Umber is kept dancing forever; nothing is ever born again.
- **Burn:** the Hush dies and takes nearly all its long-term sleepers with it, including Tallow's sister.

## 5. Confusions
- The Night 4 coal choice: keeping the coal means "burning through the sleepers". Did I just kill people to unlock the Burn ending? That is a huge moral cost stated in half a line.
- "(Until Night 4.)" in rule 3: is it the lampwing line or the whole unseen rule that ends?
- Spin epilogue: "His partner's seed case splits" right after a Tallow sentence; I first read it as Tallow's partner.
- Lantern counting on Nights 5 and 6 ("second", "last") was hard to follow.
- Glide, jump and dash are lost in Spin, but they were never introduced.
- Was the dish grown from the sister's cells? The poster hints at it but never says so. I liked that, but I'm not sure it was meant.

## 6. What I'd remember
Sorrel curled round his partner's seed-case while root closes over the lantern, and then one wingtip moves. Would I buy and finish it? **Yes.** The wingtip tap is a motif I'd actually feel by the end, and the lab memory (each worker leaving hisses like a missed ball) reframes the villain in a way I'd want to dig into through item text.
