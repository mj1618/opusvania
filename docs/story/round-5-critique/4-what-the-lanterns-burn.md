## 2. Previous problems

1. Split antagonist and relationship: **mostly fixed.** The Taper holds the role, but her final act happens off screen.
2. Tallow's death copies Kuro's: **fixed.** "It lives."
3. The ending needs a wiki: **partly.** The eye chain is gone, and planting vs burning is now shown in the Grove. "The old forest eats the Bequest" from one ember, and the lanterns guttering everywhere, still don't follow.
4. The midpoint is a flashback: **fixed.** The fold-over-the-pod rhyme now carries the turn.
5. The underestimate is told, not played: **fixed.** Empty moths are invisible, the glide bends toward lanterns, and the storm dives where the pull would take a moth.
6. The reversal is a known shape: **partly.** The faith now holds weight and Grafted keeps the rite. The Hollow Knight and Nine Sols echoes remain, and the Taper adds a third (3.5).
7. The Grove's clues sit below the threshold: **fixed** by the two sim changes. "It hides three frames of an open hand" brings the old problem back: three frames is 50 ms.
8. Too much lore: **fixed.**

## 3. Problems now, ranked

1. **The climax happens off screen.** "At the Root's lantern lies the Taper, out of glints and fading for the last time." Nothing shows why. She last relit the Censer's lantern, and then she is simply empty. The story's cost is paid between scenes. *Fix:* make the Root her fight too. She follows Vesper into the lampwing storm and spends her last re-forms at the Root lantern, Bracing over Vesper against the snatches. She becomes Clasp, as Vesper did, and the player watches the glass count reach zero.

2. **Doing the right thing is also the cheapest option, so there is no dilemma.** A grown pod "becomes a re-form point that costs nothing." Once Plant exists, burning is just impatience, and then "You burned them to keep playing" scolds a player the design gave no reason to burn. The fade cost also contradicts itself: the table says "One glint, every time", but the Grove section says "A fade's lost third of the wallet flies into the lantern." *Fix:* pods give no sanctuary (creatures harvest at them and burrbacks fold over them), they grow slowly, and boss arenas have only lanterns. Burning then becomes a real trade made under pressure, and the hollows in the tree count choices, not laziness. Pick one fade cost.

3. **The central gesture means opposite things.** In the prologue, the Taper's wing "turns her back to the light", which means it turns Vesper to face the light, away from Tallow. At the end, the same words describe the opposite gesture, made "into a gift". In the Grove, "Her wing turns Vesper's back to the lanterns", the opposite of what the Taper wants there. If the writer can't keep straight which way Vesper faces, a wordless camera can't either. *Fix:* stage it as facing. The Taper turns Vesper's face toward the glass every time until the end, when she turns it toward the earth. Use the same framing all three times.

4. **Grafted is unearned.** "Every moth in the Censer turns its back to Vesper", and then, with no scene between, "Vesper leads the procession again." One moth's choice also makes the lanterns "gutter one by one" everywhere. Kindled has the same jump: one ember, "the old forest eats the Bequest." *Fix:* the moths follow the Taper's reed, which Vesper carries out of the Root, not Vesper herself. Keep both endings to this valley and cut the world-scale claims.

5. **Derivative beats.** The prologue copies Ori's: a guardian takes in a foundling at night, then "The moon wheels through its phases" is Naru's seasons montage. A mentor extending her life by burning human remnants is the Solarians' fuel farming in Nine Sols. Moths devoted to a devouring light are Hollow Knight's Moth Tribe and the Radiance. Grief-bots are Black Mirror's "Be Right Back". *Fix:* cut the rescue-and-montage and open on the Taper's hundredth re-form, with Vesper as a face in the crowd. Let the rescue surface later as a place: the Taper's wing-silk is Vesper's own cocoon silk, found in the hollow.

6. **The theme is said aloud, and the ambiguity is gone.** "She looked every time" appears four times. §10 speaks in the second person ("Knowing what it cost…"). Every glint image (an open hand, cupped *human* hands, faces) declares that the glints are people, so "the game never settles" is false on screen, and the Taper's side loses its defence. *Fix:* add one counter-image. The open hand flickers identically every time, a reflex loop like DishBrain's Pong. And in her hollow, the hundreds of scratched faces are all the same face. Say the refrain once, in the hollow.

## 4. The one change

**Make the Taper and Vesper re-form from the same glass.** At the Censer and the Root, every time the player fades, the glass the Taper lives on drops by one. Stage her last re-forms on screen at the Root as she shields Vesper. The player's own deaths spend the mentor, one press at a time. That fills in the missing climax, makes the cost mechanical rather than spoken, and removes the need for the closing question.

## 5. Verdict

**Close.** The mechanics-as-meaning layer is at the bar, and the Hush is now a played revelation worthy of Hollow Knight. Three things separate it from yes: the cost the ending depends on happens off screen; the moral route costs nothing, so the indictment is unfair; and the prologue is Ori's beat, which the brief forbids.
