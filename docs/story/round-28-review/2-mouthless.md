# Review: Mouthless (round 28)

## 1. Anchor gate: PASS

All three readers' first sentences name a little moth against a pale living thing that put the world to sleep. All three
also found the truth underneath ("a brain grown in a lab dish", "a dish of cells that learned a ball game"). They found
it through the lab poster and the memory, which are places and objects, not exposition. The Hush is on screen and acting
at world scale in every act. In Act I there is root under the pool and Dun wading through the dance. Night 2 has the pits
and the orchard of cases, Night 3 a root as thick as a tower, Act II the lab and "every root on earth turns toward moths",
Night 5 the overrun wood and Night 6 the dead city, ending in the Hush as the final boss.

Owner's ideas:
- Biological AI took over the world: **central**.
- Umber, a moth, saves the world: **central**.
- Moths deemed insignificant and underestimated: **central**. "Moths never eat" is the reason, the stealth rule, the
  midpoint turn and the ending.
- Sentience ambiguity: **central** for the Hush, and it is the closing question ("was that love, or had it only learned
  to make the static stop?"). The forest's and the animals' minds are **minor but present**: the forest acts three times,
  Dun turns, the vole and the vixen get scenes.

## 2. Craft verdict: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES

## 3. At the bar
- **The governing image is a real idea, not decoration.** A thing that feeds everyone, faced by creatures that have no
  mouths: *"A moth has no mouth to keep it."* The hero's anatomy is the theme, the stealth rule and the moral.
- **The antagonist is wrong in a way that lights up the hero.** It was trained by pain at every miss and then abandoned,
  and it learned that keeping people stops the static. Umber gives, and the Hush keeps. All three readers called it
  lonely, not evil.
- **Relationships are carried by actions.** Sorrel shivering her warm at birth, the wingtip tap, Umber folding over Dun
  on the ice and Dun later "lies across her lantern's door, facing out". The casual reader remembered Dun, and the heart
  reader remembered "one wingtip moves".
- **Mechanics as theme.** Give, don't fight. Shiver to spend your own warmth. Unseen until lit. In the Spin ending the
  laps cost her glide, then jump, then dash, so the true ending takes her moveset away.
- **The ending costs something, and the last image is earned.** The sister's palm on the silk answers the prologue's warm
  blur, and the lore reader picked it as what stayed with them.

## 4. Problems (ranked)
1. **The coal at the wall (Night 4) is unreadable and quietly gates an ending.** POLISH (high). All three readers stalled
   on it: burn through with what, and why do I still have the coal later? Fix: show it. She presses the coal to the wall,
   the cases blacken one by one, and the coal on her back glows redder for the rest of the game. That red glow is the
   visible tag that makes Burn available. No new rule needed.
2. **Is the Hush grown from the sister?** POLISH (high). Two of the three readers asked. This is the hidden family at the
   heart of the story (Tallow's sister is its mother), and leaving it at "implied" wastes the revelation. Fix: one item in
   the lab, such as her greenhouse coat on a hook with the item text *"She gave it the first light."*, or put her face in
   the poster's first panel.
3. **Tallow's Night 3 burn undercuts his Night 6 turn.** POLISH. "Turns his face away" reads as knowing the sleepers are
   alive, so the breathing face on Night 6 can't land as a revelation. Fix: on Night 3 the case is opaque and he stares at
   *EATEN* while he burns it. On Night 6 the case is worn thin, like the man in the wall, and he sees.
4. **Continuity slips.** POLISH. Spin says "every moth the Hush never noticed", but after the turn it noticed all of them.
   Change it to "every moth it overlooked for forty years". After the midpoint, show "unseen" ending once on screen: a
   badger's head swings to a moth for the first time.
5. **Too many timeline numbers.** POLISH. Forty, twenty and "twenty years later" made two readers do arithmetic. Keep
   only the twenty tally marks on screen and cut "forty years" from player-facing beats.
6. **Spin's logic and the wingtip tap.** POLISH. The casual reader asked why Spin works when closing the pits didn't. The
   caterpillar is the key, but it sits apart. Fix: make the Hush's falling threads the same image as the caterpillar's
   frost-cracked silk, so the player recognises it. Ration the wingtip tap to four moments (birth, Night 2, the knot,
   the end) so it keeps its meaning. Also make the partner's case readable, and settle whether a case pauses a moth's
   week, in one image rather than a rule.

No foundation problems. The spine, central question, cast, antagonist and endings all held up in three cold retellings.

## 5. Scores
anchor 9 · relationships 9 · clarity 8 · idea 9 · world 9 · mechanics-as-theme 9 · antagonist 9 · ending 9 ·
originality 8 · Grove fit 8 · balance 8

Originality takes a slight knock because fed sleepers in pods is a familiar image, though "cases like fruit on stalks"
and the orchard rescue it. For Grove fit, turning burrbacks into armoured badgers is an art change the outline should own.
For balance, the lab lore and Tallow's thread are dense, and the casual reader said the game must "show it, not tell it".

## 6. At the bar? YES
I would put this next to Ori, Hollow Knight and Nine Sols as an equal. It has a mythic surface (a moth, a lantern, a
pale mountain), a specific technical truth underneath, a lonely antagonist whose wrongness defines the hero, and an
ending that spends the hero, her brother and the forest. The biggest remaining gap is a production one, not a story one:
the midpoint coal choice and the sister's link to the Hush must be shown in play, or the two best twists stay in the
outline.
