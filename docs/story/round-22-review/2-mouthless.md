# Review: The Moth and the Mouth (round 22, outline 2)

## 1. Anchor gate: PASS

All three readers open the same way: a little moth with no mouth against "a pale living thing called the Mouth" that "sealed every person and animal". All three name the truth too: "a brain grown from human cells in a lab" (lore), "a brain people grew and abandoned" (heart), "a lab-grown brain" (casual). The players discover it in the lab, through the dish, the handprint and the Pong screen, with no technical words. The Mouth is on screen and active in every act. In Act I the root creeps into the Grove and the mountain breathes on the horizon. On Night 2 a mouth in the road takes a hare. Night 4 is the reveal, the Grove is overrun on Night 5, and on Nights 6–7 the lanterns go out one by one. Its momentum grows after the midpoint instead of fading.

Owner's ideas:
- **Biological AI that took over the world:** central.
- **Umber the moth saves the world:** central.
- **Moths deemed insignificant and underestimated:** central. "It notices only mouths" is the stealth rule, the hook and the title. This is the best version of this idea so far.
- **Sentience:** central for the Mouth ("was that love, or had it only learned how to make the static stop?"). Minor for the forest, which shows its mind through one carving and one river of gold.

## 2. Verdict: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES

## 3. At the bar

- **The reversal works for every reader.** "It has not been eating the world. It has been feeding it" moves the whole cast at once. Tallow's forty years of "husks" turn into a crime, and the dots the player burned on the map stay still while every other dot breathes. The lore reader's keepsake is "every dot on the map breathes, except the line I burned". That is the same authorship trick as Nine Sols's Apeman reveal.
- **Kindness costs something.** "A lampwing turns its lamp, slowly, onto Sorrel, who carries no light at all." The heart reader puts it as "my kindness is what made it see my brother". That reader remembered the moral of the midpoint as a feeling.
- **The relationship has a recurring physical gesture.** The wing-fold, the shiver and the wingtip tap run through the prologue, Night 2, the caterpillar, Night 6 and the death. Both feeling readers named it.
- **The Mouth's origin is told in images.** "A screen, still lit, where a ball bounces between two bats and nobody plays." The surface is mythic and the technical truth sits underneath, as the brief asks.
- **Tallow's turn happens on screen.** He burns a case close enough to see the face inside breathe, then drops his torch.

## 4. Problems, ranked

1. **The sister thread is the emotional hinge, and it doesn't read (POLISH, top priority).** She "turns off the lights and goes", yet she is found sealed in the chair with unopened feed jars. The heart reader can't tell "abandoned, or came back too late?" Fix: one image in the lab. A dated notice on the door, *closed*, sits over a second, later trail of her footprints in dust leading to the chair. She came back years later, too late. Her guilt and Tallow's grief then point the same way.
2. **The two reveals read as a contradiction (POLISH).** The midpoint says glints feed it. The finale says "it was never the feed. It was the hand." Two readers felt the theme flip. Fix: cut that bold line and let play carry it. Glints stop the root (it takes nothing more) but the static stays, and only warmth (the shiver from rule 2, already taught) clears it. Gift, then presence. No new rule needed.
3. **"Grown to feed us": feed us how? (POLISH).** Two of three readers lost the Mouth's original job. Fix: on the poster, the dish is wired to greenhouses, orchards and trucks. It was grown to run the food. Then "No one goes hungry" reads as its literal task carried to the end.
4. **Dun's turn is unmotivated, and all three readers flagged it (POLISH).** Fix with an existing rule: "only a gift makes it let go." On Night 5 Umber has no glints, so she shivers her warmth into Dun, and the leash snaps because Dun was given something. This also rehearses the ending.
5. **The document spoils itself (POLISH).** Rule 1's "From Night 4 the Mouth sees moths" gives away the midpoint, and the litmus line gives away the reveal. Move both to a designer note. "The pool" and "coral lamps" first appear on Night 5: seed the pool on Night 1 and call the coral lamps lampwings. Make Tallow "the last man awake in this country", or cut "the few".
6. **Pods of fed sleepers echo The Matrix (POLISH).** Keep the cases hung "like fruit" in orchards and cold stores, and never in tidy industrial rows.

There are no foundation problems.

## 5. Scores

## 6. At the bar? Yes

The hook, the reversal and the sibling gesture hold up beside Ori's prologue and Nine Sols's Apeman turn. The idea fits in one line and all three readers would finish. The biggest remaining risk is the sister thread. It is the human hand under the whole metaphor, and until the player can tell whether she abandoned the brain or came back too late, the last image ("She doesn't lift her hand") lands at half strength.
