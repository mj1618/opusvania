# Review: Mouthless (round 26)

## 1. Anchor gate: PASS

All three cold readers open on the litmus. Casual: "A giant living thing called the Hush has grown over the world... but it can't see moths because moths don't eat." All three found the dish brain, the ball game and the abandoned lab without help. The poster, the flooded room of dead siblings and the replayed prologue make it a revelation in the Hollow Knight way, and no player-facing text is technical. The Hush is on screen and acting at world scale in every act: the prologue's spreading frost, root under the pool and the breathing mountain (I), pits, orchards and the tower root (I–II), the global pulse and every root turning on moths (II), the wood overrun (low point), the city papered with its picture of Umber and the final fight (III).

Owner's ideas:
- Biological AI that took over the world: **central**.
- Umber the moth saves the world: **central**.
- Moths deemed insignificant and underestimated: **central**. It is the litmus, a rule ("notice only things that eat") and the turn at Night 4.
- Sentience ambiguity: **central**. Its first picture is a moth, the forest acts three times, and the last line asks "was that love, or had it only learned how to make the static stop?"

## 2. Verdict: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES

## 3. At the bar
- **The anchor is the verb.** "Warmth the forest grows to give away. A moth has no mouth to keep it." The mouthless moth, the eater-blind Hush and give-don't-fight are all one idea, and the game plays it.
- **The brother.** "A dark knot round a pale seed... until one wingtip moves." Two readers named this as what they'd remember. The wingtip tap at birth, at parting and at "Neither moth rises" is Ori-prologue grade.
- **An ending that takes your moves.** "Each lap costs her glide, then jump, then dash." The lore reader would tell friends about it.
- **Tallow's reversal is shown, not said.** "As it splits, they both see the face inside breathing. Tallow lays the torch down." The payoff is him sealed palm to palm with his sister "because he came without fire."
- **The last image.** "Something presses back from inside. She doesn't lift her hand." It answers the prologue's blur and leaves the sentience question open on the right note.

## 4. Problems, ranked
1. **Timeline contradiction (POLISH, top priority).** Two readers did the maths, and the heart reader asked "Who else was awake?" "Everyone on earth but Tallow" for forty years clashes with the sister coming back with a torch and being "kept twenty years." *Fix:* "everyone but Tallow and his sister, who kept a fire in their ring of ash. Twenty years ago she walked up the road with a torch and didn't come back." That one line also turns his tally and his burning into grief for someone who went looking.
2. **Feed or eat? (POLISH, verging on foundation).** The spine says "it feeds the world," Tallow writes *EATEN*, and Spin wakes people "old and hungry." Nobody could say what the cases do, and the heart reader felt the motive "reads like a lab note rather than a feeling." *Fix (no new rule):* in the Night 4 flashback, each worker who walks out of the closing greenhouse floods the dish with the same static as a missed ball. Leaving and missing become one pain the player sees. Then show one case from inside: a stalk feeding a sleeper, who is thin but alive. Tallow is wrong and the Hush is wrong too, so "old and hungry" reads as the cost of being kept, not as a plot hole.
3. **Night 4 is overloaded (POLISH).** It holds the coal choice, the siblings room, the poster, the flashback, the world pulse, Dun and the vixen, the first picture, Sorrel targeted, the chase and the forest acting. *Fix:* move the dead-siblings room to Night 7's approach. Cut "every case breathes, except the burned ones," which nobody parsed. Move the lampwing turning on Sorrel to *after* Umber flies, which also clears the rule contradiction the lore reader caught.
4. **Night 1 setup gaps (POLISH).** Readers asked how the lanterns light without the coal, and what the "high island" and "summit" are. *Fix:* rule 3 already covers it once reworded as "glints light a lantern for a night; a coal lights it for good." Then show the high island and the summit dance tree in the first ninety seconds of Night 1 play.
5. **The coal's thread is lost (POLISH).** "If she kept the coal" in the endings sent two readers back to Night 4. *Fix:* if she gave it, the Night 4 lantern is visible burning at the final chamber's mouth. The player sees where the coal went, and Burn's absence explains itself.
6. **Stay's "three" and the final fight's outcome (POLISH).** *Fix:* in Stay, the root lifts the partner's seed case up beside the two of them before the skin shows three dancers. In the boss, change "She has nothing left but warmth" to a visible beat where the pits close but the dish stays grey, so the player reads "won the fight, not the war."

## 5. Scores
Anchor 9 · Relationships 9 · Clarity 7 · Idea 9 · World 9 · Mechanics-as-theme 9 · Antagonist 8 · Ending 9 · Originality 9 · Grove fit 9 · Balance 8

All three readers would finish it. The casual player's warning, "the middle has a lot of rules and lore to keep in my head", is the cost to watch.

## 6. At the bar? Yes

I'd put this next to Ori and Nine Sols as an equal. The fable is simple, the reveal is earned, the relationships are carried by gesture and the true ending is paid for in mechanics. The biggest remaining gap: the Hush's motive has to be *felt* in one image, not reconstructed from three item texts. Fix 2 is the most important note in this review.
