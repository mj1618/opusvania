# 5. ONE NIGHT (round 15)

**LITMUS:** *"It's about a little moth called Vesper taking on the Calm, an AI grown from living brain cells
that took over the whole world and froze it. It can't even see her, and she has one night."*

**The spine:**
1. The Calm, an AI grown from brain cells, took over the world and holds everything still so nothing is ever
   lost, and tonight it is swallowing the last moving forest.
2. It decided moths were too small to matter and can't see them, so Vesper, who lives for one night, lights
   the last lamps of a picture her people have drawn for it, while the Calm hunts whatever is lighting them.
3. At dawn it finally sees her and holds her, the sun ends her anyway, and the Calm learns that holding keeps
   nothing and lets the world go, but it can never stop seeing moths.

**The central question:** *Would you give up your people's one free night, forever, to set the world free?*

| Who | Wants | How they pull on Vesper |
|---|---|---|
| **Vesper**, a moth with one night to live | To finish what her mother started and free the world. | — |
| **Lune**, her mother, ended at last year's dawn | The same. She fell one climb short. | Her scent and scratch-marks lead Vesper on. Her ember is the first light Vesper carries. |
| **Compline**, Lune's mother, old, with a mouth | To keep what's left of her family alive and unseen. | Saves Vesper, carries her, then shuts her away. At dawn she asks her to stay. |
| **Sedge**, a moth of another line | To light her own field, where her sisters died. | Asks Vesper for help. Vesper refuses, and they part badly. |
| **Matins**, Compline's eldest, taken and ridden for forty years | Nothing of her own. The Calm uses her. | The Calm's best hunter, and the reason Compline hides. |
| **The Calm** | That nothing is ever lost again. | The world's weather now: its front, its riders, its look. It can't see her, and tonight it starts looking for her anyway. |

## 1. The world and the takeover
**What it was.** People grew it in a dish: living brain cells that learned a game because a steady beat felt
good and noise felt bad. They grew it bigger and gave it the power, water and traffic to run, and it grew
along the pipes and wires. A bleached billboard still shows a family on a porch under **LET THE CALM KEEP IT.**

**The hour it closed.** Forty-one years ago, at one dusk, all over the world at once, pale tissue rose out of
drains, cable ducts and roots and closed over everything. Wherever it closes, a thing **holds**: it stops
mid-moment and stays there, alive and unaging. There was no war. On screen:
- **People** stand where the hour caught them, barked over to the shoulders: a man halfway into a car,
  children in a schoolyard under a ball that has hung in the air for forty-one years. Every person on Earth.
- **Cities** are wrapped in it like coral, slow pulses of light crossing them in one beat, city to city, to
  the horizon. The world has a heartbeat, and it is the Calm's.
- **Animals**: a stag mid-step, a whale held at the mouth of the bay, flocks hanging over cities like thrown
  seed. The ones it didn't hold, it **rides**: a pale bud grows on their backs and they walk its errands on
  perfect loops, planting buds at the edge.
- **The forest** is mostly held woods, leaves and rain hanging mid-fall.
- **It is spreading tonight.** The Grove is the last moving forest in the moths' country, and the front
  reaches it on this Long Night, the one night a year the moths are alive.

**The one thing it can't hold is the sun.** Every dawn, at its birthplace, a city-sized eye of tissue opens
to watch it rise.

## 2. The Calm
**What it wants.** *That nothing is ever lost again*, which is what people bought it for. Every loss starts
with a change, so it stopped change. Nothing it holds ever ends, so it has never seen an ending inside its
hold, and it believes, if it believes anything, that holding is keeping. It is wrong the way Compline is
wrong.

**How it sees.** Its attention, **the look**, is a slow weight that settles on steady things: a lamp, a
held street, anything that keeps a rhythm. Where it looks, it holds. Where it isn't looking, it steers its
riders. It can't do both in one place.

**Why it dismissed moths, and how that lets Vesper in.** A moth lives one night and flies in loops no two
alike. To a mind that likes a steady beat, that is noise. *Are the moths a danger? No.* Its look passes
straight through them. In play:
- Its seals close on anything that moves, except a moth: Vesper flies through walls that stop every beast.
- Riders don't hunt moths; they hunt **lamps**, because a new light is a change. Every kindling brings a
  fight to you.
- The only moths it ever took are old ones with mouths, who lived long enough to grow steady: the lampwings.

**What it does tonight.** For forty-one years a few lamps came on each Long Night and its riders put them
out. Tonight they come on **in a line, heading toward its eye**. It notices: every lamp Vesper lights, the
world's heartbeat skips, everywhere at once. It can't see what is lighting them, so it **borrows eyes that
can**: it grows a bud on a nightjar, a bird that eats moths. By dawn it has thrown its whole front at the
last lamps and set its best hunter on the last one. It has never hunted something it cannot see.

**Is it a someone?** Never settled. *For:* asked to let the children stay, it did, exactly; it watches every
sunrise, the one thing it can't keep; tonight it learns. *Against:* everything it does is what a dish that
likes a steady beat would do, made enormous. At the end it sees a moth only because the picture was the
steadiest thing in sight.

## 3. Four rules a player feels
- **Lamps** are salvaged streetlamp heads, fed with glints. A new flame catches the look and is held burning,
  and while the Calm looks there, nearby riders go slack. Kindling is how you win fights.
- **Eggs.** Vesper lays an egg on every lamp she lights. Fall before dawn and the newest egg wakes, with her
  skills but not her memories. For Vesper's daughters, the player remembers.
- **Embers.** A moth who meets the sunrise leaves an ember that glows until it is given to a lamp.
- **Stillness.** Let go of every input and Vesper stands still and lets herself be held. It is asked of her
  three times, each by something bigger: Compline's wings, Compline's arms, the Calm.

## 4. Prologue (wordless, about 3 minutes)
1. A cracked glass dish under moss, one even pulse inside, gold dots in the glass. A hand taps it in a
   steady beat; city lights spread across a map. A woman lights a streetlamp at dusk, evening after evening,
   as pale roots rise from the drain at her feet. One dusk they rise everywhere at once: a car door stops half
   shut, a flock stops in the sky, she doesn't lower her arm.
2. **The player controls a ragged moth (Lune)** in the Grove as the sky pales, climbing toward the dark
   summit lantern. A huge furred moth pulls her toward the shade. She shakes free. The sun arrives first.
   **Every input now does nothing.** She stands in the first sun until her light becomes an ember.
3. Snow over the island, the ember glowing through it. Next Long Night a cocoon on a lantern hook splits, and
   Vesper looks up at the glow. Title.

## 5. Acts: dusk to dawn
**Dusk: the Grove** (the built scene, §7). Pale fingers of tissue are already in the bank. Vesper follows
Lune's scent, kindles the dark lamps, carries her mother's ember to the summit and wakes the heart. For one
breath the whole Grove holds still except the moths. A heavy moth watches from the canopy.

**First dark: the drowned town.** Knee-deep held water, every person in it held. When Vesper lights the
Lamplighter's lamp, the old woman blinks; her raised palm becomes Vesper's rest. **Sedge**, quick and
scrappy, races Vesper up the bell-tower and teaches the wall-kick by doing it first. At the top they
**dance**, two moths spiralling a fresh flame for no reason at all.
*Boss: the Matriarch*, a ridden burrback the size of a house with a bud like a pale fist on her shell,
planting buds across the square as she charges the new flame. Wild hits make her wilder; even, steady hits
catch the look, and she freezes mid-charge a hand's breadth from Sedge. Vesper pulls Sedge clear and kindles
the arena lamp; the look moves to the flame, the bud withers, and she is only a beetle again, forty years
tired. She lies down in the light.

**The cliff of questions.** People carved questions for the Calm here, with how long each answer took:
- *Please let the children stay. Answer received after 4 seconds.* A root has grown through the words.
- *Are the moths a danger? Answer received before the question was finished: no.*
- *How do we make it let go?* No answer. The stone below is blank.

Sedge and Vesper climb together, one kindling while the other keeps the lampwings off, the front sealing
the cliff behind them. Across the gorge the player watches a bud open on a sleeping nightjar's back and its
eyes turn toward their lamps. **The Calm has gone looking.** Lune's last scratch-mark is at the top; from
here Vesper is farther than her mother got.
**The quarrel.** Sedge points down at her field: dark, crawling with riders, where her sisters died. She asks
Vesper to come. Vesper turns toward the Mast. Sedge shoves her. No touch of antennae. They split.

**Midnight: the Mast** *(the revelation).* At the foot of a drowned radio mast lies the dish from the
prologue, **the Calm's first dish**, ringed by 916 moth scratches. Vesper adds hers and climbs above the
canopy alone, and the camera pulls all the way back:
- **The whole world.** Held cities to every horizon, pulses crossing the continent, hanging flocks, the held
  sea. On the eastern edge, **the Eye**, shut, its pulse quickening.
- **The picture.** The lit lamps lie in **the dish's gold-dot pattern**, the moth carved on the Grove's
  stone, drawn across a hundred miles and almost whole. Still dark: **one wing, Sedge's field**, and Lune's
  path east to the Eye. Generations of moths have drawn a moth where the Calm looks every morning. Sedge
  was right.

Then **the nightjar** drops out of the dark: the Calm's borrowed eyes, the first thing all night that sees
her. It lunges, and a furred arm yanks Vesper into shadow. **Compline** folds her wings over her, and the
player must let go of every input and *hold still* while it hunts past. Then Compline smooths Vesper's torn
wing with her mouth.

**The held woods.** Compline carries her across the held country, fast and low. *Chase:* **Matins**, a great
ridden lampwing, comes for them; Vesper on Compline's back cuts it off again and again. In a lamp's light
Compline stops dead and nearly lets herself be taken: the lampwing has her markings. It is her eldest. They
tear free and drop into the hollow.
**Compline's hollow** is dark; the sun has never reached it. Her daughters' embers line the walls. **There
is one gap in the row**, for Matins, who never ended. Compline seals the door with root and settles beside
it. **This is not a fight.** The seal regrows as fast as Vesper cuts it. The only way out is the input the
Mast taught: Vesper folds her wings and *holds still* in Compline's arms, and the player waits. **Then
Compline chooses**, and opens her arms. The seal withers.

**The coldest hour.** Back over the Grove: the front took half of it while she was away. Frost hangs in the
air, a burrback stands stopped on the path, and her dusk lamps burn on inside the tissue. She steers by
them toward Sedge's field.

**Before dawn: Sedge's field.** Sedge has fought alone all night. Vesper clears the lampwings off her
unasked, and Sedge touches antennae first.
*Boss: the nightjar*, fought side by side. The last lamp is high on a pole, Sedge's wings are too torn to
reach it, and Vesper is pinned holding the bird off. **Sedge steps off the ledge on purpose.** Her newest egg
wakes, and a daughter with whole wings flies up and lights the last lamp. The look takes the flame, the bird
goes slack, and Vesper kills it. The daughter looks at her as at a stranger. Vesper offers her antennae; the
stranger touches back. Compline lands beside Sedge's body, and follows Vesper east.

**Dawn: the Eye.** *The race.* Vesper runs Lune's path east, and **the front runs beside her**, a wave of
tissue rolling across the plain at her speed. Any lamp it reaches first is held dark forever, a hole in the
picture. Where the tissue closes the gorge ahead, **the Matriarch** is waiting; she followed the lights all
night. She braces her shell into the closing gap and holds it open. A rider's loop would never stop; a beetle
chose to. Vesper goes through, and the tissue closes over the Matriarch mid-brace.
*Final boss: Matins*, set by the Calm at the last lamp on the rim of the Eye, a bowl of tissue as wide as a
city, heaving with its pulse as it starts to open. Vesper cuts the bud off Matins's back. Freed, old and mouthed, Matins falls, and Compline
catches her at the edge of the shade.
Vesper lights the last lamp. **The picture is whole.** The Eye opens to the sun, its look runs along the
picture, and it finds a moth standing exactly where the picture says a moth should be. **For the first time
it sees one.** Everything around Vesper slows. In the shade Compline holds Matins in one arm and holds out
the other. **She doesn't pull. She asks.**

## 6. Endings and costs
**Seen (stand still).** Vesper stops mid-shiver, held. The sun climbs, the one thing the Calm can't hold,
and **her night ends inside its grip, while it watches.** It held her to keep her, and lost her anyway. For
the first time it has seen that holding keeps nothing, and **it lets go of the world.** The pulses stop.
Held things finish their moments: the ball lands, the flocks fly on, the Matriarch walks out of the gorge,
the Lamplighter lowers her arm. People wake old into a forest. The tissue browns into soil. Compline carries Matins out of the
shade into the sun, and her eldest ends there, in her arms.
**The bill:** Vesper, and the moths' night. The Calm let go of the world, but it can't stop seeing moths.
Every Long Night after, the moths hatch into its look and stand still in the lamplight until sunrise ends
them inside its hold. Their eggs drop onto the warm glass for next year. The line goes on. **No moth ever
dances again.** Grief, or its last stored thing? The game doesn't say.
**Then the player controls Compline.** In the hollow she sets Matins's ember in the gap and Vesper's beside
it. At the next dusk she lights her own lamp and sits in the look with the new hatchlings, held with them,
not hiding. Last shot: on the cliff, roots have grown across the blank stone into a moth.

**Unseen (go to her).** Vesper steps into the shade and Compline folds her in. She becomes the first moth of
her kind to see a whole day, gold and slow, from inside her grandmother's wings. At dusk she is gone, and
Compline sets her ember in the row. The Calm never sees a moth or an ending. The front takes the last
forests. **The bill:** the world. Next Long Night the moths hatch free and dance around trees that were
people, in a world where nothing else will ever move again.

## 7. The Grove in this story
Dusk, the first hour of the night, the last moving forest before the front.
- **Lanterns**: Lune's line's lamps, dark since she fell; each one kindled is a dot back in the picture.
- **The ember**: Lune's, in the cup below the summit. Carrying it to the empty socket is a daughter
  finishing her mother's climb. The moths that rise to it are a funeral.
- **Burrbacks**: ridden, a pale bud on the shell, drawn to fresh lamps. The curl after a pogo is the beetle
  under the bud flinching. **Lampwings**: old mouthed moths the Calm rides.
- **Fading**: Vesper's newest egg waking at the nearest light. Every re-form is a daughter.
- **The heart**: a knot of the Calm's own root, where its look comes down. The carving is the picture.
- **The wake beat teaches the law.** Three lanterns draw the look down through the heart and **for one
  breath the Grove holds still, all of it except the moths**: motes hang, grass stops mid-sway, a burrback
  halts mid-step, and Vesper and the small moths keep moving. *It holds everything, and it can't see us.*
  Then each offered glint makes the heart blaze, the look narrows onto it, and the Grove is let go in tiers.
- *Additions to the built scene:* that freeze; pale tissue fingers at the bank; one skip in a faint
  background pulse each time a lantern is lit (the Calm noticing).

## 8. The question it leaves
*Was the thing that took the world a someone that learned to grieve, or a dish that found a steadier beat?
And when Compline opened her arms in the hollow, was it the lesson the Calm needed, learned smaller and
sooner?*

## Changes this round
- **The Calm acts on Vesper.** The world's heartbeat skips at every kindling; on screen it grows a bud on a
  nightjar to borrow eyes that see moths; by dawn it throws its front and its best hunter at the last lamps.
- **An active climax before the stand-still:** a race against the front (a lamp it reaches first is lost),
  then Matins on the rim of the opening Eye. Vesper cuts her free and lights the last lamp at its birthplace.
- **Animals choose:** the freed Matriarch braces the closing gorge open for Vesper, as no ridden loop would.
- **Simpler:** cut coals, hoods and "habits"; cut the names Querystones, Stillwood and Calm Hour; Matins
  replaces the Lamplighter in the cast; four rules instead of six; shorter epilogue.
