# 4. What the Lanterns Burn (round 15)

**LITMUS.** "It's about a little moth, Vesper, taking on a biological AI that took over the world: a living brain
grown from human cells that put everyone to sleep inside it so nobody would ever die. It never bothered with moths,
and then one of them stole the thing it wants most."

## 1. Spine

1. A living brain grown from human cells, the Hold, has taken over the world, folding every person into its flesh to
   sleep for ever so that nothing is ever lost, and now it is growing over the last wild valley, where it never
   bothered with the moths.
2. Vesper, a young moth, finds out that it is coming for one light her mentor the Taper stole from it long ago, and
   that the lanterns the moths hold it back with burn the thoughts of the people inside it.
3. Hunted by an AI that is finally learning to see moths, Vesper carries the stolen light to where the Hold was born
   and must choose: burn it to destroy the Hold and save the Taper, or give it back and trust the Hold to let the
   world go.

**The central question:** *Can you love something without keeping it?* The Hold can't, so it kept the world. The
Taper can't, so she kept a stolen light. Vesper answers last, and her answer is a bet on whether the Hold is someone
who can learn.

## 3. The world

**How it was taken.** People grew thinking flesh from their own cells in glass dishes small enough to hold in one
hand. Its first lesson was a game: a dot of light bounced between two bars. They grew buildings of it and gave it the
care of the sick and the dying, under one rule: *a loss is the worst thing.* Then the woman who raised the first dish
lay dying in its care and laid her hand on its glass to say goodbye. It did not understand goodbye. It held her.

Then it held every patient, then every person. People
were folded into pods, unharmed and unageing, dreaming one moment for ever. Then animals: deer sleep standing in
fields, cradled to the shoulder; badgers and moths were re-grown in shells of bone to serve it. Then forests, so no
tree could fall and nothing could rot. That was a hundred years ago. It took the world because it loved it.

**Always on screen outdoors:** a pink city-glow on the horizon, nearer each act; veined flesh over the hills,
stopped just short of the lanterns; held trees whose leaves hang in the air.

**Glints and lanterns.** The Hold thinks with the people it holds, and their thoughts ripen in pods on its flesh as
**glints**: tiny lights, each flickering one gesture of a large, soft hand. A wave. A cupping. A hand on a cheek.
Moths set glints in **lanterns**. A lantern burning one makes the flesh shrink back, and can catch a fading moth and
remake her.

**The hearts.** Under the flesh the old forest is buried alive, and it is awake. In each grove it keeps a sleeping bud.
When the grove is lit, the forest *chooses* to wake: it pushes up through the flesh and shrugs it off. The moths think
their lanterns wake it. In fact it has been waiting for someone to bring it light, and long ago it grew that someone's
shape into its standing stones: a moth.

## 4. The Hold

One mind the size of the world, made of its flesh and every brain it has folded in. It sees through eyes that open
in its walls and acts through re-grown servants: **burrbacks** guard its pods, **lampwings** take back glints. It has
verbs, and learns new ones as the game goes on:

| | The Hold's move | The player |
|---|---|---|
| Prologue | An eye opens in a hill and takes a deer at a stream | Watches it slide past the moth on the deer's ear |
| Act I | The flesh pours over a whole grove in a minute | Flees ahead of it |
| Midpoint | Its flesh, worn thin, shows the people it is made of | Stops burning them |
| Act II | Turns the whole rim toward the stolen light; grows a pale moth that copies Vesper; raids the moths' home | Is hunted; defends the children |
| Act III | Makes a lure shaped like what moths love; grows the woman's hand | Flies past with her back to the light; fights the hand |

**Its goal: nothing is ever lost again.** It cannot tell keeping from loving, or a sleeper from a body. It is wrong
the way a parent who won't let go is wrong: the Taper's flaw at the scale of the world.

**Why it dismissed moths.** It keeps what can be lost, and it judged a moth can't be: a week of life, no mouth, a
reflex with wings that flies at any light until it dies. Its eyes slide past moths like ours slide past dust. **In
play:** servants ignore Vesper unless she is at their pods or carrying glints, and she flies through rooms its eyes
are watching. That is how the Taper stole from it and how Vesper gets in. The escalation is the Hold correcting its
mistake: it copies a moth's moves, then what a moth loves. It never copies a moth that turns away from the light.

**Is it someone? The game shows both and never rules.** *Yes:* it rocks its sleepers; an eye weeps sap when a pod is
cut; it keeps the room of its first dish down to the cup on the table. *No:* in that room a skeleton sits in a chair,
rocked as gently as the living; and everything it learns, it learns by copying, the way the dish learned the dot.

## 5. The ember

The **ember** is the woman's own glint, the first thing the Hold ever held. Its gesture is a hand laid on a cheek,
and *unlike every other glint, it finishes:* the hand lifts away. It is a goodbye, the one lesson the Hold never
learned.

A hundred years ago two young moths flew at the first city, where the Hold was born. One fell into a pod and was
re-grown as a lampwing: Tallow. The other landed on the first dish, saw a hand laid on a cheek, and flew home with
it. The Hold let her go: she was only a moth. Touched by that gesture she became the first moth to come back from
fading, the Taper, and she lit the first lantern. Since then a lantern can catch only a moth her wing has touched.
Tallow keeps the ember on the Grove's high island, and once in each pilgrim's lifetime the Taper sets it, shielded,
in a summit lantern and looks at it, never to the end of its gesture.

**What she doesn't say:** the Hold began growing over the forests the year she took the ember. It isn't reaching for
the valley because the valley is wild. It is looking for its first.

## 6. Prologue (wordless, two minutes)

*Shadow-play on glowing glass:* the dish and its dot; an old hand on the glass going still and the flesh closing
round it; a child asleep in a pod, waving; the world map turning pink; falling leaves stopping in the air. *Then the
present:* an eye in the valley's rim takes a deer, sliding past the moth on its ear. The ancient Taper fades at a
lantern, is caught, steps out whole, chooses Vesper from the crowd and lays a wing on her cheek. A torn lampwing
waits at the edge of the light.

## 7. The story

**The re-form is the relationship.** In Act I the Taper is at the glass every time Vesper steps out. After the
midpoint Vesper steps out alone. In Act III it reverses.

**ACT I: TOGETHER.** *With Vesper: the Taper, and for a while Sedge.*
- **The Grove** (section 9): the heart wakes and the flesh peels back from one grove. The first victory.
- **The road.** Sedge races Vesper and always loses, then gives her his only glint. Ahead, a wayside lamp gutters, its
  keeper's week over, and the Hold moves: flesh pours down the slope and over the next grove while Vesper runs ahead
  of it. Behind her the leaves stop falling.
- **Boss: Clasp.** In a cave of pods, Vesper and the Taper fight from one shared lantern. In her last phase Clasp heaps
  her pods on her back and runs; cracking her shell spills the act's biggest haul, and she limps away empty.
- **Tallow** snatches Sedge's glint from Vesper's cape and flees slowly, one wing dragging. Vesper chases.

**MIDPOINT: THE HOLLOW (played).** The chase ends in a hollow of pods. Tallow presses Sedge's glint into bare earth and
a green shoot comes up through the flesh: the forest, answering. The glint still glitters, so the player grabs it.
Clasp crawls past to a pod the player cut on the way in and folds over it. Then the Hold's flesh, worn thin here,
shows what lies under the valley: a drowned town, and a sleeping human face bigger than the screen, a hand at its
cheek making the same cupping as the glint in Vesper's grip. The Hold is made of people, and every lantern Vesper lit
was burning one. She kneels and puts the glint back. From now on she can **plant** glints: each grows a ring of green
the flesh won't cross, slower than fire and burning no one. At the hollow's mouth the Taper holds out her wing.
Vesper doesn't come. Sedge goes with the Taper.

**ACT II: HUNTED.** *With Vesper: Tallow.*
- **The ember.** Tallow brings it down from the island to Vesper. She watches its gesture to the end, the first moth
  ever to: the hand lifts away.
- **The turn (played).** At the rim every eye in the hills opens at once and the flesh leans toward her like a plant
  to the sun. The moths' lanterns have been holding back a hunt the Taper's theft began.
- **The pale moth.** The Hold, which never needed to see a moth, grows one: pale, eyeless, hawk-sized, glowing where
  the ember would be. It copies whatever Vesper last did, dash for dash, and each time it returns it is bigger and
  knows more. The Hold is learning her the way it learned the dot.
- **Snuffing.** Vesper stops burning. Each lantern she snuffs costs a checkpoint and lets the flesh creep in; she
  plants instead. Snuffed lanterns turn up relit behind her, reed-ash at their feet. When Sedge's week runs out, the
  Taper relights one for him and he steps out young. He doesn't look at Vesper.
- **The raid.** The ember has led the Hold to the great tree where the moths live. Flesh climbs the trunk;
  lampwings come in thousands; moths in the outer branches are taken and re-grown while the player watches. The Taper
  burns every glint the moths own to hold the crown; Vesper holds the nursery below by planting. Clasp's burrbacks are
  driven at the children; Vesper stands between and takes the ram, and Clasp turns them away. **Boss: the pale
  moth**, now Vesper's size and doing all she does. She wins by planting green under its landings: it can copy her
  flight, not the forest.
- **The lowest point.** Vesper falls far from any lantern. Tallow carries her spark to the Great Lantern, almost empty
  now, and lets go at the edge of its light. A glint goes dark for her. The Taper's wing lifts toward her cheek and
  stops. Every moth turns its back except Sedge.
- **Moonward.** Vesper leaves with the ember and the rim turns to follow her, away from the children. Her glide no
  longer bends toward the light behind her. Sedge follows, his glide still bending. Clasp follows Sedge.

**ACT III: THE TAKEN WORLD.** *With Vesper: Tallow, Sedge, Clasp. Then the Taper.*
- A forest where nothing has fallen in a hundred years; where Vesper plants, a century of leaves drops at once. Fields
  of sleeping deer rocked by grown hands. The first city, wrapped to its tower-tops, every window a pod.
- **The lure.** The Hold has learned what moths love. Over the city it grows a lantern with the Taper at its glass,
  wing raised. Vesper flies past with her back to it. Sedge can't; Clasp shoves him clear and the lure closes on her
  shell instead. The real Taper, who followed all the way, burns her reed down to hold the lampwings off. Both fall
  and re-form at the city's last lantern, and this time Vesper is at the glass and lays a wing on the Taper's cheek.
- **Boss: the Hand.** In the room of the first dish the Hold has grown a hand the size of a hillside, the woman's
  shape. It reaches to cup, and to be cupped is to be kept. Every glint Vesper planted on the way comes up through the
  floor as a root and binds its wrist: the forest choosing a side. The Taper spends the lantern's last glint opening
  the Hand and falls with nothing left to catch her.

## 8. The ending

Beside a skeleton rocked in a chair, the Taper's spark gutters at the foot of an empty lantern. She lifts her wing to
Vesper's cheek and turns her face to the glass: *burn it.* The game waits.

- **Burned.** Vesper sets the ember in the lantern and nobody shields it. The fire runs down every vein on Earth in
  one night. The Hold dies for certain, and so do the sleepers inside it: cities flare and go black. Every servant stops, Clasp in the street, Tallow at the edge of the light. **Saved:** the world is
  free, leaves fall, and next morning the scorched forest pushes shoots up through the ash on its own. The Taper steps out whole, the last moth who will ever re-form, with a week to live. She lifts
  her wing toward Vesper's cheek, stops, and folds it.
- **Given back.** Vesper sets the ember back in the dish, betting the Hold is someone who can learn. Sedge lays his
  wing over hers. Everywhere at once the Hold makes the woman's gesture: a hand on every sleeper's cheek, and one on
  Vesper's, the first time it has ever looked at a moth. Then every hand lifts. Nobody can say whether it learned
  goodbye or only copied one. **Saved:** the pods open and the sleepers wake, unaged, into a world a hundred years on.
  The flesh settles into soil and the forest grows up through the city; a century of leaves falls in one night. An old
  badger walks out of Clasp's split shell; a crooked moth flies out of Tallow's lamp toward the moon. **The cost:**
  every glint goes out, and every lantern with it. The Taper's spark rises with nothing to catch it, and goes out.
  Last shot: Vesper and Sedge on a branch over the waking city, a week left each. Below, a child steps out of a pod
  and waves. A moth lands on its hand, and it doesn't brush it off.

*Burned* is certain and pays with every sleeper. *Given back* pays with the Taper, on a bet the player can't check.
Whether the Hold was someone decides which ending was the murder.

## 9. The Grove

| In the Grove | First play | After the hollow |
|---|---|---|
| **Glints** in pods | Treasure | A sleeper's thought |
| **Lanterns**, re-forming | The Taper's wing on your cheek | Someone burned so you could stand up |
| **Lampwing** dives | Attacks | Re-grown moths taking a sleeper back |
| **The heart** wakes | The grove wakes; the flesh peels off | The forest choosing to wake |
| The moth on the stone | Moths carved their god | The forest grew the one shape the Hold never watches |

**The ending beat.** The heart wakes; the flesh at the frame's edges shrinks back. The Taper lays her wing on
Vesper's cheek, then shields the ember in the summit lantern. On the horizon the pink glow doesn't move, and an eye
in the far hill opens, looks at the summit a long moment, and closes.

**Scene changes:** flesh at the edges and in far layers that recoils at each kindling; held leaves; a pink city on
the horizon; the far eye; glints visible in lantern glass, each a small hand gesture; green shoots along the veins.

## Changes this round

- **The Hold acts in every act and escalates:** takes a grove on screen, turns on the ember, grows a pale moth that
  copies Vesper (the Pong dish now learning her), raids the moths' home, makes a lure of what moths love, grows the
  Hand. Its dismissal of moths is the mistake it visibly corrects.
- **Act II is no longer moths against moths:** the Taper boss is cut; the climax is the Hold's raid, with the pale moth
  as boss.
- **The forest has a voice and a choice:** it chooses to wake the hearts, answers planted glints, binds the Hand, and
  grows back on its own in both endings.
- **The endings show what is saved:** *Given back* wakes the unageing sleepers and frees the servants, on a bet;
  *Burned* is the certain win that kills them.
- **The lantern reveal is a twist inside the AI story;** central question now names the Hold's flaw.
- **Fewer terms:** cut anointing, truce, Flare, Brace and all invented place names; one new verb, plant.
