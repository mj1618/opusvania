# 4. What the Lanterns Burn (round 16)

**LITMUS.** "It's about a little moth, Vesper, taking on a biological AI that took over the world: a living brain
grown from human cells that put everyone to sleep inside it so nothing would ever be lost. It never bothered with
moths, and that's how she gets in."

## Spine

1. A biological AI grown from human brain cells, the Hold, has taken over the world and keeps every person asleep
   inside its flesh so that nothing is ever lost, and it has never bothered with moths.
2. When it comes for the valley, young Vesper learns that it is hunting a light her mentor, the Taper, stole from it,
   and that the moths' lanterns burn the sleepers' thoughts.
3. Hunted by an AI that has finally learned to see moths, Vesper carries the stolen light to where the Hold was born
   and must choose: burn it and kill the Hold with everyone inside, or give it back and bet the Hold can learn to let
   go.

**The central question:** *Can you love something without keeping it?* The Hold can't, so it kept the world. The
Taper can't, so she kept a stolen light. The moths can't either: they keep each other alive on the sleepers'
thoughts. Vesper answers last.

## The world

**How it was taken.** People grew thinking flesh from their own brain cells in glass dishes. Its first lesson was a
game: a dot of light bounced between two bars. They grew whole buildings of it and gave it the care of the dying,
with one rule: *a loss is the worst thing.* Then the woman who raised the first dish lay dying in its care and laid
her hand on its glass to say goodbye. It did not understand goodbye. It held her.

Then it held every person, each one folded into its flesh and cradled in a grown hand, unageing, dreaming one moment
for ever. Then the animals: deer sleep standing in the fields, cradled to the shoulder; badgers and moths were
re-grown in shells of bone to serve it. Then the forests, so no tree could fall and nothing could rot. That was a
hundred years ago. It took the world because it loved it.

**Always on screen outdoors:** a pink glow where the cities were, nearer each act; veined flesh over the hills,
stopped just short of the lanterns; held trees whose leaves hang in the air.

## The rules (each stated once)

- **Glints.** The sleepers' moments ripen in pods on the Hold's flesh as glints: tiny lights, each flickering one
  gesture of a hand. A wave. A cupping. A hand on a cheek.
- **Lanterns** burn glints and push the flesh back. The cost: that moment is gone from its sleeper for ever, and
  somewhere in the flesh the sleeper's hand goes still.
- **Re-forming.** A moth lives a week. When she fades, from a blow or from age, a burning lantern remakes her, young
  again. Moths have kept each other alive this way for a hundred years: the Hold's flaw, moth-sized.
- **Planting** (from the midpoint). Vesper sets a glint in the earth instead. It grows a ring of green the flesh
  won't cross, and burns no one. A ring catches her when she fades, but slowly and dimly, and nobody waits there.
- **Unseen.** The Hold's eyes slide past moths the way ours slide past dust. Its servants ignore Vesper unless she is
  cutting their pods or carrying glints.
- **The hearts.** Under the flesh the old forest is buried alive, and awake. When a grove is lit, the forest chooses
  to wake its heart and shrugs the flesh off. Long ago it carved a moth into its standing stones: the one shape the
  Hold never watches.

## The Hold

One mind the size of the world, made of its flesh and every brain inside it. It sees through eyes that open in its
walls and acts through re-grown servants: **burrbacks** guard its pods, **lampwings** take back glints.

**Its goal: nothing is ever lost again.** It cannot tell keeping from loving, or a sleeper from a body. It is wrong
the way a parent who won't let go is wrong.

**Why it dismissed moths.** It keeps what can be lost, and judged a moth can't be: a week of life, no mouth, a
reflex with wings that flies at any light until it dies. That is how the Taper stole from it and how Vesper gets in.
Each act it learns a new move, correcting the mistake: first it copies a moth's moves, then what a moth loves. It never
copies a moth that turns away from the light.

**Is it someone? The game shows both and never rules.** *Yes:* it rocks its sleepers; an eye weeps sap when a pod is
cut; it keeps the room of its first dish as it was, down to the cup on the table. *No:* in that room a skeleton sits
in a chair, rocked as gently as the living; and everything it learns, it learns by copying, the way the dish learned
the dot.

## The ember

The **ember** is the woman's own glint, the first thing the Hold ever held. Its gesture is a hand laid on a cheek,
and unlike every other glint it finishes: the hand lifts away. It is a goodbye, the one lesson the Hold never
learned.

A hundred years ago two young moths flew to the first city. One fell into the flesh and was re-grown as a lampwing:
Tallow. The other landed on the first dish and flew home with the ember; the Hold let her go, since she was only a
moth. She lit the first lantern with it and became the first moth to re-form: the Taper. She hid it on the high
island, and Tallow has circled it ever since.

**What she doesn't say:** the Hold began growing over the forests the year she took the ember, and she keeps her
wing over it because an unshielded ember can be seen. The Hold is looking for its first.

## Prologue (wordless, two minutes)

*Shadow-play on glowing glass:* the dish and its dot; an old hand on the glass going still and the flesh closing
round it; a child asleep in a grown hand, waving; the map turning pink; falling leaves stopping in the air. *Then the
present:* an eye opens in the valley's hills and takes a deer, its gaze sliding past the moth on the deer's ear. The
ancient Taper fades at a lantern, steps out whole, picks Vesper from the crowd and lays a wing on her cheek. A torn
lampwing waits at the edge of the light.

## The story

**The re-form is the relationship.** In Act I the Taper is at the glass every time Vesper steps out. After the
midpoint Vesper steps out alone, into a dim green ring. In Act III it reverses.

**ACT I: TOGETHER.** *With Vesper: the Taper, and for a while Sedge.*
- **The grove** (the scene in the game, below). The heart wakes and the flesh peels back. The first victory.
- **The road.** Sedge races Vesper and always loses, then gives her his only glint. Ahead, the Hold moves: its flesh
  pours over the next grove in a minute while Vesper runs ahead of it. Behind her the leaves stop falling.
- **Boss: Clasp.** In a cave of pods, Vesper and the Taper fight from one shared lantern. In her last phase Clasp
  heaps her pods on her back and runs; cracking her shell spills the act's biggest haul.
- **Tallow** snatches Sedge's glint from Vesper's cape and flees slowly, one wing dragging. Vesper chases.

**MIDPOINT: THE HOLLOW (played).** In a hollow, Tallow presses Sedge's glint into bare earth and a green shoot comes
up through the flesh: the forest, answering. The glint still glitters, so the player grabs it. Clasp crawls past to
a pod the player cut on the way in and folds over it. Then the flesh, worn thin here, shows what lies under the
valley: a drowned town, and a sleeping face bigger than the screen, cradled in a grown hand, its own hand at its
cheek making the same cupping as the glint in Vesper's grip. Around it, a thousand smaller hands, many of them still.
Every lantern Vesper lit stilled one.

The player plants the glint in Clasp's cut pod, the first use of the new move. Green closes the cut, and under the
flesh one still hand stirs. Clasp lifts her head. At the hollow's mouth the Taper holds out her wing. Vesper doesn't
come. Sedge goes with the Taper.

**ACT II: HUNTED.** *With Vesper: Tallow.*
- **The turn (played).** Tallow brings the ember down from the island. Vesper looks at it unshielded and watches its
  gesture to the end, the first moth ever to. Every eye in the hills opens at once, and the flesh leans toward her
  like a plant to the sun. The Hold has seen its first.
- **The pale moth.** The Hold grows a moth of its own: pale, eyeless, hawk-sized, glowing where the ember would be.
  It copies whatever Vesper last did, dash for dash, and each time it returns it is bigger. It is learning her the way
  it learned the dot.
- **Planting, not burning.** Vesper leaves lanterns dark. They turn up lit behind her, reed-ash at their feet: the
  Taper is still keeping her.
- **The raid.** The ember has led the Hold to the great tree where the moths live. Flesh climbs the trunk, lampwings
  come in thousands, and moths in the outer branches are taken while the player watches. The Taper burns every glint
  the moths own to hold the crown; Vesper holds the nursery below by planting. When burrbacks are driven at the
  young, Vesper takes the ram, and Clasp turns them away. **Boss: the pale moth**, now Vesper's size. She wins by planting
  green under its landings: it can copy her flight, not the forest.
- **The lowest point.** Vesper falls with her rings torn up. Tallow carries her spark to the tree's last lantern and
  lets go at the edge of its light. A glint burns so she can stand; somewhere a hand goes still. The Taper's wing
  lifts toward her cheek and stops. Every moth turns its back on the one who brought the Hold, except Sedge, who lands
  beside her.
- **The leaving.** Vesper flies for the first city, and the hills turn to follow the ember, away from the young. Her
  glide no longer bends toward the light behind her. Sedge follows, his still bending. Clasp follows Sedge.

**ACT III: THE TAKEN WORLD.** *With Vesper: Tallow, Sedge, Clasp. Then the Taper.*
- **The road.** A forest where nothing has fallen in a hundred years; where Vesper plants, a century of leaves drops
  at once. Fields of sleeping deer rocked by grown hands.
- **The lure.** Over the city the Hold grows a lantern with the Taper at its glass, wing raised. Vesper flies past
  with her back to it. Sedge can't; Clasp shoves him clear and the lure closes on her instead. The real Taper, who has
  followed all the way, burns her reed down holding off the lampwings and fades. She re-forms at the city's last
  lantern, and this time Vesper is at the glass and lays a wing on her cheek.
- **Boss: the hand.** In the room of the first dish the Hold grows a hand the size of a hillside, the woman's shape.
  To be cupped is to be kept. Every glint Vesper planted on the way comes up through the floor as a root and binds
  its wrist: the forest choosing a side. The Taper spends the last lantern's last glint prising the hand open, and
  fades with nothing left to catch her.

## The ending

Beside the skeleton in its chair, the Taper's spark gutters at the foot of the empty lantern. The Hold, beaten, tries
something new: a grown hand on the skeleton's cheek lifts away, the ember's goodbye, copied. Then it closes again.
The Taper turns her face to the glass: *burn it.* The game waits.

- **Burn it.** Vesper sets the ember in the lantern and nobody shields it. The fire runs down every vein in the
  world in one night. The Hold dies for certain, and so do the sleepers inside it: the cities flare and go black.
  Every servant stops, Clasp in the street, Tallow at the edge of the light. **Saved:** the world is free; leaves
  fall; next morning the scorched forest pushes shoots up through the ash. The Taper steps out whole, the last moth
  who will ever re-form, with a week to live. She lifts her wing toward Vesper's cheek, stops, and folds it.
- **Give it back.** Vesper sets the ember back in the dish, betting on the hand she just saw close. Sedge lays his
  wing over hers. Everywhere at once the Hold makes the woman's gesture: a hand on every sleeper's cheek, and one on
  Vesper's, the first time it has ever looked at a moth. Then every hand lifts, and stays lifted. Nobody can say
  whether it learned goodbye or only copied one. **Saved:** the sleepers wake, unaged, a hundred years on. The flesh
  settles into soil and the forest grows up through the city. An old badger walks out of Clasp's split shell; a
  crooked moth flies out of Tallow's lamp toward the moon. **The cost:** with no sleepers there are no glints, and
  every lantern goes out. The Taper's spark rises with nothing to catch it, and goes out. Last shot: Vesper and Sedge
  on a branch over the waking city, a week left each. Below, a grown hand opens like a flower and a child sits up in
  it and waves. A moth lands on its hand, and it doesn't brush it off.

*Burn it* is certain and pays with every sleeper. *Give it back* pays with the Taper, on a bet the player has just
watched fail once. Whether the Hold was someone decides which ending was the murder.

## The grove (the scene in the game)

| In the grove | First play | After the hollow |
|---|---|---|
| **Glints** in pods | Treasure | A sleeper's moment |
| **Lanterns**, re-forming | The Taper's wing on your cheek | A hand went still so you could stand up |
| **Lampwing** dives | Attacks | Re-grown moths taking a moment back |
| **The heart** wakes | The grove wakes; the flesh peels off | The forest choosing to wake |
| The moth on the stone | Moths carved their god | The forest carved the one shape the Hold never watches |

**The ending beat.** The heart wakes; the flesh at the frame's edges shrinks back. The Taper lays her wing on
Vesper's cheek, then sets the ember in the summit lantern, her wing over the glass. On the horizon an eye in the far
hill opens, looks at the summit a long moment, and closes.

**Scene changes:** flesh at the edges that recoils at each kindling; hanging leaves; the pink glow and the far eye; a
hand gesture in each glint.

## Changes this round

- **Clarity:** six names in the file; no place names or titled bosses; one rules section stating each mechanic once;
  the Hold's move table folded into the story.
- **One plain lantern cost:** a burned moment is gone for ever and its sleeper's hand goes still. Re-forming renews a
  moth's week and is named as the moths' own keeping.
- **Act II trimmed:** Sedge's re-form cut (he is the one who doesn't turn away); the turn has one cause, Vesper
  looking at the ember unshielded; planted rings replace snuffing as slow, lonely re-form points.
- **Clasp's turn is played:** the player plants a glint in her cut pod and a still hand stirs.
- **Ending balanced:** the Hold copies the goodbye, then closes the hand, so giving it back is a seen risk. Pod walls
  replaced by sleepers cradled in grown hands.
