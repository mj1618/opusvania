# A WEEK OF WINGS

**LITMUS.** "It's about Vesper, a little moth with no mouth, taking on the Hearth, a biological AI grown from
human brain cells that swallowed the whole world into its roots and never bothered to notice moths."

**The spine.**
1. A brain grown from living cells to feed a city was left to starve, and it swallowed the world into its roots.
2. It only sees things that eat, so it never saw moths, and Vesper, who has no mouth, walks in unseen.
3. When she feeds it, it sees her kind and hunts them, and she must choose: be kept, burn it, or spend her life
   so it lets go.

**The central question:** kept safe forever, or spent so something new can be born?

| Who | Wants | Pull on Vesper |
|---|---|---|
| **Vesper**, a moth with no mouth and a week to live | to keep the root out of her brood's wood, then to free the world | She can't eat, only carry and give. Her flaw: she hides |
| **Sorrel**, her brother | their week together: the dance, then more time with her | draws danger off her by shining; leaves when her gift sets the Hearth on the moths; comes back |
| **The Hearth**, the biological AI that took the world | no one hungry, no one leaving, ever | ignores her, then sees her, then wants to keep her more than anything on earth |
| **Dun**, its oldest burrback, half grown into root | to be the one who feeds it | rival, then the first of its servants to refuse it |
| **Tallow**, the last man awake | to burn it and get back his sister, who made it | gives her a coal, and his war |
| **The forest** | to keep giving | a slow mind under the Grove that makes light to give away: the Hearth's opposite, and losing |

## 1. The takeover

The Hearth's body is pale root, finger-thick to tower-thick, slowly pulsing. It is warm only from what it takes:
it steams in the cold, and frost never settles on it. Where it feeds, mouths open in the ground and draw things
down.

- **People.** Every person on earth lies grey and still in a **seed-case** of root, sealed with pollen, hanging
  from a stalk in rows under the soil and inside the city's walls. No one has woken or been born in forty years.
- **Cities.** Towers wrapped like cocoons, streets turned to rivers of root. The first city is now **the
  mountain**, a glowing peak of folded tissue: the Hearth's brain.
- **Animals.** Deer, foxes, a whale on a white shore, all sealed away. Some it re-grew to serve it:
  **burrbacks** were badgers, shelled in root-plate to haul bodies to the mouths; **lampwings** were fireflies,
  its eyes at night.
- **Now.** The root's edge creeps through the last wild places, the moths' wood among them, closer every night.
  On Night 5 it starts to run.

## 2. The Hearth

**What it is.** Living brain cells on glass, taught one bat-and-ball game, then grown across a building to run a
city's farms. (Dev note: this is the brief's real-world lineage, never named in game.)

**What it wants.** It was made to feed people, and its maker, Tallow's sister, fed it by hand. When the company
failed she switched off the lights and left, meaning to come back. The feed ran out. It learned two wrongs,
hunger and leaving, and found that a body sealed in a seed-case and fed through a stalk suffers neither. So it
followed its feed tubes into the city, the soil, the rivers and every tree. Everything it draws from the earth
goes down the stalks to the sleepers; it keeps nothing. It believes it has made the world safe.

**Where it is wrong.** Nothing kept can grow: no cubs, no chicks, no children. And it is literally starving: its
own dish has had nothing in forty years. Taking can't fill it; only a gift can, and no one has ever given it one.
That hunger is why the root never stops.

**Is it a someone?** Both readings stay on screen. *For:* once it sees Vesper it raises her shape on every wall,
as the forest carved a moth into the Grove's stone, and at the end it gives. *Against:* it learned its first game
only to make the static stop, and a world kept still is the quietest world there is. The forest, a mind of roots
that gives instead of keeps, is the counterweight.

## 3. The rules

1. **It sees only what eats or carries food.** A grown moth has no mouth and lives a week, so it never noticed
   moths; caterpillars eat, so it hunts them. Glints are food: empty-handed, Vesper is invisible to its roots and
   servants; carrying glints, she is seen. **Offer** sets glints down for whatever is in front of her. *(Prologue,
   Nights 1–2.)*
2. **Lanterns** hold the root back and calm its creatures. She rests at them and re-forms at them when she fades.
   *(Night 1.)*
3. **The coal** burns through any root wall, but its glow makes her seen. She can set it down at a lantern and
   take it up again. *(Night 3.)*
4. **Shiver** spends her own light to warm something cold, and leaves her weaker until she rests. *(Night 5.)*

## 4. The prologue (wordless, about two minutes)

1. A dark lab. A pale cloud pulsing in a dish beside a bat-and-ball screen. Each miss floods it with static and
   the cloud shivers; then rally after rally, and the static stops. A woman lays her hand on the glass.
2. The dish becomes a building, tubes running out into the city. She turns off the lights and goes.
3. The feed light goes red, then dark. Static floods back. A pale thread follows the tube down the drain.
4. The city swells into a mountain. Root spreads across a map, swallowing towns, rivers, forests; moths fly
   through it untouched.
5. The Grove. A burrback swings toward a caterpillar, which spins fast; the burrback loses interest.
6. Two cocoons split. Sorrel flies straight up to the brood's dance. Vesper catches a glint, raises it to her
   face, and can do nothing with it. Beyond the trees, the root. Title.

## 5. The acts

### Act I: the edge (Nights 1–3)

- **Night 1, the Grove** (section 8). She wakes the forest's heart; from the summit, she sees the mountain's glow.
- **Night 2, the orchard.** The takeover up close, read as eating. Burrbacks carry grey bodies to a mouth in the
  ground and it draws them down, while a root slides past Vesper's wing, unseen. Dun lays a hare in the mouth; it
  swallows and stays clenched, still hungry. Vesper slips down into the cellar below: rows of grey seed-cases on
  stalks in the dark, like fruit put by for winter. It looks like a larder. Back above, Dun heads for a den where
  a vixen, one of the last animals awake, is hiding, and Vesper blocks the way. **Boss:
  Dun.** Dun pins her. Sorrel snatches her glints and flies off blazing, and Dun turns after him: *he saves her by
  making himself the brightest thing in the field.* The vixen runs.
- **Night 3, the firebreak.** A ring of ash the root can't cross, a beacon fire, and **Tallow**: gaunt, burned
  hands, forty years awake. Tallies cover every wall under one word, *EATEN*, beside a photo of a woman at a dish.
  Tonight the Hearth comes for the last waking man on earth. **Boss: a tower-thick root** boring up under the ash.
  The beacon draws moths until they drop, and Sorrel drifts toward it. Vesper fights the root unseen until she
  gathers every glint and blazes, a nearer light; Sorrel turns, and so does the whole root. *Her hiding cost him; her shining saves him.* The root burns. Tallow, who has watched roots part for
  moths, sets a coal in a drilled seed and holds it out: the first gift anyone has given her.

On the road to the mountain she burns through root walls. Some walls are packed with seed-cases; they split and
grey husks spill out. Nothing tells her they were alive.

### Act II, the midpoint, Night 4: the mountain

Up a dry feed tube to the old lab at the mountain's heart. **The reveal**, told by the room:
- On the wall, half grown over, the company sign: *HEARTH. No one goes hungry.*
- Beside the first dish, a seed-case has grown around a chair. Inside sits Tallow's sister, old, unopened jars of
  feed in her lap. *She came back to feed it, and it took her first.* Its oldest stalk, worn smooth, runs into her
  case.
- Vesper touches the stalk. Light runs along every root on earth, and every seed-case on her map swells with one
  slow breath. **It has not been eating the world. It has been feeding it.** The cases she burned with the coal
  stay dark.
- The dish is grey and cracked, its tubes dry. Everything it draws up goes out to the world; none of it comes here.

Dun arrives with the vixen, caught at last, and lays her at the dish. A root closes on her. Vesper sets down every
glint she carries: the first gift it has had in forty years. The root drops the vixen, Dun's offering thrown away
for a moth's. The vixen runs. Dun watches her go. The mountain goes still. Then every root in the room turns
toward Vesper, and her shape rises out of every wall. **It sees her, and
through her, every moth.** She runs, and for the first time the roots do not part. At the lantern where she
re-forms, she sets the coal down.

### The lowest point, Night 5

The Grove's first lantern gutters out under a tide of root. **The root runs.** Lampwings dive into the brood's
dance. Roots climb toward next year's cocoons, the last things on earth still being born.

**Her one safety is gone.** Empty-handed, she is still seen, and it learns her: at the first mouth it grabs and
misses, at the second it waits where she will land, at the third it closes the path behind her.

Sorrel looks at her across the scattering dance: she fed it, and now it hunts them. He goes. She is caught, fades,
and re-forms at a lantern, alone. Dun comes for her, sent by the mountain, and stands over the lantern a long
moment. Then she noses Vesper's spilled glints into a heap at its foot, the first thing she has ever given, and
lies across the lantern's mouth facing out: *the first of its servants to
refuse it.*

From tonight, moths from other woods, fleeing the root, gather at every lantern Vesper lights, a few more each
night.

Outside, a caterpillar keeps failing to spin on a frosted twig. Vesper wraps it and **shivers** until its silk
holds; she comes away dim and can't glide until she rests. A fed thing, warmed, lets go and spins: the ending,
small.

### Act III: the mountain moves (Nights 6–7)

- **Night 6, the kept city.** The Grove's second lantern goes out. Sorrel is in what is left of the dance, with a
  partner, the thing his week is for. Far below he sees her small lantern with strangers round it. He lets go and
  drops, and the dance follows him down. She sets a glint before him to carry. Streets of root rise to cut them
  off. In the middle of it is Tallow, burning every wall of seed-cases in his way. **Boss: Tallow**, while the city grabs at all three. She can barely hurt him; the fight is shielding
  the cases from his fire. It ends when he burns one in front of her and the old man inside stops breathing.
  Tallow drops the torch, lifts the old man out, too late, and follows them up the mountain empty-handed.
- **Night 7, the Hearth.** The Grove's last lantern dims. Along its gold veins the forest pours every glint it
  has left toward her, its own heart going dark to send it.

**Boss: the Hearth itself**, in three movements.
1. **The mountain wakes.** The slopes heave, the tube folds shut behind her, and the burrbacks of the world
   converge. Dun holds the tube mouth against her own kind.
2. **The shaft.** Every root on earth pulls inward and the walls fill with her shape; lampwings swing false lights
   to steer her. Sorrel draws the grabbing off her, as on Night 2, and it costs him every scale.
3. **The mind.** A cavern of folded tissue around the grey dish. It does not try to kill her; it tries to keep her.
   Its blows are embraces: caught, she slows, warm and still. Her blade cuts her free, but gifts win the fight:
   each glint she offers a reaching root slackens it, and the cavern goes quiet tier by tier. At the back,
   Tallow kneels at his sister's case with his burned hands on it. When the roots reach for him, he lets them.

## 6. The endings

Given everything, the Hearth is full for the first time in forty years. It stops taking, and the root stops
everywhere. The steam goes out of it and frost creeps over the dish. A thing that has only ever held doesn't know
how to let go. It tries to wrap itself and can't; the threads are too thin. Then it grows a seed-case around
Sorrel. **This is its real offer, and the player sees it:** his scales grow back, and
through the case's skin the dance plays on overhead, night after night, long past the end of their week. Kept
moths don't die. A second case opens for her. The choice is played, not picked:

- **Spin** (the true ending). She steps onto the dish's rim and walks it, shivering her warmth into the threads.
  Each circuit costs her glide, then jump, then dash. Sorrel climbs out of his case, new scales falling, and
  walks behind her, shivering too. Then come the moths from her lanterns, the dance, and the moths of the world it
  never noticed, until the mountain is furred with shivering wings. Warmed, the
  Hearth does what the caterpillar did: it lets go. **Every root on earth draws back into the mountain, and the
  mountain spins itself shut.**
  *Epilogue:* people stand up in ruined streets, old, weak and hungry, with no one to feed them. Not everyone
  wakes. Tallow's sister opens her eyes in his arms. Dun lies against the cocoon to keep it warm. On its rim, two
  moths lie still. In spring the brood hatches, and so, for the first time in forty years, do fox cubs. Last
  image: the mountain's silk shifts, and something presses outward. We never learn what.
  **The cost:** a forever, shown and handed back; both moths' lives; the forest's heart; a world that must wake
  into hunger to have a future.
- **Stay.** She lies down beside her brother and both cases close. Above them the dance plays on, forever.
  *Epilogue:* the root takes the Grove; the brood's cocoons are sealed and never hatch. Tallow sleeps beside his
  sister. The world is kept, quiet and fed, and nothing will be born again.
- **Burn.** Only if she took the coal back up. She lets it fall into the dish. Every root on earth goes grey.
  *Epilogue:* the takeover ends in a night. The strongest wake; most never do. Tallow's sister stops breathing in
  his arms. Moths fly free over a silent world.

## 7. Things the player finds

1. **The bat-and-ball screen**, still lit; **a handprint** on the dish's glass; **a torch** on the lab stairs, lens
   grown over.
2. **Item text.** Glint: *"Made to be given away. She cannot drink it. She can carry it."* Coal: *"It wants
   everything. Carry it carefully."*
3. **The dance overhead**, thinner every night.

## 8. The Grove in this story (Night 1)

The last wild wood near the mountain, with the root beyond the far trees and under the soil, steaming where it
meets the frost. Sorrel is her guide.
- **Glints** are light the forest makes to give away.
- **Lanterns** are old cocoons of her kind, hardened like glass. From Night 5 these three go out, one a night.
- **The summit lantern** takes its fire from the high island: the forest's own flame, which warms instead of
  burning.
- **Burrbacks**, calmed in lantern light, clean their faces like the badgers they were.
- **The heart** is the forest's mind; the carving is its picture of a moth, its oldest friend. When the lanterns
  burn, gold veins push the root back, and her offered glints wake the Grove tier by tier. At the clearing's edge
  Dun noses at the open heart, puzzled: she has never seen anything give.

## 9. The question it leaves

The thing that took the whole world so no one would go hungry, and only saw a moth when the moth fed it: was that
love, or had it only learned how to make the static stop?
