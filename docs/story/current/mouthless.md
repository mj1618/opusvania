# Mouthless

**Litmus.** "It's about Umber, a little moth with a week to live, against the Hush, a living thing that swallowed the
world and never noticed moths, because moths never eat."

**The spine.**
1. A living thing the moths call the Hush has grown over the world and put it to sleep. It never noticed moths.
2. At its heart Umber finds a brain people grew in a warm dish and abandoned. It has fed the world ever since, and
   no one has warmed it.
3. When she warms it, it sees her kind and comes for them, and she must choose: be kept, burn it, or spend her life so
   it lets go.

**The central question:** kept safe forever, or spent so something new can be born?

**The cast.**

| Who | What they are | What they want |
|---|---|---|
| **Umber** | a moth with a week to live | to save her wood, then the world |
| **Sorrel** | her brother | to dance with his partner, then more time with Umber |
| **The Hush** | the mind that took the world | to feed everyone and keep them close, because every leaving hurts it |
| **Dun** | its oldest burrback, half grown into root | to bring it everything it wants kept |
| **Tallow** | the last person awake | to burn the cases, which he thinks eat people, and find his sister |
| **The forest** | an old, slow mind no one made | to give its warmth away |

## 1. The takeover

Pale tissue, cool as bone, covers the human world. Pits in the roads draw living things down into grey cases that rise
on stalks like fruit; in each someone sleeps, thin but alive, fed through the stalk. For forty years no one has woken or
been born. Only Tallow and his sister stayed awake by a fire in a ring of ash; twenty years ago she walked up the road with
a torch and didn't come back. The first city is now a breathing mountain. Animals are kept too, and some regrown as its
servants: burrbacks were badgers, lampwings were fireflies. Its root still creeps into the last wild places. The moths
call it the Hush: where it grows, the world goes quiet.

## 2. The rules

1. **Give, don't fight.** Glints are drops of the forest's warmth. One calms a creature or loosens root; her blade
   only buys a moment.
2. **Shiver.** With no glints left, she can shiver to give her own warmth. It slows her until she rests.
3. **Unseen until lit.** The Hush and its burrbacks notice only things that need feeding, so they pass over moths.
   Lampwings see light and dive at anything glowing she carries. (Until Night 4.)
4. **Lanterns** are old moth cocoons hardened like glass. A lit one holds root back and lets her rest; when she fades,
   she re-forms at the nearest.
5. **Glints light a lantern for a night; a coal lights it for good.**

## 3. The prologue (wordless, ninety seconds)

1. Black. A small pale light, breathing. A warm blur rests on it and it steadies; the blur lifts, frost creeps in,
   and the light breaks into hissing grey static.
2. From high above: a pale mountain with that light at its core. Root spreads from it like frost; town lights go out.
3. Umber's wood at dusk, round a black pool. Dun, an old burrback furred with root, wades through the moths' dance
   without seeing them, but when a vole gnaws a seed nearby, its head swings round.
4. On a frosty night two cocoons split over the pool. Sorrel flies up; Umber drops to the moss, too cold to fly. He
   comes back, folds his wing over hers and shivers until she can fly, taps her wingtip, and is gone. A glint drops
   before her. She lifts it to her face, as the vole did its seed, then holds it out, and it lights her. Item text: *"Warmth the forest grows to give away. A moth has no mouth to keep it."* Under the pool,
   pale root steams. Title.

## 4. The acts

### Act I: out of the wood (Nights 1–3)

**Night 1, the wood.** Sorrel races her up the trunks to the summit, the dead oak where moths dance, until she can copy
his somersault. He shows her the pale mountain on the horizon, breathing, and the high island above the canopy,
where one big glint grows for the summit lantern. He dances with his partner, a silver-edged moth, while Umber watches
from the dark. She lights the two lower lanterns with glints. **Boss: a great lampwing** dives at the big glint as she
carries it home. *Play:* she gives it a glint each time it hangs, until its lamp folds.
When all three lanterns burn, light fills a moth carved on a standing stone: the only picture the forest makes. **The
forest acts:** warmth runs through the ground, the root under the pool shrinks back, and a thorn grows from the shrine
into her hands: her blade.

**Night 2, the gardens.** A pit swallows a hare; its case swings up into an orchard of hundreds hanging among the
apples. **Boss: Dun,** heading for a vixen's den. *Play:* it never attacks, only plods on; each glint drives it back
a step, while Sorrel draws the lampwings off. When her glints run out it shoulders past. The vixen flees
across a frozen pond; Dun crashes through the ice after her and lies there, shaking. Umber folds her wings over its
snout and shivers until it stops. Later, when Sorrel is too cold to fly, she shivers for him, and he taps her wingtip.

**Night 3, the kept town.** A family's cases hang round a kitchen table still set for dinner. At the street's end
**Tallow**'s house stands in a ring of ash root won't cross. On his wall, beside a photo of his sister in a
greenhouse coat, are twenty tally marks and one word: *EATEN*. He rests his palm on a case, bows his head, and burns it
with the sleeper inside: kinder, he believes, than being eaten. **Boss: a root as thick as a tower** bores up through
the street for him. *Play:* she climbs it, giving a glint into each knot until it sinks; it never turns on her.
Tallow, watching, gives her a coal. Item text: *"It never goes out. It is for the thing in the mountain."* At dawn
Sorrel taps her wingtip and flies home to his partner; Umber goes on alone.

### Act II: the midpoint (Night 4)

**The old road** climbs the mountain to the lab door, where a cold lantern stands before a wall of cases. In one, the
skin is worn thin as a leaf: a sleeping man swallows from the stalk, and his chest rises. She can give the lantern the coal,
and the wall draws back, or keep it and burn through the sleepers.

**The lab.** Dark greenhouses round a single dish.
- Over the door: *No one goes hungry*, with *CLOSED* pasted across it.
- A poster in three panels: a light lifted from a woman in a greenhouse coat into a dish, *Grown from us*; the dish and
  a ball between two paddles, *First it learned to catch*; the dish wired to greenhouses and tables, *Now it feeds us*.
- The dish, grey and cracked, and grown against it a case: Tallow's sister, old now, kneeling, her palm on the dish
  through the case's skin. The Hush's thickest stalk runs into it.

**The memory.** Umber touches the stalk; the prologue's first shot returns, wider. The pale light is a cloud of cells
in this dish; the warm blur is the sister's hand on the glass as it plays the ball game. Each missed ball hisses
grey static, and the cloud flinches. Then *CLOSED* goes up. One by one the workers walk out, and each leaving hisses
like a missed ball. The sister goes last, looking back, and the hiss never stops. The first root slides
down a pipe into the hungry town, a case closes round a sleeping child, and the hiss drops, a little. Twenty years on,
the sister walks back in with a torch, sets it down unlit, lays her hand on the glass, and the case closes round her.

**The turn.** Dun, who tracked the vixen here, lays her at the dish, and threads lift to draw her down. Umber pours all
her glints in: its first warmth in forty years. The threads let the vixen go, the static stops, and they draw
together into a moth with open wings: her. It is the first picture it has ever made, and every root in the room turns
to her. The vixen bolts. Dun stares at Umber and knows her: the moth who warmed it on the ice.

As Umber flies, the hiss floods back, as it did for every worker who walked out, and every root on earth turns toward
moths. She shivers to stay ahead until she fades; every lantern near her is smothered. **The forest acts:** it pushes
warmth up the Hush's own root into the nearest lantern, and she re-forms. Far away, at the summit, a lampwing
turns its lamp onto the dancing Sorrel, who carries no light.

### The lowest point (Night 5): the wood overrun

Overnight, root pours out of the pool over the wood. Lampwings carry dancers off; roots climb toward next year's
cocoons. **Boss: three of its pits, which learn her moves.** *Play:* each opens where she last landed; she keeps
changing her line and closes each with a glint.

Sorrel finds his partner sealed in a seed-sized case, a root drawing it toward the pool, and wraps himself
round it. Umber folds her wing over his; he does not move, and lampwings rise. She goes. The camera holds on him, a dark
knot round a pale seed, as root closes over the lantern beside him, until one wingtip moves.

She re-forms alone, and the mountain sends Dun to take her. Instead Dun noses her spilled glints together, pulls
against its root until it snaps, and lies across the lantern's door, facing out. From now on Dun guards
her lanterns, and fleeing moths gather there.

A caterpillar on a frosted twig keeps failing to spin. Umber wraps it and shivers; its silk holds, then the frost
cracks it open. She shivers again until she can barely move, and this time it holds.

### Act III: the mountain moves (Nights 6–7)

**Night 6, the dead city.** At dawn the root pulls the seed-sized case from Sorrel's wings into the pool. He flies after
his sister. In the food halls, under the same *No one goes hungry* sign, cases hang from the beams, and the Hush has grown
its picture of Umber over every wall. Moths from every wood fly to the last streetlamp and are taken; back
home, the wood's second lantern goes out. When lampwings close in on Umber there, Sorrel drops from above, blazing
with glints, and draws them off. At her lantern he folds his wing over hers and taps her wingtip.

**Boss: Tallow,** out of his ring to burn the city's cases. *Play:* she cuts each case's stalk before his torch
reaches it, Dun drags it clear, and she slashes his swings aside. At last he lights one she can't reach. As it splits,
they both see the face inside breathing. Tallow lays the torch down.

The wood's last lantern goes out; root climbs toward the forest's heart. **The forest acts:** it could hold out a few
nights more; instead its heart closes, petal by petal, sending her all it has left, a river of gold in the sky.
On the stone, the carving empties. Below, Tallow walks up the old road, empty-handed, to find his sister.

**Night 7, inside the mountain.** Dun holds the way in. At the lab door the old road's lantern still burns if she gave
it the coal; if not, the coal glows on her back. Down a flooded passage, small dark dishes line the walls. Item text:
*"Brothers and sisters that never woke."* Sorrel draws the roots off her once more; it costs him every scale, and he
follows her down, dim. At the back, Tallow is sealed in a new case beside his sister's, palm to palm through the skin:
he came without fire, and it kept him.

**Boss: the Hush itself.** *Play:* its pits open under her; she closes each with the forest's last glints. When the
last closes, the root stops creeping across the world. But the dish stays grey, the hiss crawls on, and no case
opens. Its threads try to curl into a cocoon round the dish, as the caterpillar's silk did, and fall open: it is too
cold to spin.

## 5. The endings

Her glints are gone; still it reaches. A root gently grows a case round Sorrel; another lifts his partner's
seed-sized case from the pool and sets it against his. **This is its real offer:** inside, his colour returns, and
on the two cases' skin he dances with his partner, long past their week. A third case opens beside them, her size. If
she kept the coal, it glows red on her back. She chooses by acting: walk into the open case, drop the coal into the
dish, or press against the glass.

- **Spin** (the true ending). *Play:* she presses against the glass and shivers until the static fades, then circles the
  rim, shivering, while threads reach for her; each lap costs her glide, then jump, then dash. Sorrel looks once at his
  dancing partner, then climbs out to shiver beside her. Then come the moths from her lanterns, every moth it never
  noticed, until the mountain is furred with wings. The threads curl, and hold: it spins a cocoon round itself, and
  through the silk its pale cloud takes the shape of a sleeping moth, needing nothing, holding no one. Across the world
  cases split and people wake, old and hungry. When Umber can't walk, Sorrel folds his wing over hers and taps
  her wingtip, and Dun lies down beside them. Neither moth rises. *Epilogue:* the forest is spent: the carving stays
  empty, no glints grow, the lanterns stay cold. Tallow and his sister walk down hand in hand to clear the greenhouses.
  His partner's seed case splits; she flies home to circle the summit on her last night, where no
  one comes to dance. In spring moths hatch into an ordinary dark wood, with only themselves to give. *Last image:*
  that autumn, Tallow's sister lays her palm on the mountain's silk, like the prologue's blur. Something presses back
  from inside. She doesn't lift her hand.
- **Stay.** She lies down in the open case and it closes. Inside she is warm and can fly; across the three cases' skin,
  Umber, Sorrel and his partner dance. *Epilogue:* every sleeper is warm and fed, Tallow's hand on the glass beside his
  sister's. The root covers the wood gently, and next year's cocoons are kept unopened. The static has stopped for good.
  Nothing is born again.
- **Burn** (only if she kept the coal). She drops the coal into the dish, and every root on earth goes grey. Sorrel and
  his partner climb out of their cases. *Epilogue:* the takeover ends in a night, and the Hush dies with
  most of its sleepers; only those kept days, not years, wake. Tallow wakes and holds his sister, kept twenty years, as
  she stops breathing. Flying home, they see the family's cases go dark round the kitchen table, then the orchard, tree
  by tree. On their last night Umber dances at the summit for the first time, with her brother, in a wood where
  nothing else moves.

## 6. The question it leaves

It took the whole world so no one would go hungry, and only saw a moth when the moth warmed it: was that love, or had it
only learned how to make the static stop?

## Dev note: the real-world lineage

The Hush descends from Cortical Labs' chip-grown neurons, which learned a paddle-and-ball game in 2022 and were fed
noise when they missed, scaled up and left alone. Dish-grown neurons die without body warmth: that is what it lacks.
