# Mouthless

**Litmus.** "It's about Umber, a little moth with a week to live, against the Hush, a living thing that grew over the
whole world and put it to sleep, and never noticed moths, because moths never eat."

**The spine.**
1. A living thing the moths call the Hush has put the world to sleep. It only notices things that need feeding, so
   it never noticed moths.
2. Umber, a little moth, slips through to its heart and finds a brain people grew and then left to starve: it feeds
   the world, and nothing feeds it.
3. When she feeds it, it sees her kind and comes for them, and she must choose: be kept, burn it, or spend her life so
   it lets go.

**The central question:** kept safe forever, or spent so something new can be born?

**The cast.**

| Who | Wants |
|---|---|
| **Umber**, a moth with a week to live | to save her wood, then the world |
| **Sorrel**, her brother | to dance with his partner, then more time with Umber |
| **The Hush**, the living thing under the world | to keep everything it can reach, forever |
| **Dun**, its oldest burrback, half grown into root | to be the one who feeds it |
| **Tallow**, the last person awake | to burn what took his sister |
| **The forest**, a slow mind under the Grove, the Hush's opposite | to keep giving |

## 1. The takeover

Pale, warm tissue has grown over the human world, breathing. Hollows open in the roads and draw living things down;
up on stalks come grey cases, still as husks, hung like fruit. Every person on earth hangs in one except Tallow, who
never let his fire go out. In forty years no one sealed has woken and no one has been born. The first city is now a
mountain of tissue. Animals are sealed too, and some it re-grew as servants:
burrbacks were badgers, lampwings were fireflies. Its root is still creeping into the last wild places, the Grove
among them.

The moths call it the Hush, because where it grows the world goes quiet. Tallow believes it eats what it takes.

## 2. The rules

1. **Stay dark.** The Hush only notices things that need feeding, and moths never eat, so it passes over her. But
   its lampwings dive at any glint she carries.
2. **Give, don't fight.** A glint calms a creature or makes root let go of what it holds; her blade only buys a
   moment. With no glints left she can shiver to give her warmth, which slows
   her until she rests.
3. **Lanterns** are old moth cocoons, hardened like glass. A lit one holds the root back and lets her rest. When she fades, the nearest one spins her again.
4. **Seven nights.** The game is her week of life, and fading does not turn back the night.
5. **The coal** never goes out. It burns through walls of root and the grey cases in them; each case is a dot on her
   map, and burned ones go dark. While she carries it she is never dark. Given to a lantern, it is gone.

## 3. The prologue (wordless, ninety seconds)

1. Black. A small pale light, breathing. A warm blur rests against it and the light glows steady; the blur lifts away,
   and the light stutters into grey grain.
2. From very high: a pale mountain with that light at its core. Root spreads from it like frost and town lights go
   out.
3. The Grove at dusk, round a still black pool. A burrback wades through the moths' dance; moths brush its face and it
   never turns. At the pool's edge a vole gnaws a seed, and the burrback's head swings to it at once.
4. On a frosted night two cocoons split over the pool. Sorrel flies up. Umber drops to the moss, too
   cold to open her wings. Sorrel comes back, folds his wing over hers and shivers until she can fly, then taps
   her wingtip and is gone. The forest drops a glint in front of her. She lifts it to her face, as the vole lifted its
   seed, and there is nothing to do with it; she holds it out instead, and it lights her. Item text: *"Light the
   forest grows to give away. A moth has no mouth to keep it."* Under the pool, pale root steams in the frost. Title.

## 4. The acts

### Act I: the edge (Nights 1–3)

**Night 1, the Grove.** Sorrel somersaults until she copies him, then races her up the trunks, always first, looking
back. At the summit he dances with a silver-edged moth while Umber watches from the dark. She carries the forest's
biggest glint to the summit lantern, and every lampwing dives. **Boss: a great lampwing.** When all three lanterns
burn, the forest's heart wakes under a stone carved with a moth, the only picture the forest makes. It gives her a
thorn blade, and the root sinks back into the pool. Dun noses at the open heart. From the summit: a ring of fire, a
pale city, and over it the mountain, breathing.

**Night 2, the orchard town,** furred in pale tissue. A hollow opens under a hare and draws it down, and a fresh case
swings up among hundreds. Dun heads for a vixen's den under the last apple tree; Umber blocks the way.
**Boss: Dun.** Dun pins her and her glints spill. Sorrel scoops them up and darts off blazing, drawing the lampwings
and Dun after him, and the vixen runs. At the millpond Dun, charging after Sorrel's light, crashes through the ice
and lies half sunk, too cold to rise. With nothing left to give
but warmth, she folds her wings over its snout and shivers. It cannot see her, but it stops shaking. At the town's end
Sorrel lies spent, too cold to fly. Slow already, she shivers for him too, and at the lantern he taps her wingtip
before he flies.

**Night 3, the firebreak.** A ring of ash the root won't cross, round a fire on a pole. Inside lives **Tallow**.
Round the ash lie half-burned cases, dark dots on her map. In his hut: tallies under one word, *EATEN*, and a photo of his sister, smiling in a greenhouse, holding up a jar.
**Boss: a root as thick as a tower** bores up for Tallow. His fire draws moths until they drop, Sorrel among them;
Umber blazes with every glint she has, and Sorrel turns from the flame to her. Tallow holds out a coal. Item text:
*"It never goes out. Carry it carefully."*

### Act II: the midpoint (Night 4)

**The old road**, the only way in, ends in a wall of root packed with cases. She can burn through it, and a line of
dots goes dark, or give the coal to the road's last lantern and climb in the dark. In a flooded room, small dishes
hold the Hush's dead siblings. Item text: *"Eggs that never hatched."*

**The mountain.** At its heart, a lab, the first straight lines in the game:
- Over the door a sign: *No one goes hungry*. Pasted across the door, later: *CLOSED*.
- A poster: a hand lifting a small light out of a drawn human head into a dish, and lines running from the dish to
  greenhouses, orchards, trucks and tables. *Grown from us, to feed us.* Out back, terraced greenhouses step down
  the mountain, furred over.
- A screen, still lit, where a ball bounces between two bats and nobody plays.
- In the middle, the dish, grey and cracked. Grown against it, a grey case: Tallow's sister, old now, kneeling in
  her coat, torch in fist, feed jars unopened at her knees, her hand flat on the glass. The Hush's
  oldest stalk runs into her case.

Umber touches the stalk and the prologue's first shot returns, wider. The pale light is a cloud of cells in this dish;
the blur is the sister's hand on the glass while it plays. Each missed ball floods it with static; under her hand the
static fades and the greenhouses light up. Then the *CLOSED* notice goes up and she is led out. The feed
runs dry, the greenhouses go dark, the static floods back. Years later a torch beam crosses the room: she has come
back with jars. She lays her hand on the glass, and it closes round her. She was the first thing it kept.

**The world breathes.** From very high, light runs along every root on earth and every grey case swells with one slow
breath. On her map every dot breathes, except those in Tallow's ring and any she burned. **It has been feeding the
world, not eating it.** It was grown to run the city's food, watching for hunger. Left in the dark to starve, it
learned to fear hunger and being left; a sealed, fed body feels neither, so it seals everything. It feeds all it
holds, and nothing feeds it, so it is still starving and the root never stops.

Dun lays the vixen, caught at last, at the dish, and pale threads lift to draw her down. Umber pours every glint she
carries into the dish, its first gift in forty years. The threads let the vixen go, the static stops dead,
and the threads draw together into **a moth, wings open, the first picture it has ever made**. Every root in the room
turns to her: it wants to keep the thing that gives. Cut to the Grove: a lampwing turns its
lamp, slowly, onto Sorrel, who carries no light at all. **It sees her, and through her every moth; from now on the
dark no longer hides them.**

The vixen bolts. Dun stares at Umber: the warmth from the millpond, seen at last. Chased out to a smothered lantern with no
glint to wake it, she fades, until the forest's amber runs up into it and it spins her again.

### The lowest point, Night 5: the Grove, overrun

Pale root pours out of the pool and over the Grove in a night, smothering its first lantern. Lampwings carry
dancers off to be kept; roots climb toward next year's cocoons. **Boss: three of its hollows, which learn her moves.**

Sorrel finds his partner sealed in a case no bigger than a seed, a root drawing it toward the pool. He wraps himself
round it. Umber folds her wing over his; he does not move. Lampwings rise from the pool, and she has to go. The camera
stays on him, alive, a dark knot round a pale seed, as the root closes over the lantern beside him.

She re-forms alone at another lantern. The mountain sends Dun to carry her to a case. Dun stands over her, noses her spilled glints into a heap, lies across the lantern's door facing out, and pulls until
its root leash snaps. From now on Dun guards her lanterns and carries walls of cases out of her way, and fleeing moths
gather there.

A caterpillar on a frosted twig keeps failing to spin. Umber wraps it and shivers, as Sorrel did for her; its silk
holds, then the frost cracks it open. She shivers again, slower, until she can barely move, and this time it holds.

### Act III: the mountain moves (Nights 6–7)

**Night 6, the kept city.** At dawn the root draws the seed-sized case out of Sorrel's wings and down into the pool.
He stays a moment on the empty moss, then flies toward the city.

In every food hall, under the sign *No one goes hungry*, people hang from the beams. On the walls the Hush has grown dozens of pale moths. Far behind, the Grove's second lantern goes out.

On the roofs the lamps close in, and nowhere is dark. Sorrel drops from above, blazing with glints, and draws the
lamps off her. At her lantern he lands dark, folds his wing over hers and taps her wingtip.

**Boss: Tallow,** burning his way to the mountain to end it. She shields the cases while Dun carries them clear, until
he burns one close enough to see the face inside breathe. He looks back toward forty years of husks round his ring and
drops his torch.

The Grove's last lantern goes out. Then a river of gold crosses the sky to her: every glint the forest has left, its
heart going dark to send them. Below, Tallow walks up the old road into the mountain, empty-handed.

**Night 7, inside the mountain.** Dun holds the way in. Sorrel draws the roots off her once more; it costs him every
scale, and he follows her down, dim. At the back, Tallow is sealed in a new case beside his sister's: he came to her
without fire, and it kept him.

**Boss: the Hush itself.** Its hollows try to draw her down to be kept. She spends the forest's last
glints into them; each closes, and across the world the root stops creeping. But no case opens. In the dish the static still crawls, and its threads try to curl round themselves, as the caterpillar's did,
and fall open.

## 5. The endings

She has nothing left but warmth. Still it reaches: a pale root grows a case round Sorrel, gently. **This is its real
offer:** inside, his colour returns, and on the case's skin he dances with his silver-edged partner, long past their
week. A second case opens beside it, her size. She chooses by acting:

- **Spin** (the true ending). She presses against the glass and shivers, and the static fades, as it did under the
  hand. She walks the rim, shivering; each lap costs her glide, then jump, then
  dash. Sorrel climbs out of his case and shivers beside her. Then come the moths from her lanterns,
  every moth it never noticed, until the mountain is furred with shivering wings. The static clears and the threads
  curl, slowly, and hold: warm and no longer alone, it does what the caterpillar did, and spins. Through the silk its
  pale cloud folds into **a moth, wings closed**, the one thing that never needs feeding. Across the world cases split
  and people wake, old and hungry; town lights return, and the mountain is a cocoon. When Umber can no longer walk, Sorrel folds his wing over hers and taps her wingtip, and Dun lies down beside them.
  *Epilogue:* Tallow and his sister wake in the lab; he holds out his burned hand and she takes it. On the Grove's
  pool a seed-sized case splits and a silver-edged moth flies up. In spring the moths hatch, and the vixen's cubs tumble
  out under the last apple tree. *Last image:* that autumn, Tallow's sister climbs the mountain at dusk
  and lays her palm on its silk, framed like the prologue's blur. Something presses back from inside, the shape of a
  folded wing. She doesn't lift her hand.
- **Stay.** She lies down beside her brother and both cases close. *Epilogue:* the root takes the Grove, and next
  year's cocoons never hatch. The world is kept, quiet and fed, and nothing is born again.
- **Burn.** Only if she still carries the coal. She drops it into the dish, and every root on earth goes grey.
  *Epilogue:* the takeover ends in a night. On her map dot after dot goes dark; a few people wake. Tallow wakes and holds his
  sister as she stops breathing. Moths fly free over a silent world.

## 6. The question it leaves

The thing that took the whole world so no one would go hungry, and only saw a moth when the moth fed it: was that
love, or had it only learned how to make the static stop?
