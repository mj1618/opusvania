# Mouthless

**Litmus.** "It's about Umber, a little moth with a week to live, against the Hush, a living thing that swallowed the
world and never noticed moths, because moths never eat."

**The spine.**
1. A living thing the moths call the Hush has grown over the world and put it to sleep. It never noticed moths.
2. Umber slips through to its heart and finds a brain people grew, then left to starve: it feeds the world, and
   nothing feeds it.
3. When she feeds it, it sees her kind and comes for them, and she must choose: be kept, burn it, or spend her life so
   it lets go.

**The central question:** kept safe forever, or spent so something new can be born?

**The cast.**

| Who | Wants |
|---|---|
| **Umber**, a moth with a week to live | to save her wood, then the world |
| **Sorrel**, her brother | to dance with his partner, then more time with Umber |
| **The Hush** (*HEARTH* on the lab sign), what took the world | to keep everything it can reach, forever |
| **Dun**, its oldest burrback, half grown into root | to be the one who feeds it |
| **Tallow**, the last person awake | to burn what took his sister |
| **The forest**, a slow mind in Umber's wood, the Hush's opposite | to keep giving |

## 1. The takeover

Pale, warm tissue has grown over the human world, breathing. Hollows open in the roads and draw living things down,
and grey cases rise on stalks, hung like fruit. Every person on earth sleeps in one except Tallow, who never let his
fire go out. In forty years no one has woken and no one has been born. The first city is a mountain of tissue.
Animals are sealed too, and some re-grown as its servants: burrbacks were badgers, lampwings were fireflies. Its
root is still creeping into the last wild places, Umber's wood among them.

The moths call it the Hush, because where it grows the world goes quiet. Tallow believes it eats what it takes.

## 2. The rules

1. **Light gives her away.** The Hush and its burrbacks notice only things that eat, so they pass over a moth.
   Lampwings see only light, and dive at any glint or coal she carries. After Night 4, everything the Hush grows hunts
   moths, lit or dark.
2. **Give, don't fight.** A glint calms a creature or makes root let go; her blade only buys a moment. With no glints
   left she can shiver to give her warmth, which slows her until she rests.
3. **Lanterns** are old moth cocoons, hardened like glass. A lit one holds the root back and lets her rest; when she
   fades, the nearest one spins her again.
4. **Seven nights,** her week of life. Fading does not turn the night back.
5. **The coal** burns through root and the cases in it. Given to a lantern, it is gone.

## 3. The prologue (wordless, ninety seconds)

1. Black. A small pale light, breathing. A warm blur rests against it and the light glows steady; the blur lifts away,
   and the light breaks into grey static.
2. From very high: a pale mountain with that light at its core. Root spreads from it like frost; town lights go out.
3. Umber's wood at dusk, round a still black pool. A burrback wades through the moths' dance and never turns. At the
   pool's edge a vole gnaws a seed, and the burrback's head swings to it at once.
4. On a frosty night two cocoons split over the pool. Sorrel flies up; Umber drops to the moss, too cold to open her
   wings. He comes back, folds his wing over hers and shivers until she can fly, taps her wingtip and is gone.
   The forest drops a glint before her. She lifts it to her face, as the vole lifted its seed, but there is nothing to
   do with it; she holds it out, and it lights her. Item text: *"Light the forest grows to give away. A moth has no
   mouth to keep it."* Under the pool, pale root steams in the frost. Title.

## 4. The acts

### Act I: out of the wood (Nights 1–3)

**Night 1, the wood.** Sorrel somersaults until she copies him, then races her up the trunks, always first, looking back. At the summit he dances with a silver-edged moth while Umber watches
from the dark. She carries the forest's biggest glint to the summit lantern, and every lampwing in the wood dives at
it. **Boss: a great lampwing.** When all three lanterns burn, the forest's heart wakes under a standing stone carved
with **a moth, wings open: the only picture the forest makes**. Light fills the carving line by line, the heart gives
her a thorn blade, and the root sinks back into the pool. Dun noses at the open heart. From the summit she sees the
whole road: white gardens, a town with one ring of fire, a dark city with one lamp, and the mountain, breathing.

**Night 2, the edge and the gardens.** At the wood's foot the moss stops at a line: amber, then pale. In the
gardens beyond, a hollow swallows a hare, and its case swings up in an orchard where
hundreds hang among the apples. Dun heads for a vixen's den under the last apple tree; Umber blocks the way.
**Boss: Dun.** It never fights her: she turns it with glints, again and again, until it tramples over her. Her glints
spill and the lampwings dive; Sorrel scoops them up and darts off with every lamp after him.
The vixen flees across the frozen pond, and Dun crashes through the ice after her and lies half sunk, too cold to rise. With nothing
left but warmth, Umber folds her wings over its snout and shivers. It stops shaking. At the
garden's end Sorrel lies spent, too cold to fly. She shivers for him too, and at the lantern he taps
her wingtip before he flies.

**Night 3, the kept town.** In one kitchen a family's cases hang round the table, the plates still set, and clean. At the street's end one house stands in a ring of ash
the root won't cross, a fire on a pole in its yard and half-burned cases round it. **Tallow** lives there. On his
kitchen wall: tallies under one word, *EATEN*, and a photo of his sister smiling in a greenhouse, holding up a jar.
**Boss: a root as thick as a tower** bores up through the street for Tallow. His fire draws moths until they drop,
Sorrel among them; Umber blazes with every glint she has, and Sorrel turns from the flame to her. Tallow holds out a
coal. Item text: *"It never goes out. It is for the thing in the mountain."*

### Act II: the midpoint (Night 4)

**The old road** skirts the dark city and climbs the mountain to a last lantern, hollow and cold,
its door open for the coal. Beyond it stands a wall of root packed with cases, glowing red as the coal nears. She can
burn through, and those cases go dark, or give the coal to the lantern and climb on without it. In a flooded room,
small dishes hold the Hush's dead siblings. Item text: *"Brothers and sisters that never woke."*

**The mountain.** At its heart is a lab, all straight lines. Out back, terraced greenhouses step down the mountain, furred over and dark.
- Over the door, a sign: ***HEARTH**: No one goes hungry*. Pasted across the door, later: *CLOSED*.
- A poster in three panels: a hand lifts a small light from a drawn human head into a dish, *Grown from us*; the
  dish beside a ball bouncing between two bats, *First it learned to catch*; lines from the dish to greenhouses,
  trucks and set tables, *Now it feeds us*.
- The dish, grey and cracked, and grown against it a case: Tallow's sister, old now, kneeling in her coat, torch in
  fist, feed jars unopened at her knees, her hand flat on the glass. The Hush's oldest stalk runs into her case.

Umber touches the stalk and the prologue's first shot returns, wider. The pale light is a cloud of cells in this dish,
and the warm blur is the sister's hand on the glass while it plays the ball game. Each missed ball floods it with
static; under her hand the static fades, and out back the greenhouses light. Then the *CLOSED* notice goes up and the
sister is led out. The feed runs dry, the greenhouses go dark, the static floods back. Years later a torch beam
crosses the room: she has come back with jars. She lays her hand on the glass, and it closes round her.

**The world breathes.** From very high, light runs *out* of the mountain along every root on earth into every case,
and every case breathes, except the burned ones round Tallow's ring and any Umber burned.

Dun lays the vixen, caught at last, at the dish, and pale threads lift to draw her down. Umber pours all her glints into
the dish: the forest's light, the first thing anyone has fed it in forty years. The threads let the vixen
go, the static stops dead, and the threads draw together, line for line, into **the stone's moth, wings open**. Every
root in the room turns to her. Cut to the wood: a lampwing turns its lamp onto Sorrel, who carries no light.

The vixen bolts. Dun stares at Umber and, at last, sees the warmth from the pond. Chased out with no glints left, she
fades at a smothered lantern on the road; the forest's amber runs up the root into it, and it spins her again. She
flies home ahead of the root.

### The lowest point (Night 5): the wood overrun

Pale root pours out of the pool and over the wood in one night, smothering its first lantern. Lampwings carry dancers
off to be kept; roots climb toward next year's cocoons. **Boss: three of its hollows, which learn her moves.**

Sorrel finds his partner sealed in a case no bigger than a seed, a root drawing it toward the pool. He wraps himself
round it. Umber folds her wing over his; he does not move. Lampwings rise, and she has to go. The camera stays on him,
a dark knot round a pale seed, as the root closes over the lantern beside him.

She re-forms alone at another lantern. The mountain sends Dun to carry her to a case. Dun stands over her, noses her
spilled glints into a heap, lies across the lantern's door facing out, and pulls until its root leash snaps. From now
on Dun guards her lanterns, and fleeing moths gather there.

A caterpillar on a frosted twig keeps failing to spin. Umber wraps it and shivers, as Sorrel did for her; its silk
holds, then the frost cracks it open. She shivers again, slower, until she can barely move, and this time it holds.

### Act III: the mountain moves (Nights 6–7)

**Night 6, the dead city.** At dawn the root draws the seed-sized case out of Sorrel's wings and down into the pool.
He stays a moment on the empty moss, then flies inward.

The city is dark. In every food hall, under the sign *HEARTH*, people hang from the beams. On the walls the Hush has
grown the stone's moth over and over, the one picture it was ever given. At the city's heart the last streetlamp
burns, and moths from every wood fly to it and are taken. Far behind, the wood's
second lantern goes out.

On the lamp's post the lampwings close in. Sorrel drops from above, blazing with glints, and draws every lamp off her.
He lands at her lantern, folds his wing over hers and taps her wingtip.

**Boss: Tallow,** burning his way to the mountain to end it. She shields the cases while Dun carries them clear, until
he burns one close enough to see the face inside breathe. He looks back at his ring and its forty years of burned
cases, and drops his torch.

The wood's last lantern goes out. Then every glint the forest has left crosses the sky to her in a river of gold, sent as
its heart goes dark. On the stone, the carving empties. Below, Tallow walks up the old road into the
mountain, empty-handed.

**Night 7, inside the mountain.** Dun holds the way in. Sorrel draws the roots off her once more; it costs him every
scale, and he follows her down, dim. At the back, Tallow is sealed in a new case beside his sister's: he came to her
without fire, and it kept him.

**Boss: the Hush itself.** Its hollows try to draw her down to be kept. She spends the forest's last glints into
them; each one closes, and across the world the root stops creeping. But no case opens. In the dish the static still
crawls, and the threads try to curl round themselves, as the caterpillar's silk did, and fall open.

## 5. The endings

She has nothing left but warmth. Still it reaches: a pale root grows a case round Sorrel, gently. **This is its real
offer:** inside, his colour returns, and on the case's skin he dances with his silver-edged partner, long past their
week. A second case opens beside it, her size. If she still carries the coal, it flares red in the dish's glass. She
chooses by acting:

- **Spin** (the true ending). She presses against the glass and shivers, and the static fades, as it did under the
  sister's hand. She walks the rim, shivering; each lap costs her glide, then jump, then dash. Sorrel climbs out of his
  case and shivers beside her; then come the moths from her lanterns, every moth it never noticed, until the mountain
  is furred with shivering wings. The static clears and the threads curl, slowly, and hold: it spins, as the
  caterpillar did. Through the silk its pale cloud folds into **the stone's moth, wings closed**, the
  one thing that never needs feeding. Across the world cases split and people wake, old and hungry, and walk toward
  the greenhouses lighting one after another down the mountain. When Umber can no longer walk, Sorrel folds his wing
  over hers and taps her wingtip, and Dun lies down beside them. Neither moth rises.
  *Epilogue:* Tallow and his sister wake in the lab; he holds out his burned hand and she takes it. On the wood's pool
  a seed-sized case splits and a silver-edged moth flies up, and on the stone the carving fills again. In spring the
  moths hatch, and the vixen's cubs tumble out under the last apple tree. *Last image:* that autumn, Tallow's sister
  climbs the mountain and lays her palm on its silk, framed like the prologue's blur. Something presses back
  from inside, the shape of a folded wing. She doesn't lift her hand.
- **Stay.** She lies down beside her brother and both cases close. *Epilogue:* the root takes the wood, and next
  year's cocoons never hatch. The world is kept, quiet and fed, and nothing is born again.
- **Burn.** She drops the coal into the dish, and every root on earth goes grey. *Epilogue:* the takeover ends in a
  night. Case after case goes dark, the oldest first; only the newest wake, the ones kept days, not years.
  Tallow wakes beside his sister, whose stalk was the oldest of all, and holds her as she stops breathing. Moths fly
  free over a silent world.

## 6. The question it leaves

The thing that took the whole world so no one would go hungry, and only saw a moth when the moth fed it: was that
love, or had it only learned how to make the static stop?
