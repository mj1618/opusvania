# Mouthless

**Litmus.** "It's about Umber, a little moth with a week to live, against the Hush, a living thing that swallowed the
world and never noticed moths, because moths never eat."

**The spine.**
1. A living thing the moths call the Hush has grown over the world and put it to sleep. It never noticed moths.
2. Umber slips through to its heart and finds a brain people grew in a warm dish, then left in the cold. It feeds the
   world, and no one has warmed it since.
3. When she warms it, it sees her kind and comes for them, and she must choose: be kept, burn it, or spend her life so
   it lets go.

**The central question:** kept safe forever, or spent so something new can be born?

**The cast.**
- **Umber**, a moth with a week to live, wants to save her wood, then the world.
- **Sorrel**, her brother, wants to dance with his partner, then more time with Umber.
- **The Hush**, the mind that took the world, wants everyone it feeds kept where they can't get away, since what gets
  away brings it pain.
- **Dun**, its oldest burrback, half grown into root, brings it everything it wants kept.
- **Tallow**, the last person awake, burns the cases, which he thinks are eating everyone, his sister too.
- **The forest**, a slow mind in Umber's wood, wants to give its warmth away.

## 1. The takeover

Pale tissue, cool as bone, covers the human world. Pits in the roads draw living things down, and grey cases rise on
stalks like fruit. In each, someone sleeps: everyone on earth but Tallow, who never let his fire go out. In forty years
no one has woken or been born. The first city is now a breathing mountain. Animals are sealed too, and some re-grown as
its servants: burrbacks were badgers, lampwings were fireflies. Its root is still creeping into the last wild places,
like Umber's wood. The moths call it the Hush: where it grows, the world goes quiet.

## 2. The rules

1. **Give, don't fight.** Glints are drops of the forest's warmth. Giving one calms a creature or loosens root; her
   blade only buys a moment. With none left she can shiver to give her own warmth, which slows her until she rests.
2. **Unseen until lit.** The Hush and its burrbacks notice only things that eat, so they pass over moths. Lampwings see
   glow and dive at any glint or coal she carries. After Night 4, everything the Hush grows hunts moths, lit or dark.
3. **Lanterns**, old moth cocoons hardened like glass, hold root back and let her rest. When she fades, the nearest lit
   one spins her again.
4. **Fire burns root,** and the cases on it, and whoever sleeps inside. Given to a lantern, the coal lights it for good
   and is gone.

## 3. The prologue (wordless, ninety seconds)

1. Black. A small pale light, breathing. A warm blur rests on it and the light steadies; the blur lifts, frost creeps
   in, and the light breaks into grey static.
2. From high above: a pale mountain with that light at its core. Root spreads from it like frost; town lights go out.
3. Umber's wood at dusk, round a black pool. Dun, an old burrback furred with root, wades through the moths' dance
   unseeing, but when a vole gnaws a seed nearby, its head swings round.
4. On a frosty night two cocoons split over the pool. Sorrel flies up; Umber drops to the moss, too cold to fly. He
   comes back, folds his wing over hers and shivers until she can, taps her wingtip and is gone. A glint drops before
   her. She lifts it to her face, as the vole did its seed, then holds it out, and it lights her. Item text: *"Warmth
   the forest grows to give away. A moth has no mouth to keep it."* Under the pool, pale root steams. Title.

## 4. The acts

### Act I: out of the wood (Nights 1–3)

**Night 1, the wood.** Sorrel somersaults until she copies him, then races her up the trunks. At the summit he dances
with a silver-edged moth while Umber watches from the dark. **Boss: a great lampwing**, diving at the big glint she
carries from the high island. *Play:* she gives it a glint each time it hangs, until its lamp folds. When all three
lanterns burn, light fills a moth carved on a standing stone: the only picture the forest makes. **The forest acts:**
warmth runs through the ground, the root under the pool shrinks back, and a thorn grows from the shrine into her hands:
her blade. Far off, the mountain breathes.

**Night 2, the gardens.** A pit swallows a hare; its case swings up into an orchard where hundreds hang among the
apples. **Boss: Dun,** heading for a vixen's den. *Play:* it never attacks, only plods on; she flies into its path and
gives it glints, each driving it back a step, while Sorrel draws the lampwings off. When her glints run out it shoulders
past. The vixen flees across a frozen pond; Dun crashes through the ice after her and lies there, shaking. Umber folds
her wings over its snout and shivers until it stops. Later, when Sorrel is too cold to fly, she shivers for him too, and
he taps her wingtip.

**Night 3, the kept town.** A family's cases hang round their set kitchen table. At the street's end **Tallow**'s house
stands in a ring of ash the root won't cross. On his wall a tally runs under one word, *EATEN*, beside a photo of his
sister in a greenhouse coat. He rests his palm on a case, bows his head, and burns it: kinder, he thinks, than being
digested. **Boss: a root as thick as a tower** bores up through the street for him. *Play:* she climbs it, giving a
glint into each knot until it sinks; it never turns on her. Seeing that, Tallow gives her a coal. Item text: *"It never
goes out. It is for the thing in the mountain."* At dawn Sorrel taps her wingtip and flies home to his partner; Umber
goes on alone.

### Act II: the midpoint (Night 4)

**The old road** climbs the mountain to a last cold lantern and a wall of cases. She can give the coal to the lantern,
and the wall draws back, or keep it and burn through the sleepers. In a flooded room, small dishes hold the Hush's dead
siblings. Item text: *"Brothers and sisters that never woke."*

**The mountain.** At its heart is a lab with dark greenhouses.
- Over the door: *No one goes hungry*, with *CLOSED* pasted across it.
- A poster in three panels: a light lifted from a woman in a greenhouse coat into a dish, *Grown from us*; the dish and
  a ball between two paddles, *First it learned to catch*; the dish wired to greenhouses and tables, *Now it feeds us*.
- The dish, grey and cracked, and grown against it a case: Tallow's sister, old now, kneeling, her palm on the dish
  through the case's skin. The Hush's thickest stalk runs into it.

Umber touches the stalk and the prologue's first shot returns, wider: the pale light is a cloud of cells in this dish,
the warm blur the sister's hand on the glass while it plays the ball game. Each missed ball floods it with static; under
her hand the static fades, and the greenhouses light. Then *CLOSED* goes up, the sister is led out, frost creeps in, the
static floods back, and the greenhouses go dark. Its first root slides down a pipe into town, and a case closes round a
sleeping child. Years later the sister comes back with a torch, sets it down, lays her hand on the glass, and the case
closes round her.

From high above, a pulse runs out of the mountain along every root on earth, and every case
breathes, except the burned ones.

Dun, who tracked the vixen here, lays her at the dish, and threads lift to draw her down. Umber pours all her glints in:
its first warmth in forty years. The threads let the vixen go, the static stops dead, and they draw together into a moth
with wings open: her. It is the first picture it has ever made, and every root in the room turns to her. At the summit,
a lampwing turns its lamp onto the dancing Sorrel, who carries no light.

The vixen bolts. Dun stares at Umber and at last knows her: the moth who warmed it on the ice. Umber flies, taking her
warmth with her; the static floods back, and every root on earth turns toward moths, one more thing that can get away.
Out of glints, she shivers to stay ahead until she fades, and every lantern near her is smothered. **The forest acts:**
it pushes its warmth up the Hush's own root into the nearest lantern, which spins her again.

### The lowest point (Night 5): the wood overrun

Overnight, root pours out of the pool over the wood. Lampwings carry dancers off; roots climb toward next year's
cocoons. **Boss: three of its pits, which learn her moves.** *Play:* each opens wherever she last landed, so she keeps
changing her line, and closes each with a glint.

Sorrel finds his partner sealed in a case no bigger than a seed, a root drawing it toward the pool. He wraps himself
round it. Umber folds her wing over his; he does not move, and lampwings rise. She goes. The camera holds on him, a dark
knot round a pale seed, as root closes over the lantern beside him, until one wingtip moves.

She re-forms alone at another lantern, and the mountain sends Dun to take her. Instead it noses her spilled glints
together, pulls against its root until it snaps, and lies across the lantern's door, facing out. From now on Dun guards
her lanterns, and fleeing moths gather there.

A caterpillar on a frosted twig keeps failing to spin. Umber wraps it and shivers, as Sorrel did for her; its silk
holds, then the frost cracks it open. She shivers again until she can barely move, and this time it holds.

### Act III: the mountain moves (Nights 6–7)

**Night 6, the dead city.** At dawn the root pulls the seed-sized case from Sorrel's wings into the pool. He flies after
his sister. In the city's food halls, under the same sign, cases hang from the beams, and the Hush has grown its picture
of Umber over every wall. Moths from every wood fly to the last streetlamp and are taken; far behind, the wood's second
lantern goes out. When lampwings close in on Umber there, Sorrel drops from above, blazing with glints, and draws them
off. At her lantern he folds his wing over hers and taps her wingtip.

**Boss: Tallow,** who has left his ring to burn the city's cases. *Play:* she cuts each case's stalk before his torch
reaches it and Dun drags it clear, while she slashes his swings aside. At last he lights one she can't reach in time. As
it splits, they both see the face inside breathing. Tallow lays the torch down.

The wood's last lantern goes out, and root climbs toward the forest's heart. **The forest acts:** it could hold out a
few nights more; instead its heart closes, petal by petal, as it sends her all it has left, a river of gold in the sky.
On the stone, the carving empties. Below, Tallow walks up the old road, empty-handed, to find his sister.

**Night 7, inside the mountain.** Dun holds the way in. Sorrel draws the roots off her once more; it costs him every
scale, and he follows her down, dim. At the back, Tallow is sealed in a new case beside his sister's, palm to palm
through the skin: he came without fire, and it kept him.

**Boss: the Hush itself.** *Play:* its pits open under her, as on Night 5; she closes each with the forest's last
glints. Across the world the root stops creeping. But no case opens, and the static still crawls. Its threads try to
curl into a cocoon round the dish, as the caterpillar's silk did, and fall open: it is too cold to spin.

## 5. The endings

She has nothing left but warmth, and still it reaches: a root gently grows a case round Sorrel. **This is its real
offer:** inside, his colour returns, and on the case's skin he dances with his partner, long past their week. A second
case opens beside it, her size. If she kept the coal, it glows red. She chooses by acting: walk into the open case, drop
the coal, or press against the glass.

- **Spin** (the true ending). *Play:* she presses against the glass and shivers until the static fades, then circles the
  rim, shivering, while threads reach for her; each lap costs her glide, then jump, then dash. Sorrel looks once at his
  dancing partner, then climbs out to shiver beside her. Then come the moths from her lanterns, every moth it never
  noticed, until the mountain is furred with wings. The threads curl, and hold: it spins a cocoon round itself, and
  through the silk its pale cloud takes the shape of a sleeping moth, needing nothing, holding no one. Across the world
  cases split and people wake, old and hungry. When Umber can no longer walk, Sorrel folds his wing over hers and taps
  her wingtip, and Dun lies down beside them. Neither moth rises. *Epilogue:* the forest is spent: the carving stays
  empty, no glints grow, the lanterns stay cold. Tallow and his sister walk down hand in hand to clear the greenhouses.
  A seed-sized case floats up out of the pool and splits; a silver-edged moth circles the summit for her last night,
  where no one comes to dance. In spring the moths hatch into an ordinary dark wood, with only themselves to give. *Last
  image:* that autumn, Tallow's sister lays her palm on the mountain's silk, like the prologue's blur. Something presses
  back from inside. She doesn't lift her hand.
- **Stay.** She lies down in the case beside her brother and it closes. Inside she is warm and can fly; on the case's
  skin the three of them dance. *Epilogue:* every sleeper is warm and fed, Tallow's hand on the glass beside his
  sister's. The root covers the wood gently, and next year's cocoons are kept unopened. The static has stopped for good.
  Nothing is born again.
- **Burn.** She drops the coal into the dish, and every root on earth goes grey. Sorrel's case splits and he climbs out.
  *Epilogue:* the takeover ends in a night, with the Hush and most of its sleepers; only those kept days, not years,
  wake. Tallow wakes and holds his sister, kept twenty years, as she stops breathing. Flying home, the moths see the
  family's cases go dark round the kitchen table, then the orchard, tree by tree. On their last night Umber dances at
  the summit for the first time, with her brother, in a wood where nothing else moves.

## 6. The question it leaves

It took the whole world so no one would go hungry, and only saw a moth when the moth warmed it: was that love, or had it
only learned how to make the static stop?

## Dev note: the real-world lineage

The Hush descends from Cortical Labs' chip-grown neurons, which learned a paddle-and-ball game in 2022 and were fed
noise when they missed, scaled up and left alone. Dish-grown neurons die without body warmth: that is what it lacks.
