# Review: Mouthless (round 27)

## 1. Anchor gate: PASS

All three readers' first one or two sentences name a little moth and a living thing that put the world to sleep and never noticed moths. All three also name the dish-grown brain in their account of the enemy ("a lab brain grown to feed people and left alone"). The truth is found in play, in the right order: the mountain on the horizon (Night 1), the greenhouse-coat photo (Night 3), the three-panel poster and the dish (Night 4), and the item text for the unwoken dishes (Night 7). The surface stays mythic and the technical layer sits underneath. The Hush is on screen and active at world scale in every act. In Act I there is Dun, the pits, the orchard of cases and the tower root. In Act II "every root on earth turns toward moths". On Night 5 it overruns the wood, and in Act III it grows its picture of Umber over every wall.

## 2. Craft verdict: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES

There are no foundation problems. Every note below is a line-level fix.

## 3. At the bar

- **The motif carries the relationship.** Sorrel "folds his wing over hers and shivers… taps her wingtip". This happens at her birth, on the ice with Dun, in the city, and at the end, where "Neither moth rises." Every reader named it unprompted. It is the Naru beat done without words.
- **The villain's reversal is one image.** In the memory, "each leaving hisses like a missed ball." Pong learning becomes abandonment, and abandonment becomes the reason it takes the world. The real-world lineage turns into the feeling behind the antagonist, which is the Hollow Knight surface-and-depth structure working.
- **The title is the mechanic and the theme.** "A moth has no mouth to keep it." Moths can't eat, so the Hush can't see them, so the player gives instead of fights. The idea, the stealth rule and the combat verb are one rule.
- **Dun's turn is earned in play.** He is warmed on the ice on Night 2, recognises her on Night 4, and on Night 5 "pulls against its root until it snaps, and lies across the lantern's door, facing out." The casual reader remembered this beat most.
- **The endings cost something real.** Spin costs her abilities one at a time and spends the forest. Stay is a gentle death of the future. Burn kills twenty years of sleepers in Tallow's arms. The last image, "Something presses back from inside", keeps the sentience question open.

## 4. Problems, ranked

1. **Pronouns in the endings (POLISH).** All three readers tripped on "His partner's seed case splits" because it follows a Tallow sentence, and two of them on Burn's "they". *Fix:* write "Sorrel's partner" by name, and in Burn write "Umber and Sorrel, flying home". Show Sorrel's partner in Burn: give her one beat at the summit dance, or cut her climbing out.
2. **The Night 4 coal choice hides a massacre (POLISH).** "keep it and burn through the sleepers" opens the path to Burn by killing people, and that is said in half a line. *Fix:* show it. Each case she burns shows the swallowing, breathing face from the door shot, so the cost is felt before Burn makes it world-sized. This is the same image Tallow learns from, so no rule is added.
3. **The Hush is understood but not yet felt before the lab (POLISH).** The heart reader said: "'Static' and 'missed balls' are clever, but I don't feel them yet." The casual reader said the middle needs to be "shown, not told". *Fix:* seed the grey static in Act I so the memory confirms what the player has seen rather than explains it. Grey flicker could run up the root when the vixen escapes Dun, when Tallow burns a case, and when Sorrel flies home at dawn.
4. **Tallow's change doesn't follow (POLISH).** On Night 3 he burns a case knowing someone is inside. On Night 6 a breathing face stops him. *Fix:* on Night 3 he burns it with his face turned away. On Night 6 he sees.
5. **Timeline and the sister's torch (POLISH).** The casual reader had to work out 40 years against 20, and asked why she brought a torch and didn't light it. *Fix:* in the memory she lights the torch at the door, sees the cloud flinch, and pinches it out before laying her hand on the glass. Put "forty winters" on the CLOSED notice.
6. **State-tracking noise (POLISH).** "(Until Night 4.)" is ambiguous, and a lampwing targets Sorrel "who carries no light". The lantern counts on Nights 5 and 6 are confusing, and so is "Dun guards the lanterns, yet they go out". *Fix:* make the change visible. The lampwings' lamps turn to the same grey as the static, and from then on they see moths. Say that Dun guards *her* re-forming lantern while the wood's lanterns fall. Cut "second" and use "the wood's lanterns go out one by one."

There is also a small fit note: the existing Grove fetches an ember from the high island, but here it is "one big glint". Either call it an ember or flag the change.

## 6. At the bar? Yes

I would put this next to Ori, Hollow Knight and Nine Sols as an equal. It has a three-sentence fable that every reader retold correctly. It has a sibling bond carried by one gesture. It has an antagonist whose wrongness (keeping everyone so the leaving stops) lights up the hero's way of acting (giving herself away). And it has a true ending paid for in abilities and in a forest. Three readers would all buy it and finish it. The remaining gap is that the Hush is felt less strongly than Sorrel, which note 3 fixes. That is polish, not foundation.
