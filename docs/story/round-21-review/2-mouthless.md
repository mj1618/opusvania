# Review: The Moth and the Mouth (round 21)

## 1. Anchor gate: PASS

The casual reader's first sentence is the litmus almost word for word: "A huge living thing called the Mouth has grown over the world... it can't see moths, so Umber, a moth with a week to live, sneaks into its heart." The lore and heart readers open with "a brain grown in a dish" and bring in the moth in their second sentence. All three name it as grown from living cells, and they learn it in play at the Night 4 lab (the Pong screen, the poster, the dish with a handprint), which works as a revelation. Every act shows it at world scale and acting: the creeping edge, the road mouths, the tower root, the Grove overrun, lanterns dying behind her, then the Mouth as last boss.

Owner's ideas:
- **Biological AI took over the world:** central.
- **Umber the moth saves it:** central.
- **Moths ignored as insignificant, Umber underestimated:** central. It is rule 1, and the prologue's burrback shows it.
- **Sentience ambiguity:** central for the Mouth (its first picture, and the closing "love, or the static stopping?"). The forest's sentience comes through gifts. The animals' is thinner: it rests mostly on the vixen.

## 2. Verdict: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES

None of the problems is in the foundation. They are logic seams that can be fixed sentence by sentence.

## 3. What is at the bar

- **The rule makes the premise.** "It never noticed moths: it only sees mouths." The underestimation, the stealth, the title and the theme all come from one fact about moths.
- **The reversal turns the villain inside out.** "It has not been eating the world. It has been feeding it." It is backed by things a player finds: "jars of feed unopened in her lap", and a dish taught one ball game whose static fades rally by rally. Mythic surface, technical underneath.
- **One gesture carries the relationship.** The wing-fold, shiver and wingtip tap comes at her hatching, on Night 2 and on the rim, and all three readers named it. "Sorrel wrapped round the pale seed as root closes over the lantern" is a Nine Sols-grade loss.
- **The images rhyme.** The forest's "only picture" is a moth. The Mouth's "first picture it has ever made" is a moth. The last image is a palm on silk: "Something presses back from inside."
- **The antagonist's flaw is one line:** "A thing that takes everything can never be given anything." The Spin ending answers it with an army of moths giving.

## 4. Problems, ranked

1. **Is mouthlessness Umber's trait or every moth's?** (All three readers.) The cast line "a moth with no mouth" makes it sound like her alone. *Fix:* in the spine and the cast table, write "a moth (grown moths have no mouths, which is why they live a week)". Make the vixen an animal the Mouth has not taken, so her seeing Umber counts as a sentience beat, not a broken rule. POLISH.
2. **Eat, feed and glint-hurt contradict each other.** Mouths "draw living things down", burrbacks "feed grey cases to a mouth", and glints "hurt" the Mouth in the final fight, yet they are a gift in the ending. *Fix:* remove "hurts" from rule 2. In the boss, each glint she gives makes a mouth close and loosen its hold. In the road, show mouths sealing and hanging cases, never swallowing, so that Tallow's *EATEN* is the misreading the reveal corrects. POLISH (it is the most important note).
3. **Sorrel's Night 5→6 return.** Two readers asked how he got out and why he left. *Fix:* open Night 6 with the seed sinking first, then him lifting off the moss with nothing left to hold. His arrival becomes a choice the player has seen. POLISH.
4. **Tallow's logic.** "Every person on earth" contradicts him being awake, and "sealed in one of his own" stopped two readers. *Fix:* write "every person but those inside fire". Change the line to "sealed in a case beside hers: he came without fire". On Night 6 give him one beat with the burned cases he swore were empty. POLISH.
5. **Why warmth makes it let go.** The casual reader didn't follow it. *Fix:* when she first shivers against it, make the static fade exactly as it did under the warm hand in the prologue. Then the player learns that it was cold and left, not hungry. Trim the Spin epilogue to four images: Tallow's hand, the silver moth, the cubs, the palm. POLISH.
6. **Night 4 forces her to burn the wall, then shows her the cost.** *Fix:* add a slow, dark way round for anyone who leaves the coal at a lantern. Then the burned line on the map is the player's own choice, which also makes the Burn ending's price feel intended. POLISH.

## 5. Scores

Anchor 9 · Relationships 9 · Clarity 6 · Idea 9 · World 8 · Mechanics-as-theme 8 · Antagonist 9 · Ending 8 · Originality 8 · Grove fit 9 · Balance 8

## 6. At the bar? Yes

The emotional core, the reversal and the antagonist's flaw are all there. All three readers would finish it and remember specific images, not summaries. The biggest remaining gap is that giving and taking must run one way only (note 2). Until they do, the midpoint reversal makes players stop to puzzle when it should hit them.
