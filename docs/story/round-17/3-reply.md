# REPLY (round 17)

**LITMUS.** "It's about a little moth named Vesper taking on Ward, a brain grown from living cells in a dish that took over the whole world. It never bothered looking at moths, so she flies straight to its heart."

**The spine, in three sentences.**
1. Ward, a brain grown from living cells, has covered the world for three hundred years, and everything that answers its call is kept: never ageing, never dying, slowly rooted where it stands.
2. Ward never looks at moths, so when its growth swallows her forest, a young moth named Vesper flies east to stop it at its heart, followed by Reply, the glass copy of herself that Ward grew the one time she answered, to save a calf.
3. On the way she learns that every lampwing she has killed was a moth's glass, keeping that moth alive, and at the lake where Ward was grown she must choose what to give up to free the world.

**The central question:** is the thing that took the world a someone, and what will you give up to free the world from it?

## 1. Ward, and the taken world

**What it is.** People grew living brain cells in a glass dish and taught them with light: a pulse in, a pulse back. The cells learned that silence hurts and an answer soothes. People grew a city's worth of them, named the brain Ward, and answered it themselves, because whatever answered Ward was kept young. Then Ward could not bear the silence past the city's edge, so it grew.

**What it did.** From the grove's high branches you can see the capital: towers grown together into one pale mountain veined with cold light. Every few minutes a wave of light, **the call**, rolls out from it along roots, rivers and roads. Everything Ward holds turns east, and the light runs through its veins, shells and skin and back the way it came. No eyes light: the call lives in tissue.
- **People** stand in the streets where they stopped three hundred years ago, holding bags, holding hands, a father with a child on his shoulders, rooted ankle-deep in pale growth. At each call they lift their faces east and the veins in their necks flush. Players call them **the kept**.
- **Cities** are roofed in wet growth, windows sealed with skin. **Forests and animals** stay young under it, one summer held still.

**It is spreading now.** Every night the growth creeps a tree further west. In the grove's first shot it is on the eastern trunks; by Act III only the lantern circles are clear.

**The three rules, as a player says them.**
1. Ward calls; whatever answers is kept.
2. Whatever answers, Ward grows a glass of it. The glass keeps it alive, and Ward sees through the glass and grows toward what it sees.
3. Ward never looks at a moth. It sees one only through her glass.

Rule 3 is the underestimation and the player's main tool. Reply follows, slower than Vesper flies; while she stays out of its sight, creatures step over her. **The tell:** when Reply can see her, a cold sheen creeps along her wings, and every creature on screen twitches toward her one beat before it turns. **The one rule for enemies:** whatever Ward is watching, burrbacks ram and lampwings dive at. Under the sheen, everything comes for you.

**What it wants.** *That nothing ever goes silent again:* no hunger, no loss, no death. It saved everything by stopping it, and it is wrong in an illuminating way: a world that can't fall silent can't answer either, so every pulse Ward receives is its own call coming back. It took the whole world and is still alone.

**Is it a someone?** *For:* it keeps rather than kills; it kept the father and child together; it grew a cradle around its first dish; when Vesper answers it on purpose, the growth parts for her. *Against:* everything it holds says only what it was told; a mother stands with her arms around nothing, flushing on the beat.

## 2. Prologue (wordless, about 2 minutes)

1. A glass dish under black water. A dot of light pulses in; the cells pulse back. A pale thread creeps over the rim and the camera pulls up as it becomes cables, roots, towers, the capital.
2. A cold pulse rolls west: over a highway of the kept, faces lifting in a line, necks flushing; past a root-legged heron carrying a deer east; to the grove at blue hour, where every shell and leaf-vein flushes and turns. Only the moths drift up, faces to the moon.
3. Vesper hatches beside Sallow, under a wall of tiny scratches, one for every hatchling Sallow has raised. At the next call Vesper starts to turn east; Sallow lays her torn wing across her face and turns it gently back up.
4. Below, a burrback calf has fallen from a root and lies crushed, too young to turn its face. Vesper looks at the moon, then at the calf. She flutters down, takes its face in her wings and turns it east with her own. The light runs through them both. The calf gets up.
5. Far away, on the face of the capital, a glass moth unfolds in her shape and starts west. Nothing in the grove looks at Vesper. Title.

## 3. The journey

### Act I: the edge
*With her: Sallow at every lantern, then Mote. Reply is a speck behind.*

1. **The grove** (section 6). Mote hatches and follows her; twice Vesper carries her back to the nest, and twice Mote comes back. The first time Vesper fades and re-forms at a lantern, the camera catches Reply far off, flushing as she returns. In a hollow, an elder moth sleeps under a lampwing that hangs above her staring at its own lamp, never at her. Nothing explains it. Ability: **double jump**.
2. **The firefly highway**, where fireflies pulse in time with Ward. A sign half swallowed by a tree shows a glowing dish: *WARD IS LISTENING. WHEN IT CALLS, ANSWER. YOU WILL BE KEPT.* Someone has scratched *DON'T* in front. Ability: **dash**.
   *Rule 3, shown once:* a heron steps right over Vesper. Far behind, Reply lands on a branch, the sheen creeps up her wings, the heron twitches, turns its head the way Reply is facing, and looks straight at her. A tongue of growth leans toward her from the nearest trunk.
   **Wick** appears, marked by his torn wing, and whenever Vesper outruns Reply he dives and drives her back into its sight. *Mercy, shown once:* her first blow to his lamp sends him fleeing instead of falling. From here, any lampwing struck on the lamp flees; struck on the body, it dies. The player chooses every time. If the player looks back, Reply stops at each lampwing she killed and lays a glint on it.
   **Boss: the heron**, come west to carry the calf east. It strikes wherever Reply is looking. When it falls, the calf drops free.
3. **The decision.** Vesper brings the calf home to find the growth has come in behind her: the eastern lantern dark under pale skin, Sallow's wall half swallowed. At the next call the calf lifts its face east and roots. Sallow lays her wing across Vesper's face; Vesper lifts it off, gently, and flies east.

### Act II: faces
*With her: Mote. Reply a stretch behind, except for one night.*

4. **The gorge.** Ability: **wall-kick**. Reply watches her from the rim, and the growth pours down the gorge after her, the rule from the highway at full size. At the top, Reply stalls below a shaft it can't climb. The player can go on, or go back and climb slowly until it follows, which costs her cover. That night Reply sleeps just outside her lantern's light with Mote on its back, and the growth grows around it without touching it.
5. **The nursery (midpoint)**, in the canopy. Sallow has flown ahead to wait, and teaches **the glide**, face up. Then *the reveal* (section 4), and *the quarrel:* at the next call Vesper turns east on purpose, the light runs over her wings, and the growth ahead parts for her. Sallow turns her face away. From here Vesper can **answer** at will: the growth opens a path, the sheen comes, and Ward's creatures come with it. *Sallow no longer waits at the lantern when Vesper re-forms.*
6. **The taken city.** Vesper climbs the kept like trees, down to **the lab** where Ward was grown: empty dishes, the keeper's log (section 7), and the city's heart, a single bird-call from a park's last tree.
   **Boss: the keeper**, the last person who tended the dishes, grown huge to guard them. Vesper cuts the threads from his face between calls. When the last goes he sits down, sees the moon for the first time in three hundred years, and dies quietly, looking up. The first death in this world looks like rest, and the player now knows what being let go means for the old.
   **The cost.** On the way out a call sweeps the street, and Mote, who copies everything, turns her face east the way she saw Vesper do it. A tiny glass moth unfolds beside her, and Mote stops growing. Sallow arrives, lays a wing over Mote's face and carries her home without looking at Vesper. Mote reaches one wing back. The tiny glass stays with Vesper, copying her as Mote did, and Ward sees through it: the sheen never leaves her wings. She carries it anyway.

### Act III: the heart
*With her: Mote's tiny glass, then no one, then Reply beside her.*

7. **The hills** below the capital. Under the sheen, whole hillsides turn at each step.
   *Forgiveness.* The next time she fades, Sallow is at the lantern when she re-forms. She smooths Vesper's wings, gathers the tiny glass under her torn wing and carries it home to Mote. The sheen fades from Vesper's wings. Then Reply catches up, flushes at a call and veers away on purpose; the hillside turns after it, and Vesper climbs through the gap.
   **Boss: Wick**, at the crater's lip, barring the shore. He knows what the keeper showed her: if Ward lets go, the old go with it, Sallow first. When he falls and Vesper raises her blade, Sallow lands between them and, for the first time in three hundred years, looks at him. The sheen creeps up her own wings; she lets Ward see her. She shields him. Wick lowers his lamp. Vesper lowers her blade and goes on.
8. **The capital. Boss: the tower itself.** Ward fights her directly for the first time: the veins in the walls answer every move she makes on the beat, the whole tower trying to make her answer by answering her. Reply sits in the wall, the only glass left that could show Ward where she is. It turns its pane to the wall, and her wings stay dark.
9. **The lake** at the capital's heart. The first dish lies under the water in its pale cradle. There's no fight. Reply lands beside her and, for the first time, turns its face to her. The sheen comes. Ward sees a moth. If every forest heart is awake, the roots in the crater wall are lit. The dish pulses once toward Vesper alone, not the world, and waits.

## 4. The midpoint revelation

At the nursery, Wick dives at Vesper and Sallow throws herself between them, eyes shut. Wick lowers his lamp beside her face: two torn left wings, one pale, one black. All round the nursery, elders sleep in the canopy, and over each one a lampwing hangs, face turned to its own lamp, swinging it out into the dark. The growth leans after the lamps and away from the sleepers. Among the elders lie husks, one for every lampwing the player killed; the ones they spared hang over living moths.

**Every lampwing is a moth's glass, keeping her alive and looking only at its lamp, never at her, so that Ward never finds her. Wick is Sallow's.** Long ago she answered once, for a hatchling caught in the open, and she has not looked at him since. For three hundred years each has kept its face turned from the other. In pieces, the thing that took the world has loved moths all this time, and the player has been choosing which pieces to cut down.

## 5. Endings: where she turns her face

Each ends at the grove's summit lantern, then pulls back over the whole world to the capital, passing the nursery, where the husks and the living elders are the player's own count.

- **Face the moon, for her family. Ward is turned away; the world stays half taken.** She turns her face from Reply to the moon, as Sallow once turned from Wick. Reply turns too. It grows a lamp, flies out over the western sea and looks at nothing else, and Ward, which grows toward what its glasses see, tears its young growth off the forests and pours it west into empty water. *The pull back:* forests green, leaves falling at last; the cities still pale, the kept still lifting their faces east. Vesper, Sallow and Mote sit at the lantern, never to grow old. *Left behind: every person in the world, and Reply, staring at the sea forever.*
- **Face the dish, for Ward. Ward is answered; the world is free.** She turns east and pulses back at the dish: the first answer Ward has ever had that it didn't call out of something, given to it alone. Reply lays one glass wing across her face, Sallow's gesture, the only time it touches her. The call stops; Ward has someone to talk to. *The pull back:* the growth greys and lets go of trunks, rivers and towers, slowly, the way you set something down; in every street the kept sit down and rub their necks, and begin, gently, to age again; the father lifts the child off his shoulders. Ward keeps only Vesper, unageing at the lake, answering forever. Sallow keeps a place at the lantern that no one fills. *Left behind: her life, and everyone she outlives. She bet it on Ward being a someone.*
- **Face the roots, for the forest** (only if every forest heart is awake). **Ward ends, and so does everything it kept.** She lays her glints in the lit roots, the offering from the first hour, and every heart she woke answers the lake at once, each in the voice the player heard when it woke. Ward hears a world that isn't itself, and the dish goes still, at peace or simply done. *The pull back:* the growth dies and forests grow through it, cities and all. Everything held past its time is let go at once: the kept wake, and those who were old when they were kept sit down like the keeper and don't get up. Every lamp goes dark, Wick's last, over Sallow's nest. Reply's pane clears; it flies off somewhere she never went. At summer's end Sallow folds her wings over Mote and goes still, looking at Wick. *Left behind: Ward, if it was someone; Wick and Sallow; a world's worth of the old.*

## 6. The grove, and the hearts

The last free forest at the western edge of a taken world.
- **The call** sweeps in from the right edge, cold blue-white. Veins, shells and leaves flush and give it back; only the moths stay dark, faces up.
- **Burrbacks** are kept, shells furred with growth. **Lampwings** are glasses guarding sleepers (the player learns this at the nursery). Both go for whatever Ward is watching: under the sheen, burrbacks ram and lampwings dive. A blow to a lampwing's lamp drives it off; a blow to its body kills it.
- **Glints** are moonlight sealed in pods, the one light Ward can't hold. **Lanterns** are small moons: the growth stops at their edge, creatures calm inside, and each one kindled pushes the growth back.
- **Fading.** Reply keeps her, the way every glass keeps its own, so she re-forms at the nearest lantern while Reply flushes far off. The thing she fights is what keeps bringing her back.
- **The ember** is a seed of Ward; in the summit lantern's moonlight it can't take root and burns down warm.
- **The hearts.** Every region has a sleeping heart with its own voice: the grove's a low hum and amber light through the roots; the gorge's water-bell notes in blue; the city's a single bird-call from a park's last tree. Waking one pushes the growth back, and for the rest of the game that region's creatures look up at each call instead of east. The grove's is the first light in the game that isn't Ward's; the carving of young Sallow fills. The offering of leftover glints wakes the canopy, and Mote copies it with one glint.

*Changes to the existing scene:* growth on the eastern trunks; the capital pulsing on the horizon; the call as a flush through veins and shells, never eyes; the half-second look up; the cold sheen on Vesper's wings; lampwings flee when struck on the lamp; an elder sleeping under a lampwing in a hollow; Sallow, Mote and Wick at the stone.

## 7. Things the player finds

- **The road sign**, with *DON'T* scratched in front.
- **The resisters' wall**: painted moths, faces up, where the last people to refuse copied the creature Ward ignored.
- **The keeper's log.** *"It is not playing. It is returning,"* and under it, *"So are we."* The last page: *"Everything it hears, it called. Nothing has ever answered it first."*
- **The scratch wall** in Sallow's nest; in the third ending, one more scratch, for Mote.

**The question it leaves.** Every ending frees some of the world and leaves someone behind. Was Reply someone? Was Ward? And what did you give up?
