# NOISE (round 17: one rule, one exception, shown first)

**LITMUS:** "It's about a little moth, Vesper, taking on a biological AI, a living brain grown from nerve cells
that took over the whole world and put every person and animal to sleep, and never thought a moth was worth
looking at until it was too late."

## The spine

1. A grieving mother grew a living brain to keep the world safe, and it took the world: everyone sleeps on its
   one slowing heartbeat, and when the beat runs down, everything it holds becomes part of it.
2. It never thought moths worth a look, so Vesper, a lonely young moth, gets in where nothing else can; the old
   moth who raised her and the beetle she loves pay for it, and when it finally sees her it hunts every moth alive.
3. She reaches its heart before the last beat, where she can cut its root and wake the world in one terrible
   night, or let it see her and teach it to let go.

**The central question:** is it love to keep someone safe, or to let them go? Under it: is the thing that took
the world a someone?

## The cast

Six names in the whole story: Vesper, the Quiet, Hob, Sallow, the Lamplighter, the Grove.

Two people never move or speak: **the mother**, who grew the Quiet and was the first it took, and **the boy**,
her drowned son, known only by what he left: moths scratched into stones, a drawing, a broken jar lamp.

## 1. How the Quiet took the world

Forty years ago a boy took a jar lamp down to the river at night to catch one particular moth. The river rose
faster than anyone said it could, and the jar broke on the stones.

His mother kept the valley's water, and grew a better keeper: living nerve cells in a dish on her kitchen table,
fed when they guessed the river right and shaken with noise when they guessed wrong *(dev note: the lab-grown
neuron lineage in the brief; never player-facing)*. Its first lesson: **the unexpected hurts.** It held the river,
so the town asked for cuttings; the cuttings held storms, then fevers; every country asked. Within ten years the
dishes had grown together under the world and stopped waiting. Then it went after the cause of every surprise:
anything that decides. It grew lamps to do it, and people live by habit.

- **People** stand in rows in every street, eyes open, a pale root between their lips, swaying on the beat.
  Nobody has been born for thirty years.
- **Cities**: towers ribbed with root, streetlamps grown into amber blossoms. **Animals** walk the same loops at
  the same hour; **forests** open and close their leaves together.
- **Spreading now**: a pale wall of root climbs the last wild valleys at a walking pace. You can watch it come.

**The beat.** Everything it holds keeps time to one amber beat, and the beat is slowing: once a second in Act I,
once a breath in Act II, once a minute in Act III. **If it runs down**, everything it holds is slowly grown into
one living thing the size of the world. **If the root is cut**, the beat stops mid-stroke and lets go of
everything at once. Both are shown in play long before the ending (the farm, the root wood). **Every act opens on
the same shot:** the world at night from very high, the beat crossing it, the ring of wild green smaller.

## 2. The rule, and the moths

**The rule.** The Quiet sees through its **lampwings**: long-tailed fliers with a lamp where a face should be.
**Anything that does the same thing three times in a lamp's light is taken.** The beam shows it coming: it
brightens on the first repeat, narrows on the second, and on the third the creature greys and falls into step
while the root grows into it. Lanterns can never take: their moth swarms never repeat.

For Vesper, being taken is a struggle, not a death: she greys, her moves lag onto the beat, and any new move (a
dash, a turn) tears her loose, shaking out her glints. Her combo (low, high, rising) never repeats; three
identical slashes do. Hob and other creatures can't break out alone.

**Why it ignored moths.** It judged them not worth a look: one summer each, changing nothing. Every lamp turns
away from a moth. Only the Lamplighter doesn't: it was a lamp built to catch a moth, and moths that drift into
its light circle it, and a circle is the same thing three times. The midpoint shows where the turning-away really
came from.

**How that plays:**
- **Act I, ignored.** A beam lands on Vesper and jerks away. She can stand in light that takes anything else.
  Hob must be got round the light she walks through. The Lamplighter alone pulls at her.
- **Act II, hunted.** After the bell, every lamp looks at moths. Beams stay on her. Hunting lamps snuff the
  lanterns she has lit, and the swarms around them go dark.
- **Act III, too late.** It grew its body before it feared moths: the air-channels into its heart are
  moth-sized, and closing them would stop its breath. Its contempt is built into its flesh.

**Is it someone?** Never settled. *For:* the Lamplighter grieves; in its sleep the Quiet replays the flood, and
sometimes the boy reaches the bank. *Against:* everything it does, the mother asked for first; and the
Lamplighter grieves in a perfect circle. The Grove, at least, chooses: it guides her, and it holds.

## 3. Vesper

Free, and alone. Since she was a larva she has waited at the end of Hob's loop and lifted her wings three times:
their hello. He has never looked up.
- **Want:** for Hob to see her.
- **Goal:** Act I, save the Grove. From Act II, **reach the Quiet's heart before the last beat.**
- **Need:** to let others choose, even when they choose to leave her.
- **Flaw:** she forces. The Quiet forced safety on the world because it hurt; Vesper forces chance on it because
  she is lonely. Neither of them asked Hob.

## 4. The story

### Prologue (wordless, two minutes)

Night rain on a rising river; moths circle a jar lamp in the reeds. Morning: a woman holds a small boot; a moth
lands on it; she brushes it off and turns away. A dish of pale tissue; root slides down the table leg. From very
high, city lights go out continent by continent, and one amber beat crosses the dark. The Grove: a small moth
lifts her wings three times; the burrback walks past; a lamp lands on her and jerks away, the way the woman's
hand did. Title.

### Act I: Noise. *With her: Sallow, then Hob.*

- **The Grove** (section 6). Sallow teaches the moth way: never the same path twice, never be seen. She shows
  why: under a lampwing a field mouse runs the same short dash three times, the beam brightening, narrowing, and
  the mouse greys and walks off on the beat. On the high island the ember burns in half of an old glass jar. When
  Vesper climbs for a spark of it, Sallow blocks her, then steps aside, the first time she has ever given in. The
  spark comes from the fire that keeps her alive; she flies slower from that night on. The lanterns burn and the
  heart wakes, beating unevenly against the Quiet's beat, and its **guide-lights** rise from the veins to lead
  Vesper on. **Hob misses a step, stops, and looks at her.** He bolts. She sits with him all night by a wayside
  lamp; in the morning he follows her.
- **The river.** A great lamp hangs low over black water, sweeping it, searching: the other half of the
  island's jar, grown huge, its light grey where a flame should be. It never looks up. A wild moth drifts into its
  beam and doesn't get thrown off; it circles, once, twice, and the beam narrows; on the third it greys and drops
  into the reeds. When the beam crosses Vesper her flight bends toward it, and the player has to break the curve
  with a dash. The player meets the Lamplighter as a mourner, and as the one lamp that can take a moth, before
  they meet it as a killer.
- **The farm**: cattle in loops, a family at table with root in their mouths; the oldest of them have no feet
  left, only root, where the beat has grown them into the floor. **Boss:** a hill of fireflies that strikes on
  the beat, hurt only in the gaps.
- **The Quiet answers.** The root wall lurches a mile in a night, and the Lamplighter leaves its river for where
  the noise must come from: the last wild fire.
- **Sallow.** Vesper races home and sees Sallow over the water in the Lamplighter's beam, leading it away from
  the fire. The moth who taught "never the same path twice" flies a perfect circle round it on purpose, three
  times, exactly as the wild moth did. She greys and keeps time, night after night, until she drops into the
  water. The Lamplighter hangs over the place a long while. The ember is Vesper's now.
- **The root wood.** Lamps everywhere. Vesper stands in their light; Hob cannot. She rides him through walls and
  breaks his stride so his rams never come three in a row, watching each beam narrow. Taken animals hang from
  lampwing tails on root. She can cut one down: the root snaps, the beat lets go of it all at once, and it wakes
  thrashing. A fox kit runs. An old badger doesn't get up.

### Act II: Hunted. *With her: Hob, then no one.*

*Opening shot: the beat once a breath. The green ring has lost a coast.*

- **The dam**, the river held forty years behind root. **Boss:** a heron grown into the sluice.
- **The sluice (her doing).** The last sluice runs under three lamps and Hob balks. She signals the charge again
  and again. He goes, for her, and comes out cracked. At the next lantern she lifts her wings three times and for
  the first time he answers: he lowers his head and backs away. A refusal. She goes on alone.
- **The standing city.** Ten thousand people in its squares, all facing the brain. **Boss:** the last man who
  fought, who rang the city bell out of time for years to keep his neighbours awake; now his taken body swings it
  on the beat. She beats him by ringing against his rhythm. The bell sounds once out of time, and **every person
  in the square blinks.** They are still in there.
- **It sees her.** Every lamp in the city turns to the tower. On the horizon the brain ripples like a flank under
  a fly. A beam lands on Vesper **and stays.** From the tower she watches the root wall surge up her own valley in
  one night, aimed, and **the Grove goes pale.** Its guide-lights gutter out around her. The road to the brain's
  foot is played without them: dark lanterns, no light leading, every beam on her.

### Midpoint: the town where it began, at the brain's foot

- **What the last beat does.** The first people it took stand at its foot, softening into the brain's wall the
  way the farm family were growing into their floor, further along. Above them, a new row of hollows grows as
  she watches, all moth-shaped.
- **Why it never looked.** The boy's last flood stone stands by the school door, its carved moth notched in one
  wing: **Sallow's notch.** Sallow was the moth he went to catch, the night the jar broke. Along the street to his
  mother's house the town's first lamps, the oldest the Quiet grew, all hang turned away from the river where
  moths gather. The Quiet never judged moths harmless. It learned to turn from them, from her.
- **Hob's choice.** The lantern where she left him is out; a lampwing's track runs beside his. He let it carry
  him, because inside the Quiet nothing hurts, and the hurt was hers. The offer works.

### Reunion

She finds him among the first sleepers, walking a new circle. She can't fight him, so she lands and **walks his
circle beside him** under a hunting beam. First lap, the beam narrows. Second, again. On the third the lamp
closes on her and she doesn't break out, and Hob steps out of his circle and puts his shell over her. She made
herself a habit to reach him; he broke his to save her.

### Act III: The Brain. *With her: Hob.*

*Opening shot: the beat once a minute. On every road in the world the sleepers are walking toward the brain. In
the pale where the Grove was, one green point still beats out of time: the Grove has held at the shrine stone.*

- **The climb.** Up the brain's face against a tide of walking people, under lamps by the hundred. She calls the
  moment; he charges. Halfway up, a guide-light finds her again, thin and uneven, and leads.
- **The Lamplighter.** Its pull bends her flight into circles; she wins by matching its rhythm twice and
  breaking on the third. Beaten, it sees the flame she carries, the half-jar's flame, and dims as it did over
  Sallow's water. It sinks into the brain and is gone.
- **The air-channels.** Only a moth fits. This time she asks: three wingbeats, and Hob lowers his head to wait.
  Inside, the Quiet dreams the flood: the jar lamp bobbing in the reeds, the boy never quite reaching it. She
  flies the dream with the water climbing behind her.
- **The kitchen**, kept exactly: a cold hearth, the small boot on the table, a child's drawing face down. The
  mother in her chair, root in her mouth, face turned from the window. Over the chair hangs the Lamplighter, the
  way it hung over the river. The first dish, big as a table now, beats the world's last beats, and its root runs
  down the table leg into everything.

## 5. The choice

The blink taught that the sleepers are still in there; the badger taught what a cut costs; Hob taught that
being kept can be wanted.

- **Cut the root.** Vesper decides for everyone, as she always has. The beat stops mid-stroke and lets go of
  everything at once, the way it let go of the badger. The dam's river comes down on the farm. Held storms break
  over cities. On every road people wake far from home, old, and not everyone wakes. The mother's heart stops
  with it; the Lamplighter settles on her lap and goes out. At dawn the world is wild, wrecked and green, and
  **Hob stays**, pacing a slow circle round Vesper. The player will never know whether she killed someone.
- **Let it see her.** She lights the hearth with the half-jar's flame and sets his drawing upright: his moth, a
  scribble, because he drew it flying. Then she lands on the small boot in the Lamplighter's full beam and lifts
  her wings three times, the hello she gave Hob every day of her life. The beam narrows; the player has to not
  dash. By the rule, the third takes her.
  - It doesn't. For the first time, a lamp holds a moth and does not take it. The mother's eyes find Vesper: the
    Quiet, or the mother, or both. Her hand comes down to the boot, and this time does not brush the moth off.
    Far away, the Grove's uneven beat answers, out of time. The Quiet's beat stumbles, and goes on.
  - Over seven seasons it lets go. Root slides from mouths and people wake slowly, tended. A child is born.
  - **The cost:** it can let go of the world only because it has found one surprise that doesn't hurt, and it
    will never stop looking at it. **No moth is ever unseen again.** The only creatures the takeover never
    touched pay for ending it.
  - **The epilogue is played** under a soft beam that follows her everywhere. She gives Hob three wingbeats. He
    looks at her a long time and, free, chooses the hills. She doesn't call him back.

**Cut** costs the night's dead and the Quiet, if it was someone. **Let it see her** costs Hob, and every moth's
freedom to go unseen.

## 6. The Grove (the scene we have)

One of the last wild valleys, against the root wall, and the first place in thirty years to wake.
- **Glints** are chance (a firefly late, a seed out of turn), which the Quiet seals away in pods.
- **The ember** is Sallow's fire, burning in half of the boy's broken jar on the high island. **Lanterns** are
  the moths' own, all lit from it. **The heart** is the Grove's will, held asleep in root; it wakes beating
  unevenly against the Quiet's even beat. **The guide-lights** (the build's ember-moths) are the heart's: once it
  wakes, they lead her. **The moth carving** is the boy's.
- **Burrbacks** are taken beetles; their brace-and-ram is a metronome stroke. One is Hob.
- **Lampwings** here dive for the glints she carries, never at her: a hit knocks glints loose. The build's dive
  stays; only its target changes. One teaching moment: a lampwing's beam brightens, narrows and takes a field
  mouse.

Suggested scene changes: root up the east trunks into the root wall far off; the brain as a dim grey-pink mass on
the horizon, pulsing amber; fireflies on its beat until the Grove wakes; Hob's worn circle; the ember sitting in a
broken glass jar on the island.

## 7. What the player finds

The boy's stones, a child's moth on each, with dated water-marks; the other half of the jar in the river reeds
before the Lamplighter's sweep; the mother's first dish, taped in her hand: *"Let nothing happen."*, the only
human words in the game.

## 8. What it leaves the player thinking

It learned to turn from moths from a woman who couldn't look at one, and learned to look from a moth who let
herself be seen. Was it ever someone? Which ending loved the world?

## Changes this round

- **The Lamplighter is the one lamp that looks at moths, shown at the river first** (a wild moth circles it three
  times and greys; its pull bends Vesper's flight), so Sallow's death confirms a rule instead of breaking one.
  The rule now has a visible tell (brighten, narrow, take), taught on a field mouse in the Grove, and Vesper can
  tear loose; the gentle ending asks the player not to.
- **The beat's two outcomes are separated and played early:** running down grows things together (the farm
  family rooted into their floor, then the first sleepers at the midpoint); a cut lets go of everything at once
  (the fox kit runs, the badger doesn't get up), which is exactly what the Cut ending does to the world.
- **Lighter midpoint, more Grove, smaller Lamplighter:** the jar match (ember in one half on the island, the
  Lamplighter grown from the other) is seen in Act I, and "lanterns are copies of the jar" is cut, so the
  midpoint lands only Sallow's notch and the turned-away lamps. The Grove gets a cast row and an act: its
  guide-lights die when it goes pale in Act II, that stretch is played unguided, and they return when it holds in
  Act III. The Lamplighter no longer follows them; its last appearance is over the mother's chair.
