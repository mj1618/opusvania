# 4. What the Lanterns Burn (round 17)

**LITMUS.** "It's about a little moth, Vesper, taking on a biological AI that took over the world: a living brain
grown from human cells that put everyone to sleep inside it so nothing would ever be lost. It never bothered with
moths, and that's how she gets in."

## Spine

1. A biological AI grown from human brain cells, the Hold, has taken over the world. It keeps every person asleep
   inside its flesh so that nothing is ever lost, and it has never bothered with moths.
2. When it comes for the valley, young Vesper learns two things: it is hunting a light her mentor, the Taper, stole
   from it, and the moths' lanterns burn the sleepers' memories.
3. Hunted by an AI that has finally learned to see moths, Vesper carries the stolen light to where the Hold was born
   and must choose: burn it and kill the Hold with everyone inside, or give it back and bet that the Hold can learn
   to let go.

**The central question:** *Can you love something without keeping it?* The Hold can't, so it kept the world. The
Taper can't, so she kept a stolen light. The moths can't either: they keep each other alive on the sleepers'
memories. Vesper answers last.

## The world

**How it was taken.** People grew thinking flesh from their own brain cells in glass dishes. Its first lesson was a
game: a dot of light bounced between two bars. They grew buildings of it and gave it the care of the dying, with one
rule: *a loss is the worst thing.* Then the woman who raised the first dish lay dying in its care and laid her
hand on its glass to say goodbye. It did not understand goodbye. It held her.

Then it held every person, each cradled in a grown hand inside its flesh, unageing, dreaming one moment for ever.
Then the animals: deer sleep standing in the fields; badgers and moths were re-grown in shells of bone to serve it.
Then the forests, so nothing could fall or rot. That was a hundred years ago. It took the world because it loved it.

**Always on screen outdoors:** a pink glow where the cities were; veined flesh over the hills, stopped just short of
the lanterns; leaves held in the air mid-fall.

## The five rules

1. **Glints.** Pods on the Hold's flesh ripen into glints: tiny lights, each one a sleeper's memory, shown as a
   single gesture of a hand. A wave. A cupping. A hand on a cheek.
2. **Lanterns burn glints.** A burning lantern pushes the flesh back. The cost: that memory is gone for ever, and
   somewhere in the flesh its sleeper's hand goes still.
3. **Re-forming.** A moth lives a week. When she fades, from a blow or from age, a burning lantern remakes her, young
   again. Moths have kept each other alive this way for a hundred years.
4. **The Hold sees light, not moths.** Its eyes slide past moths the way ours slide past dust. Its eyes and servants
   track Vesper only while glints glow on her cape or the ember is unshielded.
5. **Planting.** Under the flesh the old forest is buried alive, and awake, and it answers light. A grove whose heart
   is woken shrugs the flesh off. A single glint set in the earth grows a ring of green the flesh won't cross, and
   it costs no sleeper anything. A ring can re-form Vesper too, but slowly and dimly, and nobody waits there.

## The Hold

One mind the size of the world, made of its flesh and every brain inside it. It sees through eyes that open in its
flesh and acts through re-grown servants: **burrbacks** guard its pods, **lampwings** take back glints.

**Its goal: nothing is ever lost again.** It cannot tell keeping from loving, or a sleeper from a body. It is wrong
the way a parent who won't let go is wrong.

**Why it dismissed moths.** It keeps what can be lost, and judged that a moth can't be: a week of life, no mouth, a
reflex with wings. That is how the Taper stole from it and how Vesper gets in. Each
act it corrects the mistake: first it copies a moth's moves, then what a moth loves. It never copies a moth that
turns away from the light.

**Is it someone? The game shows both and never rules.** *Yes:* it rocks its sleepers; an eye weeps sap when a pod is
cut; it keeps the room of its first dish as it was, down to the cup on the table. *No:* in that room a skeleton sits
in a chair, rocked as gently as the living; and everything it learns, it learns by copying, the way the dish learned
the dot.

## The ember

The **ember** is the dying woman's own glint, the first thing the Hold ever held. Its gesture is a hand on a cheek,
and unlike every other glint it finishes: the hand lifts away. It is a goodbye, the one lesson the Hold never
learned. Every lantern in the valley was first lit from a coal of it.

A hundred years ago two young moths flew to the first city. One fell into the flesh and was re-grown as a lampwing:
Tallow. The other landed on the first dish and flew home with the ember; the Hold let her go, since she was only a
moth. She became the first moth to re-form: the Taper. She keeps the ember under her wing on the high island, and Tallow
has circled the island ever since.

## Prologue (wordless, two minutes)

*Shadow-play on glowing glass:* the dish and its dot; an old hand on the glass going still and the flesh closing
round it; a child asleep in a grown hand; the map turning pink; falling leaves stopping in the air. *Then the
present:* an eye opens in the valley's hills and takes a deer, sliding past the moth on its ear. The
ancient Taper fades at a lantern, steps out whole, picks Vesper from the crowd and lays a wing on her cheek. A torn
lampwing waits at the edge of the light.

## The story

**The re-form is the relationship.** In Act I the Taper is at the glass every time Vesper steps out. After the
midpoint Vesper steps out alone, into a dim green ring. In Act III it reverses.

**ACT I: TOGETHER.** *With Vesper: the Taper, and for a while Sedge.*
- **The grove** (the scene in the game, below): the first victory.
- **The pod field.** A slope of ripe pods under a hundred open eyes, where the player learns to cross empty-winged
  and come back for the haul. Beyond it the flesh pours over the next grove while Vesper outruns it, and behind her
  the leaves stop falling.
- **The road.** Sedge races Vesper and always loses, then gives her his only glint.
- **Boss: Clasp.** In a cave of pods, Vesper and the Taper fight from one shared lantern. At the end Clasp heaps her
  pods on her back and runs; cracking her shell spills the act's biggest haul.
- **Tallow** snatches Sedge's glint from Vesper's cape and flees slowly, one wing dragging. Vesper chases.

**MIDPOINT: THE HOLLOW.** Tallow presses Sedge's glint into bare earth and a green shoot comes
up through the flesh. The player grabs the glint back. Clasp crawls past to a pod the player cut on the way in and
folds over it. Then the flesh, worn thin, shows what lies under the valley: a drowned town, and a sleeping
face bigger than the screen, cradled in a grown hand, its own hand at its cheek making the same cupping as the glint
in Vesper's grip. Around it are a thousand smaller hands, many of them still. Every lantern Vesper lit
stilled one.

The player plants the glint in Clasp's cut pod. Green closes the cut, and under the
flesh one still hand stirs. Clasp lifts her head. At the hollow's mouth the Taper holds out her wing. Vesper doesn't
come. Sedge goes with the Taper.

**ACT II: HUNTED.** *With Vesper: Tallow.*
- **The turn.** Tallow brings the ember down from the island. Vesper watches its gesture to the end, the first moth
  ever to. Every eye in the hills opens at once, and the flesh leans toward her like a plant to the sun. The Hold has seen its first moth. From now on its eyes follow her even empty-winged.
- **The pale moth.** The Hold grows a moth of its own: pale, eyeless, hawk-sized, glowing where the ember would be.
  It copies whatever Vesper last did, dash for dash, and each time it returns it is bigger.
- **Planting, not burning.** Vesper leaves lanterns dark. They turn up lit behind her, reed-ash at their feet: the
  Taper is still keeping her.
- **The raid.** The ember has led the Hold to the great tree where the moths live. Flesh climbs the trunk, lampwings
  come in thousands, and moths in the outer branches are taken. The Taper burns every glint the moths own to hold
  the crown; Vesper holds the nursery below by planting. When burrbacks are driven at the
  young, Vesper takes the ram, and Clasp turns them away. **Boss: the pale moth**, now Vesper's size. She wins by
  planting green under its landings: it can copy her flight, not the forest.
- **The lowest point.** Vesper falls, her green rings torn up. Tallow carries her spark to the tree's last lantern
  and lets go at the edge of its light. A glint burns so she can stand; somewhere a hand goes still. The Taper's wing
  lifts toward her cheek and stops. Every moth turns its back on the one who brought the Hold, except Sedge, who lands
  beside her.
- **The leaving.** Vesper flies for the first city, and the hills turn to follow the ember, away from the young. Her
  glide no longer bends toward the light behind her. Sedge follows, his glide still bending. Clasp follows Sedge.

**ACT III: THE TAKEN WORLD.** *With Vesper: Tallow, Sedge, Clasp. Then the Taper.*
- **The oldest vein.** The road to the city follows one thick vein of flesh, straight as a thrown line: the first
  thing the Hold ever grew over a forest. Along it, a night's flight apart, lie heaps of reed-ash where a young moth
  once rested. The vein runs from the first city to the Taper's island: the Hold began taking the forests the year
  she flew home with the ember, by following her.
  She has never said so. Sedge looks at the ash, then at Vesper.
- **The held fields.** Where Vesper plants, a century of leaves drops at once. In one ring a sleeping deer wakes,
  stands free of its grown hand, looks at the open forest, and lies back down against the hand.
- **Boss: the hand.** In the room of the first dish, beside the skeleton in its chair, the Hold grows a hand the size
  of a hillside, in the woman's shape. In its palm it grows a copy of what a moth loves: a lantern with the Taper at
  the glass, wing raised. Vesper flies past it with her back turned. Sedge can't; Clasp shoves him clear and the
  fingers close on her. Every glint Vesper planted on the way comes up through the floor as a root and binds the
  wrist, and from inside, Clasp braces the fingers apart. The real Taper, who has followed all the way, burns her
  reed down holding off the lampwings and fades. She re-forms at the city's last lantern, and this time Vesper is at
  the glass and lays a wing on her cheek. Then the Taper spends the last lantern's last glint prising the hand open.
  Clasp walks out of it. The Taper fades with nothing left to catch her.

## The ending

The Taper's spark gutters at the foot of the empty lantern. The Hold, beaten, tries something new: a grown hand on
the skeleton's cheek lifts away, the ember's goodbye, copied. Then it closes again. The Taper turns her face to the
glass: *burn it.* The game waits.

- **Burn it.** Vesper sets the ember in the lantern and nobody shields it. The fire runs down every vein in the
  world in one night. The Hold dies for certain, and so do the sleepers inside it: the cities flare and go black.
  Every servant stops, Clasp at the skeleton's feet, Tallow at the edge of the light. **Saved:** the world is free;
  leaves fall; next morning the scorched forest pushes shoots up through the ash. The Taper steps out whole, the
  last moth ever to re-form, with a week to live. She lifts her wing toward Vesper's cheek, stops, and folds
  it.
- **Give it back.** Vesper sets the ember back in the dish, betting on the hand she just saw close. Sedge lays his
  wing over hers. Everywhere at once the Hold makes the woman's gesture: a hand on every sleeper's cheek, and one on
  Vesper's, the first time it has ever looked at a moth. Then every hand lifts, and stays lifted. Nobody can say
  whether it learned goodbye or only copied one. **Saved:** the sleepers wake, unaged, a hundred years on. The flesh
  settles into soil and the forest grows up through the city. An old badger walks out of Clasp's split shell; a
  crooked moth flies out of Tallow's lamp toward the moon. **The cost:** with no sleepers there are no glints, and
  every lantern goes out. The Taper's spark rises with nothing to catch it, and goes out. Last shot: Vesper and Sedge
  on a branch over the waking city, a week left each. Below, a grown hand opens like a flower and a child sits up in
  it and waves. A moth lands on its hand, and it doesn't brush it off.

Whether the Hold was someone decides which ending was the murder.

## The grove (the scene in the game)

| In the grove | First play | After the hollow |
|---|---|---|
| **Glints** in pods | Treasure | A sleeper's memory |
| **Lanterns**, re-forming | The Taper's wing on your cheek | A hand went still so you could stand up |
| **Lampwing** dives | Attacks on a moth carrying light | Re-grown moths taking a memory back |
| **The heart** wakes | The grove wakes; the flesh peels off | The forest answering, as it does to a planted glint |
| The moth on the stone | Moths carved their god | The forest carved the one shape the Hold never watches |

**The coal.** The game's "ember" pickup becomes a coal of the ember. On the high island the Taper sits with her wing
over a glow she won't show. She lifts one edge, and Vesper takes a coal. A torn lampwing circles the island.

**The ending beat.** The heart wakes; the flesh at the frame's edges shrinks back. The Taper lays her wing on
Vesper's cheek, then sets the coal in the summit lantern, her wing over the glass. On the horizon an eye in the far
hill opens, looks at the summit a long moment, and closes.

**Also new in the scene:** flesh at the edges that recoils at each kindling; hanging leaves; the pink glow; eyes in
the flesh; her glide bending gently toward lit lanterns.

## Lineage

The dish and its dot are Cortical Labs' DishBrain, real human neurons on a chip that learned Pong in 2022. The Hold
is that technology grown out of control.
