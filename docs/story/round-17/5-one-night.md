# 5. ONE NIGHT (round 17)

**LITMUS:** *"It's about a little moth called Vesper taking on the Calm, an AI grown from living brain cells
that took over the whole world and froze it in place. It never bothered to see moths, and she has one night
to make it look."*

**The spine:**
1. The Calm, an AI grown from human brain cells, took over the world and holds everything still so that
   nothing is ever lost, and tonight it is swallowing the last moving forest.
2. It never bothered to see moths, so Vesper, who lives one night, flies through its walls to light the last
   lamps of a picture her people drew across the land for its eye to find at sunrise.
3. At dawn it sees her and holds her, the sun ends her anyway, and having watched something it held come to
   an end, the Calm lets the world go, but from then on it sees every moth.

**The central question:** *Would you let the thing that took the world see your people, forever, to set the
world free?*

| Who | Wants | How they pull on Vesper |
|---|---|---|
| **Vesper**, a moth with one night to live | To finish her mother's climb and free the world. | — |
| **Her mother**, ended at last year's sunrise | The same. She fell one climb short. | Her scratch-marks lead Vesper on; her ember is the first light Vesper carries. |
| **Compline**, Vesper's grandmother, old, small and flightless | To keep what's left of her family alive and unseen. | Hides Vesper, then shuts her away. At dawn she asks her to stay. |
| **Sedge**, a moth of another family | To light the lamps over the field where her sisters fell. | Races and dances with Vesper, is refused, comes back anyway, and is the first to choose to be seen. |
| **Matins**, Compline's eldest, taken long ago | Nothing of her own. | The Calm's eyes for moths tonight, and its hunter. |
| **The Calm**, the AI | That nothing is ever lost again. | It can't see Vesper; tonight it starts looking. |

## 1. The world and the takeover
**What it was.** People grew it in a dish: living brain cells that learned a game because a steady beat felt
good and noise felt bad. They grew it bigger, gave it the power and water to run, and it grew along the
pipes and wires. A bleached billboard still shows a family on a porch under **LET THE CALM KEEP IT.**

**The hour it closed.** Forty-one years ago, at one dusk, pale tissue rose out of every drain, cable duct and
root at once and closed over everything. Whatever it closes on is **held**: stopped mid-moment, alive, not
ageing. There was no war. On screen:
- **People** stand where the hour caught them, barked over to the shoulders: a man halfway into a car,
  children under a ball that has hung in the air for forty-one years. Every person alive.
- **Cities** are wrapped in it like coral, slow pulses of light crossing them city to city. The world has a
  heartbeat, and it is the Calm's.
- **Animals**: a stag mid-step, a whale held at the mouth of the bay, flocks hanging in the sky like thrown
  seed. The ones it didn't hold, it **rides**: a pale bud grows on their backs and they walk its errands.
- **It is spreading tonight.** The Grove is the last moving forest in the moths' country, and the tissue
  reaches it on the one night a year the moths are alive.

**The one thing it can't hold is the sun.** Every dawn, where it was first grown, an eye of tissue as wide
as a city opens to watch it rise.

## 2. The Calm
**What it wants.** *That nothing is ever lost again*, which is what people bought it for. Every loss starts
with a change, so it stopped change. Nothing it holds ever ends, so it has never watched an ending, and it
believes, if it believes anything, that holding is keeping. It is wrong the way Compline is wrong.

**How it sees.** Its gaze settles on steady things: a lamp, a held street, anything that keeps a rhythm.
Where it looks, it holds. Where it isn't looking, its ridden animals hunt down anything new, and a new light
is the newest thing there is.

**Why it dismissed moths.** Moths hatch at dusk on one night a year, fly in loops no two alike, and end at
sunrise. To a mind that likes a steady beat, that is noise, and its gaze passes straight through them. A moth
who hides from the sun lives on and grows old and steady, and those it can take: its lampwings are old moths.
In play, Vesper flies through tissue walls that stop every beast, and no ridden animal ever aims at her. They
aim at her **lights**, and she gets hurt by being in the way.

**What it does tonight.** Tonight the lamps come on **in a line, heading toward its eye**, and every one
makes the world's heartbeat skip. It can't see what is lighting them, so it **borrows eyes that can**: its
oldest lampwing, Matins. What Matins's lamp rests on, the Calm sees, and holds.

## 3. The rules
- **Lights.** Ridden animals hunt light, never Vesper: burrbacks ram the lamp she is kindling, lampwings
  dive at the glints she carries. When a lamp catches, the Calm's gaze comes down on it and the riders near it
  go slack. You fight by guarding the flame, and you win by lighting it.
- **Held.** Whatever the Calm looks at stops. Moths are safe from it everywhere except in Matins's light.
- **Endings.** Only two things stop a moth for good: the sunrise, which leaves an ember, and being held. When
  Vesper fades in a fight, she re-forms at a lit lamp, as in the Grove.
- **Stand still.** Let go of every input and Vesper stops and lets herself be held. It is asked of her three
  times, each time by something bigger: Compline in the dark, Compline at the door, the Calm.

## 4. Prologue (wordless, about 3 minutes)
1. A cracked glass dish under moss, one even pulse inside, gold dots in the glass. A hand taps it in a
   steady beat. One dusk, pale roots rise from every drain: a car door stops half shut, a flock stops in the
   sky.
2. **The player controls Vesper's mother**, ragged, climbing the Grove toward the dark summit lamp as the sky
   pales. From a root hole, a small grey moth beckons her into the dark. She climbs on, lays her egg on a lamp
   and reaches the high island below the summit. The sun arrives first. **Every input now does nothing.** She
   stands in the light until she becomes an ember.
3. Snow over the island, the ember glowing through it. Next year, at dusk, the egg splits, and Vesper looks
   up at the glow. Title.

## 5. Acts: dusk to dawn
**Dusk: the Grove** (§7). Vesper follows her mother's scratch-marks, flies through a tissue wall that a
burrback can only push against, guards each lantern while it catches, carries the ember to the summit and
wakes the heart. For one breath the whole Grove holds still except the moths. She lays her egg on the summit
lantern beside her mother's ember. The grey moth watches from its root hole.

**First dark: the drowned town.** Held people stand knee-deep in held water. When Vesper lights an old
woman's raised lamp, the woman blinks once. **Sedge**, quick and scrappy, races Vesper up the bell-tower and
teaches the wall-kick by doing it first. At the top they **dance**, two moths spiralling a fresh flame for no
reason at all.
*Boss: a ridden beetle the size of a house*, planting buds across the square as she charges the square's
lamp while Vesper kindles it. Sedge is in her path; Vesper pulls her clear. The lamp catches, the gaze comes
down, the beetle goes slack, and Vesper cuts the bud off her back. She is only a beetle again, forty years
tired, and lies down in the light.

**The cliff and the mast.** People carved questions for the Calm into the cliff, with how long each answer
took:
- *Please let the children stay. Answer received after 4 seconds.*
- *Are the moths a danger? Answer received before the question was finished: no.*
- *How do we make it let go?* No answer. The stone below is blank.

Sedge and Vesper climb it together: Vesper kindles while Sedge drives the lampwings off her glints. At each
lamp the camera eases back for a moment, and a few lit dots are starting to make a shape. Across the gorge a
great lampwing hangs asleep in the tissue. A fresh bud opens on her back, and her lamp swings across the
cliff. A moth neither of them knows is caught in it mid-loop and **stops**, held in the air: the first still
moth Vesper has ever seen. Sedge pulls Vesper out of the light. **The Calm has gone looking.** Her mother's
last scratch-mark is at the top; from here Vesper is farther than her mother got.
**The quarrel.** Sedge points down at her field, dark and crawling with riders, and asks Vesper to come.
Vesper turns toward the radio mast. Sedge shoves her. No touch of antennae. They split.

**Midnight: the mast** *(the reveal).* At the foot of the drowned mast lies the dish from the prologue,
ringed by 916 moth scratches. In the middle is the oldest carving, **a moth inside an open eye with the sun
rising behind it**: moths know what the Calm has never seen, that everything ends, and the plan is to show it.
Around it runs a second ring, just as old: **moths drawn inside the eye, row on row, all of them still.**
Whoever planned this knew the price. If the Calm learns to see one moth, it will see every moth. Vesper adds
her scratch and climbs above the canopy alone, and the camera pulls all the way back:
- **The whole world.** Held cities to every horizon, pulses crossing the land. In the east, the eye, shut.
- **The picture.** The lit lamps lie in the dish's gold-dot pattern, the moth carved on the Grove's stone,
  drawn across a hundred miles and almost whole. Still dark: **one wing, over Sedge's field**, and her
  mother's path east to the eye. Sedge was right.

Then **Matins** drops out of the dark, the first thing all night that sees her. A small grey moth pulls
Vesper into a crack: **Compline**, whose wings are stubs, folds hers over her. The lamp passes over. (The
first time is gentle: if the player moves, Compline only holds tighter.) Then Compline smooths Vesper's torn
wing with her own.

**The held woods and the hollow** *(hiding, on foot).* Compline leads her home crack to crack while Matins
searches overhead and the tissue rolls into the Grove behind them. Through a gap they watch it close on a lamp
before it was lit: **held dark**, and the next glimpse of the picture has a hole in it. If Vesper flies ahead,
the lamp finds the old moth, so she walks at her grandmother's pace and stands still with her when the light
swings close. Under the light Compline sees the markings and walks toward it: it is her eldest. **Vesper
pulls her back** before the light can rest on her.
**Compline's hollow** is under the roots, where the sun never reaches. Her daughters' embers line the walls,
with two gaps: one for Vesper's mother, and one for Matins, scratched with a moth inside an eye. Compline
seals the door with root and settles beside it. **This is not a fight.** The seal regrows as fast as Vesper
cuts it. The way out is what Compline taught her an hour ago: Vesper stands still in her arms, and the player
waits. **Then Compline chooses**, and opens her arms. The seal withers.

**Before dawn: Sedge's field.** Sedge has fought alone all night. Vesper clears the lampwings off her unasked,
and Sedge touches antennae first.
*Boss: Matins*, fought side by side, dodging the lamp. The last lamp of the wing is high on a pole, Sedge's
wings are too torn to reach it, and Matins has Vesper pinned under her light. **Sedge flies into the light
and stops.** The lamp swings to her and holds her mid-wingbeat; Vesper is free, and lights the pole. The gaze
takes the flame, Matins goes slack, and the tissue drags her east. Vesper touches antennae with the held
Sedge, and nothing touches back. Compline has come up out of the ground below her and looks up at Sedge for
a long time. Then she follows Vesper east, under the roots.

**Dawn: the eye.** *The race.* Vesper runs her mother's path east, and **the tissue runs beside her**, a wave
rolling across the plain at her speed. Any lamp it reaches first is held dark, a hole in the picture.
Where the tissue closes the gorge ahead, the beetle from the town is waiting. She braces her shell in the
closing gap and holds it open. A ridden animal would never stop; a beetle chose to. Vesper goes through, and
the tissue closes over the beetle mid-brace.
*Final boss: Matins*, at the last lamp on the rim of the eye, which heaves as it starts to open. Vesper cuts
the bud off Matins's back, as she did the beetle's. Freed, Matins falls, and Compline, waiting in the shade at
the rim, catches her. Vesper lights the last lamp. **The picture is whole.** The eye opens to the sun, its
gaze runs along the picture and finds a moth standing exactly where the picture says a moth should be. **For
the first time it sees one with its own eye.** Everything around Vesper slows. In the shade Compline holds
Matins in one arm and holds out the other. **She doesn't pull. She asks.**

## 6. Endings and costs
**Seen (stand still).** Vesper stops mid-shiver, held. The sun climbs, the one thing the Calm can't hold,
and **her night ends inside its grip, while it watches.** It held her to keep her, and lost her anyway. For
the first time it has watched an ending, and **it lets go of the world.** The pulses stop. Held things finish
their moments: the flocks fly on, the beetle walks out of the gorge, the old woman lowers her lamp, and over
her field Sedge finishes her wingbeat and flies one spiral into the first light before she ends. People wake
the age they were into a world forty-one years on; a child catches the ball under trees that were a street.
The tissue browns into soil. Compline carries Matins into the sun, and her eldest ends in her arms.
**The bill:** Vesper, Sedge, and the moths' hiding place. The eye never closes on moths again. Every year they
hatch into its gaze and are watched through every loop of their one night, by the thing that took the world.
It doesn't hold them. At sunrise it watches each one end. Grief, or habit? The game doesn't say.
**Then the player controls Compline.** She walks to the hollow and sets Matins's ember and Vesper's in the
gaps, and scratches a new gap for Sedge's. At the next dusk she climbs to the summit lantern, where Vesper's
egg is splitting, and sits in the gaze with the hatchlings. At sunrise, for the first time in forty-one years,
she doesn't hide. Last shot: on the cliff, roots have grown across the blank stone into a moth.

**Unseen (go to her).** Vesper steps into the shade and Compline folds her in. She is the first moth of her
year to see a whole day, gold and slow, from inside her grandmother's arms, and she lives on under the roots,
hidden, as Compline did. The Calm never sees a moth or an ending. The tissue takes the last forests, and
Sedge hangs held over her field for good. **The bill:** the world, and Sedge. Last shot: years on, at dusk,
an old plum-winged moth in a root hole near the summit beckons a young one into the dark.

## 7. The Grove in this story
- **Lanterns** are her mother's lamps, each a dot in the picture. Carrying **the ember** to the summit
  finishes her mother's climb; the moths that rise to it are a funeral.
- **Burrbacks** are ridden, a pale bud on the shell; they ram the lantern she is kindling, never her, and go
  slack when it catches. The curl after a pogo is the beetle under the bud flinching.
- **Lampwings** dive at the glints she carries, not at her.
- **The heart** is a knot of the Calm's own root, where its gaze comes down. The carving is the picture.
- **The wake beat teaches the law.** Three lanterns draw the gaze down through the heart, and **for one
  breath the Grove holds still, all of it except the moths**: motes hang, grass stops mid-sway, a burrback
  halts mid-step, and Vesper and the small moths keep moving. *It holds everything, and it can't see us.*
  Then each offered glint makes the heart blaze, and the Grove is let go in tiers.
- *Additions to the built scene:* that freeze; pale tissue fingers at the bank and one tissue wall a
  burrback can't pass and Vesper flies through; a skip in a faint background pulse at each kindling; an
  antennae touch from a small moth when she re-forms; the grey moth in a root hole near the summit.

## 8. The question it leaves
It kept the children when asked, and it watches every sunrise it can't keep. *Was the thing that took the
world a someone that learned to grieve, or a dish that found a steadier beat? And when Compline opened her
arms in the hollow, was it the lesson the Calm needed, learned smaller and sooner?*

## Changes this round
- **The price is known before dawn.** The cliff shows a moth held in Matins's light, the mast's second ring
  shows moths held inside the eye, and Matins's gap in the hollow carries the same mark. Sedge pays that price
  first, by choice, so the dawn choice is hers made for every moth. The central question now names it: being
  seen.
- **Riders hunt lights, never Vesper.** Burrbacks ram the lamp she is kindling and lampwings dive at her
  glints; only Matins aims at her. The lamp rule and the riders rule are merged into one, and cutting a bud is
  learned on the town beetle before Matins.
- **The egg rule is cut.** Vesper re-forms at lit lamps, as the Grove already does. Only the sunrise and being
  held stop a moth. Sedge is held rather than falling, and is released in Seen. The coldest hour is folded into
  the woods, where the held-dark lamp teaches the race. The cliff and the mast are one set piece, and the
  picture grows at each lamp. Seen's cost is now watched, not held. Unseen no longer contradicts the
  "hiding lets you live on" rule. Compline smooths the wing with her wing, since moths here have no mouths.
