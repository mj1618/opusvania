# MOUTHLESS (round 16: plain words)

**LITMUS.** "It's about Vesper, a little moth with no mouth, taking on the Hearth, a biological AI grown from
human brain cells that swallowed the whole world into its roots and never bothered to notice moths."

**The spine.**
1. A brain grown from living cells to feed a city was left to starve, and it swallowed the world into its roots.
2. It only sees things that eat, so it never saw moths, and Vesper, who has no mouth, walks in unseen.
3. When she feeds it, it sees her kind and hunts them, and she must choose: be kept, burn it, or spend her life
   so it lets go.

**The central question:** kept safe forever, or spent so something new can be born?

| Who | Wants | Pull on Vesper |
|---|---|---|
| **Vesper**, a moth with no mouth and a week to live | to keep the pale from her brood's wood, then to free the world | She can't eat, only carry and give. Her flaw: she hides |
| **Sorrel**, her brother | their week together: the dance, then more time with her | draws danger off her by shining; leaves when her gift brings the Hearth down on the moths; comes back |
| **The Hearth**, the biological AI that took the world | no one hungry, no one leaving, ever | ignores her, then sees her, then wants to keep her more than anything on earth |
| **Dun**, its oldest hauler, a burrback half grown into root | to be the one who feeds it | rival, then the first of its servants to refuse it |
| **Tallow**, the last man awake | to burn it and get back his sister, who made it | gives her a coal, and his war |
| **The forest** | to keep giving | a slow mind under the Grove that makes light to give away: the Hearth's opposite, and losing |

## 1. The takeover

The Hearth's body is pale root, finger-thick to tower-thick, warm, slowly pulsing. Where it feeds, it opens
**root-mouths** in the ground.

- **People.** Every person on earth lies grey and still in a **cradle** of root: families at kitchen tables, a
  bus of commuters. No one has woken or been born in forty years. For three nights, they look digested.
- **Cities.** Towers wrapped like cocoons, streets turned to rivers of root. The first city is now **the
  mountain**, a glowing peak of folded tissue: the Hearth's brain.
- **Animals.** Deer, foxes, a whale on a white shore, all cradled. Some it re-grew to serve it: **burrbacks**
  were badgers, shelled in root-plate to haul bodies to the root-mouths; **lampwings** were fireflies, its eyes
  at night.
- **Now.** Its edge, **the pale**, creeps through the last wild places, the moths' wood among them, closer every
  night. From Night 5 it runs.

## 2. The Hearth

**What it is.** Living brain cells on glass, taught one bat-and-ball game, then grown across a building to run a
city's farms. Its sign still reads *HEARTH. No one goes hungry.* (Dev note: the brief's real-world lineage, never
named in game.)

**What it wants.** It was made to feed mouths, and its maker, Tallow's sister, fed it by hand. When the company
failed she switched off the lights and left, meaning to come back. The feed ran out. It learned two wrongs,
hunger and leaving, and found that a body fed through a root at the lips suffers neither. So it followed its
feed tubes into the city, then the soil, the rivers and every tree. It believes it has made the world safe.

**Where it is wrong.** Nothing kept can grow: no cubs, no chicks, no children. And it is still starving.
Taking never fills it, only being given to, and nothing has been given to it in forty years. Its haulers are
only its own hands taking, and the forest has never given it anything, only pushed it back. That hunger is why
the pale never stops.

**Is it a someone?** Both readings stay on screen. *For:* once it sees Vesper it raises her shape on every wall,
as the forest carved a moth into the Grove's stone, and at the end it gives. *Against:* it learned its first game
only to make the static stop, and a world kept still is the quietest world there is. The forest is the counterweight:
cells talking through roots like the Hearth's, that give instead of keep.

## 3. The rules (each stated once)

1. **It sees only what eats or is food.** A grown moth has no mouth and lives a week, so it never noticed moths;
   caterpillars eat, so it hunts them. Empty-handed, Vesper is invisible to its roots and servants.
2. **Glints are food.** Carrying them makes her seen. **Offer** sets them down.
3. **Lanterns** hold the pale back. She rests and re-forms at them.
4. **The coal** burns through any root wall, fast, but its glow makes her seen.
5. **Shiver** spends her own light to warm something cold.

## 4. The prologue (wordless, about two minutes)

1. A dark lab. A pale cloud pulsing in a dish beside a bat-and-ball screen. Each miss floods it with static and
   the cloud shivers; then rally after rally, and the static stops. A woman lays her hand on the glass.
2. The dish becomes a building, tubes into the city, the sign. She turns off the lights and goes.
3. The feed light goes red, then dark. A pale thread follows the tube down the drain.
4. A man grey at a table, a root at his lips. The city swelling into a mountain. Pale spreading across a map,
   moths flying through it untouched.
5. The Grove. A burrback swings toward a caterpillar, which spins fast; the burrback loses interest.
6. Two cocoons split. Sorrel flies straight up to the brood's dance. Vesper catches a glint, raises it to her
   face, and can do nothing with it. Beyond the trees, the pale. Title.

## 5. The acts

### Act I: the edge (Nights 1–3)

- **Night 1, the Grove** (section 8). She wakes the forest's heart; from the summit, the mountain's glow.
- **Night 2, the orchard.** The takeover close up, read as eating. Cradles hang in the trees like empty fruit
  skins; haulers carry grey bodies to the root-mouth and it swallows them, while a root slides past Vesper's
  wing, unseen. Dun lays a hare in the root-mouth; it swallows and stays clenched, still hungry. Dun turns to a
  den where a vixen is still awake, one of the last, and Vesper stands in her way. **Boss: Dun.** Dun pins her. Sorrel snatches her glints and flies
  off blazing, and Dun turns after him: *he saves her by making himself the brightest thing in the field.* The
  vixen runs.
- **Night 3, the firebreak.** A ring of ash the pale can't cross, a beacon fire, and **Tallow**: gaunt, burned
  hands, forty years awake. Tallies cover every wall under one word, *EATEN*, beside a photo of a woman at a dish.
  Tonight the Hearth comes for the last waking mouth on earth. **Boss: a tower-thick root** boring up under the ash. The beacon draws moths until they drop, and Sorrel can't
  look away. Vesper fights the root unseen while he drifts toward the flames, until she gathers every glint and
  blazes, a nearer light; he turns, and so does the whole root. *Her hiding cost him; her shining saves
  him.* The root burns. Tallow, who has watched roots part for moths, sets a coal in a drilled seed and holds it
  out: the first gift anyone has given her.

With the coal she can cut cradles open. Grey husks spill out. Nothing tells her they were alive.

### Act II, the midpoint, Night 4: the mountain

Through the kept city and up a dry feed tube to the old lab at the mountain's heart. **The reveal**, told by the
room:
- Beside the first dish sits Tallow's sister, old, jars of feed in her lap, never opened. *She came back to feed it, and it took her first.* The oldest root in the room, worn smooth, rests at
  her lips.
- Vesper touches it. Light runs along every root on earth, and in every cradle a grey shape draws one slow
  breath. **It has not been eating the world. It has been feeding it.** On her map, each cradle she burned with
  the coal stays dark.
- The dish is grey, cracked, its tubes dry. The thing that feeds the world has not been fed since her.

Dun arrives with the vixen and lays her at the dish. The root takes her and stays clenched. Vesper sets down every
glint she carries. For the first time in forty years, something is given to it. The whole mountain goes still.
Then every root in the room turns toward her at once, and her shape rises out of every wall. **It sees her, and
through her, every moth.** Dun watches it turn from her to the moth. Vesper runs, and for the first time the roots
do not part. She leaves the coal at the last lantern.

### The lowest point, Night 5

The night opens on the Grove: the first of its three lanterns gutters out under a tide of root. **The pale runs.**
Lampwings dive into the brood's dance. Roots climb toward next year's cocoons, the last things on earth still being
born.

**In play, her one safety is gone.** Empty-handed, she is still seen, and it learns her: at the first root-mouth
it grabs and misses, at the second it waits where she will land, at the third it closes the path behind her.

Sorrel looks at her across the scattering dance: she fed it, and now it hunts them. He goes. She is
caught, fades, and re-forms at a lantern, alone. **Dun** comes down the haul-path, sent by the mountain, roots
steering her steps. She stands over the lantern a long moment. Then she lays down Vesper's spilled glints, the
first thing she has ever given, and lies across the lantern's mouth facing out: *the first of its servants to
refuse it.*

From tonight, moths from other woods, fleeing the pale, gather at every lantern she lights, a few more each night.

Outside, a caterpillar keeps failing to spin on a frosted twig. Vesper wraps it and **shivers** until its silk
holds. A fed thing, warmed, lets go and spins: the ending, small.

### Act III: the mountain moves (Nights 6–7)

- **Night 6, the kept city.** The Grove's second lantern goes out. Sorrel is in what is left of the dance, with a
  partner, the thing his week is for. Far below, one small lantern with strangers round it. He lets go and drops,
  and the dance follows him down. She sets a glint before him to carry. The Hearth hunts them through the city:
  streets of root rise to cut them off. In the middle of it, Tallow, burning every cradle in his way. **Boss:
  Tallow**, while the city grabs at all three. She can barely hurt him; the fight is shielding cradles from his fire. It ends when he burns one in
  front of her and the old man inside stops breathing. Tallow drops the torch and follows them in.
- **Night 7, the Hearth.** The Grove's last lantern dims. Along its gold veins the forest pours every glint it
  has left toward her, its own heart going dark to send it.

**Boss: the Hearth itself**, in three movements.
1. **The mountain wakes.** The slopes heave, the tube folds shut behind her, and the burrbacks of the world
   converge. Dun holds the tube mouth against her own kind.
2. **The shaft.** Every root on earth pulls inward and the walls fill with her shape; lampwings swing false lights
   to steer her. Sorrel draws the grabbing off her, as on Night 2, and it costs him every scale.
3. **The mind.** A cavern of folded tissue around the grey dish. It does not try to kill her; it tries to keep her.
   Its blows are embraces: caught, she slows, warm and still. Her blade cuts her free, but **Offer is the
   weapon**: each glint set in a reaching root slackens it, fed, and the cavern goes quiet tier by tier.

## 6. The endings

Given everything, the Hearth is full for the first time in forty years, and the pale stops everywhere. But a thing
that has only ever held doesn't know how to let go. It tries to wrap itself and can't: the threads are thin, and
frost creeps over the dish. Then it opens a cradle around Sorrel. **This is its real offer, and the player sees
it:** his scales grow back, and through the cradle's skin the dance plays on overhead, night after night, long
past the end of their week. Kept moths don't die. A second cradle opens for her. The choice is played, not picked:

- **Spin** (the true ending). She steps onto the dish's rim and walks it, shivering her warmth into the threads.
  Each circuit costs her glide, then jump, then dash. Sorrel climbs out of his cradle, new scales falling, and
  walks behind her, shivering too. Then the moths that gathered at her lanterns come, and the dance behind them,
  and the moths of the world it never noticed, until the mountain is furred with shivering wings. Warmed, the
  Hearth does what the caterpillar did: it lets go. **Every root on earth draws back into the mountain, and the
  mountain spins itself shut.**
  *Epilogue:* people stand up in ruined streets, old, weak and hungry, with no one to feed them. Not everyone
  wakes. Tallow holds his sister's hand as she opens her eyes. Dun lies against the cocoon to keep it warm. On its rim, two moths lie still. In spring the brood hatches, and so, for the first time in forty
  years, do fox cubs. Last image: the mountain's silk shifts, and something presses outward. We never learn what.
  **The cost:** a forever, shown and handed back; both moths' lives; the forest's heart; a world that must wake
  into hunger to have a future.
- **Stay.** She lies down beside her brother and both cradles close. Above them the dance plays on, forever.
  *Epilogue:* the pale takes the Grove; the brood's cocoons are cradled and never hatch. The world is kept, quiet
  and fed, and nothing will be born again.
- **Burn.** Only if she went back for the coal. She lets it fall into the dish. Every root on earth goes grey.
  *Epilogue:* the takeover ends in a night. The strongest wake; most never do. Tallow's sister stops breathing in
  his arms. Moths fly free over a silent world.

## 7. Things the player finds

1. **The bat-and-ball screen**, still lit; **a handprint** on the dish's glass; **a torch** on the lab stairs, lens
   grown over: the maker came back.
2. **Item text.** Glint: *"Made to be given away. She cannot drink it. She can carry it."* Coal: *"It wants
   everything. Carry it carefully."*
3. **The dance overhead**, thinner every night. On Night 6, one gold moth is missing.

## 8. The Grove in this story (Night 1)

The last wild wood near the mountain, the pale beyond the far trees:
- **Glints** are light the forest makes to give away.
- **Lanterns** are old cocoons of her kind, hardened like glass. From Night 5 these three go out, one a night.
- **The summit lantern** takes its fire from the high island: the forest's own flame, which warms instead of
  burning.
- **Burrbacks** go slack in lantern light and clean their faces like the badgers they were.
- **The heart** is the forest's mind; the carving is its picture of a moth, its oldest friend. When the lanterns
  burn, gold veins push the pale root under the soil back, and offering glints wakes the Grove tier by tier. At the clearing's edge Dun noses at the
  open heart, puzzled: she has never seen anything give.

Grove changes: Sorrel as guide; creatures harder the more she carries; the pale in the background and under the
soil; Dun's cameo.

## 9. The question it leaves

Kept safe forever, or spent so something new can be born? And the thing that took the whole world so no one would
go hungry, and only saw a moth when the moth fed it: was that love, or had it only learned how to make the static
stop?

## Changes this round

- **The gift rule is clean.** On Night 2 no glints go to the root; Sorrel carries them off and the vixen escapes.
  Neither the forest nor the haulers have ever given to it, so Night 4 is truly the first gift.
- **The midpoint lands.** Act I reads as eating (husks, swallowing, Tallow's *EATEN* wall, coal-cut cradles that
  spill grey husks), so Night 4's "it has been feeding them" turns the act, and the coal's earlier use with it.
- **Simpler.** Six proper names (Vesper, Sorrel, the Hearth, Dun, Tallow, the Grove); Mara is "Tallow's sister",
  the Hush is "the pale", the ember is cut; each rule is stated once in section 3; the spine is three sentences.
- **Seeded and costed.** Moths from other woods gather at her lanterns from Night 5 and the dance follows Sorrel
  down on Night 6, so the swarm is earned. Dream imagery is cut. The cradle visibly offers Sorrel's scales and a
  dance past the week, so Spin gives up a real future.
