# Round 30 review: Mouthless

## 1. Anchor gate: PASS

All three readers' first sentences name a little moth against a grey living thing that swallowed the world. The
casual player wrote that it "can't see moths because moths don't eat". All three found the grown brain at the lab: the
lore-hunter found "cells in a dish that learned a ball game", the casual player "a brain grown from a scientist's cells".
That discovery is a mid-game revelation found in objects (poster, coat, dish), with no technical words. The Hush is
active at world scale in every act. In Act I its root sits under the pool, its regrown servants are out, and the mountain
breathes on the horizon. In Act II it turns every root on earth toward moths. In Act III the wood is overrun, the city is
lost, and the mountain itself is the final boss.

- Biological AI has taken over the world: **central**
- Umber saves the world: **central** (resolved three ways, each with a real cost)
- Moths deemed insignificant: **central**. "Notices only things that eat" ties directly to having no mouth.
- Sentience (the AI, the forest, the animals): **central**. The Hush ends on an open question, the forest is a mind
  that spends itself, and Dun turns.

## 2. Craft verdict: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES

Nothing below is a foundation problem. The spine, the cast and the rules held up across three cold reads.

## 3. What is at the bar

- **The underestimation is biological, and it is the theme.** "The Hush and its badgers notice only things that eat."
  The first item text says "A moth has no mouth to keep it." The owner's idea became one rule that drives stealth, the
  danger from lampwings and the meaning of the ending.
- **The antagonist's wound is the real experiment.** "Each leaving hisses like a missed ball." DishBrain's feedback
  noise has become loneliness. Its motive, to feed everyone and keep them close, is understandable and wrong in a way
  that lights up Umber's motive, which is to give her warmth away. That is the Pale King / Apeman tier.
- **Images people retell.** The lore-hunter remembered the sister: "She pinches it out, lays her hand on the glass,
  and a case closes round her." The casual player remembered the case Tallow can't see into: "To him it is grey and
  blind as stone."
- **One gesture carries the relationship.** The heart reader built a whole emotional memory on the wingtip tap, from
  the prologue to the partner who "taps Sorrel's wingtip, and flies home". That is Ori-grade economy.
- **Dun on the ice** is a turn earned through play rather than through words.

## 4. Problems, ranked

1. **Spin's causal link doesn't show (POLISH, top priority).** Two of the three readers couldn't say why the cocoon
   frees the world. *Fix:* make it one image. As the silk closes, every stalk is drawn out of the cases and spun into the
   cocoon. The Hush turns its own mouths into silk and becomes mouthless, like her. Because no rule is added, the first
   item text pays off.
2. **The visibility rule looks broken after Night 2 (POLISH).** The casual player asked: "Is she invisible or not?"
   *Fix:* the hidden rule is already "you are seen by what you warm". Show it the first time. On the ice, Dun's eyes
   find her as she shivers over its snout. It sees her at the turn for the same reason. No new rule is needed, only a
   shot.
3. **Too many hand-offs to track (POLISH).** The partner's case goes in and out of the pool, the vixen reappears at the
   lab, and the lampwing that lands on Sorrel is never paid off. *Fix:* cut the lampwing beat. Move the partner's case
   only once, to the pool on Night 5, where it stays until the ending. At the lab, have Dun bring a sleeping hare from
   the orchard instead of the vixen.
4. **The lab is dense (POLISH).** The heart reader wrote "a lot to take in". The casual player needed two reads to get
   from a ball game to a world-eater. *Fix:* cut the poster's middle panel, because the memory already shows the ball
   game. In the memory, show the greenhouses going dark as the workers leave, so the root's first act reads as doing
   its old job, feeding a hungry child.
5. **Timeline and staging slips (POLISH).** The Burn ending's "last night" implies an eighth night, and the casual
   player asked which glass "the glass" is. Dancing "on the skin of the cases" reads as either a dream or something
   real. *Fix:* in Burn, say "at dawn". Say "the dish". Show the dance as silhouettes lit from inside the case, so it
   clearly reads as a dream.
6. **Relentless sadness (POLISH).** The casual player said so. *Fix:* give Night 6 one bright beat, such as moths from
   every wood dancing round Dun's lantern before the streetlamp, so the losses have something to fall from.

## 6. At the bar? Yes

I would put this outline beside Ori, Hollow Knight and Nine Sols as an equal. It has a mythic surface, a technical truth
found in objects, an antagonist whose wound is the source experiment, a sibling bond carried by one gesture, and three
endings that each cost something. All three readers would finish it, and two of them chose the true ending unprompted.
The one gap left is problem 1: the true ending has to show why it works, not only feel right.
