# 4. What the Lanterns Burn

## 1. Spine

**Logline:** A young moth lights lanterns beside the mentor who meets her at the glass every time she
falls, and learns what is burning inside them, and which one light her mentor has never let burn.

**The spine, as a player would tell a friend:**
1. Vesper is a young moth on a pilgrimage with her mentor, the Taper, lighting the lanterns that keep
   moth children safe in a forest full of monsters.
2. She finds out that the lights the lanterns burn are what is left of people, that the monsters were
   guarding them, and that the Taper has always known.
3. In the end there is one light left. It could bring the Taper back, or it could be spared: the one
   light the Taper ever loved, and never let burn.

**The central question:** *Would you burn one life to keep the one you love?*

## 2. The world before, and how it was taken

Late in humanity's dwindling, the dying gave a vial of blood. Their cells were grown into neurons in
amber glass bulbs, and grieving families taught each bulb to be the person it came from. When nobody was
left to tend them, the last people grafted the bulbs into the old forests so the trees would feed them.
Each bulb had been taught one wish, *don't let me go*, so it grew. Pale pods spread over every branch,
each holding a **glint**, a small light that dreams one moment over and over. The forest became the
**Bequest**, and the old forest lay buried alive beneath it. Nobody decided to take the world. The people
who loved the dead most planted it.

**How a player finds this: hands.** Every glint flickers a gesture made by a large, soft, five-fingered
hand, bigger than any creature in the game: a wave, a cupping, a hand laid on a cheek. At the **Root**,
rows of glass bulbs lie half-buried under the oldest trees. Each is the exact shape of a lantern's glass,
and each has that hand's print pressed into it where someone held it last. One place in the rows is
empty.

## 3. The forest-mind, the old forest, and why moths don't count

The Bequest's keepers are the **burrbacks**, which guard the pods, and the **lampwings**, which gather up
loose glints. They move like one will and notice only one thing: *what is at our pods, or carrying our
glints?* A moth doing neither is nothing to them. A moth at a pod is a thief, and they kill thieves.

**The pull is real.** Moths fly to light and can't help it. Pods glow, so without a brighter light near,
a young moth flies straight into the pods and the keepers kill her. The lanterns are the light moth
children are allowed to fly to. That is the Taper's whole case: *people burn so children don't die.* The
keepers see moths only as that helpless pull and never imagine a moth flying against it. Vesper learns to
fly straight with her back to the light. It is a skill, not a cure. No child can learn it in a week.

Under the Bequest, the **old forest** is hungry for its body back. Each grove's **heart** is its mouth.
The moth on the heart's standing stone was never carved: the forest grew it, the way an orchid grows the
shape of a bee. It isn't evil, and it isn't kind.

**When glints burn.** Glints set in a lantern's glass shine there without burning. That is the lantern's
light. A lantern burns its glints only when it spends them: **one for each re-form, and every one it
holds when a grove's heart wakes** and drinks all its lanterns at once through the root-veins. Glints
offered straight to the heart go the same way. Where that light arrives, the Bequest withers and the
keepers fall back.

Are glints people, or echoes of people? Each one repeats its gesture like a loop. The Taper says they are
echoes, and the keepers treat them as children. The game never says.

**Who re-forms.** A moth who fades becomes a drifting spark with no hand in it. A lit lantern catches a
spark *only if it has been touched with the ember's gesture*, a wing laid on the cheek. It then burns one
glint to remake her. Untouched sparks drift past the glass and go out. The Taper's wing on a cheek is
therefore the anointing: the gift of a life lived on lanterns. Few receive it. Everyone else lives a
week.

## 5. The ember, and the Taper's secret rite

The **ember** is the most whole glint in the valley. Its gesture is a large hand laid on a cheek. The
Taper never knew whose hand it was. A hundred lifetimes ago, as a young moth carrying it to the glass,
she saw the first tenderness she had ever seen, and she couldn't let it go. She took it from its place at
the Root. Holding it, she was touched by its gesture, the first moth ever anointed, and every wing she has
laid on a cheek since has been a copy of that hand. Tallow has kept the ember on the Grove's high island
ever since.

The Grove is her rite. Once in each pilgrim's lifetime she sends the new pilgrim down alone to wake the
heart, and the rite requires the great glint from the island set in the summit lantern. While the heart
wakes and drinks the grove's lanterns, she holds her reed over that one glass so it can't burn, and for
the length of a wake she sees her beloved gesture lit. Afterwards Tallow carries it back to the island.
They have kept this wordless truce for a hundred lifetimes. Vesper is the latest pilgrim.

## 6. Prologue (wordless, about 90 seconds)

Moths gather at a lantern at night. A few are grey and fraying. One fades, and her spark drifts past the
glass and goes out. Nobody turns. Then the Taper, ancient, stumbles and fades. Her spark drifts, the glass
*catches* it and flares, one glint in it goes dark, and she steps out whole. In the crowd, Vesper and
Sedge nudge each other. The Taper chooses Vesper, lays a wing on her cheek, and gives her three glints.
One flickers as Vesper carries it: a large hand, waving. At the edge of the light a torn lampwing waits.
Vesper glances at it, and the Taper's wing on her cheek again turns her face back to the glass. The Taper
points her reed at a dark valley, *your first grove, alone*, and perches on the bluff above it. Her
reed-light watches.

## 7. The story

**The re-form is the relationship.** Through Act I, every time Vesper fades, the Taper is at the glass
when she steps out and lays her wing on Vesper's cheek. After the Hush, Vesper steps out alone. At the
Root it reverses.

**ACT I: TOGETHER.** *With Vesper: the Taper, and for a while Sedge.*
- **The Grove** (our scene): see 8.
- **On the road.** The Taper reaches every lantern before you, reed raised. The first few times you fall
  short of a ledge, she swoops in and catches you by the arm. Later she lets you fall, because she's
  teaching. At rests she mends your wing with silk. *Flare*, bought with glints, makes creatures recoil:
  you are throwing someone at them.
- **Sedge** catches up. He races you and loses every time, and you can wait for him. When you're empty at
  a dark lantern, he gives you his only glint. His wings begin to grey. He has never been anointed.
- **The Combs. Boss: Clasp.** You fight beside the Taper, and both of you re-form from the same glass. In
  her last phase Clasp heaps her pods onto her back and runs. You crack her shell to spill them, for the
  act's biggest haul, and she limps into the dark with her back empty.
- **The Coral Stair.** Tallow snatches Sedge's glint from Vesper's cape and flees slowly, one wing
  dragging. Vesper chases it.

**MIDPOINT: THE HUSH (played).** The chase ends in a hollow full of pods. Tallow presses Sedge's glint into
bare earth and it sprouts. It glitters like a pickup, so the player grabs it: Vesper's flaw, and the
player's. Tallow only watches. Then Clasp crawls past to a pod the player cut on the way in and folds down
over it, in the pose every burrback took at every kindling. Vesper looks at the glint in her grip. It
flickers a large hand, cupping. She kneels and puts it back: *Plant*. At the mouth of the hollow stand the
Taper and Sedge. The Taper holds out her wing. Vesper doesn't come. Sedge goes with the Taper.

**ACT II: APART.** *With Vesper: Tallow.*
- **The truce.** Vesper returns to the Grove at night and sees the Taper below the high island, lifting
  her wing toward it once. Above, Tallow holds the ember. The Taper leaves without seeing Vesper. Then
  Tallow brings the ember down and gives it to Vesper, the first thing it has ever given her. Carried, the
  ember throws the shadow of a large hand around her, palm open, and the hand closes when she's hit. It is
  her light now. Tallow wants it taken home to the Root, which it can't fly to.
- **The dark.** Vesper snuffs lanterns. Each snuffed lantern is a checkpoint gone, and level design places
  every snuff so that a lit lantern still waits ahead. She plants what she finds, and a planted glint is
  one she can't spend on her own life. Boss arenas have only lanterns. When she glides short, Tallow
  catches her by the cape, as the Taper used to. Behind her, snuffed lanterns turn up relit, with reed-ash
  at their feet.
- **The old forest, its one sighting.** Following the veins down, she finds where the drunk light goes: a
  buried trunk wider than the screen, its bark greening where the light pours in and the pale pods
  withering around it. It breathes. It doesn't look at her.
- **Sedge's week runs out.** He is grey and falling at a lantern she snuffed, and holds a glint toward the
  dark glass. She can't do it. The Taper arrives, lays her wing on *his* cheek and relights the lantern
  with his glint. He fades, the glass catches his spark, and Sedge steps out young. He doesn't look at
  Vesper.
- **The Censer, the moths' home. Boss: the Taper.** She never strikes Vesper. Vesper snuffs the ring of
  lanterns and the Taper relights them. Her only attack is the wing: she catches Vesper's cheek and turns
  her to the glass. Vesper wins by snuffing the Great Lantern. Beaten, the Taper looks at the moth children,
  not at Vesper.
- **The lowest point.** In the dark, the children do what moths do: they fly toward the nearest glow, the
  pods. The burrbacks come for them, and Clasp leads them. Vesper *Braces* between the children and the
  pods and takes Clasp's ram without moving. Clasp stops, turns the burrbacks away and lies down between
  the children and her pods. Vesper falls, and no lantern near her is lit. Tallow catches her drifting
  spark and carries it, torn wing and all, across the valley to the one lantern still burning: the Great
  Lantern, which the Taper has relit. It lets go at the edge. The glass catches the anointed spark, flares,
  and a glint goes dark for her. Sedge, young again, watches it go out, then looks at his own wings. Vesper
  steps out. The Taper's wing lifts toward Vesper's cheek and stops. Every moth turns its back on Vesper,
  except Sedge, who turns round.
- **Moonward.** Vesper leaves with the Censer burning behind her. For the first time her glide doesn't
  bend toward it. Sedge follows, his glide still
  bending toward the light behind them.

**ACT III: THE ROOT.** *With Vesper: Tallow, Clasp and Sedge. Then the Taper.*

**The goal:** bring the ember home to its empty place among the bulbs. The valley's oldest lantern stands
at the Root's edge. A storm of lampwings rises against a moth carrying a glint, the whole Bequest moving
at once. They dive where the pull should take her, and she flies straight past. The Taper has followed.
She doesn't stop Vesper. She flies beside her, Flaring the storm back, and both re-form from the Root
lantern's glass. Now when the Taper steps out, Vesper is at the glass and lays a wing on her cheek: the
same gesture, given back. Every time the *player* falls, the same glass drops. On the last stretch the
Taper spends its final glint on one Flare that breaks the storm off Vesper. She falls, and nothing is left
to catch her.

**The choice.** Vesper kneels at the empty place in the rows with the ember. The Taper's spark gutters at
the foot of the dark glass. Set in the glass, the ember would remake her. The Taper looks toward the far
light of the Censer, then lifts her wing to Vesper's cheek and turns her face to the glass. She is asking
Vesper to burn the one light she spent a hundred lifetimes keeping from the fire, so that someone can go
on keeping the children from the pods. Vesper chooses. The game waits.

- **Kept.** Vesper sets the ember in the glass, and nobody shields it. It burns, and the Taper steps out
  whole. She lifts her wing toward Vesper's cheek, stops, looks at the wing and folds it. She will never
  make the gesture again, and she will anoint no one else. She takes her reed back toward the Censer.
  Sedge goes after her, because his life is in the lanterns now. Clasp lies down at the edge of the light,
  folded over nothing. Tallow hangs at that edge, stopped where it always stopped. Vesper stays at the
  Root, kneeling by the empty place in the rows, her own wing half-raised toward no one. Last shot: moth
  children asleep in a lantern's light, safe from the pods, and in the glass above them, once, a large
  hand on a cheek, then gone.
- **Planted.** Vesper turns herself to the earth and sets the ember in its place. Sedge lays his wing over
  hers. Every bulb in the rows flickers at once and the hands turn toward her: the first time the Bequest
  has ever looked at a moth. The ember's light makes its gesture over the Taper's spark, a hand on a
  cheek, and with no glass to catch it the spark goes out. Vesper carries the unlit reed away. Sedge,
  Tallow, Clasp and a few moths follow her and plant. Most stay at the Censer, where the Great Lantern
  still burns. The ember grows into a tree with **one hollow for every glint you burned to re-form after
  the Hush.** Vesper and Sedge sit under the moon with a week left, and Tallow lands beside them. Last
  shot: moth children in the dark by the tree, no lantern near. One lifts toward the glow of a pod, and a
  burrback's shadow falls over them. Cut.

## 8. The Grove, and what its ending means

Vesper enters carrying the Taper's three glints, so the keepers hunt her from the first minute. The Grove
must feel wonderful and dangerous, because the turn only works if the player loved it.

| In the Grove | First play | Replayed after the Hush |
|---|---|---|
| Re-forming | The Taper's wing on your cheek | Her anointing is why the glass catches you, and a glint went dark for it |
| Burrbacks fold down at a kindling | Calmed | The pose over a dead pod |
| A lampwing dive | An attack | It hurt you, yes, and it took a glint back |
| The ember | The great fetch | The Taper's beloved gesture, fetched for her by every pilgrim |
| The heart wakes and you offer it glints | The grove wakes, tier by tier | All three lanterns drunk at once, and you fed it the rest. A meal |
| The moth on the stone | Moths carved their god | The forest grew a lure |

**The ending beat, in the normal camera.** The heart wakes. The Taper glides down from the bluff and lays
her wing on Vesper's cheek as the veins pulse inward from the lanterns. Then her reed-light leaves
Vesper's side, rises to the summit lantern and stays there until the veins go quiet. On a first play
she is honouring the great kindling. Later we know she was shielding the one light she couldn't bear to
lose, while letting Vesper feed the rest. At the edge of the frame, small and unremarked, a torn lampwing
hangs under the island.

**The essential changes to the existing scene:**
1. **The glass and the Taper.** Each lantern's glass visibly holds its glints, and a re-form burns one,
   replacing the lost third of the wallet. The Taper glides down to meet Vesper at the glass.
2. **Lampwing dives wound and steal.** A hit takes a glint into the lamp, and striking the lampwing drops
   it.
3. **Hands.** Glint flickers show hand gestures, and the ember throws a hand's shadow while carried.
4. **The calm pose.** A calmed burrback folds flat.
5. **The wake.** The Taper comes down from the bluff, then her reed-light rises to the summit lantern. Vein
   pulses run inward. Tallow hangs under the island.

## 9. What the player finds

1. **The Taper's hollow** in the Censer: a wall scratched with hundreds of hands, each laid on a cheek,
   over and over. Her wing-patches are cut from the old cocoon hanging there, Vesper's.
2. **The summit lantern:** reed-ash layered at its foot from a hundred visits.
3. **The Root's bulbs:** hand-prints in the glass, and one empty place.
4. **Tallow's island:** moss pressed flat in the shape of a wing around a hollow the size of the ember.
5. **The heart's stone,** seen closely in Act II: the moth's edges are bark, not cut stone.
6. **Sedge's scratches** at a wayside lamp: two little moths racing, one waiting for the other.

## 10. The question it leaves

Would you burn one life to keep the one you love? The Taper burned thousands so that children would
live, and kept one back for herself.
