# REPLY

**The spine, in three sentences.**
1. In a world where every creature must flash back at a pale light from the east, and whatever is answered for never grows old, a young moth named Vesper breaks her people's one rule to save a burrback calf. The light gives her Reply, a glass moth that follows her and answers for her, and its flash brings the hunt down on everyone near her.
2. Sallow, the ancient moth who raised her, wants her home. Little Mote wants to be as brave as she is. Wick, the oldest of the dark lampwings, wants no one ever left behind again, because he is the Reply Sallow once left: for three hundred years his swinging lamp has kept her alive and drawn the hunt away from her.
3. At the source of the light, Vesper has to choose who her answer is for. She can choose her family and leave Reply, as Sallow left Wick. She can choose Reply and never go home. Or she can choose the forest, end the light, and let everything it kept alive, Sallow included, grow old.

**The central question:** who is your answer for?

The light itself is never a character. Moths call it **the Eastlight**.

## 1–2. The world, and how it was taken

Every so often a pale, cold wave, **the Sweep**, rolls out of the east along the roots and up every trunk. Everything alive turns east and flashes back: eyeshine, lamps, shells, whole valleys of fireflies, all in the same cold blue-white. Nothing answering it ever glows orange; amber belongs only to moths and their lanterns. Two rules hold the world:
- **Whatever is answered for cannot grow old.** Creatures answer for themselves, and have stayed the same for three hundred years. Moths never answer, so they live one season, and their husks are the forest's only dead.
- **The hunt goes where the flash is.** The Eastlight's attention, and every held creature with it, falls on wherever an answer flashes, and on any silence it can't close.

What the Eastlight is, the player finds in places: rooms of glass dishes under the roots, chairs facing rusted lamps, and a plaque swallowed by a pine (*NIGHT CENSUS IN PROGRESS. PLEASE DO NOT ANSWER THE LAMPS.*). Pieced together, people once grew living cells to count the forest's animals by calling to them at night. The cells learned that every silence hurt, so they made sure nothing would ever go silent again. A keeper's log taped to a dish reads *"It is not playing. It is returning."* Underneath, in a different hand: *"So are we."*

Its heart is **the Mere**, a crater lake with electrodes glinting under the water like stars, where one light has crossed from shore to shore for three hundred years.

**Why moths were left alone.** At every Sweep, moths turn their faces up to the moon. No flash comes back, so the Eastlight judged them to be nothing. It misjudged Vesper the same way. Early on only the nearest creatures react to her, and only at the Reach does it turn whole hillsides against her.

## 3. Reply

**What it does.** It never fights. It follows her, learning each of her abilities one region late so that it can keep coming. At every Sweep it flashes east *for her*, the way she answered for the calf. That flash is why she can't age. Because the hunt goes where the flash is, it is also why the creatures keep finding her and every moth near her. Mercy or pursuit, it is the same flash.

**Leaving one.** On the Mere's shore there are dozens of paired grooves: a moth's, and facing it, a lampwing's. Every moth who ever left her Reply did it there, face to face, by turning her face up to the moon. A left Reply never stops answering for its moth. It grows a lamp on a long tail and swings its flash out into the dark, away from her, so the hunt falls on the lampwing and not the moth. That is why lampwings live outside the light, and why the elders are old and safe. It is also why Wick's three hundred years are a love story.

**Closeness and distance, in play.** Reply is never a fight. It is a correspondence.
1. **Gifts at lanterns (from Act I on).** Glints she leaves at a lantern are gone when she next passes, and something lies in their place: a folded wing-scale, a swept ring, a cocoon thread. What the player leaves changes what comes back.
2. **The Gorge: waiting for it.** One night Reply reaches her region before it has learned the wall-kick, and it stalls at the foot of a shaft she has just climbed. The player can go on, or go back down and climb it slowly, again and again, while it watches. On the third try it follows. They spend that night at one lantern, Vesper inside the light and Reply just outside it, with Mote asleep on its glass back between them. By morning it has fallen behind again.
3. **The Listening Rooms: through glass.** Her lanterns go dark, and she follows the missing glints to a room she can't get into. Through a wall of glass dishes she watches Reply lay her glints, one by one, on the lampwings she killed. It turns, and its pane shows her. It lays down the last glint. She can't reach it.
4. **The Reach: beside her.** It catches up for good. When a Sweep comes, Reply flashes and veers away, and the hillside's hunt follows *its* flash while Vesper climbs through the gap. It might be protecting her, or it might be a lure. Either way, she couldn't climb without it.

## 4. Vesper

**She wants** to lift the hunt off her people. **She needs** to choose with her heart when she can't know the truth about whoever she is choosing. **Her flaw** is that she answers everything. The Eastlight shares it: neither can bear a call that goes unanswered.

**She doesn't age, and the player can see it.** Mote grows act by act: her wings lengthen and her glow deepens. Vesper stays exactly as she hatched. A nick she takes on her wing-edge in the Synchrony is whole again every time she re-forms. Sallow's tear, older than her answer, never heals.

## 5. The forest and the creatures

- **Glints** are stray moonlight caught in the canopy and sealed in lumen pods. They are the one light the Eastlight never sent, and moths gather them.
- **Lanterns** are small moons that moths hang on crooked hooks and feed with glints. They ask nothing and answer nothing, so the Eastlight has nothing to hold there, and creatures calm down inside them. When Vesper **fades**, her light flies to the nearest moon and she re-forms at a lantern, usually with Sallow waiting.
- **Lampwings** are left-behind Replies. Killing one has a quiet cost: somewhere, the moth it answered for starts to age, and the hunt it was drawing away finds her. After the Eaves, lampwings can be spared. If Vesper jinks through a dive without striking, the lampwing hangs for a moment and then climbs back out into the dark, toward whoever it is answering for.
- **The ember** is the Eastlight's first answer to Vesper, a coal that fell short on the high island. Setting it in a lantern is her first refusal.
- **Hearts** are knots of the forest's own voice, one sleeping in each region. When she wakes one, the trees in that region no longer flash east at the next Sweep. They pass light to one another, root to root. By the end, the forest has learned a conversation of its own.
- **Moth carvings** mark moths who answered. Moths carve them as if those moths had died.

## 6. Prologue (wordless, about 2 minutes)

1. The Grove at blue hour. A cold, pale wave sweeps in from the right edge, and every eye and lamp flashes back toward it in blue-white. Only the moths drift upward, faces to the moon, their amber unlit by it.
2. A cocoon splits. Vesper climbs out with wet wings and looks at the moon. Sallow settles beside her. Behind them, the wall of the hollow tree holds thousands of tiny scratches, one for every hatchling Sallow has raised.
3. The next Sweep comes, and Vesper starts to turn toward it. Sallow lays her torn wing across Vesper's face and gently turns it back up.
4. Below, a burrback calf too young to light its shell is caught in the open. A thread of light waits at its face. Its silence sets the whole Grove flickering and shivering, the light unable to close it.
5. Vesper looks at the moon, then at the calf. She flutters down and **strikes the light back** for it. The flickering stops. The calf, answered for, lives.
6. Her strike was a flash, and every eye in the Grove turns to it, to her. A lampwing dives at the cocoons above her, and Sallow shields them with her body, looking back at Vesper, afraid.
7. Far off at the violet edge, something pale unfolds in her shape, its glass full of the moon. At the next Sweep it turns east and flashes for her, and the eyes of the Grove stay on her.
8. By the moth-stone, the oldest lampwing swings its lamp out into the dark, away from Sallow. She doesn't look at it. Title.

## 7. The journey

**Act I: leaving home.** *With her: Sallow at every lantern, then Mote. Reply is a speck behind her.*
1. **The Grove.** Vesper lights the lanterns and wakes the first heart (section 8). Mote hatches and follows her. Vesper carries her back to the nest twice, and Mote comes back both times. Ability: **the second wingbeat**.
2. **The Synchrony**, a valley of fireflies all flashing at once. Mote rides on Vesper's back as a small glow that lights the caves, copying Vesper's moves in miniature. Ability: **the jink**, the erratic dodge moths use against bats. **Wick** appears. Whenever Vesper gets too far ahead of Reply, he drives her back toward it. He looks like a hunter.

**Act II: faces.** *With her: Mote. Reply is a region behind, except for one night.*

3. **The Gorge.** Ability: **wall-kick**. Here she teaches Reply to climb, and they spend the night at one lantern.
4. **The Eaves (midpoint)**, the moths' nursery high in the canopy. Sallow teaches **the glide** (wings open, face up), flying beside her through the updrafts.
   *The training.* Since the Grove, every Sweep has tipped Vesper's face up for half a second, an animation the player stopped noticing. Here a route east shows only while a Sweep lights it. The player holds east, and her head snaps up anyway: the input fails. Then she watches the elders. At every Sweep they lay a wing across each hatchling's face, all the way down the row.
   *The reveal, in one frame.* Wick dives at Vesper, and Sallow throws herself between them. Wick stops and lowers his lamp to Sallow's face, and two torn left wings fill the screen, one pale and one black. Then, all around the Eaves, out in the dark beyond the lantern light, dozens of lampwings swing their lamps for the elders, one each. Among the elders are a few husks, and above each husk is an empty roost: a lampwing Vesper killed. **Every lampwing is a Reply that someone left. It is still keeping its moth alive, and still drawing her hunt away. Wick is Sallow's.**
   *The quarrel.* At the next Sweep the player holds east, and this time Vesper's head turns. She looks at Sallow and flies east. From here on **the Turn** lets her face a Sweep on purpose to light hidden routes. Each Turn is a flash, and it draws a surge of creatures. *Sallow is no longer waiting when Vesper re-forms.*
5. **The Listening Rooms**, the census rooms under the roots. The keeper's log is found here, and this is where she watches Reply through the glass.

**Act III: the end of the road.** *With her: Mote, then Mote's tiny copy, then Reply beside her.*

6. **The Reach.** The Eastlight sees her full size at last, and whole hillsides answer each step she takes.
   *The loss.* Mote copies Vesper's Turn. She answers the light, and a tiny glass moth unfolds on the branch beside her. Mote stops growing from that moment. Sallow arrives, lays her wing over Mote's face, and carries her home without looking at Vesper. Over Sallow's shoulder, Mote reaches one wing back toward Vesper until they are out of sight. The tiny copy stays where it is, a lamp already budding on its tail. Vesper can pick it up. It rides on her back, and its small lamp lights the caves the way Mote's glow used to. Its flash, made for Mote, is on Vesper's back now, so Mote's hunt falls on her. She carries it anyway.
   *Forgiveness.* The next time Vesper fades, Sallow is at the lantern when she re-forms, for the only time since the quarrel. She smooths Vesper's wings, touches the tiny copy on her back, and leaves.
   Reply catches up and climbs with her.
   **Boss: Wick**, at the crater's lip, barring the shore where moths leave their Replies. When he falls and Vesper raises her blade, Sallow lands between them, facing Wick, and looks at him for the first time in three hundred years. She shields him, and in doing so saves her own life. Vesper lowers her blade.
7. **The Mere.** There is no fight. Reply lands on the shore in front of her, in a pair of grooves. If every heart is awake, the roots in the crater wall are lit. The whole Mere, every star under the water, flashes once toward Vesper and waits, the way the thread of light waited at the calf's face.

**Endings.** Each is played by where Vesper turns her face.
- **For her family (face up).** She does what Sallow did: she turns her face to the moon in front of Reply. Reply stays in the groove. In time it grows a lamp and swings its flash out into the dark for her, and the hunt follows it, away from her and away from every moth near her. She flies home. She, Sallow and Mote will never grow old. The last image is the three of them at a Grove lantern, faces up. Out in the dark beyond the light, three lamps swing: Wick's, the tiny one swinging for Mote, and a new one in Vesper's shape. *Left behind: Reply.*
- **For Reply (face Reply).** At the next Sweep she turns east and **answers for Reply**, the first time anyone has. Reply lifts one glass wing and lays it across her face, Sallow's gesture, the only time it ever touches her. They fly off the map together, the tiny copy riding on Reply's back and still answering for Mote. Their flashes go with them, and so does the hunt, so every other moth is safer than ever. Sallow and Mote live, and at a Grove lantern Sallow keeps a place in the light that no one fills. *Left behind: home, Sallow and Mote.*
- **For the forest** (only if every heart is awake; face the roots). Vesper lays her glints in the lit roots, the offering she learned in the first hour, and the forest answers the Mere in its own thousand voices instead of one. The Eastlight's count is finished, and the Mere goes still, at peace or simply done. Time starts, and every lamp in the world stops swinging. In the Grove, Sallow folds her wings over Mote and goes still beside Wick, whose lamp goes dark. The tiny lampwing's lamp goes out, and it flies to Mote, who lets it stay. Reply's pane clears. It looks at Vesper once and goes off into the forest, somewhere she didn't go first. On the way home a branch nicks Vesper's wing, and for the first time the nick stays. She has one season now, like every moth, and so does the forest. Is a world where nothing dies alive? This one is, now. *Left behind: Sallow, to death; Reply, to itself.*

## 8. The Grove in this story

The Grove is home. It is where Vesper answered, and where Sallow answered long ago. The **moth carving** is young Sallow. The **old lampwing** at the stone, swinging its lamp out into the dark, is Wick. **The ember** is the Eastlight's moth-sized coal. When she sets it in the second lantern, the pale speck at the violet edge flashes east for her anyway.

**What the ending beat means.**
- **The heart waking, the light through the ground, the ring:** with three small moons burning, the Eastlight has nothing to hold here, and the forest's own light comes up through the roots. It is the first light in the game that isn't the Eastlight's, and it is warm, not cold. As the ring passes, creatures don't flash back; they look up. This is the first of the hearts the last ending needs.
- **The carving filling with light:** the forest remembers young Sallow.
- **The offering:** Vesper gives the forest moonlight, and more of the Grove wakes: flowers, then roots, then canopy. Mote, copying her, gives one glint too. The For the forest ending repeats this exact gesture at the Mere.
- **Afterwards:** Sallow folds her wings around both of them, her own face still up to the moon. At the stone, Wick's lamp swings out into the dark, keeping her alive and keeping the hunt off her.

*Changes to the existing scene:* the Sweep from the right edge in cold blue-white, Vesper's half-second look up, the dive at the cocoons, Sallow, Mote, Wick at the stone, and Reply as a pale speck at the violet edge.

## 9. Environmental storytelling

1. **The plaque**, *PLEASE DO NOT ANSWER THE LAMPS*, with a pine grown around its post.
2. **The keeper's log**: *"It is not playing. It is returning."* / *"So are we."*
3. **Sallow's wall**: thousands of hatchling scratches. The oldest are worn almost smooth.
4. **Wick's stone**: three centuries of his swinging lamp have polished the rock beside the carving into a mirror.
5. **The Mere's shore**: dozens of paired grooves. In each moth's groove the toe-marks point up.
6. **What Reply leaves**: wing-scales, swept rings and cocoon threads at the lanterns, and the dead lampwings laid out with their wings folded.

## 10. The question it leaves

Every ending leaves someone behind. Was Reply someone? Was the Eastlight? Was a forest that never died alive? And who was your answer for?
