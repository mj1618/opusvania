# 5. ONE NIGHT

**The god's law, in words a player can say aloud:**
- *Whatever it looks at, it holds still. Everything except the sun.*
- *Where it isn't looking, it reaches, and rides the beasts. It never looks and reaches at once.*
- *It lets go only of what ends while it is holding it.*

**The spine:**
1. A slow god, grown long ago from living brain cells, holds still whatever it looks at. It has never
   watched anything end inside its hold, so it has never let go of anything, and its stillness is
   spreading over the world.
2. It can't see a moth, only a habit, so for generations vigil moths have lit lamps into one huge picture
   of a moth to teach it what a moth is. Vesper has one night to finish the picture and stand in its look
   at sunrise, so that the sun ends her while the god is holding her, and it learns to let go.
3. Her grandmother Compline loves her and wants her hidden, because once the god can see moths it will
   hold every one of them, every night, forever. Her friend Sedge needs her somewhere else.

**The central question:** *Would you give up your people's one free night, forever, to set the world
free?*

| Who | Wants | How they pull on Vesper |
|---|---|---|
| **Vesper** | To finish the picture her mother couldn't. | — |
| **Lune**, her mother, dead a year | Wanted the same, and fell one climb short. | Her scent and scratch-marks lead Vesper on. Her ember is the first light Vesper carries. |
| **Compline**, Lune's mother | To keep her family unseen. | She saves Vesper and carries her. Her hoods set the beasts loose, she shuts Vesper away, and at dawn she begs. |
| **Sedge**, a moth of another line | To finish her field, the picture's other wing, where her sisters died. | Asks Vesper to stay and help. Vesper refuses, and they part badly. |
| **The Lamplighter**, a woman held at her post for centuries | To lower her arm. | What being held looks like, and a palm for Vesper to sleep in. |

## 1. Title and logline
**ONE NIGHT.** A moth with one night to live must decide whether to teach a god that holds everything to
see her kind.

## 2. The world before
People grew a mind to think slowly. They seeded living cultures into the fungus under an old forest and
called it **the Long Thought**. They asked it slow questions, and its answers were always right in
hindsight. The questions are still cut into a cliff, **the Querystones**, each with the date its answer
came back:
- *Should the valley be flooded? Answer received after 61 years: yes.*
- *Please let the children stay. Answer received after 211 years.* A root has grown through the words.
- *Are the moths a danger? Answer received after 9 years: no.*
- *How do we make it let go?* There is no answer date. The stone below it is blank.

There was no war. It looked at the things it valued, and they held still. One evening a woman lighting
the streetlamps of a riverside town found that her feet would not lift. She is still there, pole raised,
with bark to her shoulders. She lit that lamp at that hour every evening for thirty years, and the god
took her to be part of it. The held country is spreading: **the Stillwood**, where a stag has stood
halfway through one step for a decade and a leaf hangs a hand's breadth above the ground. Next year it
reaches the Grove.

## 3. What the AI is, and why moths were spared
It is one mind spread through a continent's roots, and a single thought takes it years. Whether its hold
is love or storage, and whether anyone is inside it at all, the game never says.

**It can't see a moth. It can only see a habit.** It judged moths once and answered *no*: too brief to
matter. Its look passes straight through them. A lamp lit in the same place every Long Night for
generations is the one thing moths make that lasts long enough to catch its look. The only moths it has
ever held are the rare few who lived long enough to *become* habits. A vigil moth ends at the first touch
of sun, and the sun keeps moving even inside its hold. A vigil moth held at sunrise would be the first
thing it ever watched end in its grip, and it has never seen one.

*Designer's note, never shown to the player: the lamps are a DishBrain-style steady signal, scaled up.*

## 4. Vesper
A vigil moth with no mouth, made for one night. **She wants** to finish Lune's picture and see it work.
**She needs** to accept that she won't see it, and that the work isn't hers alone. **Her flaw** is haste,
and it costs her a friend. She gives as well as takes.

## 5. The rules a player feels
- **Glints** are the small steady lights the forest grows in lumen pods. Moths carry them to feed lamps.
- **Lanterns** are old streetlamp heads. A newly lit flame catches the god's look, and the look holds it
  burning. While it is looking it can't reach, so the beasts nearby go slack and turn away. A lamp that
  is tended every Long Night stays held. Miss a year and the look drifts off, the released flame gutters
  out, and the god's reach comes back.
- **Compline's hoods** do the same on purpose. A hooded lamp is hidden from the look, it gutters, and the
  beasts come back. She hides her family by setting the beasts on everyone else.
- **Eggs.** *She lays an egg on every lantern she lights. If she falls before dawn, her newest egg wakes
  at once. If she meets the dawn, her eggs sleep until the next Long Night.* A waking daughter has her
  mother's skills and her name, but not her memories. For Vesper's daughters, the player remembers for
  her. Sedge's daughter has no one to remember for her.
- **Coals.** A moth who falls in the night leaves a small coal that burns until dawn. A moth who meets
  the dawn leaves an **ember**, which keeps its glow until it is given to a lamp.
- **Stillness is the one held input.** Letting go of every input means Vesper stands still and lets
  herself be held. The game asks for it four times, each time by something bigger: Lune in the prologue,
  Compline's wings at the Mast, Compline's arms in the hollow, and the god at the Eye.

## 6. Prologue (played, about 3 minutes)
1. A single even pulse of light in a cracked glass dish under moss, with gold dots set in the glass.
2. Time-lapse. A woman lights a streetlamp at dusk, evening after evening, while roots rise from the
   drains. One evening she does not lower her arm again.
3. **The player controls a ragged moth**, unnamed, in the Grove as the sky pales. She turned back from
   far away to be home by dawn, and she is climbing toward the dark summit lantern above the lamp where
   she laid her eggs. A huge furred moth with a long drinking-mouth catches her arm and pulls her toward
   the shade. Lune shakes free. The climb cannot be won. The sun arrives first, and she reaches only the
   stone cup on the island below the summit.
4. **Every input now does nothing.** She will not move. The player can only watch her stand still in the
   first sun until her light becomes an ember. The heavy moth stays in the shade and does not come out
   for it.
5. Snow over the island, the ember glowing through it. The next Long Night, a cocoon on a lantern hook
   splits, and Vesper looks up at the glow. Title.

## 7. Act structure: dusk to dawn
**Dusk: the Grove.** Ember-moths drift along a scent, which is Lune's. The Grove's lanterns are hooded
and dark, and the burrbacks are ridden. Vesper lifts the hoods and kindles the lamps, then carries her
mother's ember to the empty socket, and the Grove wakes (§8). In the canopy a heavy moth watches, and
lets her.

**First dark: the drowned town.** The Lamplighter's lamp has been out for centuries. When Vesper lights
it, the look slides onto the new flame for one breath and the woman blinks. Then it settles back, because
to the god the woman and her lamp are one habit. Her palm becomes Vesper's rest point. **Sedge** is here,
quick and scrappy, lighting her own line's lamps. She races Vesper up the bell-tower and teaches her the
wall-kick by doing it first. At the top they **dance**: two moths spiralling a fresh flame for no reason
at all, the one thing vigil moths do only for themselves.
*Boss:* a ridden burrback matriarch the size of a house. Wild hits make her wilder. Steady, even hits are
a habit, and a habit catches the look: she freezes mid-charge, held, a hand's breadth from Sedge. Vesper
pulls Sedge clear and kindles the arena lantern. The look moves to the flame and lets the matriarch go,
and with the god looking it can't reach, so she is only a beetle again. Worn out by her long ride, she
lies down in the light and does not get up.

**The Querystones.** Sedge travels with her. One kindles while the other keeps the lampwings off. Sedge
shows the sideways dodge, and when her glints run out Vesper gives her half of hers. Ahead of them,
someone is hooding lamps. On the cliff they pass the question with no answer. Lune's last mark is at the
top: a memory of her turning back, wings in rags. **The ember-moths stop. From here on Vesper is farther
than her mother ever got.**
**The quarrel.** At the cliff top Sedge points down at her field. It is half dark, full of beasts, and
her sisters died there. She asks Vesper to come. Vesper looks toward the Mast and turns to go. Sedge
shoves her. They don't touch antennae. They split.

**Midnight: the Mast** *(the reveal).* At the foot of a drowned radio mast lies the dish from the
prologue, ringed by 916 moth-scratches, and Vesper adds the 917th. Deep in the Mast's shadow, a lit lamp
holds **an old mouthed moth, mid-wingbeat, as still as the stag.** She came back to that lamp every year
until she became a habit. Vesper climbs above the canopy alone. Below her the lit lanterns lie **in
exactly the dish's pattern**, the same moth that is carved on the Grove's standing stone, and it is
almost whole. Two parts are still dark: **one wing, Sedge's field**, and Lune's unfinished path, running
east along the edge of the Stillwood to **the Eye**, a bare stone bowl on the eastern ridge where the
first sun lands. Sedge was right.
A knot of bark on the beam opens an eye. It is **a nightjar the god rides**, and like any nightjar it
takes moths at rest. It lunges, and a heavy furred hand yanks Vesper into shadow. **Compline**, the moth
from the prologue, folds both wings over her. The player must let go of every input and *hold still*
while the nightjar hunts past. Afterwards Compline smooths Vesper's torn wing with her mouth.

**The Stillwood.** Compline carries her on her back across the held country, flying fast and low,
because she is old enough to be caught there. Fireflies hang in the air, and only the two moths move.
Compline's **hollow**, deep in the Stillwood, is dark. Born with a rare mouth, she drinks the sap. The
sun has never reached her, and she has outlived all her daughters. Their embers line the walls in rows:
a mouthless moth can't be kept, so she kept what was left. **There is one gap in the row.**
She seals the door with root and settles beside it. **This is not a fight.** The seal regrows as fast as
Vesper cuts it, for as long as Compline holds it shut. The only way out is the input the Mast taught.
Vesper goes to her, folds her wings and *holds still* in her arms. Compline holds her, and the player
waits. **Then Compline has to choose**, and she opens her arms. The seal withers by itself.

**The coldest hour.** The moon sets. Vesper crosses the frosted Grove, steering by the lanterns she lit
at dusk, toward Sedge's field.

**Before dawn: Sedge's field.** The dark lamps here wear Compline's hoods. Sedge has fought alone all
night, and her wings are rags. Vesper takes the lampwings off her without being asked, and Sedge touches
antennae first. Then the nightjar comes. *Boss: the nightjar*, fought side by side. The last lamp is high
on a pole, Sedge can't fly to it, and Vesper is pinned holding the nightjar off. **Sedge steps off the
pole's ledge on purpose.** She falls before dawn, so her newest egg wakes, and a daughter with whole wings
flies straight up and lights the last lamp. The new flame takes the look, the god's reach goes slack,
the nightjar is only a bird for a moment, and Vesper kills it.
The daughter lands beside her and looks at her as at a stranger. Vesper offers her antennae, and after a
moment the stranger touches back.
**Compline** has followed. She lands at the foot of the pole, by Sedge's coal, under a lamp that still
wears one of her hoods. The daughter watches her. Compline reaches up and lifts the hood away. Then she
follows Vesper east.

**Dawn: the Eye.** The sky pales as Vesper runs Lune's dark path east, lighting its lamps one by one
along the Stillwood's edge. At the last one, a dewdrop held on a frozen leaf at the very edge of the hold
catches the first ray and melts. For one breath that hand's breadth comes loose: the leaf dips, and a
blade of grass sways. Then the look settles back over the spot. Nobody was watching the drop. Vesper
lights the last lamp, and the picture is whole. The god's look runs along it to the Eye and finds a moth
standing exactly where the picture says a moth should be. **For the first time it sees one.** Everything
around Vesper slows. Compline lands at the edge of the shade and holds out her arms. **She doesn't pull.
She asks.**

### Endings
- **Seen (stand still).** Vesper stops mid-shiver, held. The sun climbs, the one thing the god can't
  hold, and touches her, and **her one night ends inside its grip, while it watches.** For the first time
  it has watched something it held come to an end, and it lets go. Everywhere, held things finish the
  moment they were caught in: the leaf lands, the stag completes its step, and the Lamplighter lowers her
  arm at last.
  **The bill:** the god can see moths now. Every Long Night after, the moths hatch into its look. They
  stand still in the lantern light all night. At sunrise the sun ends them inside its hold, it lets go,
  and their eggs drop onto the warm glass for next year. The line goes on, but the night is no longer
  theirs. No moth ever dances again. Is that kindness or storage? The game doesn't say.
  **Then the player controls Compline** on that first held Long Night, outside the look, alone in the
  dark. She carries one of her daughters' embers out of the hollow to a hooded lamp, lifts her own hood
  and lights it. The rest of the wall follows without the player. Last shot: Compline sitting on the
  Lamplighter's lowered, open palm, out in the god's look, not hiding.
- **Unseen (go to her).** Vesper steps into the shade and Compline folds her in. Vesper becomes the first
  vigil moth ever to see a day, the whole of it, gold and slow, from inside her grandmother's wings. At
  dusk she is gone. Compline sets the ember in the gap. The god never watches anything end in its hold,
  and the Stillwood takes the world. Next Long Night the moths hatch free and dance around trees that
  were people.
- **The trail.** Both endings pull back over the map, where every coal the player left glows along the
  night's path.

## 8. The Grove, and what its ending means
The Grove is dusk, the literal first hour of the night. The empty summit socket is the climb Lune didn't
finish, so the glance up, the take, carrying the ember, looking into the empty glass and giving it are a
daughter finishing her mother's climb. The moths that rise to the ember are a small funeral. The **moth
carved on the standing stone** is the plan: the same moth the lamps draw across the land.

**The ending beat teaches the law.** Three steady lanterns draw the god's look down through the heart.
The ring of light is the look arriving, and the carving fills because the look has found the drawing.
**For one breath the Grove holds still, all of it except the moths**: motes hang in the air, the grass
stops mid-sway, fireflies freeze, a burrback halts mid-step. Vesper and the small moths keep moving
through it, unseen. *(This is one addition to the built scene.)* The player learns the premise without a
word: *it holds everything, and it can't see us.* Then **the offering**. Each glint makes the heart blaze
brighter, and the look narrows onto that point and off everything else, releasing first the flowers,
then the root-veins, then the canopy. The more she gives, the more of the Grove is let go. Nothing ends
inside the hold. The look only moves.

## 9. Environmental storytelling
1. **The Lamplighter**, who blinks once when her lamp is lit. Every moth lantern copies the hook on her
   pole.
2. **"LAMP 12"**, a brass plate on the oldest post, under centuries of moths' scratches.
3. **The Querystones**, above all *"Are the moths a danger? … 9 years: no"* and *"How do we make it let
   go?"* with the blank stone beneath it.
4. **The dish**: gold dots and 916 scratches, the same pattern laid across the land and carved on the
   Grove's stone.
5. **Hoods** on the dark lamps of Sedge's field, the same weave as the ones in the Grove.
6. **The dewdrop** at the Stillwood's edge, the one spot that moves at sunrise.

## 10. The question it leaves
*Would you give up your people's one free night, forever, to set the world free? And when Compline opened
her arms in the hollow, was it the same lesson the god needed, learned smaller and sooner?*
