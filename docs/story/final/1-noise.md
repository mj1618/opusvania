# NOISE

## The spine (tell it to a friend)

1. A boy drowned chasing a moth. His mother wished that nothing would ever happen again, and the
   living machine she grew made the whole world safe and still.
2. A young moth, Vesper, wakes her only friend, an old beetle stuck walking in circles. Every crack
   she makes in the stillness, the dam clamps down harder. Its great lamp hunts the last wild fire,
   and the old moth who raised Vesper dies leading it away.
3. Vesper reaches the mother's kitchen carrying that fire. She can cut the clock and free the world by
   force, or light the hearth, sit still, and let the mother choose whether to look.

**The central question:** is it love to keep someone safe, or to let them go?

## The cast

| | Who | Wants | Wants from Vesper | Pull on her |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vesper** | A young moth no lamp has ever looked at. | To be seen by someone. | | |
| **Hob** | An old burrback who has paced the same circle by the shrine all Vesper's life. | Safety; later, a life of his own. | To be left alone, then protected, then for her to stay in the circle; at last, to be let go. | He is why she starts, and why she keeps wanting to stop. |
| **Sallow** | The oldest moth, older than any moth should be. Keeper of the last wild fire. She raised Vesper. | For moths to survive the old way: unseen. | Come home and hide. | Home and safety. Her secret is the midpoint; her death is a debt. |
| **Maud** | A very old woman who walks the dam top with a lamp every evening. The last person alive. | For nothing to happen to anyone, ever again. | For the flicker to stop. She doesn't know it is a moth. | Her grief is easy to understand; her one deed is hard to forgive. |
| **The Lamplighter** | The great lampwing that flies Maud's walk and does what she wants without being told. | To keep Maud undisturbed. | For Vesper to keep away. | Guard, then Sallow's killer, then the one who grieves her. |

**Wim**, the drowned boy, never appears. He is present only in what he left behind: moths scratched
into stones, a drawing, a jar lamp.

## 1. The world, and how the Quiet took it

Sixty years ago, in the flood valley of the Wend, Wim Ferrier took a jar lamp down to the flood stone
one night to catch one particular moth. The river rose faster than anyone had said it could. It took
him and ten other children.

His mother Maud kept the valley's water. She grew a better keeper: living nerve cells in a dish on
her kitchen table, rewarded when they predicted the river *(dev note: DishBrain/CL1; never
player-facing)*. The first regular thing the dish ever felt was her longcase clock, and it learned
that time.

It held the river, so the town asked for cuttings. Every house in Lowmere, the town below the dam,
grew a dish on its windowsill and asked it for one thing more: hold back the storms, keep the fox
from the hens, keep Dad's heart steady. The dishes rooted together, and other valleys asked too. Now
one soft swell a second runs through the world, and every living thing keeps step with it. They called
it **the Quiet**. Nothing has died badly since. Nothing new has happened either: no child has been
born in the Wend since Wim's year. The people grew old in safety and died in their sleep, one house
at a time, and Maud is the last.

## 2. The Quiet, and why the moths were left alone

**The rule of the beam.** The Quiet sees through its lampwings' lamps, and what a lamp holds, it
keeps. Settled creatures were *taken* long ago and keep its time, so beams pass over them. Anything
else that repeats itself in a beam is narrowed on, and the third repeat takes it: it greys and falls
into step. A moth flickers, so a lamp slides off it; repeat yourself and the flicker stops, and you
become a pattern the lamp can hold. The player feels one rule: in a lamp's light, never do the same
thing three times. Only identical actions count: her three-hit combo changes height on every strike
(low, high, rising), so it never repeats, but three slashes the same way do. **Fading** is being
taken; Vesper re-forms at a lantern, where the swarm shakes her loose.

**Underestimated.** The lamps flinch from moths. In the Grove a lampwing's beam lands on Vesper, jerks,
and swings away. It reads as contempt, as if moths aren't worth the bother. Much later the player
recognises it as a woman on a riverbank turning her face from a moth. No one weighed the moths and
judged them harmless. Something couldn't bear to look at them.

**Is it someone?** Both, never settled. For: a faint shudder when a pod is cut; the Lamplighter
grieves. Against: everything else it does, Maud did first, and a lamp flinches from a falling leaf
exactly as from Vesper.

## 3. Vesper

She is free as nothing else is, and alone. Since she was a larva she has waited at the end of Hob's
loop, on a spot her own small feet have worn, and lifted her wings three times: their hello. He has
never once looked up.

- **Want:** for Hob to see her.
- **Need:** to let others choose, even when they choose to leave her.
- **Flaw:** she flits, and she forces. Every pattern looks to her like a cage, so she breaks things
  open "for their own good". Maud forced safety on the world out of love; Vesper forces chance on it
  out of loneliness. Neither of them asked Hob.

## 4. The forest and its creatures

Sallow's rule: *"Everything alive does something you didn't ask it to."*

- **Two signals.** What the Quiet holds swells evenly, all together. What is truly alive flickers
  unevenly, like a heartbeat.
- **Glints** are chance: a firefly flashing late, a spark off the beat. The Quiet seals stray chance
  in **lumen pods**, and cutting one lets it out.
- **Lanterns** are moth work: a glass bulb on a crooked hook. A lit lantern draws a swarm into tumbling
  orbit. Nothing in a swarm repeats, so no lamp can take there, and burrbacks turn back at its edge.
- **The ember** is a living coal that Sallow keeps in a stone cup on her island. She calls it "the last
  wild fire" and never says where it came from.
- **The moth-stones**: old flood stones where Wim scratched a moth at child height, each in a
  different pose of flight. Each teaches Vesper a move: she flies the way a dead boy drew moths
  flying.

## 5. Prologue (wordless, about two minutes)

1. Black. A soft amber swell of light, once a second.
2. Night rain on a rising river. Moths circle a jar lamp in the reeds. The water climbs a stone with a
   child's moth scratched into it.
3. Grey morning. A woman on the bank holds a small boot. A moth lands on it. She brushes it off and
   turns her face away. Hold on the turned face.
4. The swell spreads along the river and the water goes glassy. Years pass. Fireflies flash in rows.
   In a town below a dam, windows go dark one by one, and no new one is ever lit.
5. Blue hour. A very old woman walks along the top of the dam with a lamp, step for step. A great
   lampwing flies beside her. Moths cross her light, and she doesn't look down.
6. The Grove today. The fireflies flash in time, and one flashes late. A small moth rides one lap on a
   burrback's shell, steps off onto her worn spot, and lifts her wings three times. He walks past.
7. A lampwing drops in front of her. Its lamp jerks off her face and away. Title.

## 6. The story

### Act I: Noise. *With her: Sallow, then Hob.*

- **The Grove** (see section 8). Sallow teaches the moth way: never the same path twice, never be
  seen. When Vesper climbs to take a spark from the ember, Sallow blocks her with her wings spread,
  then steps aside. It is the first time she has ever given in. Vesper lights the lanterns and the
  heart wakes. **Hob misses a step, stops, and looks at her.** He bolts. She finds him shaking in a
  hollow, lights a wayside lamp beside him, and sits with him through the night. In the morning he
  follows her. *Move:* **Tumble** (double-jump).
- **Tollmeadow.** *Move:* **Dart** (dash). **Boss: the Choir**, a hill of fireflies that strikes on
  the swell and can only be hurt in the gaps. When it breaks, the meadow flickers raggedly and some
  fireflies have burned out. It was the most beautiful thing in the game, and you broke it. A moment
  later every swell in the world hardens, and the meadow is pressed back into rows. Far off, a great
  lamp leaves the dam and heads for the ember island.
- **Sallow.** Vesper races home, leaving Hob behind, and arrives to see Sallow out over the water in
  the Lamplighter's beam, leading it from the stone cup. The moth who taught "never the same path
  twice" flies a perfect circle on purpose, once, twice, three times, far beyond any lantern. She greys
  and keeps time. From the shore Vesper watches her fly that grey circle on the beat, night after
  night, until her wings fail and she drops into the water. The ember lives; Vesper carries it now.
  - **What changes in play:** the ember-moths now follow Vesper, and the lanterns are hers to tend.
    A lantern she leaves gutters out unless she feeds it a glint, so every rest now costs something.
- **The Ruled Wood.** *Move:* **Turn** (wall-kick). Grief makes her hard and fast; Hob keeps up
  anyway. She drops glints to him from ledges he can't climb, rides him through walls, hides under him
  from lamps. He starts to wait at the foot of every climb. **The Stag of Three**, sixty years at the
  same three sips, blocks the path. She startles it; Hob watches it run.

### Act II: Asked For. *With her: Hob, then no one.*

- **The Weir.** *Move:* **Drift** (glide). **Boss: the Weirkeeper.** Every hit makes the held water
  surge, replaying the original wound. When it falls, the sluice gates open onto the town below. That
  night, from the sluice top, Vesper sees the Lamplighter circling the place where Sallow fell, its
  lamp dimmed on the empty water.
- **The sluice (her doing).** Grief makes her push. The last sluice runs under three lamps, and Hob
  balks at it. She gives the charge signal anyway, again and again, the same call she will use on the
  Spillway. He goes, for her, and comes out the far side with his shell cracked. At the next lantern
  he stops. She lifts her wings three times, and for the first time in the game he answers: he lowers
  his head and backs into the light. It is a refusal. She feeds the lantern a glint and goes on
  alone, as she always has.
- **The Levee.** The old woman walks her round. At every flood stone she passes, her hand reaches out
  and touches the carved moth without her ever looking down. Vesper lands on her lamp, then her
  sleeve, then the back of her hand. Maud's eyes slide off her, and **in the same instant every lamp
  in the valley slides away with them.** The Lamplighter drives Vesper off.
- **Lowmere (midpoint).** A cracked dish on every windowsill, roots braiding down the high street and
  up the dam to one lit window. Every bed made. Eleven small coats on the school pegs, none newer. On
  the hill, graves in perfect rows, no child's after Wim's year, and a last stone cut with no name.
  - By the school door stands Wim's last flood stone. Its carved moth has a notch in one wing. **It is
    Sallow's notch**, the first time the player sees the match.
  - In the reeds below lies the jar lamp: a glass bulb on a crooked hook. **Every lantern the player
    has rested at all game is a copy of the lamp a child died carrying.** The rest points were
    memorials all along.
  - Sallow was Wim's moth. The ember is his lamp's flame, carried out of the flood; it kept her alive
    so long. "Never be seen" began that night: a people taught to hide by one moth who believed being
    chased killed a child. She died circling a lamp, as she once circled his.
- **Hob's choice.** The lantern where she left him has gone out. She finds him walking a new circle
  between the rows. He walked back into the Quiet because it doesn't hurt there, and the hurt was hers.
  He can't see her any more, and the beams pass over him. This is her debt. Nearby, the Stag lies dead in a ravine it never had to look at
  before.

### Reunion. *With her: no one, then Hob.*

This is her apology. She can't fight him, so she doesn't. She lands and **walks his circle beside him** on foot, under a
lampwing's beam. That is the one thing the rule forbids. The first lap, the beam narrows. The second,
it narrows again. On the third the lamp closes on her, and Hob steps out of his circle and puts his
shell over her. She made herself a habit to reach him, and he broke his to save her. He carries her
again.

### Act III: The Mother. *With her: Hob, later the Lamplighter.*

- **The Spillway.** They climb the dam face in a storm. She calls the moment, and he charges.
- **The Lamplighter.** It waits on the dam top. Its lamp is the only one that doesn't follow Maud's
  eyes; it watches whatever comes towards her. She wins by matching its rhythm twice and breaking on
  the third. Then it sees the flame she carries and knows it: Sallow's fire. Its lamp dims the way it
  dimmed over her water, and drops. It follows them in, grieving.
- **The Bellhouse.** Maud's kitchen. Nothing here is dusty or stopped. Everything is kept exactly:
  one cup, washed and hung on the same hook, and a cold hearth swept clean. Above her chair, the
  clock's pendulum has grown into a root as thick as a tree, and her heart keeps time with it. Vesper
  pieces the deed together herself:
  - the clock key, still warm;
  - a bright new notch where it was wound past its last tooth, on the night Sallow fell;
  - a child's drawing, turned face down on the dresser.

  Maud sits awake in her chair. Through the window, Vesper's ember-moths wait in their swarm.

## 7. The choice

Both paths are open. The Levee has already taught the player what each one costs.

- **Cut the root.** Vesper decides for everyone, as she always has. Maud's heart stops, face still
  turned away. Everything the Quiet took wakes at once into one night, and the river held for sixty
  years comes down on the meadows and the Ruled Wood. The herds and burrbacks still keeping time there
  drown in their sleep. The moths stay unseen for ever, as Sallow wanted.
  - The Lamplighter's lamp goes dark. It settles on Maud's still lap and doesn't leave. The Quiet's
    last eye grieves her the way she grieved her son.
  - At dawn the world is wild and green, and **Hob stays**. He guards Vesper while she rests, pacing a
    slow circle around her. She has her friend, and the moths have their hiding. But his circle is
    around her now. Is that love, or a new cage?
- **Light the hearth.** Vesper kindles it with Wim's own flame and sets his drawing upright: his moth,
  drawn as a scribble, because he drew it flying. Then she does the hardest thing she knows. She lands
  on the table in plain sight and **stays still**: no tumble, no dart. She offers, and she waits.
  - The Lamplighter moves first. It is **the first lamp ever to look at Vesper on purpose**: the eye
    that took Sallow, turned on her while she sits still and doesn't flee. Maud's eyes follow its
    light down to the table, and **Maud chooses to look.**
  - She holds out a finger and Vesper climbs on. Maud takes her other hand off the clock key and never
    winds it again. The pendulum hitches and swings on unevenly, as a healthy heart does, and over
    seasons the world loosens without a flood.
  - But Maud has only ever loved by keeping. Her hand curls gently round the moth. Her gaze lets go of
    the world and settles on the moths, and every lamp settles with it. **From now on every moth lives
    inside a beam**, kept, out of love, as she kept everything else.
  - **The epilogue is played.** The rule of three now holds everywhere, not only in lampwing light.
    Vesper flies among the young moths and teaches them Sallow's rule, because now it is true. One
    shot shows what the beam is: at dusk Maud sits at her window with her lamp lowered so its light
    pools softly on the sill, where a moth sleeps in her open palm. She watches it the way she once
    watched a boy sleep. It is a soft cage, not a punishment.
  - Hob stands in the doorway. Vesper gives him three wingbeats. He sees her completely and then,
    free, chooses the hills. This time she doesn't give the charge signal or call him back. She lets
    him go. Maud lives on, unevenly, by the fire.

Neither path is clean.
- **Cut** keeps Hob and keeps the moths hidden. It costs Maud, the drowned herds, and the person
  Vesper could have become.
- **Light** gives Maud her son's moth and gives the world a gentle thaw. It costs Hob, and it costs
  every moth its safety, for good.

## 8. The Grove (the scene we have)

The Grove is the first chapter, and its ending is the first time Vesper is seen.
- **The ember** on the island is Sallow's mother-coal. Vesper carries only a spark of it down.
- **Three lit lanterns** make a ring of swarms where no lamp can take, so the forest's own heart beats
  again.
- **The veins of light** running out to the lanterns are that heartbeat. They pulse unevenly.
- **The moth carving** is Wim's. It fills with light and teaches Vesper the Tumble.
- **The ring of light** is the first unplanned thing in sixty years. There is no cutaway: the Grove's
  even swell simply breaks into a flicker.
- **The offering** gives the forest back its chance. Flowers open at the wrong hour, and the canopy
  cocoons split early.
- **Then Hob looks at her.**

Scene changes: a shrine burrback becomes Hob (worn circle, her spot beside it); fireflies swell
together while the Grove sleeps and flicker once it wakes; the lampwing dive takes on a third repeat
in its beam; the island is Sallow's, her wing notched. The slice keeps the full kit; the full game's
Grove grants only the Tumble.

## 9. What the player finds

1. **Wim's stones**, with dated water-marks above each child's moth.
2. **Lowmere**: the dishes, the eleven coats, the churchyard's nameless last stone.
3. **The jar lamp** in the reeds, air holes punched in its lid, shaped like every lantern.
4. **Maud's first dish**, with a strip of tape on it in her hand: *"Let nothing happen."* These are
   the only human words in the game.
5. **The clock key**, still warm, with one bright new notch.
6. **Item: Even Heart**, a pacing knot. *"A healthy heart stumbles a little. This one never has."*
7. **Sallow**, as she steps aside: *"Sixty years I kept it. Don't keep it."*

## 10. What it leaves the player thinking

Maud kept the world safe by stopping it. Sallow kept the moths safe by hiding them. Both of them were
grieving the same boy. Vesper can keep everyone safe herself, or she can let them choose, even when
what they choose is to keep. Which is love? And if the Quiet only ever looked where Maud looked, whose
grief was it?
