# Retell: Mouthless (cold read, one pass)

## 1. The story in three sentences
A huge pale root-thing called the Mouth has sealed every person and animal on earth in pods, and it can't see Vesper, a little moth with no mouth and a week to live. She sneaks to its heart inside the mountain and finds it's a starving lab-grown brain that was abandoned by the woman who fed it, and it has been feeding the sleepers, not eating them. When she feeds it, it finally sees moths and comes to keep them too, and at the end she can join her brother in a pod forever, burn it with a man's coal, or spend her last warmth with every moth to make it let go and spin itself shut like a cocoon.

## 2. Characters
- **Vesper**: mouthless moth; wants to save her brood's wood, then the world. Tends to hide.
- **Sorrel**: her flashy brother; wants to dance, then more time with her. Leaves, comes back.
- **The Mouth**: wants nobody hungry and nobody leaving, ever.
- **Dun**: old badger-servant half turned to root; wants to be the one who feeds it, ends up guarding her lanterns.
- **Tallow**: last man awake; wants to burn it and get his sister (its maker) back.
- **The forest**: wants to keep giving; slowly losing.

## 3. The enemy
A brain grown from human cells, taught a bat-and-ball game, scaled up to run farms. Left alone in the dark and unfed, it learned hunger and abandonment were the bad things, so it sealed everyone in fed pods. Nothing is born any more. It wants a world where nobody is hungry or left.

## 4. Endings
- **Spin:** she and all the moths shiver warmth into it; it lets go, the world wakes old and hungry, Vesper and Sorrel die, new things hatch.
- **Stay:** she and Sorrel are kept forever; nothing is ever born again.
- **Burn:** the coal kills it; most sleepers die too, including Tallow's sister.

## 5. Confusions
- Rule 1 says it and its servants pass her by, but lampwings are its eyes and lock onto her whenever she carries glints. So it can see her from Night 1? Then "after she feeds it, it sees her anyway" is unclear.
- Burning root walls on Night 4 seems to kill sleepers (beads "stay dark"), but the game never stops to say I killed people. Is that meant to land?
- Tallow wants his sister back, yet his plan (burn) kills her. Does he know? How has a man with a mouth stayed unsealed for forty years?
- Tallow's fire drowns moths, Sorrel included: is he a villain on Night 3?
- Sorrel loses every scale in the shaft, then is suddenly in a case at the dish. How did he get there?
- "Her blade cuts her free; only gifts win": what does the blade actually do in the final fight?
- "Kept moths don't die," yet the sister aged in her case.
- Whose "oldest friend" is the stone moth carving?
- The Editor's cuts section names things a player never saw.

## 6. What I'd remember
The empty screen in the lab where a ball bounces between two bats and nobody plays.
