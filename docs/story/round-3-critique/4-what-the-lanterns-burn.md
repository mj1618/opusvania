## 2. Where it is already at the bar

- **Death means something.** "After it, every re-form at a lantern burns a glint", and the post-credits tree has "one hollow in its bark for every glint you burned to re-form after you knew." This is the Hollow Knight standard: the respawn system is the moral argument. The player is complicit through the one system they can't avoid.
- **Old Clasp.** You can only finish her while she shields her pods, and you're paid "the act's biggest haul" for it. Then at the lowest point Vesper Braces in front of the moth children and "becomes Old Clasp." That rhyme between a boss and the hero is Nine Sols quality: the fight you won comes back as your own posture.
- **The Taper's hollow.** "Hundreds of tiny faces scratched on the wall… She looked every time." In one image, the antagonist is understandable and wrong. It is the best line in the document.
- **The Grove re-read table.** The same kindle, calm, drop and offering read differently after the Hush, and the surface of the scene doesn't change. This is how Ori and HK reuse places.
- **Moth biology as the hidden power.** A moth "keeps its back to the light", and Moonward works "only where no lantern burns". Real biology becomes a traversal ability and a theme. "The first time anything human really looked at a moth" pays off the owner's underestimate idea in one shot.
- **The counter falling to zero,** one press per glint. It's a eulogy you perform with your thumb.

## 3. Problems, ranked

1. **The antagonist and the relationship are both split, so neither holds.** Tallow is mute, never enters the light and has no shared scene before the midpoint. Ori had six minutes of Naru feeding Ori before the loss. The Taper is the one figure who is mentor, believer and knowing accomplice, and she gets a prologue shot, one boss fight and a wall. *Fix:* make the Taper the face of the antagonist and give her screen time in every act: she hands you glints, she watches your kindlings, she leaves at your side the lantern you will later snuff. The heart and the Bequest stay forces. She is the person.

2. **Tallow's ending is Kuro's ending.** "Crosses the edge of the light for the first time. It comes out burning with the ember in its lamp, gives it to her and goes still." Kuro in Ori and the Blind Forest is the same image: a dark winged creature that shuns the light carries the light into the tree and burns. Vesper waking "in Tallow's folded wings" also echoes Naru taking in Ori. *Fix:* keep Tallow alive and give the cost to Vesper or the Taper. Or have Tallow refuse the light and Vesper go in for it.

3. **The ending needs a wiki.** Caterpillars eat pods, light pools behind the eyes, the eyes hold a stranger, her eyes hold a shard of the ember, and that is why Tallow followed her. That is five hidden links, and the climax ("Last she plants her eyes") depends on all of them. "Planting" and "burning" are never shown to differ. "Lanterns go dark everywhere" doesn't follow from one moth's act. And the rain-window belongs to someone the player has never met. *Fix:* pick one visible rule and show it in the Grove: a glint set in the earth grows a pod, and a glint set in glass runs down to the heart. Show the caterpillar's eyes turning amber on screen (shot 3 almost does this) and cut the "Tallow followed her" chain.

4. **The midpoint is a flashback.** At the Hush, "the glints merge into one sharp memory: the procession… from the human side. Weeping people carry keeplights in." That is the lore told as a cutscene. The real revelation is already in the room: a burrback "folded over a dark pod in exactly the 'calm' pose from every kindling." *Fix:* cut the human-side vision, or move it to the ending, and let the pose rhyme plus Hold (creatures stop hunting you) carry the turn. The player should work it out from play.

5. **The underestimate is told, not played.** "Nothing in the world has a model for that." The Bequest is a crowd with no attention, so nothing in it can underestimate anyone. *Fix:* give the lure a mechanical pull. Lantern light tugs a gliding moth inward from the first scene. Moonward is the moment Vesper breaks that model, and some keeper (a lampwing or the Pitcher) visibly fails to track her when she flies with her back to the light.

6. **The reversal is a known shape.** Moths devoted to a light that turns out to devour echoes the Moth Tribe and the Radiance in Hollow Knight. "The creatures you killed were the caretakers" is Nine Sols' Apeman turn seen from the other side. *Fix:* make the moths' faith more than wrong: it should be load-bearing. The doc says this ("The moths aren't wrong, only incomplete"), but the endings punish the faith outright. Let the Grafted ending keep something of the rite. Moths still carry glints, but down to the earth.

7. **The Grove's clues sit below the threshold.** A player won't read the pulse direction, or letters glowing for "one second". *Fix:* make the sim change mandatory ("creatures ignore Vesper while she carries no glints") and make the lampwing dive a snatch, not a wound. Those two changes let a first-time player feel that something is off, with nothing written.

8. **Too much lore.** There is the Bequest, keeplights, pod-wood, pitcher-flowers, the Censer, the Night of Wings, Tapers, the creed, the Bestiary, the caterpillar diet and the eyes. Cut a third. Keep whatever a place or a mechanic can show.

## 4. The one change

**Make the Taper the story's centre: the mentor who loves Vesper, the face of the rite, and the antagonist who "looked every time".** Make the lowest point her refusal, and make the ending's cost something between the two of them. That fixes the emotional core, the antagonist and the Kuro problem in one move.

## 5. Verdict

**Close.** The mechanics-as-meaning layer is at the bar already. The emotional core, the antagonist and the logic of the ending are not.
