## 2. Where it is already at the bar

- **Prologue shots 5 to 8.** Sallow "lays a wing across her face and gently turns it back up" is pure Ori: one gesture carries a culture's commandment, a parent's fear and the inciting crime, with no words. The burrback dying inside an unanswered call, and Vesper answering *for* it, gives her an act of mercy that is also her original sin. That is Nine Sols-grade moral shape in thirty seconds.
- **The moth biology.** The jink (anti-bat evasion), steering by the moon, and moths rising to the ember are real and specific, and they explain why moths were spared without anyone choosing to spare them.
- **Objects that hold history.** Wick's lamp has polished the stone into a mirror. The Mere has two worn ridges and a small third groove. The pine's rings have grown around the plaque's horn. Hollow Knight tells its lore this way: wear marks, not text.
- **The Grove's last frame.** Reply is lit for an instant by the ring, with its wing raised like hers. That is a perfect sting for a vertical slice.
- **The Keep Answering epilogue.** A lampwing swings its lamp toward a small husk. It turns the midpoint into the ending's grief without a word.

## 3. Problems, ranked

1. **The undecidability depends on facts the player cannot see.** "The same number in every playthrough", "where *the player* idled longest" and "built from recorded inputs" can only be learned from a wiki or a datamine. Inside one playthrough, the "pattern" column is invisible, and the "someone" column wins by default. *Direction:* keep two or three pairs where both halves happen on screen in the same moment, like the lit eyes under the moon, or the gift of the glint during the reversed route. Cut the rest.

2. **The reversal reframes the side characters, not the journey.** Ori has Kuro, Nine Sols has the Apeman, and Hollow Knight has the vessel's lie; each one changes what *you* did. Here, the midpoint tells you some lampwings were sad. Round 1 had a stronger beat, that Vesper passes the same sentience test, and round 2 dropped it. *Direction:* point the revelation at Vesper (see section 4), and let the census backstory be *found* at the Listening Rooms instead of narrated up front.

3. **The endings are derivative and the true ending costs too little.** Keep Answering repeats Hollow Knight's "Hollow Knight" ending: you take the sealed vessel's place forever and the world goes free. A dark mirror self that replays your inputs is Celeste's Badeline. Reply waiting at the edge of the light where you fade is the Shade. Moonward, the *best* ending, costs Vesper nothing ("two dark moths climb out"), and the Asking pays the bill. *Direction:* the hidden ending must take something from Vesper or the player that cannot come back, and Keep Answering needs a shape Hollow Knight doesn't own.

4. **The Silksong vocabulary is already taken.** A compelling toll, bells, a boss called "the Choir", a "Bellwether", and creatures turning toward a distant centre are close to Silksong's Choir, Bellhart, Bell Beast and pilgrims drawn to the Citadel. *Direction:* move call-and-answer into the moth's own senses (light pulses, wingbeat, ultrasonic clicks) and rename the Choir and the bell set.

5. **The internal logic breaks under questions a player will ask.** If creatures only move "until it is called", why do burrbacks and lampwings hunt Vesper between tolls? The lumen-pod glint "is still asking", yet lanterns are lights "that ask nothing" and are fed glints. The Unanswered ending says the Asking stops "as the first dish stopped when nothing came back", but moths have not answered for centuries. Why does one more silence end it? *Direction:* make it explicit that since the prologue, creatures answer *her*, and that is the combat. Say what a glint becomes inside a lantern. Tie the Asking's end to *the first ball*, not to silence in general.

6. **The heart of it is cold.** Reply is faceless, mute and adversarial, and Sallow turns away. There is no Naru or Shuanshuan, so nothing in the game is warm toward Vesper. *Direction:* give Reply one cooperative stretch that you *play*, such as a gorge where its one-beat lag is the only foothold. Give Sallow one late gesture that reverses the prologue.

7. **The hidden ending is over-built.** It has a control swap, then a sub-choice inside it, then the line "the only mind you ever saw in it was your own", which says the theme aloud. *Direction:* keep one choice and cut the explaining sentence.

8. **The owner's "underestimated" beat is lost.** Nothing ever judges moths, so there is nothing to defy. *Direction:* after the prologue, let the Asking keep classing Vesper as a moth-shaped glitch, and let its responses stay sized to a moth until the Mere.

## 4. The one change that would most raise it

Turn the reversal onto Vesper. At the midpoint, show the player in play, not text, that "face the moon" is also a trained answer. Sallow's wing across her face in the prologue was the training: moths are a call-and-answer system too, just one with a quieter caller. The gesture the player read as tenderness becomes conditioning, and Reply's undecidability becomes Vesper's own. That gives Moonward a real cost (she has to break *both* callers) and would lift the emotional core, the reversal and the structure together.

## 5. Verdict

**Close.** The images, the mechanics and the prologue are at the bar. The revelation, the logic of the endings and the warmth are not.
