# Critique: MOUTHLESS (round 2)

## 1. Scores

| Axis | Score | Why |

## 2. Where it already reaches the bar

- **Mouthlessness carries the premise, the hero and the mechanics.** It explains why moths were spared ("not worth a reach"), it gives her wound and her currency ("She can be given to; she can't take"), and it shapes the health system ("kindnesses"). This is Hollow Knight-grade unity: one fact that the fiction, the body and the systems all agree on.
- **The tally rim.** "The early ones are neat; around the eightieth they begin to wander." This is Ori's prologue logic, grief in one prop. The 114th line being scratched by a newborn at the lowest point is the best beat in the document.
- **"It doesn't remember her. It remembers what she did."** The tissue opens once in her wing-shape and closes again. That is wordless, and it is devastating.
- **The carving drawn from beneath.** It retroactively makes the existing moth-stone the saddest object in the Grove.
- **Thread found in her own body.** The answer to the reveal is a memory of her caterpillar life, and that memory gives her a new ability. It is Nine Sols-level fusion of revelation and ability.
- **"The same walk, the other direction."** Burrbacks hauling glints down. The reversal of the enemy's verb is the theme, staged.
- **Spin is played, not picked.** The player has to keep flying while the cocoon costs light, which is the right instinct.

## 3. Problems, ranked

1. **The central relationship is off screen for half the game.** "Her one relationship: the Nursling." But on Nights 1 to 3 it is a hum underground. Ori gives you Naru in minute one and Nine Sols gives you Shuanshuan early. Here the player is asked to love a petri dish on first meeting. *Fix:* the hearts are its mouths, so make every heart the same creature visibly learning. The Grove bud opens clumsily into "eyed wings". The Larder's heart opens better, and the Mere's opens almost right. A fed root-tip trails her through each region like a hand. The Warm Room should then be a reunion, not an introduction.

2. **The Firstling undoes the second revelation and breaks the doc's own principle.** "Never full" is moving because it is systemic: a starving thing dies before it can grow up. The Firstling turns it into a culprit ("drunk from every newborn since, which is why none was ever full"), which can be fixed with a boss fight. It is also the sympathetic part of the Nursling ("remembered hands"), so Vesper does defeat the sympathetic creature, against the doc's own note. *Fix:* cut the Firstling. Let the Granary's hoarding, or simply the world's locked surplus, be the only reason it was never full. End Act III on a feeding, not a fight.

3. **The memory rules contradict each other.** Three hundred years of six-month lives is about 600 lives, yet the rim holds 113. Each life "remembers nothing", but the roots trace the moth "by habit" and the Firstling "remembers". Afterhungers are "no one inside", but the Firstling is someone. *Fix:* choose one law. The mind forgets and the body keeps shapes. Habit lives in the roots, never in a self. Then the tally, the carving and the taught wing-shape all obey it, and the numbers can be made to add up.

4. **"Empty-handed, she is a ghost" breaks the game.** If nothing sees her when she carries nothing, the Grove's burrbacks and lampwings are optional, and the best strategy is never to pick anything up. *Fix:* her own light is always carried, so she is always faintly visible. Glints brighten her, which makes aggression scale with wealth, the way Hollow Knight's geo-and-shade turns greed into risk. Offer then becomes a tactical shedding of that brightness.

5. **The true ending costs less than it claims.** She dies at the seventh dawn in both endings, because that is a moth's life. The listed costs ("forgotten", "never sees what it becomes") are emotional abstractions, and forgetting was guaranteed from the logline. *Fix:* make Spin cost the player something tangible. Each loop of silk strips scales, her wings go clear like a glasswing's, and glide, then the double jump, go away mid-sequence. The last loops are done climbing on foot. She gives up the moth she is, not only her week.

6. **The derivative pull is strong.** An unloved infant that grows into the land's devourer, reached by a small light-bearer, is close to Shriek in *Will of the Wisps*. The Firstling's grievance at the hands that left is Kuro's grief turned around. "Remembered or forgotten", asked about a moth, is Hollow Knight's Radiance and Moth Tribe axis (the Seer's "remember the light"). A hungry mimic that is calmed because the heroine won't take is No-Face. The Spin epilogue, with a new brood, the heart opening unbidden and light running up, rhymes with the closing renewals of both Ori games. *Fix:* cutting the Firstling removes the Kuro and Shriek echo. Shift the closing question from memory to what is only this story's own: carrying what you cannot use. Replace "light runs up" with an image only this world has, such as the Sleepers waking in their cradles.

7. **A seven-night clock in a metroidvania.** Backtracking, optional areas and ability-gated returns fight a Majora's Mask timer. "Each night her wings hold fewer scales" is never given a mechanical meaning. *Fix:* nights are chapters triggered by resting at a heart, not a clock. Scale loss is visible attrition: the wings thin on screen, and the sibling dance overhead shrinks. That is the doc's own best timer.

8. **Lore that needs a wiki.** Nursling, milkroot, afterhungers, Firstling, warmbloom, Sleepers, Weaning, kindnesses, mouth-stones, Rootdeep, Holm, Larder, Combs, Granary. The larva reveal depends on knowing that a starving larva cannot pupate. *Fix:* halve the lexicon. Seed the biology wordlessly by Night 2: in the Larder, a starved caterpillar tries to spin, fails, and is taken. The flashback at the lowest point then completes a picture the player has already half-drawn.

## 4. The one change that would most raise it

**Put the Nursling in the player's hands from the Grove onwards.** Make every heart its mouth, learning her a little better each time, and give it a fed root-tip that follows her like a reaching hand. That makes the emotional core playable and wordless. It turns the Warm Room into a reunion. It earns the 114th line and the clumsy moth in the Stay epilogue. And it lets the "learning or love?" ambiguity build through behaviour, region by region, instead of being argued in Section 3.

## 5. Verdict

**Close.** The premise, the mechanics-as-theme and the midpoint-to-lowest-point run are at the bar. The absent relationship, the over-explained Act III, and an ending whose cost is mostly guaranteed are not.
