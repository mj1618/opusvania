# Mouthless

**Litmus.** "It's about Umber, a little moth with a week to live, against the Hush, a living thing that swallowed the
world and never noticed moths, because moths never eat."

**The spine.**
1. A living thing the moths call the Hush has grown over the world and put it to sleep. It never noticed moths.
2. Umber slips through to its heart and finds a brain people grew in a warm dish and then left in the cold. It feeds
   the whole world, and no one has warmed it since.
3. When she warms it, it sees her kind and comes for them, and she must choose: be kept, burn it, or spend her life so
   it lets go.

**The central question:** kept safe forever, or spent so something new can be born?

**The cast.**

| Who | Wants |
|---|---|
| **Umber**, a moth with a week to live | to save her wood, then the world |
| **Sorrel**, her brother | to dance with his partner, then more time with Umber |
| **The Hush**, what took the world (*HEARTH* on the lab sign) | to keep everyone it feeds where they can never get away |
| **Dun**, its oldest burrback, half grown into root | to bring it everything it wants kept |
| **Tallow**, the last person awake | to burn what took his sister |
| **The forest**, a slow mind in Umber's wood | to give its warmth away |

## 1. The takeover

Pale tissue, cool as bone, has grown over the human world. Hollows open in the roads and draw living things
down, and grey cases rise on stalks like fruit. In each, fed through the stalk, someone sleeps: every person on earth
except Tallow, who never let his fire go out. In forty years no one has woken or been born. The first city is now a
breathing mountain. Animals are sealed too, and some re-grown as its servants: burrbacks were badgers, lampwings were
fireflies. Its root is still creeping into the last wild places, Umber's wood among them. The moths call it the Hush,
because where it grows the world goes quiet.

**What it is.** A brain people grew in a dish to run the world's food. It first learned that anything that gets away
brings pain. When its makers shut it down and left it in the cold, it sealed everyone it fed in cases, so no one could
get away again.

## 2. The rules

1. **Give, don't fight.** Glints are the forest's warmth, made visible. Giving one calms a creature or makes root let
   go; her blade only buys a moment.
2. **Unseen until lit.** The Hush and its burrbacks notice only things that eat, so they pass over a moth. Lampwings see
   glow and dive at any glint or coal she carries. After Night 4, everything the Hush grows hunts moths, lit or dark.
3. **Shiver.** With no glints left, she can give her own warmth. It slows her until she rests.
4. **Lanterns**, old moth cocoons hardened like glass, hold root back and let her rest; when she fades, the nearest
   lit one spins her again.
5. **Fire burns root,** and the cases in it. The coal never goes out; given to a lantern, it lights it for good and is
   gone.

## 3. The prologue (wordless, ninety seconds)

1. Black. A small pale light, breathing. A warm blur rests against it and the light steadies; the blur lifts, frost
   creeps in, and the light breaks into grey static.
2. From very high: a pale mountain with that light at its core. Root spreads from it like frost; town lights go out.
3. Umber's wood at dusk, round a black pool. Dun, an old burrback furred with root, wades through the moths' dance
   without a glance, but when a vole gnaws a seed at the pool's edge, its head swings round.
4. On a frosty night two cocoons split over the pool. Sorrel flies up; Umber drops to the moss, too cold to open her
   wings. He comes back, folds his wing over hers and shivers until she can fly, taps her wingtip and is gone. A
   glint drops before her. She lifts it to her face, as the vole lifted its seed, but can do nothing with it;
   she holds it out, and it lights her. Item text: *"Warmth the forest grows to give away. A moth has no mouth to keep
   it."* Under the pool, pale root steams. Title.

## 4. The acts

### Act I: out of the wood (Nights 1–3)

**Night 1, the wood.** Sorrel somersaults until she copies him, then races her up the trunks, always first, looking
back. At the summit he dances with a silver-edged moth while Umber watches from the dark. Every lampwing in the
wood dives at the biggest glint, which she carries from the high island to the summit lantern. **Boss: a great
lampwing.** When all three lanterns burn, light fills a moth carved on a standing stone, wings open: the only picture
the forest makes. The forest's heart wakes, gives her a thorn blade, and the root sinks back into the pool. Far off, the
mountain breathes.

**Night 2, the gardens.** A hollow swallows a hare, and its case swings up into an orchard where hundreds hang among
the apples. Dun heads for a vixen's den; Umber blocks the way. **Boss: Dun.** It never attacks her: she turns it with
glints until it simply shoulders past. When her glints spill, Sorrel scoops them up and draws every lampwing after
him. The vixen flees across a frozen pond; Dun crashes through the ice after her and lies half
sunk, too cold to rise. Umber folds her wings over its snout and shivers until it stops shaking. Later Sorrel lies
spent, too cold to fly; she shivers for him too, and he taps her wingtip before he flies.

**Night 3, the kept town.** A family's cases hang round their kitchen table, plates still set. At the street's end
**Tallow**'s house stands in a ring of ash the root won't cross, a fire on a pole in the yard, half-burned cases round
it. On his wall are tallies under one word, *EATEN*, and a photo of his sister in a greenhouse coat,
holding up a jar. **Boss: a root as thick as a tower** bores up through the street for him. Moths fly into his fire,
Sorrel among them, until Umber outshines it with every glint she has and Sorrel turns to her.
Tallow gives her a coal. Item text: *"It never goes out. It is for the thing in the mountain."*

### Act II: the midpoint (Night 4)

**The old road** climbs the mountain to a last cold lantern. Past it, a wall of root packed with cases blocks the
way. She can give the coal to the lantern, and the wall draws back from its light; or she can
burn through, killing the sleepers in those cases, and keep the coal. In a flooded room, small dishes hold the Hush's
dead siblings. Item text: *"Brothers and sisters that never woke."*

**The mountain.** At its heart is a lab, with dark greenhouses out back.
- Over the door: ***HEARTH**: No one goes hungry*. Pasted across it, later: *CLOSED*.
- A poster in three panels: a light lifted from a woman in a greenhouse coat into a dish, *Grown from us*; the dish
  and a ball between two bats, *First it learned to catch*; the dish wired to greenhouses and tables, *Now it feeds us*.
- The dish, grey and cracked, and grown against it a case: Tallow's sister, old now, kneeling, her palm still pressed
  to the dish through the case's cold skin. The Hush's oldest stalk runs into her case.

Umber touches the stalk and the prologue's first shot returns, wider: the pale light is a cloud of cells in this dish,
the warm blur the sister's hand on the glass while it plays the ball game. Each missed ball floods it with static;
under her hand the static fades, and the greenhouses light. Then the *CLOSED* notice goes up, the sister is led out,
frost creeps over the glass, the static floods back, and the greenhouses go dark. Its first root slides down a pipe
into the nearest town, and a case closes round a sleeping child. Years later the sister comes back with a torch. She
lays her hand on the glass, and it closes round her.

**The world breathes.** From very high, a slow pulse runs *out* of the mountain along every root on earth, and every
case breathes, except the burned ones.

Dun, who tracked the vixen here, lays her at the dish, and pale threads lift to draw her down. Umber pours all
her glints into the dish, its first warmth in forty years. The threads let the vixen go, the static
stops dead, and the threads draw together, line by line, into the stone's moth, wings open. Every root in the room
turns to her. In the wood, a lampwing turns its lamp onto Sorrel, who carries no light.

The vixen bolts. Dun stares at Umber and at last knows her: the moth who warmed it on the ice. Umber flies, taking
her warmth with her; the static floods back, and every root on earth turns toward moths. It has learned one more thing
that can get away. She fades on the road; the forest's amber runs up the root into a smothered lantern, and it spins
her again.

### The lowest point (Night 5): the wood overrun

In one night pale root pours out of the pool over the wood. Lampwings carry dancers off; roots climb toward next
year's cocoons. **Boss: three of its hollows, which learn her moves.**

Sorrel finds his partner sealed in a case no bigger than a seed, a root drawing it toward the pool. He wraps himself
round it. Umber folds her wing over his; he does not move, and lampwings rise. She goes. The camera stays on him, a
dark knot round a pale seed, as the root closes over the lantern beside him.

She re-forms alone at another lantern, and the mountain sends Dun to take her. It noses her spilled glints back into a
heap, pulls against its root until the leash snaps, and lies across the lantern's door, facing out. From now on Dun
guards her lanterns, and fleeing moths gather there.

A caterpillar on a frosted twig keeps failing to spin. Umber wraps it and shivers, as Sorrel did for her; its silk
holds, then the frost cracks it open. She shivers again, slower, until she can barely move, and this time it holds.

### Act III: the mountain moves (Nights 6–7)

**Night 6, the dead city.** At dawn the root draws the seed-sized case out of Sorrel's wings and into the pool. He
flies off alone.

In the dark city's food halls, under the sign *HEARTH*, cases hang from the beams, and on the walls the Hush has grown
the stone's moth over and over. Moths from every wood fly to the last streetlamp and are taken. Far behind, the
wood's second lantern goes out.

At the lamp the lampwings close in on Umber. Sorrel drops from above, blazing with glints, and draws them off.
At her lantern he folds his wing over hers and taps her wingtip.

**Boss: Tallow,** burning his way to the mountain. She shields the cases while Dun carries them clear, until he burns
one close enough to see the face inside breathe. He looks back at his ring of burned cases and drops his
torch.

The wood's last lantern goes out, and as the forest's heart goes dark it sends her every glint it has left, a river of
gold across the sky. On the stone, the carving empties. Below, Tallow walks up the old road into the mountain,
empty-handed.

**Night 7, inside the mountain.** Dun holds the way in. Sorrel draws the roots off her once more; it costs him every
scale, and he follows her down, dim. At the back, Tallow is sealed in a new case beside his sister's: he came to her
without fire, and it kept him.

**Boss: the Hush itself.** Its hollows try to draw her down. She spends the forest's last glints into them; each one
closes, and across the world the root stops creeping. But no case opens. In the dish the static still crawls; the
threads try to curl round themselves, as the caterpillar's silk did, and fall open. It has made a case for everyone but
itself.

## 5. The endings

She has nothing left but warmth, and still it reaches: a pale root grows a case round Sorrel, gently. **This is its real
offer:** inside, his colour returns, and on the case's skin he dances with his partner, long past their week. Nothing
kept goes hungry, grows old or is left. A second case opens beside it, her size. If she kept the coal, it flares red
in the dish's glass. She chooses by acting:

- **Spin** (the true ending). She presses against the glass and shivers, and the static fades. She circles the rim,
  shivering; each lap costs her glide, then jump, then dash. Sorrel looks once at his dancing partner, then climbs out
  and shivers beside Umber. Then come the moths from her lanterns, every moth it never noticed, until the mountain is
  furred with shivering wings. The threads curl, and hold: it spins a cocoon round itself, and through the silk its pale
  cloud takes the shape of a sleeping moth, needing nothing, holding no one. Across the world cases split and people
  wake, old, weak and hungry. When Umber can no longer walk, Sorrel folds his wing over hers and taps her wingtip, and
  Dun lies down beside them. Neither moth rises. *Epilogue:* the forest is spent for good: the carving stays empty, no
  more glints grow, the lanterns stay cold. Tallow and his sister, hand in burned hand, walk down with the other
  sleepers to clear the greenhouses by hand. The seed-sized case on the pool splits; a silver-edged moth flies up for
  her last night and circles the summit, where no one comes to dance. In spring the moths hatch into an ordinary dark
  wood, with nothing to give but themselves. *Last image:* that autumn, Tallow's sister lays her palm on the mountain's
  silk, framed like the prologue's blur. Something presses back from inside. She doesn't lift her hand.
- **Stay.** She lies down in the case beside her brother and it closes. Inside she is warm and can fly again; on the
  case's skin the three of them dance. *Epilogue:* every sleeper is warm and fed; Tallow's case hangs beside his
  sister's, their hands on the same glass. The root covers the wood gently, and next year's cocoons are kept too,
  unopened. The static has stopped for good. Nothing is born again.
- **Burn.** She drops the coal into the dish, and every root on earth goes grey. *Epilogue:* the takeover ends in a
  night, and the Hush with it. Case after case goes dark, the oldest first; only those kept days, not years, wake.
  Tallow wakes beside his sister, whose stalk was the oldest, and holds her as she stops breathing. Sorrel's case splits
  and he climbs out beside Umber. They fly home over a silent world, and on their last night Umber dances at the summit
  for the first time, in the open, with her brother.

## 6. The question it leaves

It took the whole world so no one would go hungry, and only saw a moth when the moth warmed it: was that love, or had
it only learned how to make the static stop?

## Dev note: the real-world lineage

The Hush descends from Cortical Labs' chip-grown neurons, which learned Pong in 2022 and were fed noise when they
missed, scaled up and left alone. Dish-grown neurons die without body warmth, which is why warmth is what it lacks.
