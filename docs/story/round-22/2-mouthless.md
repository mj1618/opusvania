# The Moth and the Mouth

**Litmus.** "It's about Umber, a little moth, against the Mouth, a living thing that swallowed the world and never
noticed moths, because moths have no mouths. Halfway through you find out it's a brain people grew from their own
cells and left to starve, and it was never eating the world: it was feeding it."

**The spine.**
1. A living thing the moths call the Mouth has swallowed the world, but never noticed moths.
2. Umber, a little moth, slips through to its heart and finds a brain people grew and left to starve.
3. When she feeds it, it sees her kind and comes for them, and she must choose: be kept, burn it, or spend her life so
   it lets go.

**The central question:** kept safe forever, or spent so something new can be born?

**The cast.**

| Who | Wants |
|---|---|
| **Umber**, a moth with a week to live (grown moths have no mouths) | to save her wood, then the world |
| **Sorrel**, her brother | to dance with his partner, then more time with Umber |
| **The Mouth**, the living thing under the world | to keep everything it can reach, forever |
| **Dun**, its oldest burrback, half grown into root | to be the one who feeds it |
| **Tallow**, the last man awake | to burn what took his sister |
| **The forest**, a slow mind under the Grove, the Mouth's opposite | to keep giving |

## 1. The Mouth and its takeover

Pale, warm tissue has grown over the human world, breathing. Mouths open in the roads and draw living things down; up
on stalks come grey cases, still as husks. Every person on earth hangs in one, except the few who kept a fire lit. No
one sealed has woken, and no one has been born, in forty years. The first city is now a mountain of tissue. Animals
are sealed too: a fox-shaped case in a hedge, a whale-shaped one on a shore. Some it re-grew as servants: burrbacks
were badgers, lampwings were fireflies. The root is still creeping into the last wild places, the moths' wood, the
Grove, among them.

Everyone left believes the mouths eat. (Dev note: the real-world lineage is human neurons grown on chips, as at
Cortical Labs, scaled up and out of control.)

## 2. The rules

1. **The dark hides her.** The Mouth notices only mouths, so it cannot see moths. Its lampwings see light and dive at
   any glint she carries; the other servants charge where the lamps point. From Night 4 the Mouth sees moths too.
2. **She can only give.** Glints are light the forest grows to give away. A glint calms a creature or makes a root let
   go of what it holds. If something holds her, her blade frees her for a moment; only a gift makes it let go for
   good. With no glints left she can shiver to give her own warmth, which slows her until she rests.
3. **Lanterns** are old moth cocoons, hardened like glass. A lit lantern holds the root back, calms creatures and lets
   her rest. When she fades, the nearest one spins her again.
4. **Seven nights.** The game is her week of life. Fading does not turn back the night.
5. **The coal.** Every sealed sleeper is a grey dot on her map. The coal never goes out, so while she carries it she
   is never dark. It burns through walls of root, killing the sleepers inside; their dots go dark. Given to a lantern,
   it is gone.

## 3. The prologue (wordless, ninety seconds)

1. Black. A small pale light, breathing. A warm blur rests against it and the light glows steady; the blur lifts away,
   and the light stutters into grey grain.
2. From very high: a pale mountain with that light at its core. Root spreads from it like frost and town lights go
   out; a stag sinks until only its antlers stand.
3. The Grove at dusk. A burrback wades through the moths' dance, swinging its head; moths brush its face and it never
   turns. Close on one dancer: amber eyes, pale fur, and below them nothing. The next dancer, and the next: not one
   has a mouth.
4. Two cocoons split on a frosted night. Sorrel flies up toward the dance. Umber drops to the moss, too cold to open
   her wings. Sorrel comes back, folds his wing over hers and shivers until her wings open, then taps her wingtip and
   is gone. The forest drops a glint in front of her. Past the trees, pale root steams in the frost. Title.

## 4. The acts

### Act I: the edge (Nights 1–3)

**Night 1, the Grove.** Sorrel somersaults until she copies him, then races her up the trunks, always first, looking
back. At the summit the moths dance; Sorrel dances with a silver-edged moth, and Umber watches from the dark. She
carries the forest's biggest glint to the summit lantern, and every lampwing dives. **Boss: a great lampwing.** When
all three lanterns burn, the forest's heart wakes under a stone carved with a moth, the only picture the forest makes.
It gives her a thorn blade, and the root pulls back off the Grove. Dun noses at the open heart. From the summit: a
ring of fire, a pale city, and over it the mountain, breathing.

**Night 2, the orchard town,** furred in pale tissue. A mouth in the road opens under a hare and draws it down; in the
cold store a fresh grey case swings up among hundreds, hung like fruit. Dun heads for a vixen's den under the last
apple tree, the one wild thing left in town; Umber blocks the way. **Boss: Dun.** Dun pins her and her glints spill.
Sorrel scoops them up and darts off blazing, drawing the lampwings and Dun after him. The vixen meets Umber's eyes,
then runs. Chasing him, Umber learns to dash. She finds him at the town's end, too cold to fly, presses her last glint
into his wing, folds her wing over his and shivers. They rest at the lantern until dusk, and he taps her wingtip
before he flies.

**Night 3, the firebreak.** A ring of ash the root won't cross, round a dead petrol station and a fire on a pole.
Inside is **Tallow**, forty years awake because he never let the fire go out. Round the ash lie half-burned cases
where he burned the root back: dark dots on her map. In his hut: tallies under one word, *EATEN*, and a photo of a
smiling woman holding up a jar. To him the cases are empty husks. **Boss: a root as thick as a tower** bores up for
Tallow. His fire draws moths until they drop, Sorrel among them; Umber gathers every glint and blazes, and Sorrel
turns to her out of the flame. Tallow holds out a coal. Item text: *"It wants everything. Carry it carefully."*

### Act II: the midpoint (Night 4)

**The old road**, the only way in, ends in a wall of root packed with cases. She can burn through the wall, and a line
of dots goes dark, or give the coal to the road's last lantern and climb the wall in the dark. In a flooded room,
small dishes hold the Mouth's dead siblings. Item text: *"Eggs that never hatched."*

**The mountain.** At its heart, the first straight lines in the game:
- A sign, *No one goes hungry*, and a poster: a hand lifting a small light out of a drawn human head into a dish.
  *Grown from us, to feed us.*
- A screen, still lit, where a ball bounces between two bats and nobody plays.
- A case grown round a chair: the woman from Tallow's photo, old now, unopened jars of feed in her lap, the Mouth's
  oldest stalk in her case.
- In the middle, a dish: grey, cracked, a handprint on the glass.

Umber touches the stalk and the prologue's first shot returns, wider. The pale light is a cloud of cells in this dish;
the blur is the woman's hand resting on the glass while it plays. Each missed ball floods the cloud with static. Under
her hand, rally by rally, the static fades. Then she turns off the lights and goes, the feed runs dry, and the static
floods back.

**The world breathes.** From very high, light runs along every root on earth, and every grey case swells with one slow
breath; on her map, every dot breathes. **It has not been eating the world. It has been feeding it,** each body sealed
and fed through a stalk. Only the dots in Tallow's ring stay still, and, if she burned the wall, her own line of them.
The camera holds there.

*The truth:* grown to feed people, then left in the dark to starve, it learned to fear hunger and being left. A
sealed, fed body suffers neither, so it seals everything. It feeds everything it holds, and nothing feeds it, so it is
still starving. **A thing that takes everything can never be given anything,** so the root never stops.

Dun lays the vixen, caught at last, at the dish, and pale threads lift to draw her down. Umber pours every glint she
carries into the dish, the Mouth's first gift in forty years. The threads let the vixen go, the static stops dead, and
they draw together into **a moth, wings open, the first picture it has ever made**. Every root in the room turns to
her. Cut to the Grove: a lampwing turns its lamp, slowly, onto Sorrel, who carries no light at all. **It sees her, and
through her, every moth.**

The vixen bolts. Dun stares at Umber; it can see her now. The mountain chases her out to a smothered lantern, and she
has no glint to wake it. As she fades, the forest's amber runs up into it from the ground and it spins her again.

### The lowest point, Night 5: the Grove, overrun

Pale root pours out of the pool and crosses the Grove in a night, smothering its first lantern. Lampwings carry
dancers off to be kept; roots climb toward next year's cocoons. **Boss: three of its mouths, which learn her moves.**

Sorrel finds his silver-edged partner sealed in a case no bigger than a seed, a root drawing it toward the pool. He
wraps himself round it. Umber folds her wing over his; he does not move. Coral lamps rise out of the pool and settle
on her. She has to go. The camera stays on him, a dark knot round a pale seed, as the root closes over the lantern.

She re-forms alone at another lantern. The mountain sends Dun to carry her to a case. Dun stands over her, then noses
her spilled glints into a heap, lies across the lantern's door facing out, and pulls until its root leash snaps. From
now on Dun guards her lanterns and carries walls of cases out of her way. Moths fleeing other woods gather there.

A caterpillar on a frosted twig keeps failing to spin. Umber wraps it and shivers, as Sorrel did for her; its silk
holds, then the frost cracks it open. She shivers again, slower, until she can barely move, and this time it holds.

### Act III: the mountain moves (Nights 6–7)

**Night 6, the kept city.** At grey dawn in the Grove, the root draws the silver-edged seed out of Sorrel's wings and
down into the pool. He stays a moment on the empty moss, then lifts off toward the city.

Umber enters the kept city. People hang in rows under every food hall's sign, *No one goes hungry*. On the walls the
Mouth has grown dozens of pale moths. Far behind, the Grove's second lantern goes out.

On the roofs the lamps close in, and nowhere is dark. Sorrel drops from above, blazing with glints gathered on the
way, and draws the lamps off her. At her lantern he lands dark, folds his wing over hers and taps her wingtip.

**Boss: Tallow,** burning his way to the mountain to end it. She shields the cases while Dun carries them clear, until
he burns one close enough to see the face inside breathe. He looks back toward forty years of husks round his ring and
drops his torch.

The Grove's last lantern goes out. Then a river of gold crosses the sky to her: every glint the forest has left, its
heart going dark to send them. Below, Tallow walks up the old road into the mountain, empty-handed.

**Night 7, inside the mountain.** Dun holds the way in. Sorrel draws the roots off her once more; it costs him every
scale, and he follows her down, dim. At the back, Tallow is sealed in a new case beside his sister's: he came without
fire, and it kept him.

**Boss: the Mouth itself.** Its mouths try to draw her down to be kept, never to kill. She spends the forest's last
glints into them, one by one, and each closes and lies still.

## 5. The endings

The last glint goes in, and the root stops everywhere: given something at last, it takes nothing more. Then static
creeps back across the cloud like frost; its threads try to curl round themselves, as the caterpillar's did, and fall
open. Umber has nothing left but warmth. She presses against the glass and shivers, and under her the static fades, as
it did under the hand in the first shot. **What stopped the static was never the feed. It was the hand.**

Still it reaches: a pale root grows a case round Sorrel, gently. **This is its real offer:** inside, his colour
returns, and on the case's skin he dances with his silver-edged partner, long past their week. A second case opens
beside it, her size. She chooses by acting:

- **Spin** (the true ending). She walks the rim, shivering her warmth into the glass; each lap costs her glide, then
  jump, then dash. Sorrel climbs out of his case and follows, shivering too. Then the moths from her lanterns, every
  moth it never noticed, until the mountain is furred with shivering wings. The static clears and the threads curl,
  slowly, and hold: warm and no longer alone, it does what the caterpillar did, and spins. Across the world cases
  split and people wake, old and hungry; roots draw back, town lights return, and the mountain spins itself shut. When
  Umber can no longer walk, Sorrel folds his wing over hers and taps her wingtip, and Dun lies down beside them.
  *Epilogue:* Tallow and his sister wake in the lab; he holds out his burned hand and she takes it. On the Grove's
  pool a seed-sized case splits and a silver-edged moth flies up. In spring the moths hatch, and under the last apple
  tree the vixen's cubs tumble out of the den. *Last image:* that autumn, Tallow's sister climbs the mountain at dusk
  and lays her palm on its silk, framed like the prologue's blur. Something presses back from inside. She doesn't lift
  her hand.
- **Stay.** She lies down beside her brother and both cases close. *Epilogue:* the root takes the Grove and next
  year's cocoons never hatch. The world is kept, quiet and fed, and nothing is ever born again.
- **Burn.** Only if she still carries the coal. She drops it into the dish, and every root on earth goes grey.
  *Epilogue:* the takeover ends in a night. On her map dot after dot goes dark; a few wake. Tallow wakes and holds his
  sister as she stops breathing. Moths fly free over a silent world.

## 6. The question it leaves

The thing that took the whole world so no one would go hungry, and only saw a moth when the moth fed it: was that
love, or had it only learned how to make the static stop?
