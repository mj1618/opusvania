# Review: NOISE (round 15, the world clock)

## 1. Anchor gate: PASS

A player's first sentence would be "a little moth taking on a living brain that took over the world and put everyone to sleep." The Quiet is never backdrop. It is a grey-pink brain on every horizon, a root wall you "can watch come," a beat that slows on a clock everyone can read, lamps that hunt moths and snuff your lanterns, a wall that takes the Grove on purpose, and in Act III the sleepers of the whole world walking toward it. It moves forward in every act, and moves faster when Vesper hurts it.

| Owner idea | Rating |
|---|---|
| Biological AI has taken over the world | Central |
| Vesper the moth tries to save the world | Central |
| It dismissed moths and underestimated her | Central (ignored, then hunted, then too late, plus the moth-sized air-channels) |
| Sentience (the AI, the forest, the animals) | Central for the AI; minor for the forest and animals |

## 2. Craft verdict: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES

The core rule works, the central relationship is felt, and the ending answers the central question. None of the problems below is in the foundation, but the moth exemption has too many explanations and has to be cut down to one before production.

## 3. At the bar

- **Sallow's death is shown through the mechanic.** The moth who taught "never the same path twice" flies "a perfect circle on purpose, three times." The player has learned the rule and watches a mentor break it to save the fire. That is Kuro-level staging, and it costs no dialogue.
- **The reunion turns the rule around.** Vesper "walks his circle beside him," the one thing the rule forbids, and on the third lap Hob breaks his loop to cover her. She made herself a habit to reach him and he broke his to save her. That sentence is the whole relationship.
- **The world clock** ("the beat crossing it, the ring of wild green smaller, the gap between beats longer") puts the takeover on screen at planet scale for the price of one repeated shot. It is cheap, readable and frightening.
- **The Bellringer** is a boss with a backstory you play: you ring against his rhythm and "every person in the square blinks." The hope of the game rests on one wordless frame.
- **The sentience ledger:** "the Lamplighter grieves in a perfect circle." That one line keeps the question open better than a lore page could.

## 4. Problems, ranked

1. **The moth exemption has three causes (POLISH, top priority).** The draft gives three reasons the Quiet left moths alone: no mouth for the root; unpredictable flight ("weighed them, found nothing"); and the midpoint's "it learned not to look, from someone grieving." The beam can still take moths (Sallow), so "no mouth" stops explaining anything. *Fix:* keep two layers. On the surface, it judged them not worth taking. The midpoint reveals that the judgement was really Maud's flinch. Make the beam the only way a moth can be taken, and say once that the Quiet never pointed a beam at one until the Lamplighter did. Cut "no mouth" as a reason.
2. **The Light ending turns on stillness, which reads as predictable (POLISH).** If the Quiet fears the unexpected, a moth sitting still is the most predictable thing in the room. *Fix:* make the surprise the *looking*. A moth that stays in the beam and lets itself be seen is what Maud never did. Stage it as the reverse of the prologue: a moth lands on the boot and this time she doesn't brush it off. Then it reads cleanly as "an unexpected thing that doesn't hurt."
3. **The Lamplighter is a mood, not a person (POLISH).** It goes from guard to killer to mourner without a want the player can follow. Why does it grieve this moth? *Fix:* show one thing early. The Lamplighter was grown from Wim's room and circles the jar lamp in the Quiet's flood dream. Then dimming over Sallow is recognition, and "first lamp to look at a moth without hunting it" lands as the end of its arc.
4. **Sallow being forty years old is unexplained (POLISH).** "Far older than a moth should be" invites the question and gets no answer. *Fix:* she has lived on the jar lamp's flame. Keeping the fire kept her, and giving it up at the ember climb shortens her life. That ties her death to her one act of letting go.
5. **The lampwings in the Grove contradict "Act I, ignored" (POLISH).** In the build, lampwings dive at Vesper. In this draft their beams "jerk and swing away" from her. *Fix:* the Grove's lampwings are wild hunters, distinct from the Quiet's pale lamp-faced eyes. Or else they attack only what she's carrying (glints, the ember), not her. Either fixes the scene without rework.
6. **Forest and animal sentience is thin (POLISH).** The heart "beating unevenly against the Quiet's even beat" is a lovely idea that is used once. *Fix:* in Act III, have the Grove's uneven beat audibly answer from far off when Vesper lights the hearth. The forest gets a vote in the ending.

Watch item: "moths, light and a dreaming plague that takes sleepers" sits close to Hollow Knight's Radiance. The hero being the moth and the plague being a grieving machine is enough distance. Keep it there and don't give the Quiet any moth imagery.
