# Review: MOUTHLESS (round 15)

## 1. Anchor gate: PASS

A player's first sentence would be "a little moth with no mouth sneaks into a biological AI that put the whole world to sleep in its roots." The Hearth is grown from brain cells, with the dish, the Pong-style game and the farm contract on screen in the prologue. It holds every city, animal and person, and it has momentum in every act. The Hush advances in Act I and runs from Night 5. Lanterns go out one a night, the city's streets rise to cut Vesper off, and the final boss is the mountain itself. It acts directly through its root-mouths, not only through servants.

- Biological AI took over the world: **central**
- Vesper the moth saving the world: **central**
- Moths deemed insignificant, then underestimated: **central**. It is the stealth rule, the Night 4 reversal and the true ending.
- Sentience (the AI, the forest, the animals): **central** for the AI and the forest. The animals' inner lives are thinner and come through as images (the fox dreaming, Dun cleaning her face).

## 2. Craft verdict: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES

The core rule works, the central relationship is felt, the ending answers the question, and no player would name a source. The notes are continuity and load, not foundation.

## 3. What is at the bar

- **The rule that sees by appetite.** "It sees by appetite: what eats, or can be eaten, and nothing else." This grows the owner's "moths are insignificant" idea into a real mechanic: carrying is dangerous, empty hands are safe, and "every choice to gather is a choice to be hunted."
- **The reversal comes from her own virtue.** "Four nights of stealth are taken away by her own kindness." The gift that fixes the antagonist is the thing that breaks the hero's safety. This is a Nine Sols-grade reversal.
- **An antagonist that is wrong in a way that lights up the hero.** It learned "two wrongs, hunger and leaving." It feeds Mara at the lips and starves itself. The Hearth keeps and the forest gives, and Vesper, who cannot take, sits between them.
- **Sorrel told through play.** "He saves her by making himself the brightest thing in the field". Then he leaves, then he lets go of his partner at the dance to come back. That is a relationship carried entirely by mechanics, in the Ori and Naru tradition.
- **The final boss's verbs.** "Its blows are embraces" and "Offer is the weapon". The ending is a played choice that uses Shiver, set up by the frozen caterpillar. Theme lives in the controller.

## 4. Problems, ranked

1. **The gift rule contradicts itself (POLISH, fix before lock).** On Night 2 Vesper sets three glints in the orchard root-mouth "and it unclenches". That is a gift. Yet on Night 4 "for the first time in forty years, something is given," and giving is what makes it see. The forest also gives light every night. *Fix:* on Night 2 she gives the glints to the fox, warming it so it slips Dun's grip, or to Dun, and never to the root. State that the forest's gold has never been offered to the Hearth, only used to push it back. The first gift to the Hearth must be Night 4.
2. **The midpoint reveal is half spent (POLISH).** The spine already says sleepers are "fed and kept." The coal already kills a cradled sleeper. So "every sleeper in the world is alive" tells the player nothing new. *Fix:* make Act I read as digestion. Tallow's wall counts *the eaten*, cradles shrink, and haulers carry sleepers *in*. Then Night 4 flips it to feeding, and the turn really lands.
3. **Too much to hold (POLISH).** The outline carries six actors plus Mara, and Hush, cradles, glints, coal, ember, Shiver and Offer. Spine sentences 2 and 3 are each two sentences long. *Fix:* keep Mara nameless until the lanyard, and cut the ember or make it the same object as the forest's Night 7 answer. Retell the spine as: "A brain grown to feed a city put the world to sleep. It can't see moths, so a mouthless moth walks in. When she feeds it, it sees her kind, and she must choose between keeping, burning and letting go."
4. **The moths of the world arrive unseeded (POLISH).** The swarm at the rim feels like a rescue unless it is planted. *Fix:* from Night 5, moths from other woods gather at her lanterns, a few more each night, unseen and unbothered. On Night 6 the dance survivors follow Sorrel down.
5. **Echoes of Hollow Knight's Radiance (POLISH).** A moth against a mind that spreads through sleep, with a sleeper dreaming, sits close to the Radiance and the Infection. *Fix:* drop the dream imagery (the fox's running paws) and lean on what is distinct: feeding, breathing in time, being kept warm.
6. **The week to live softens the sacrifice (POLISH).** If both moths die within days anyway, the spin costs less than it seems. *Fix:* make the cradle's offer explicit. Sorrel's scales grow back and the dance plays on beyond the week, so the true ending gives up a real, shown future.
