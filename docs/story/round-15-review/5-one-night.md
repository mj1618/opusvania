# Review: 5. ONE NIGHT (round 15)

## 1. Anchor gate: PASS
A player's first sentence would be: "A little moth with one night to live takes on the Calm, a brain-cell AI that froze the whole world." The Calm is on screen and active at world scale in every act. At dusk its tissue is in the bank and the world's heartbeat skips at each kindling. At first dark its rider, the Matriarch, plants buds. At midnight it grows a bud on a nightjar to borrow eyes. Before dawn its front swallows half the Grove. At dawn its front races Vesper across the plain and its Eye opens. It acts through proxies a lot, but the front, the look and the Eye are the Calm itself, and they escalate.
- Biological AI that took over the world: **central**.
- Vesper the moth saves the world: **central**.
- Moths judged insignificant and underestimated: **central**. It drives play (she passes through seals, riders hunt lamps, not moths) and the plot ("Answer received before the question was finished: no").
- Sentience (AI, forest, animals): **central** for the AI and the animals (the Matriarch "chose to"). The forest's own sentience is **minor**.

## 2. Craft verdict: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES
No foundation problems block this. The core rule (it holds what is steady, it can't see noise, it can't hold the sun) has a clear cause, and it pays off: "It held her to keep her, and lost her anyway." The ending answers the central question with a real choice and a real bill.

## 3. At the bar
- **An antagonist that is wrong in an illuminating way, and wrong the same way as a loved one.** "Every loss starts with a change, so it stopped change." Compline's sealed hollow is the Calm's error at family scale. She opens her arms first, "the lesson the Calm needed, learned smaller and sooner."
- **Specific, chilling images of the takeover:** "children in a schoolyard under a ball that has hung in the air for forty-one years"; the billboard "LET THE CALM KEEP IT"; the cliff "Please let the children stay. Answer received after 4 seconds."
- **Stillness as an input.** Letting go of every input is how Lune dies in the prologue, how Compline hides Vesper, how Vesper escapes the hollow and how the world is saved. Few outlines get a verb this close to the theme.
- **The egg rule pays off in the story.** Sedge "steps off the ledge on purpose" so that her daughter can light the lamp, and the stranger touches antennae back. The hero's death mechanic becomes another character's sacrifice.
- **The wake beat teaches the law in the built scene.** "For one breath the Grove holds still, all of it except the moths."

## 4. Problems (ranked)
1. **The egg/lifecycle rule contradicts itself (POLISH, high).** In the prologue an egg waits a year ("Next Long Night a cocoon... splits"), but when Vesper falls, an egg wakes within minutes with "whole wings". Every fade also swaps in a stranger, yet Compline and Sedge keep treating her as Vesper. *Fix:* add one rule: a fresh flame quickens an egg laid on it within the hour, and an egg without a flame waits the year. At each re-form, the nearest companion re-touches antennae, a two-second beat of recognition the player sees. That beat makes "the player remembers" mean something.
2. **The moths' plan is unclear until the climax (POLISH, high).** The Mast reveals the picture, but not what generations of moths thought it would do. *Fix:* the ring of 916 scratches around the dish should include an older carving: a moth inside an open eye, at sunrise. Then the player arrives at the Eye already knowing that the plan is to be seen, and what being seen costs.
3. **It contradicts its own description of being held (POLISH).** Held people are "alive and unaging", but in the ending they "wake old". *Fix:* they wake at the same age into a world 41 years on. The schoolchild catches the ball under trees that were a street. That is stronger anyway.
4. **Too many names (POLISH).** Lune, Compline, Sedge, Matins, the Calm, the Matriarch, the Lamplighter and the nightjar are a lot for a game with almost no dialogue. *Fix:* leave the Lamplighter and the Matriarch unnamed ("the old woman", "the great beetle"). Consider folding Matins into the nightjar's role so that the borrowed eyes belong to Compline's daughter.
5. **Echoes of the reference games (POLISH).** A playable prologue that ends with the mother's death, a big furred guardian who carries the hero away from a hunting bird, and eggs/embers in a lantern-lit forest will all read as Ori (Naru/Kuro/Ku). *Fix:* make Compline small and earthbound (she can't fly, so Vesper drags her), and make the chase a hiding sequence on foot, not a flight.
6. **The Seen ending blurs the Calm's lesson (POLISH).** It "lets go of the world" but still holds every moth forever, which can read as if it learned nothing. *Fix:* keep the ambiguity, but give one image of the difference. It holds the moths only from the last lamp to sunrise, and lets each one go a breath before the sun takes it, so it watches the ending instead of holding the moth through it.
