# Review: Round 15, "Reply"

## 1. Anchor gate: PASS

A player would start with something close to the litmus line: "a little moth taking on a brain grown in a dish that took over the world." Ward is on screen from the first shot of the prologue to the last. The prologue opens on the dish and pulls back to the Crown. The call sweeps across every scene. The growth moves west on the map and onto the Grove's eastern trunks. Its servants hunt her (the Heron, the Keeper, the Held Hills), and in Act I it swallows her home. It has momentum in every act. Its goal is concrete and it acts at world scale. Every ending pulls back to show what happened to the world.

- AI that took over the world: **central**
- Vesper the moth saving the world: **central** (the Act I "decision" makes it explicit)
- Moths dismissed as insignificant and underestimated: **central**, though the prologue undercuts the rule (see problem 1)
- Sentience: **central** for Ward ("is it a someone" is the bet in every ending). For the forest and the animals it is **minor**: the forest heart has a voice in the Grove and in the third ending and nowhere else.

## 2. Craft verdict: GREENLIGHT WITH NOTES

The foundation holds. There is a clear antagonist with an illuminating flaw, a reversal that changes the meaning of the combat, a central relationship the player feels, and endings that answer the central question at a price. The notes below are about rule hygiene, crowding and one stock trope. None of them requires rebuilding the story.

## 3. What is at the bar

- **The antagonist's flaw.** "A world that can't fall silent can't answer either, so every flash Ward receives is its own call coming back. It took the whole world and is still alone." The motive is understandable, the error is specific, and it mirrors the hero, a moth who "can't pass anything calling for help."
- **A reversal at Nine Sols level.** "Every lampwing is a piece of Ward that a moth left behind, still keeping that moth alive. Wick is Sallow's." The husks among the elders mean the player has been killing grandmothers since the Grove. The two torn left wings deliver it in one image, without dialogue.
- **Mechanics as theme.** "The thing she fights is what keeps bringing her back." Respawning, gifts left at lanterns, and lantern circles that hold the growth back are all story.
- **Images that get retold.** The kept father with a child on his shoulders, paid off in the second ending when he lifts the child down. *DON'T* scratched onto the road sign. The Keeper's death, "the first death in this world, and it looks like rest."
- **A gesture that carries the relationship.** Sallow lays her wing across Vesper's face, Vesper lifts it off, and in the second ending Reply returns the gesture. A whole relationship told in three silent beats.

## 4. Problems, ranked

1. **The prologue spends the underestimation before play begins. (POLISH, must fix.)** The stated rule says that "whatever flashes back is kept, and Ward always knows where it is." Vesper flashes in the prologue, a Reply unfolds on the Crown, and "every eye in the Grove turns to her." Then the outline says the growth "doesn't register her" while she hasn't flashed. A player will read that as a contradiction. *Fix:* add one plain rule: Ward never looks at moths, only at their Replies. It follows Reply, not her, so as long as she stays a region ahead she is invisible. Show it in Act I: a heron walks past Vesper and stops over Reply. Her cover then wears thin because Reply closes the gap, not because of an abstract rule.
2. **The rule count is higher than the "cut to three" claim.** (POLISH) There are 3 rules for the call and the kept. Section 2 adds four more: a glass copy appears, you leave it by facing the moon, the left copy becomes a lampwing, and the lampwing keeps you young. "Reply" is also both a character's name and the general term. *Fix:* call the general thing "a glass" ("every glass you leave grows a lamp") and keep **Reply** as her copy's name only. Teach the lampwing rule only at the Roost, through what the player sees.
3. **The last boss before the heart is a mirror of the hero, not Ward.** (POLISH) "The Pale Vesper" is a stock mirror-boss, and together with a copy that follows her and a reconciliation ending it puts Celeste's Badeline within reach of a player's comparison. It also turns the climax away from the antagonist. *Fix:* make the Crown fight Ward's own: the capital's veins answering every one of her moves on the beat, the whole tower as the arena. Keep Reply's quiet refusal to join in.
4. **Act III is crowded.** (POLISH) Mote's loss, Mote's glass, Sallow's return, Reply's diversion, the Wick fight, Sallow's shield, the Crown, the Lake. That is too many emotional peaks in a row. *Fix:* move Mote's loss to the end of the Kept City, so Act II closes on a cost. Act III then holds forgiveness, Wick and the Lake.
5. **The first ending asserts its premise without earning it.** (POLISH) "She is the one thing Ward wants more than the world" is never set up. *Fix:* set it up at the Lab. The keeper's log notes that the moths at the lake are the only things that ever left a glass behind, and Ward has been reaching west after each one ever since.
6. **The forest's sentience shows up only at the beginning and the end.** (POLISH) *Fix:* give every region a heart with its own voice: a sound, a colour, an offering. Waking one makes its local creatures look up instead of east. This also makes the third ending's "thousand voices" something the player has heard before.

Also note: the lake's "dozens of paired grooves" quietly says that many moths reached the heart before her. That is good (they all made Sallow's choice), but say it on purpose in the log, so it doesn't weaken the idea that Ward underestimated moths.
