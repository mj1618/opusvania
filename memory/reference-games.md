# Reference games: what to look at when designing X

The research behind this is in `docs/research/story-direction.md` (2026-10-16): sales, the story-light verdict, and
the opening rules. This file is the lookup table. Find the thing you're designing and study the games listed for it.
Sales labelled "est." are third-party Steam estimates.

## By design problem

**Bosses**
- *Hollow Knight*: the Mantis Lords, Hornet and the Radiance. Readable telegraphs, a fair 1v1 that you learn by dying,
  and a name card. The boss's look says who it is.
- *Cuphead*: phases, each of which changes the screen. Pure pattern mastery.
- *Shadow of the Colossus*: the boss is a landscape you climb. One idea per boss, and killing it should feel ambiguous.
- *Ori 1/2*: the escape sequences (Ginso Tree, Kuro). A "boss" can be a chase that tests movement, not combat.
- *Celeste*: Badeline's chase. A mirror of your own inputs as the threat.
- *Rain World*: apex predators (lizards, vultures) roam the world as systems instead of waiting in arenas.
- *Nine Sols*: built around parry, so bosses are rhythm duels. *Hyper Light Drifter*: fast, punishing, wordless bosses.
- *Hollow Knight / Blasphemous*: after a boss dies, the world changes (a door, an NPC, a lore line). The boss is a story beat.

**The opening, first 30–60 s**
- *Inside / Limbo*: playable from the first frame, one continuous camera, the first rule taught by a death.
- *Journey*: one lit goal framed the moment control starts, with the title over it.
- *Rain World*: a ~30 s wordless intro with one causal chain (family, rain, fall, alone). The thing that separates you
  is the thing you fight all game.
- *Hollow Knight*: a short poem, then a fall, then you're playing. *Animal Well*: you step out of a flower, no words.
- *Ori 1*: the exception. A ~10-minute playable prologue built to make you cry, and only possible with hand-keyed animation.

**World mystery and secrets (instead of a plot)**
- *Animal Well* (no text; est. ~437K, 96%): three layers. Ability gates, then movement-tech secrets, then knowledge puzzles.
- *Tunic*: illustrated manual pages you find. Knowledge is the key.
- *Outer Wilds*: knowledge-only progression.
- *Fez*: a cipher hidden in the world.
- *Hyper Light Drifter*: a full plot is written as a hidden bible but barely shown. Ruins and bones carry it.

**Creatures and enemies**
- *Rain World*: creatures with needs that hunt each other and you, without scripted patrols.
- *Hollow Knight*: each enemy is a readable silhouette with one behaviour.
- *Dead Cells*: enemies made to be juggled, built for combat feel.

**Relationships without dialogue**
- *Ico*: holding hands is the relationship. Will you keep holding?
- *Brothers: A Tale of Two Sons*: one stick per sibling. Losing one loses an input, and the climax is a button press.
- *Journey*: wordless chirps between strangers.
- Rule: make the relationship a mechanic, never staged acting by code-drawn puppets.

**Emotion and tone without words**
- *Ori*: animation plus music.
- *Gris*: colour as progress, restored chapter by chapter. A global palette uniform can do this.
- *Inside*: the hero's breathing is the only voice.
- *Journey*: the music leads the player.

**Movement mastery and replay**
- *Celeste*: one-screen rooms, instant respawn, B-sides, assist mode.
- *Neon White*: medal times and ghosts. Our deterministic sim makes ghosts almost free.
- *Pseudoregalia* (est. ~392K, 97%): chainable movement tech and sequence breaks that speedrunners love.
- *Super Meat Boy*: proof that you can skip story entirely.

**Light, cheap NPCs and lore**
- *Hollow Knight*: Cornifer, Quirrel and Zote. Recurring characters with 2–4 lines per visit that change as you progress.
- *Blasphemous / Salt and Sanctuary*: lore in item descriptions, always optional.

**Structure fallback**
- *Dead Cells* (10M+): a hub plus biome runs, hand-made room chunks assembled procedurally, permanent abilities as runes,
  and a mute hero with physical comedy.

## Warnings from the market
- What players punish: worlds that feel "empty or aimless" (*Pseudoregalia*'s negative reviews) and games that are
  "beautiful but a very poor game" (*Ultros* and *Afterimage*, 77%). Negative reviews are about movement, combat,
  bosses, difficulty and getting lost, not plot.
- Being called a clone: *Haiku*'s only real knock was that it was too close to *Hollow Knight*. We need our own hook.
- Wordless cinematic games (*Inside*, *Gris*, *Journey*) are the most heavily *directed* genre. That is the skill our
  prologue failed at, so take their rules, not their scripted scenes.
